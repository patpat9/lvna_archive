    President’s Column 
    - Phyllis Murphy 
    “What part of No Turn On Red  do 
    you not understand?” 
    As all of us are or should be aware 
    there on “No Turn On Red signs” posted 
    at the off-ramps at Lawrence and 101.  
    They are actually there for a reason. It is not 
    safe to make a right turn on to Lawrence in the 
    mist of traffic traveling at 50+ miles per hour.  
    I have been almost hit several times, as have 
    my teenage children.  Someone who could not 
    stand to wait the extra 30 seconds hit my 
    neighbor.  It is very difficult to continuously

    X continued from previous column 
     
    see the same cars making unsafe illegal turns 
    on to Lawrence and then turn into our 
    neighborhood.  If you don't care that you are 
    breaking the law and putting yourself in danger 
    at least think about that fact that you are 
    putting one of your neighbors in danger.  
    Lakewood Village is a special place to me and I 
    feel that the people that live in this 
    neighborhood has a unique tie, a bond.  I do

    A thoughtful and generous Lakewood Village 
    resident proposed that we hold a writing 
    contest for Lakewood Village’s children and 
    young adults. He and other residents have 
    contributed the prize money to support it. The 
    contest is open to Lakewood Village residents 
    from Kindergarten to the 12th grade.

    essay telling us what you would do if you were 
    in charge of your neighborhood. Use your 
    imagination. Here are some ideas to help you 
    start, or come up with your own idea!

    how would you solve it?

    • What would you ask the City to do? 
    • What makes Lakewood Village a great place 
    to live in?

    This Month in Lakewood History… 
    - Brian Smithson 
     
    44 years ago: In 1959, A “Lakewood Village 
    Aquatic Club” was announced. It was to 
    be a members-only club at Manzano 
    and Oak Creek, with 2 adult pools, 1 
    child pool, clubhouse, outdoor games 
    area for croquet and horseshoes, and 
     plans for miniature golf and tennis  
     
    X continued in next column 
    X continued from previous column 
     
              courts. Eventually, something was built, 
    but later torn down. Does anyone 
    remember or have information 
    about this? Please contact me! 
     
    40 years ago:  In 1963, the president of the 
    LVRA proclaimed the month of May to 
    be “Lakewood Beautiful” month. 
    Shouldn’t every month should be 
    “Lakewood Beautiful” month? 
     
    36 years ago: Speaking of beauty, in May 1967 
    the first (and only?) “Miss Lakewood 
    Village” beauty contest was held. 
    Entrants were judged for poise, talent, 
    and beauty. No formalwear was 
    required, but they did need to display 
    themselves in one-piece bathing suits.  
     
    32 years ago: At the other end of the beauty 
    scale, in May 1971, citizens of 
    Lakewood Village and surrounding areas 
    mounted a successful campaign to stop 
    the construction of a slaughterhouse 
    located (I believe) where there is now 
    one of the mobile home parks. 
     
    Share your history! 
    Are you a long-time resident of Lakewood 
    Village? Do you have old photographs, real 
    estate brochures, newspaper clippings, or old 
    LVRA/LVNA newsletters sitting in your garage? 
    I’ll copy items and return the originals. Call 
    738-5939 or email editor@lvna.net.  

    Thank You! 
     
    On April 10, our West Highland White 
    Terrier, Brodie, managed to dig under 
    two fences and get out of our back yard.  
    My husband scoured the neighborhood and 
    Fairwood Park that night looking for him 
    but to no avail.  The next morning as my 
    husband was leaving for work Brodie was 
    tied to a tree in our front yard. 
     
    We have just learned today (April 15) 
    that Mr. Trotter, our neighbor two doors 
    away on Prescott Ave., was the kind 
    person who returned Brodie to us.  I 
    would like to thank Mr. Trotter and 
    express our gratitude for recognizing 
    Brodie and returning him us. 
     
    A very big THANK YOU, Mr. Trotter. 
     
    - Regards, 
      Peter and Marian Poon

    Sunnyvale Opens Skatepark 
    Facility considered one of the best 
    in Northern California 
     
    Skateboarders will have a new home in 
    Sunnyvale as the City opened its new skatepark 
    on Saturday, May 3. 
     
    The 18,500 square foot facility includes areas 
    for both bowl/vertical and street course 
    varieties of skating. Ma ny of those who have 
    reviewed the design consider this to be the 
    finest public skatepark in Northern California. 
     
    “We have always felt the importance of 
    providing City recreational facilities that meet 
    the needs of our community,” said Sunnyvale 
    Mayor Julia Miller. “That’s why our City Council 
    recommended construction of this park and why 
    we are particularly pleased to see the project 
    open to an eager public.” 
    X continued in next column 
    X continued from previous column 
     
    The skatepark is located at Fair Oaks Park, 540 
    Fair Oaks Ave., in Sunnyvale, and will be open 
    every day, 7 a.m. to sunset. Use of the 
    skatepark is similar to other park features: it is 
    unsupervised and is used at the participant’s 
    own risk. Skaters are required to wear a 
    helmet, elbow and knee pads.
