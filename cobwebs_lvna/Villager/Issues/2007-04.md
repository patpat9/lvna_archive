    President’s Column 
    -Michael Majchrowicz 
    As the new president of the Lakewood Village 
    Neighborhood Association, I hope to have the 
    opportunity to meet and work with as many of you as 
    possible to make Lakewood Village a great place to live 
    and play. 
    A short bio: I have lived in Lakewood Village since 
    childhood. I grew up playing at the Fairwood and 
    Lakewood parks, playing two-hand-touch football in the 
    street, and playing Little League baseball (which was the 
    only sport I was really terrible at). 
    I am an alumnus of Grove City College, in western 
    Pennsylvania, an hour from Pittsburgh. I graduated with 
    a degree in Business Communications and then obtained 
    my real estate license. I am a Realtor with Coldwell 
    Banker, and am partnered with the #2 agent in all of 
    Silicon Valley, Bill Gorman. This is one area in 
    particular where I would like to serve the needs of 
    Lakewood Village. Here are some of the areas I want to 
    focus on this year:  
    Our Homes. Many people have done small or extensive 
    remodeling to their homes. Even something like having 
    your home repainted or your yard landscaped can make a 
    significant difference in the look of our neighborhood, 
    and I commend those who have made the effort to do so. 
    I believe that property ownership is the greatest right in a 
    free society, and we need to take pride in ownership. It is 
    no different for those of us who rent. We need to take 
    pride in our homes and in our neighborhood. In the past, 
    we have received help from the city to assist some 
    people in updating their homes, and I will work to get 
    this started again. 
    Safety. Recently, some disturbing events have been 
    reported in our neighborhood, such as a young boy being 
    robbed at knife-point on the Lawrence Expressway 
     
    overpass. I have younger brothers and sisters, and their 
    safety comes first and foremost with me. I want this to 
    be a safe neighborhood for children. Come to the general 
    meetings to hear what has been happening in the 
    neighborhood, and also to meet other residents of 
    Lakewood Village so that we can create a stronger 
    neighborhood watch. We are unlikely to look out for 
    those we do not know, but if we build relationships with 
    each other and respect each other, a lot of good can be 
    done. 
    Of course we will continue to do the signature 
    Lakewood Village activities like Dumpster Day, the 
    Neighborhood Picnic, the Holiday Parade, and the 
    Lighting Contest. Thanks to those of you who 
    consistently volunteer your time! For those who haven’t 
    had an opportunity to volunteer, please contact me or 
    anyone from the LVNA if you would like to serve in any 
    way. 
    I am looking forward to this year, and am hoping for the 
    support of all of you in Lakewood Village. I understand 
    the time constraints of many; we all have activities that 
    keep us busy. But it is the small things that can make a 
    difference.  Come to the LVNA general meetings, greet 
    your neighbors, take pride in your homes, and make 
    Lakewood Village a great place to live. ■

    Register for Kindergarten  
    Kindergarten registration for both Village schools is 
    from 10:00 a.m. to 2:00 p.m. Monday through Thursday. 
    A registration packet can be picked up in the office of 
    Fairwood or Lakewood school. Incoming 
    kindergarteners must be 5 years old before December 2. 
    The parent must present: three proofs of residence 
    (current picture ID–California driver license or ID, a 
    current mortgage statement or lease agreement, and 
    another ID—current-month PG&E bill, bank statement, 
    or car registration, etc. (no phone bills are accepted); 
    immunization records; and verification of birth, to 
    register your child for kindergarten. ■

        April in Your Yard and Garden 
    When the garden soil is dry enough to work in 
    without the soil clinging to the shovel, dig in lavish 
    quantities of compost or other organic amendments. 
    This is particularly important in the vegetable garden. 
    ☼ Before digging into the spring gardening season, 
    clean and repair garden tools; sharpen the blades of 
    pruning tools and shovels; service the lawn mower. 
    ☼ When spring-blooming bulbs fade, leave the dying 
    foliage intact to draw nutrients down into the bulb for 
    next year’s bloom. To cover the fading bulb foliage, 
    set out six-packs of lobelia and sweet alyssum. 
    ☼ Begin weeding while the weeds are young and the 
    ground is still wet. 
    ☼ In preparation for the coming hot, dry season, 
    apply a mulch. 
    ☼ Take cuttings for new plants from azaleas, 
    carnations, chrysanthemums, geraniums, and 
    succulents. 
    ☼ Fertilize roses and crape myrtles. Fertilize azaleas, 
    camellias, and rhododendron when they finish 
    blooming. If you did not fertilize them last month, 
    fertilize citrus, flowering plants about to bloom, trees, 
    shrubs, and cool-season lawns. 
    ☼ Prune spring-blooming plants as they finish 
    blooming. Prune hydrangeas, fuchsias, and 
    bougainvilleas. 
    ☼ Plant out summer bulbs. Set out summerflowering plants. 
    ☼ Watch out for snails and earwigs, both of which

    Get Rid Of Your Unwanted Vehicle–Free 
    Sunnyvale residents who have a vehicle taking up 
    parking space may have the vehicle disposed of at no 
    cost during Sunnyvale’s Spring Clean-up Tow-away 
    program.  
    Through April 30, residents may call the Sunnyvale 
    Department of Public Safety (DPS) and request a 
    pick-up appointment. Towing will take place in May. 
    Requests will be sent to a city-approved towing 
    company, which will call residents directly to arrange 
    for pick-up of the vehicle. Vehicle owners must sign 
    over ownership and turn in the vehicle’s pink slip to 
    the approved towing company. Vehicles must be 
    towable, with four inflated tires, and have all debris 
    and garbage removed.  
    Vehicles removed during the spring clean-up do not 
    qualify as an IRS tax donation. For additional 
    information on the program and to schedule your 
    vehicle pick-up, contact Michelle Tani in DPS at 
    (408) 730-7104, TDD (408) 730-7501. ■

    CitySkills Session Begins April 19 
    CitySkills, a community education program, teaches 
    people living and working in Sunnyvale how to 
    effectively address issues with city government and 
    take a leadership role in the community. CitySkills 
    presentations combine instructor material with 
    opportunities for student input. Students are 
    encouraged to ask questions and will even participate 
    in a mock Council Meeting. 
    The next session of CitySkills begins April 19, and 
    runs for four consecutive Thursday evenings, through 
    May 10. The cost for CitySkills is just $15, which can 
    be substituted for 2 volunteer hours with the City of 
    Sunnyvale. 
    If you are interested in attending CitySkills, please 
    download an application from CitySkills.in 
    Sunnyvale.com or contact Nathan Truitt, Community 
    Outreach coordinator at (408) 730-7472 or 
    ntruitt@ci.sunnyvale.ca.us. ■

    Exchange Your Mercury Fever Thermometer 
    Mercury is a toxic substance, yet many of us still have 
    mercury-based thermometers in our medicine 
    cabinets. On Friday, April 20, the City of Sunnyvale 
    will hold a fever thermometer exchange at the 
    Sunnyvale Community Center, 550 E. Remington 
    Drive, from 10 a.m. to 2 p.m., and again from 4 to 6 
    p.m. To avoid breakage and contamination, the 
    thermometer must be in a closed, unbreakable 
    container, such as the original case or a soda or water 
    bottle with screw-on cap. That container must be put 
    in a sealable plastic bag. For more information on the 
    mercury fever thermometer exchange, visit 
    wpcp.sunnyvale.ca.gov, or call the Environmental 
    Services division at (408) 730-7738. ■

    BUYERS BEWARE! 
    My family lives on the Lakewood side 
    of our neighborhood. We recently 
    decided to try switching our ISP 
    from cable to a DSL connection. 
    After signing up with Earthlink I 
    noticed we could not keep the 
    internet connection alive. I spent 
    upwards of 20 hours on the phone 
    with their customer service trying 
    to get the Internet service to work, 
    only to find out from Earthlink that 
    DSL service is Not Technically 
    Feasible at our home address. 
    Needless to say, we canceled the DSL 
    and went back to our cable internet 
    service provider.  
    Orlando Banos 
     
    April Is Spring Clean-Up Month 
    During April, Specialty Solid Waste & Recycling 
    provides curbside pick-up of extra debris and garbage 
    for Sunnyvale residents in single-family, duplex, 
    triplex, and mobile homes. Just place debris neatly out 
    at the curb the evening before or by 6:00 a.m. on the 
    morning of your regularly scheduled collection day. 
    Do not put any of your curbside material on top of 
    water meter boxes, and do not include hazardous 
    waste (paint, thinners, pesticides, etc.).  
    Note: Drivers will start collection at 6:00 a.m. during 
    Extra Garbage Pick-Up instead of the usual 7:00 a.m. 
    Larger items will be picked up by a separate truck on 
    your regularly scheduled pick-up day. ■

    Health & Safety Fair Slated for May 
    It will soon be time once again for Sunnyvale’s annual 
    celebration of health, safety, and wellness. Bring the 
    family to the Columbia Neighborhood Center, at 785 
    Morse Avenue on Saturday, May 12, 2007 from 11 
    a.m. to 3 p.m. for the Health & Safety Fair 2007.   
    This fun, outdoor event features health screenings, 
    entertainment, activities for the kids and more. All the 
    activities are free! 
    More than 70 exhibitors at this event will provide 
    health- and safety-related information to everyone. 
    Children can play in the soccer tournament, take a 
    helmet safety class, or bring their bikes in for minor 
    repairs. Clowns, face-painters, child safety seat 
    inspections, and document shredding will also be 
    available. This event will feature entertainment like 
    the Perfection on Wheels Bike Stunt Team. 
    If you are part of a health- or safety-related 
    organization and would like to participate as a fair 
    exhibitor, or for more information about the fair, visit 
    HealthandSafetyFair.inSunnyvale.com or contact 
    Karen Rivers at (408) 730-7532. If you would like to 
    volunteer for this event, contact the Volunteer 
    Resources Office at (408) 730-7533 or visit

    Earth Day Events Include Citywide Garage Sale 
    Earth Day, Sunday, April 22, will be celebrated 
    through an array of community events in Sunnyvale 
    this year. During the week of April 16–22, the city is 
    promoting a variety of ongoing and new Earthfriendly events throughout the community, including 
    the annual Sunnyvale Citywide Garage Sale on 
    Saturday, April 21. This 15-year-old program is 
    designed to encourage circulation and reuse of 
    household goods rather than wasting or discarding 
    them in landfills. 
    Other Earth Day events include: a bird-watching tour, 
    Wednesday, April 18, noon–1 p.m., at 1444 Borregas 
    Avenue (near the closed landfill at Borregas and Carl 
    Road); a green kitchens workshop, Wednesday, April 
    18, 7–8 p.m. at the Sunnyvale Public Library, 665 W. 
    Olive Avenue; and a compost workshop, April 21, 10 
    a.m.–noon at the Sunnyvale Community Center, 550 
    E. Remington Drive.  
    The City of Sunnyvale is also joining a county-wide 
    campaign to promote the reuse of grocery shopping 
    bags. The city’s Earth Day programs are free of 
    charge. 
    For additional information on Sunnyvale Earth Day 
    programs, contact the Sunnyvale Recycling Program 
    at (408) 730-7262, TDD (408) 730-7501. ■
