    On Saturday, May 20, there was a community meeting at Columbia Middle School to discuss
    transportation options for youth living in North Sunnyvale. The meeting was jointly sponsored by
    Sunnyvale for Equity in Education (SEE), Fremont Union High School District (FUHSD), VTA, and the City
    of Sunnyvale. Vice Mayor and District 1 Councilmember Omar Din was the emcee. Mayor Larry Klein,
    Councilmembers Linda Sell, Murali Srinivasan, and Richard Mehlinger, FUHSD board members Stanley
    Kou and Rod Sinks, FUHSD Superintendent Graham Clark, SSD trustee Eileen Le, and SSD
    Superintendent Mike Gallagher all attended. 
    The meeting began with an overview of a recent transportation study that SEE completed. The study
    found that students who live in North Sunnyvale have a very different experience getting to FHS
    compared to students who live south of El Camino Real. As students live farther north they have longer
    commutes, rely on others (parents, VTA, even Uber) to get to school instead of walking or biking, are
    more likely to be absent or tardy, and are more likely to miss out on extracurricular activities because
    of a transportation problem.
    The next topic was an overview of transportation options for Fremont and Homestead High Schools.
    Superintendent Clark discussed district history and the impact of Prop 13 and declining enrollment on
    Summer School and bus options in the 1980’s (when Sunnyvale High School was closed), the school bus
    pass program, and a transportation survey sent to students. Students who have not already completed
    the survey should do so as soon as possible.
    In the last presentation VTA highlighted two important youth programs: the Summer Youth Pass, which
    gives youths 5-18 years old unlimited rides on VTA bus and light rail for June-August, and the Youth
    Outreach Program, which includes information on how to safely use VTA services. VTA clarified that
    they have aligned the bus schedule to match the FHS bell schedule. (Continued on Page 4)

    Students do not feel safe on VTA buses and are sometimes harassed by members of the public
    who also ride the bus. Students have witnessed drug use and have been bullied while riding the
    bus. One audience suggestion was for VTA to have a “transit ambassador” on the bus to reinforce
    the rules.
    Many students have to walk 10-15 minutes (or more) to the bus stop, and many students also have
    to take more than one bus to get to school.
    Buses are sometimes canceled with little notice, giving students no time to identify alternative
    means of transportation. Several audience members proposed that VTA provide more notice (e.g,
    at least one hour) for cancellations.
    Students are already busy and transportation should not add to their stress. Today, students have
    to go to the school office monthly to get a bus pass, and if they forget, they don’t have a way to
    get to school. Proposed solutions include giving out bus passes for the whole academic year or
    auto-refilling bus passes each month. 
    If students are late to school because the bus is late, they are marked tardy. If a student gets
    marked tardy too many times, it negatively impacts their school record and academic experience.
    Information on transportation for families of incoming freshmen needs to be shared when
    students register for high school. FUHSD should also come to the middle schools to share
    information.
    Free bus passes are helpful, but it is unclear who qualifies for the program. FUHSD and VTA
    employees also need this information so they can provide accurate information to parents who
    call with questions.
    The VTA schedule aligns well with the morning and afternoon bells, but it is not good for
    students who want to participate in extracurricular activities after school or on weekends.
    While biking is a good idea in theory, it’s not safe due to busy roads and bad drivers. It’s also
    hard to bike when students need to bring heavy backpacks, musical instruments, sports
    equipment, etc, to school.
    People were excited that FUHSD is doing a student survey, but frustrated by the low response
    rate. To ensure a high response rate, audience members suggested FUHSD have students
    complete the survey during homeroom and advertise the survey on social media/via text.

    RECAP: NATIONAL RIVER CLEAN-UP DAY
    May 20 was National River Clean-Up Day, which aims
    to bring together various organizations and volunteers
    to participate in a significant environmental effort. In
    collaboration with the City of Santa Clara and
    community members, the Creek Connections Action
    Group organized a comprehensive clean-up initiative
    along the section of Calabazas Creek that stretches
    from Wildwood Ave to Tasman Ave. Volunteers cleared
    debris, collected litter, and restored the natural beauty
    of the area. 
    With the support of the City of Santa Clara, which
    recognizes the importance of environmental
    stewardship, and the dedication of the Creek
    Connections Action Group, the clean-up effort
    received substantial backing and resources. The
    collaboration between these entities highlights a
    shared commitment to maintaining the health and
    beauty of the local environment. In addition to the
    clean-up efforts, the neighborhood officially adopted
    this section of Calabazas Creek as part of the Santa
    Clara Valley Water District's Adopt-a-Creek program.
    By adopting the creek, the community has made a
    long-term commitment to protect and nurture it for
    years to come. As part of this commitment, future
    clean-up events will be scheduled 2-3 times a year.
    These regular clean-ups will ensure the sustained
    cleanliness and well-being of the creek, benefiting
    both the environment and the residents who enjoy its
    beauty. This commitment to the long-term care of the
    creek showcases the power of community involvement
    in protecting and preserving our precious waterways.

    After the death of Sunnyvale resident and FUHSD (Fremont Union High School District) Trustee Roy Rocklin, the
    FUHSD began its process to fill the vacancy. Eight initial applicants were invited for a first round of interviews,
    which took place on Monday, May 1. From the initial round of interviews, three finalists were selected to move to
    a second round of interviews (scheduled for Monday, May 8). The three finalists were:
    The May 8 meeting began with a second round of questions for each candidate. Then there was more than an
    hour of public comment with residents voicing support for the three candidates. After public comment closed,
    the meeting quickly arrived in a deadlock - Trustees Naomi Nakano-Matsumoto and Rosa Kim supported Allison
    Joe, and Trustees Jeff Moe and Stanley Kou supported Rod Sinks. 
    All of the current FUHSD board members live south of El Camino Real. Two live in the Sunnyvale 94087 zip code
    and two live in the Cupertino 95014 zip code. In the history of the FUHSD board, only two trustees have lived
    north of El Camino Real (both in the 94086 zip code). The FUHSD has never had a trustee who lived north of
    Central Expressway (either the 94085 or 94089 zip code). The current board also lacks anyone who speaks
    Spanish, anyone who is Latino, and anyone who has kids in Sunnyvale School District (which feeds into FUHSD).
    Rod Sinks is a resident of Cupertino. He has substantial experience working as an elected official in Cupertino,
    but minimal knowledge about North Sunnyvale, an area that has historically been underrepresented and
    underserved. Allison Joe has not been an elected official, but in her time as a San Miguel parent, she built a
    strong, inclusive, and multi-cultural grass-roots community within a diverse, Spanish-speaking neighborhood
    within the FUHSD district. She speaks Spanish and her kids are in the Sunnyvale School District.
    After several hours of debate, the deadlock broke in favor of Rod Sinks. On May 16, he was sworn in as the
    newest FUHSD board member to fill the remainder of Trustee Rocklin’s term, which ends in 2024.

    California Park Spotlight: Lassen Volcanic National Park
    This national park is home to steaming fumaroles, meadows freckled with wildflowers, clear mountain lakes, and
    numerous volcanoes. July to September marks the busiest time as that’s when all the activities, services are
    open. The Manzanita campground is popular and camping reservations are required. Stargazing is particularly
    popular here as the park is one of the last sanctuaries of natural darkness and away from the light pollution of
    civilization. When planning a trip to this national park, also consider putting Lava Beds National Monument on
    your itinerary. More than 800 caves, Native American rock art sites, historic battlefields and campsites, can be
    experienced. More details can be found here:
