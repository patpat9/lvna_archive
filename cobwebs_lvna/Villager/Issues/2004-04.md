    April in Your Yard and 
    Garden 
    When the garden soil is dry 
    enough to work in without the 
    soil clinging to the shovel, dig in 
    lavish quantities of compost or 
    other organic amendments. This 
    is particularly important in the 
    vegetable garden. 
     
    ¾ Before digging into the spring gardening 
    season, clean and repair garden tools; 
    sharpen the blades of pruning tools and 
    shovels; service the lawn mower. 
    ¾ When spring-blooming bulbs fade, leave the 
    dying foliage intact to draw nutrients down 
    into the bulb for next year’s bloom. To cover 
    the fading bulb foliage, set out six-packs of 
    lobelia and sweet alyssum. 
    ¾ Begin weeding while the weeds are young 
    and the ground is still wet. 
    ¾ In preparation for the coming hot, dry 
    season, apply a mulch. 
    ¾ Take cuttings for new plants from azaleas, 
    carnations, chrysanthemums, geraniums, and 
    succulents. 
    ¾ Fertilize roses and crape myrtles. Fertilize 
    azaleas, camellias, and rhododendron when 
    they finish blooming. If you did not fertilize 
    them last month, fertilize citrus, flowering 
    plants about to bloom, trees, shrubs, and 
    cool-season lawns. 
    ¾ Prune spring-blooming plants as they finish 
    blooming. Prune hydrangeas, fuchsias, and 
    bougainvilleas. 
    ¾ Plant out summer bulbs. Set out summerflowering plants. 
    ¾ Watch out for snails and earwigs, both of 
    which devour seedlings. Use a strong spray 
    from the hose to wash away aphids. Wrap 
    fruit tree trunks with a sticky tape to control 
    ants. Wrap low so that birds, particularly 
    hummingbirds, will not be attracted. 
     
    These tips are from the book Northern California 
    Gardening: A Month-by-Month Guide, by Katherine 
    Grace Endicott, published by Chronicle Books, San 
    Francisco, ©1996, and are used with the publisher’s 
    permission.

    April is Spring Cleanup Month 
    - Brian Smithson 
    April brings us many opportunities to clear up 
    junk and debris that has been accumulating in 
    and around your home during the fall and winter 
    months. Here are some things you can do to 
    get rid of your extra stuff: 
     
    Sell it on Garage Sale Day 
    April 24th is Sunnyvale’s city-wide Garage Sale 
    Day. For more information about how to 
    conduct 
    a successful garage sale, go to 
    www.sunnyvalerecycles.org 
    and 
    click 
    on 
    “Garage Sales and Donations”, then download 
    their Garage Sale Tips booklet. Or call (408) 
    730-7262 and they’ll mail one to you. 
     
    Sell it online, or have it sold for you 
    Consider selling your item on an online auction 
    service like eBay (www.ebay.com). Don’t want 
    to be bothered with selling it online yourself? 
    Then consider using a consignment service like 
    AuctionDrop (www.auctiondrop.com) to auction 
    it for you. These are only two examples; there 
    are many more choices. Download the “Selling 
    Electronically” booklet or call 730-7262. 
     
    Donate it to a good cause 
    There are many charities that accept everything 
    from cellphone batteries to salvageable building 
    materials. For a list, download the “2004 Reuse 
    Resources” booklet or call 730-7262. 
     
    Give it to someone you don’t know 
    Do you have something that you know someone 
    must want, but you just don’t know who? Try 
    groups.yahoo.com/groups/freecyclesunnyvale 
    for information about how to find someone in 
    the Sunnyvale area who wants your junk.

    Summertime is here again: drive carefully 
     
    My family has been residing in Lakewood Village 
    for almost 10 years now. We really enjoy the 
    community here. My kids grew up here and 
    have several friends around the area. 
     
    In the summertime kids hang out with other 
    children in the community. They go out to play 
    games, run around, enjoy the sunny day and 
    good weather. We are lucky to have parks on 
    both sides of the village where children and 
    their families go to relax and play. Most children 
    are free spirited, they run, play tag, use their 
    scooters, roller blades, bike, skateboards, or 
    just simply walk leisurely. Others play in front of 
    their houses with other kids. 
     
    I just want to remind everybody to drive 
    carefully and safely maintain the speed limit. It 
    is scary to hear the screeching sound of a 
    vehicle when the driver steps on the brake 
    suddenly. I have seen people double parking 
    while chatting to the residents of a household. 
    Children may not see the approaching vehicle 
    because it is blocked by the double-parked car. 
    We should be parking our cars properly when 
    we do this. I also hear drivers playing loud 
    music and children might get startled and run 
    across the street. Occasionally, I see Sunnyvale 
    Police Cars rounding our village, but let us not 
    wait for the police to catch us, or worse get 
    someone into an accident for being a reckless 
    and careless driver. Let's be courteous and keep 
    our village a safe and fun place to be. Let's keep 
    our children active, enjoy outdoor activities, and 
    socialize with other children. After all we have 
    been children ourselves and we are here 
    because we have neighbors in our community 
    who genuinely care. Let's drive carefully for the 
    good of our community, today, tomorrow, and 
    everyday whether it is a gloomy or sunny day!!! 
     
    Marian Salvaleon 

    Sun Protection Tips 
    - Sarah Steinert 
    It's the time of year that everyone looks forward 
    too.  It's that time of year when everyone starts 
    coming out of their winter hibernation to enjoy 
    the wonders of spring. With the cold days of 
    winter now melting away to the warming rays of 
    the glorious sunshine we come to an age-old 
    problem:  Sunburns.  
     
    As a Mary Kay Consultant, I have learned the 
    value of good skin care and protection from the 
    sun that I would like to share with everyone.  
     
    1. Avoid the sun from as early as 10am to 
    4pm, as the ultraviolet (UV) radiation is the 
    strongest. 
    2. Wear sunglasses with full UV protection & 
    wraparound frame.  
    3. Apply sunscreen (SPF 30 & above) on 
    exposed areas like the neck, nose, cheeks, 
    lips, ears, shoulders & arms. Apply 15 to 30 
    minutes before going outdoors & re-apply 
    often. Use generously.  If you're swimming, 
    you'll need to re-apply after 80 minutes.  
    4. Children need protection too.  
     
    SPF stands for Sun Protection Factor. It 
    indicates the strength of a sunscreen to block 
    out the sun's UV rays. It ranges from 2 to 60. 
    For example, if a person under the sun gets a 
    burn after 20 minutes, then with a sunscreen of 
    SPF 30 it would take 30 times of the 20 minutes 
    to get a burn. A higher number means longer 
    protection. 
     
    Of course, sometimes even the best of us forget 
    to go out with protection on our skin, and it 
    does get burned.  In this event, apply a 
    soothing lotion or gel containing pure aloe vera.
