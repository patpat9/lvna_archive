    Need Help with Eating Problems? 
    Food Addicts in Recovery Anonymous (FA) is a program for 
    individuals recovering from addictive eating and anorexia; it is 
    based on the 12 steps of Alcoholics Anonymous. There are no dues 
    or fees at meetings, and there are no weigh-ins. Contact the FA 
    office for more information at 781-321-9118, or visit their Web site 
    at www.foodaddicts.org.  
    Three FA meetings are held in this area every week. ■ 
     
     
     
     
     
    May in Your Yard and 
    Garden 
     
    •Plant frost-tender plants such as 
    citrus and bougainvillea. 
    •Weed while the weeds are 
    young and the soil is still damp 
    enough to make pulling the 
    weeds easy. Do not let the weeds 
    form seed heads. 
    •Add young, seedhead-free 
    weeds to the compost pile. 
    Remember to keep the pile as damp as a wrung-out 
    sponge for faster composting. 
    •Watch out for snails and earwigs, both of which 
    devour seedlings. 
    •Use a strong spray from the hose to wash away aphids. 
    •Wrap fruit tree trunks with a sticky tape to control 
    ants, but remember to wrap low so that birds, 
    particularly hummingbirds, will not be attracted. 
    •Prepare for the coming hot, dry season by applying a 
    mulch. Rice hulls are an excellent, inexpensive soil 
    amendment. Contact a rice growers’ cooperative to find 
    the nearest local source. 
    •Prune spring-blooming plants as they finish blooming. 
    To keep old lilacs vigorous, prune a few of the lilac 
    stems to the ground each year, and prune suckers off 
    grafted varieties of lilacs. 
    •Fertilize lawns, roses, and all flowering plants about to 
    bloom. Fertilize azaleas, camellias, and rhododendrons 
    when they finish blooming. If you did not fertilize them 
    last month, fertilize citrus, trees, and shrubs. 
    •Plant out summer bulbs such as callas, cannas, dahlias, 
    gladiolus, lilies, tigridia, and tuberous begonias. 
    •Set out summer-flowering plants. 
    •Plant the vegetable garden and thin vegetable 
    seedlings that were sown too close together by snipping 
    them with scissors. 
     
    These tips are from the book Northern California Gardening: A 
    Month-by-Month Guide, by Katherine Grace Endicott, 
    published by Chronicle Books, San Francisco, copyright 1996, 
    and are used with the publisher’s permission. ■

    Columbia Music Groups Win Awards 
    -Wendy Stanley 
    The choirs from Columbia Middle School went to 
    West Valley College in Saratoga to attend the 
    California Music Education Association (CMEA) 
    Choir Festival on March 31. The results were 
    awesome. Out of ratings of Superior, Excellent, 
    Good, and Fair they scored: 
    6th grade Choir―Excellent 
    Intermediate Choir―Excellent 
    Advanced Choir―Unanimous Superior (all three 
    judges gave them a Superior rating, the highest rating 
    there is). 
    The bands performed at the CMEA Band Festival at 
    Cal State, East Bay on April 29. Not only was this a 
    Saturday, but the Advanced and Intermediate Bands 
    had to be at Columbia at 6:00 a.m. for the bus trip to 
    Hayward. That’s dedication! 
    Here are their results: 
    6th grade Band―Good overall (pretty nice work, 
    considering they just started playing their instruments 
    in September)  
     
     
     
     
    Intermediate Band―Good overall (but one judge 
    gave them an Excellent) 
    Advanced Band―Excellent overall  
    Let's give all of our Columbia Middle School music 
    groups a big hand for doing their best and making us 
    all proud! ■ 
     
    City News 
    Council Approves New Utility Rates 
    The Sunnyvale City Council has approved increases 
    for water, sewer, and garbage utility rates for the 
    coming fiscal year. Recommended rate increases are 
    11.5% for water, 6% for wastewater, and 5.5% for 
    solid waste.  
    These increases translate to a 7% increase in total 
    monthly utility costs for an average residential 
    customer. However, the new rates, which will 
    average approximately $77.80 for a typical monthly 
    utility bill, are still about 25% less than the average of 
    those in surrounding communities. The city's 
    commercial rates will increase by the same overall 
    percentage as residential rates.

    Letter 
    It has been noticed that there are 
    scavengers going around Lakewood 
    Village, especially on Sunday evenings, 
    and removing items from the recycling 
    bins. Per Sunnyvale DPS, this is in 
    violation of the City's municipal code 
    and is considered stealing City 
    property. I was surprised to find out 
    that the City's sanitation department 
    relies on a certain amount of recycling 
    items to curtail the City's garbage 
    service cost. 
    It’s very possible that these 
    scavengers are not aware that it is 
    /illegal. So, if you see anyone picking 
    through your garbage bins, tell them it 
    is illegal and to return the items to 
    the bins. If you don't want to confront 
    the people or if they ignore your 
    warning, please call 911 and provide 
    DPS with the number and description of 
    the people, street name, and direction 
    of travel the scavengers were last seen 
    heading. 
    Thanks for Supporting Your 
    Neighborhood! 
    A Lakewood Village Resident ■

    City News, from page 3 
    Sunnyvale’s utility rates are based entirely on the 
    city’s costs for operating and maintaining its water, 
    wastewater, and solid-waste facilities. The primary 
    cause of the recommended increase in water rates is 
    the significant rise in costs for water purchased from 
    the San Francisco Public Utilities Commission (Hetch 
    Hetchy system). The increase in wastewater rates is 
    necessary to cover needed infrastructure and capital 
    costs for the city’s aging wastewater treatment and 
    collection system. Solid-waste rates are projected to 
    rise to cover the city’s annual increases in contractual 
    costs for collection, recycling, transfer, and disposal.  
    Sunnyvale Opens Community Award Nominations 
    Each year, the City of Sunnyvale honors exceptional 
    members of the Sunnyvale Community. This year’s 
    awards include: Distinguished Resident of the Year, 
    Outstanding High School Senior of the Year, 
    Outstanding Community Volunteer, Outstanding 
    Businessperson of the Year, Outstanding 
    Contribution to the Arts, and Outstanding 
    Environmental Achievement. The nominations for this 
    year’s awards will close on Friday, May 19 at 5 p.m. 
    Awards will be presented at the Mayor’s State of the 
    City celebration on Tuesday, July 4 at 11 a.m. at 
    Washington Park.  
    You can download nomination forms on the city’s 
    Web site, www.sunnyvale.ca.gov. ■
