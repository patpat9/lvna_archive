    July in Your Yard 
    and Garden 
     
    ☼Weed. Do not let the weeds form 
    seedheads. 
    ☼Add young, seedhead-free weeds to the compost 
    pile. Remember to keep the pile as damp as a wrungout sponge for faster composting. 
    ☼Watch out for snails and earwigs, both of which 
    devour seedlings. 
    ☼Plant bulbs that bloom in autumn. 
    ☼Use a strong spray from the hose to wash away 
    aphids. 
    ☼With fruit crops ripening this month, watch out for 
    ants. Wrap fruit tree trunks with a sticky tape to control 
    ants (wrap low so that birds, particularly hummingbirds, will not be attracted) and if necessary, spray. 
    ☼Water plants as needed. 
    ☼Insulate the garden soil from the hot, dry weather by 
    applying a two-inch-thick mulch. 
    ☼Fertilize plants if you see signs of pale, lackluster 
    growth. Fertilize the lawn. Fertilize all flowering 
    plants that are about to bloom. Fertilize camellias. 
    Fertilize roses, except most antique roses. In really hot 
    areas, roses will probably stop flowering until the 
    weather cools. 
    ☼Pinch off dead flowers. Make sure you get the entire 
    flower head when deadheading annuals—particularly 
    the swollen ovaries where seeds form. 
    ☼Transplant summer-flowering plants, such as 
    cosmos, petunias, and zinnias that will endure heat, into 
    the bedding plot. Hot weather is hard on newly 
    transplanted plants. Shade them for a few days with the 
    temporary shelter of an umbrella or wooden shake. 
    Stake tall sprawling flowers such as dahlias. 
    ☼Continue to plant the vegetable garden. Thin 
    vegetable seedlings that were sown too close together. 
    Harvest vegetables as they ripen. Pick them early in the 
    day. 
    ☼Soak all newly transplanted plants. Prune berry vines 
    when you have finished harvesting. 
     
    These tips are from the book Northern California Gardening: A 
    Month-by-Month Guide, by Katherine Grace Endicott, published by 
    Chronicle Books, San Francisco, copyright 1996, and are used with 
    the publisher’s permission.■

    President’s Column 
    -Ursula Syrova, LVNA President 
    Dumpster Day Monitors Need to Come to the General 
    Meeting on July 13. All volunteers who are signed up 
    to be dumpster monitors: come to the July 13 LVNA 
    general meeting to get your dumpster monitor signs, 
    badges, and information. 
    Dumpster Day Sale! Come by the Dumpster Day Sale 
    to shop and support your neighborhood. Usable items 
    will be rescued from the dumpsters and put on sale in 
    the parking lot near the Shell station at Lawrence and 
    Lakewood. New this year: the sale volunteers will be 
    selling raffle tickets for $1 each for a chance to win a 
    free dinner at one of the restaurants in the Lakewood 
    Village Shopping Center. The winning number will be 
    printed in the Villager in August. 
    Raccoons May be a Danger to Cats. As reported in the 
    San Jose Mercury News on June 24, 2006, there have 
    been reports in Cupertino that a raccoon may be killing 
    and eating cats. The late ripening of fruit this year may 
    be to blame. We do have raccoons here in Lakewood 
    Village too, so cat owners would be wise to keep close 
    track of their pets. 
    Calabazas Creek Trail Completed. You may have 
    noticed that the new pedestrian/bicycle bridge crossing 
    Calabazas Creek is now open. It connects Lakewood 
    Village to the newly completed Calabazas Creek Trail. 
    The 1.5-mile trail connects Mission College, the John 
    W. Christian Greenbelt at Fairwood Park, VTA’s 
    Reamwood Light Rail Station and the San Tomas 
    Aquino and Bay Trails via Old Mountain View–Alviso 
    Road. A low-water undercrossing of Tasman Drive will 
    be constructed in the future as phase II of the project. 
    Lakewood Park Swimming Pool Is Open. Need to cool 
    off? Head to the pool at Lakewood Park. The hours for 
    recreational swim are 1:45–3:45 p.m. Monday through 
    Friday, and 1:30–4 p.m. Saturday and Sunday. The 
    entrance fee is $1. For more information you can call 
     
     
    730-7721, or you can try the pool directly at 734-8190 
    during the open hours. 
    Report Tree Burning in Lakewood Park to the Police. 
    There is still a problem with people cutting down 
    sapling trees to use as firewood in the fire pit at 
    Lakewood Park. This is vandalism and should be 
    reported to the police. 
    Please Keep Dogs on Leashes. Residents walking their 
    dogs in the neighborhood have reported ongoing 
    problems with other dogs attacking them—usually 
    dogs hanging out in front yards. Please keep your dog 
    under control at all times—you never can tell what 
    will set your dog off.■

    Letter 
    Thank you for your newsletter. I 
    appreciate reading about the local 
    events around Lakewood. I am writing 
    to you today because of something in 
    your June issue. I think it is 
    irresponsible to instruct people to 
    call 911 for non-emergency 
    situations. In this issue we were 
    being told to call 911 if we see 
    someone using the grills or firepits 
    in Lakewood Park after dark. This is 
    not an emergency. This is a nuisance. 
    No one’s life is in danger. 
    Similarly, in an earlier issue, we 
    were told to call 911 if we see 
    someone going through our recycling 
    materials. This is not an emergency 
    situation. You know what would be a 
    crime? If someone who had a real 
    emergency could not get through to a 
    911 dispatcher because of people 
    tying up the lines with ridiculous 
    calls. You publish the Public Safety 
    Non-Emergency number as 730-7100 on 
    the back of your newsletter. That is 
    the number that you should tell your 
    readers to call for ”non-emergency” 
    situations. 
    Jerry Wright 
    Editor’s Note: Department of Public Safety personnel told 
    those who attended the May LVNA general meeting to 
    call 911 if people were seen using the Lakewood Park fire 
    pits after dark. Anyone who wants to talk to the DPS 
    Neighborhood Resource Officer should attend the LVNA 
    general meeting.■

    No Hazwaste in the Dumpsters! 
    Don’t plan on putting paint, pesticides, solvents, 
    batteries, fluorescent bulbs, or automotive fluids (oil, 
    brake fluid) into the dumpster in your area. These items 
    are hazardous waste, so they need to go to the 
    Household Hazardous Waste Drop-Off Event Site near 
    the SMaRT Station off Caribbean Drive and Borregas 
    Avenue (it’s a short trip north on Lawrence 
    Expressway to Caribbean and Borregas). The site will 
    be open on July 15 to take these materials and on the 
    third Saturday of every month, from 8 a.m. to 1 p.m.. 
    Donate or give away (try sunnyvalefree on the Web) 
    TVs, computer monitors, and laptop computers. They 
    cannot be put into your trash or the dumpsters.■
