    This month it will be time to select new 
    leaders for the LVNA as we leave 2015

    “changing of the guard” where old LVNA officers will

    President’s Column 
      
    (Continued from page 1) 
     
    The homeowners in our neighborhood got to 
    participate in the boom instead of being left out. 
     
    The LVNA has a rich history of neighborhood 
    leadership.  Back when I was president the first 
    time we were blessed with strong leaders who 
    took on major problems and solved them.   
     
    Two very special people, Mel and Elena Anderson, made it 
    their business to raise the money needed to publish the 
    newsletter.  For years Mel would sell advertising and  
    collect payments from local vendors, while Elena kept the 
    books for us.   They were the solid foundation that many 
    neighborhood initiatives were built on.  I always tried to find 
    ways to thank them for their selfless service, but somehow I 
    was never really able to do it.  They saw that playing their 
    role was thanks enough, and no other  
    thanks were needed. 
     
    Another special person was Jessica Lee. She was 
    one of the LVNA founders (along with Judy Fernandez) and 
    served as LVNA president during the famous “graffiti wars” 
    of the mid-1990s.  She is noteworthy for facing down a City 
    of Sunnyvale park employee who was threatening to have 
    our volunteer (Gene Goldsmith) arrested for painting out 
    graffiti on the Lakewood Park building.  She straightened 
    herself up to the full (not very tall) height, looked the park 
    guy straight in the eye and told him, “If you are going to ar-
    rest Gene you'll have to arrest me too!” Thanks to her, the 
    City became the LVNA's ally in the graffiti struggle instead  
    of its adversary. Instead of equating the painting over of  
    tags with putting the tags there in the first place (which the 
    City formerly did) . 
     
     
    Jessica got city hall to realize how silly that policy 
    was. Thanks in no small part to her, the war on graffi-
    ti was finally won, and we have our clean buildings, 
    fences and signs as proof of that victory. 
     
    There are many more stories to tell.  Craig Crawford 
    served as LVNA president just before me and helped 
    introduce me to the many issues we were facing.  Jim and 
    Helen Lane were “Mr. and Mrs. Dumpster Day” for many 
    years and organized our annual neighborhood clean-up.  
    Rick Flovin took up the burden of being LVNA president after 
    me and did a fine job.  I can go on and on. 
     
    I'm telling you about these people because it is important to 
    understand who has come before.  It is also important to un-
    derstand that time moves on.  Elena Andersen passed away 
    in 2014.  The last I heard, Mel is living in Florida and suffering 
    from Alzheimer’s.  Jessica Lee served on the Sunnyvale 
    School Board after leading the LVNA, but no longer lives in 
    the neighborhood.  Craig and Leah Crawford retired and 
    moved to Alaska several years ago.  Jim and Helen Lane 
    passed away a few years ago.  
     
    Times change... 
     
    It is time for me to lay down the presidency of the LVNA.  It is 
    time for me to move on and join the ranks of the people I've 
    described.  I've been at this for 20 years now, off and on.  It is 
    time for new blood and new energy. 
     
    The great thing about Lakewood Village is that new leaders 
    always come forward!  In 1996 I stepped up and took over 
    from Craig Crawford, who was burned out and needed a rest.  
    20 years later it is time for someone to step up and take over 
    from me.  I'm burned out and need a rest. 
    Accepting responsibility has its rewards.  After all, leading the 
    LVNA put me on a path that lead me to being the Mayor of 
    Sunnyvale.  There have been other rewards that are perhaps 
    less visible but much more valuable to me.  If I were 20 years 
    younger, I would do it all again. 
     
    I've had my day.  It is time for me to make room for the next 
    generation of leadership.   
     
    Please come to the March meeting, put your name on the 
    ballot and get yourself elected.  If you re the right kind of per-
    son, you will never regret it. 
     
    Thanks, whoever you are, from the bottom of my heart. 
     
    --Fred   

    Discover and Go  
    Free Museum Pass 
    Program 
     
    Looking for a free, fun, educational outing for the family 
    or an excursion for out-of-town guests? Sunnyvale  
    Public Library has joined with other libraries to offer  
    Discover & Go passes for Library cardholders to many 
    Northern California museums and cultural institutions. 
    Discover & Go is a new service that allows Sunnyvale 
    Library cardholders to print passes online for specific 
    museums. Each Discover & Go pass expires the day 
    after the reservation date. Library cardholders can  
    reserve up to two passes at one time.  
     
    Some of the museums that are available for free or at 
    discounted prices include: The Marine Mammal Center,  
    Charles M. Schulz Museum, The Tech Museum and the 
    Pacific Pinball Museum.  
     
    The full list of participating museums that are available 
    to Sunnyvale Library cardholders can be found by going 
    to : 
    https://sunnyvalelibrary.discoverandgo.net

    Police Blotter 
     
    Commercial Burglary – 1000 Blk. Escalon 
    Tuesday February 2nd @ 6:28 pm  
    Officers responded to a report of a burglary which just occurred 
    at a construction site. Officers determined copper piping had 
    been stolen and the suspects fled in a truck. Officers 
    broadcasted a description of the truck, and shortly thereafter 
    Santa Clara PD reported locating the truck in their city. The 
    suspects fled but were captured after a foot chase. Both 
    suspects were arrested and booked into County Jail. The 
    copper piping was recovered.  
     
    Emergency Medical Response – 1300 Blk. Dunnock 
    Saturday February 6th @ 1:42 am 
    DPS personnel responded to a residence on a report of a 
    subject unconscious and not breathing. Patrol officers were first 
    to arrive, found the patient in cardiac arrest, and applied a 
    shock with an AED.  Fire personnel and paramedics arrived and 
    delivered another shock. The patient had regained a pulse and 
    was breathing, and was transported to the hospital. 
     
    Major Injury Accident – Wolfe & Kifer   
    Tuesday February 9th @ 8:56 am 
    DPS personnel responded to the area of Wolfe and Kifer on a 
    report of a bicyclist down in the roadway. Personnel located the 
    bicyclist who was semi-conscious and suffered head injuries. It 
    was determined the bicyclist lost control and fell and was not 
    stuck by a vehicle. The bicyclist was transported to the hospital 
    and was in stable condition. 
     
    Robbery – 1000 Blk. Reed Terr. 
    Sunday, Feb 14th @ 7:58 pm 
    The victim posted property for sale on Craigslist. The unknown 
    suspects came to the victim’s residence to pick up the 
    property. Once the property was loaded into the suspect’s 
    vehicle, the victim was physically assaulted and the suspects 
    fled without paying for the property. 
     
    Felon in Possession of a Firearm – 1200 Blk. Wildwood 
    Thursday February 25th @ 11:51 pm 
    Officers located a subject who had outstanding arrest 
    warrants. The suspect fled on foot and into a local bar. The 
    suspect was found hiding in a storage cabinet and was in 
    possession of a firearm. The suspect was arrested and booked 
    into County Jail.  
     
    Haz Mat – 1095 Dunford 
    Monday February 29th @ 3:18 pm 
    DPS personnel responded on a report of a dumpster fire. It was 
    determined not to be a fire, but a chemical reaction due to items 
    thrown into the dumpster. The chemical reaction caused a 
    plume of smoke. Some residences in the area were evacuated 
    and nearby schools were sheltered in place. The DPS Haz Mat 
    team monitored the situation until the chemicals were contained 
    and removed.
