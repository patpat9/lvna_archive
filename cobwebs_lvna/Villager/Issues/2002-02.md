    President’s Column 
    - Craig Crawford  
    I was invited to a meeting that included all the 
    Neighborhood Associations in the City of 
    Sunnyvale. Several members of Neighborhood 
    Preservation were in attendance along with 
    Public Safety, and Council Member Howe. This 
    was a chance to share information wit h other 
    organizations and to find out how other 
    organizations are handling problems. 
     
    I was surprised to learn that other 
    neighborhoods are battling the same problems 
    that are happening in Lakewood. Everyone has 
    problems with junk cars, weeds, and shopping  
    carts, to name some of the common problems. 
    Most of these seem to be small but when you 
    add in garbage cans that are left in the street 
    or the front yard along with Christmas lights  
     that are left up all year, you start to see a 
    neighborhood that needs work. 
     
    We did not come up with a citywide solution, 
    but we did start a dialog with other 
    organizations that we need to put our collective 
    heads together and find a way to deal with 
    some of out common problems. The City 
    Council is starting a new forum for receiving 
    public comment. The Council will come out to 
    the Neighborhood Association meeting and 
    conduct a Council meeting that will focus 
    entirely on the neighborhood. They will be 
    listening and taking notes on what we have to 
    say. This seems to be a big s tep in the right 
    direction. 
     
    Communication between the neighborhoods 
    and the city is how things get done. The 
    council can set a direction that the city can use 
    in providing services and help that every 
    neighborhood needs. I will be setting up a date 
    for th is meeting in the near future. To be 
    effective we have to have a good turnout, so 
    be looking for the date in an upcoming 
    newsletter. See you at the next meeting,

    Mattson Technology.   He not only loved 
    science, but was always concerned about the 
    fact that kids (especially those from 
    underprivileged backgrounds) were not getting 
    the kind of access to hands -on science 
    education that they deserved.   Every year 
    during the holiday season, Dr. Burd used to 
    contribute a toy (and it was nearly always a 
    science learning kit!) to either The Giving Tree 
    or Toys fo r Tots.   He loved teaching --he used 
    to tutor people, including me!   
     
    Dr. Burd was also an environmentalist and a 
    supporter of county and state parks, as well as 
    wildlife refuges. A civic -minded person, he 
    used to help me collate the monthly “LVNA 
    Villager”, and would accompany me on my 
    monthly LVNA newsletter delivery route.

    Family Child Daycare 
    The Business – The Profession 
     
    - James L. Daugherty  
    The Business 
     
    My wif e was always in family child daycare 
    since she began working 35 years ago. Child 
    daycare (baby sitting) at one time was not 
    considered a profession, but more of an 
    unreliable means of a second income, or 
    something to do for extra spending money. 
    This was back in 1966-67. 
     
    Today, family daycare is licensed by the State 
    with regulations covering the size of the 
    business, how long you must be employed in 
    the business before being allowed to expand, 
    and CPR training requirements. Also, city 
    business regulations  com into play with local 
    business licenses and fire permits, depending 
    on size. 
     
    The Economics 
     
    A new daycare business would be limited to six 
    children with a limit of three infants under the 
    age of two, or four infants and no other 
    children. This limit i s for the safety of the 
    children. It gives the new provider an 
    opportunity to see if this is really what is 
    wanted in a home -based business. It tests your 
    maturity and ability to handle stressful 
    situations involving interaction between 
    yourself and childr en of different disciplines 
    and cultures and their parents.  
     
     To succeed, you will learn to budget, set 
    prices, write contracts, and manage public 
    relations. You will learn the value of self -
    reliance, and develop skills in supervision, 
    teaching, and hop efully, have a knack for arts 
    and crafts.  
     
    If your business is successful after the first 
    year, you may expand your business to twelve 
    children with a maximum of four under the age 
    of two years old. If you are willing to take on 
    school-age children, you ma y hav4e two of 
    these children that would drop in after school. 
    This would allow you a maximum of fourteen 
    children at any one time. Rates for after -
    schoolers can be daily or hourly. 
     
    Some daycares will structure their prices on 
    the number of children the S tate would allow at 
    their maximum. Others will average the cost -
    per-child, regardless of age. Example: A 
    daycare with four infants may charge $175 per 
    week per child for a total of $700 per week. 
    Another daycare with three infants and three 
    preschoolers ma y charge $150 per week for 
    the three infants and $100 per week for the 
    preschoolers over the age of two, for a total of 
    $750 per week. Still another daycare may 
    charge $125 per week per child, regardless of 
    age. The few extra dollars per week for older 
    children is justified because they will be eating 
    more, require more diverse care, and require a 
    larger variety of toys, playground space, and 
    sleeping arrangements. 
     
    Daycares that are licensed for twelve also 
    require a second person on the premises. 
    Watching twelve children by yourself doesn’t 
    seem to be that big of a task to some. 
    Remember, though, that there will be times 
    when you will need to relieve yourself, cook 
    meals or just want a coffee break. This will no 
    doubt be when Little Johnny pushes Little Su sie 
    off the swing set, the baby beings to cry for a 
    change and its next feeding, and Mike gets into 
    a scrap with Billy and bites him on the arm. 
    You’ll be blessed if you have a good partner or 
    helper. Taxes included, it will take about two 
    and one -half chi ldren to support your helper. 
    The current minimum wage rate is $6.75 per 
    hour, plus your share of payroll taxes and 
    workman’s compensation insurance.

    Discipline and Supervision 
     
    Keeping control o f your young children is going 
    to require a ton of patience and self -discipline. 
    In supervising young children, it is very 
    important to maintain yourself as the person in 
    charge. Playing or participant with them in 
    their activities requires you to, or they  may see 
    you as one of the kids and you will lose your 
    control. If this occurs, they may ignore you to 
    some extent when it comes to following 
    directions, accepting discipline, or even their 
    relationships with each other.  
     
    In dealing with the parents, a wri tten contract 
    is an invaluable too. This contract should lay 
    out the arrival time and departure time per 
    day, how much will be charged, at what time 
    overtime will be started, and how much extra 
    that will cost. It should outline the parents’ 
    responsibilities as well as yours. it should cover 
    such subjects as transportation of children, 
    field trips, vacation time for you both, meals, 
    snacks, changes of clothes, who buys the 
    diapers, etc. A section should be included to 
    allow for handwritten negotiated changes  in 
    the contract.  
     
    The Reward 
     
    The reward is knowing that your are giving a 
    part of yourself to these children and their 
    families, and that they are better off because 
    of you. 
     
    Family child daycare is a profession you can be 
    proud of. It isn’t for everyone . Being an 
    outgoing and giving person and willing to 
    change the rules to accommodate the fast 
    living and changing Silicon Valley lifestyle is a 
    big asset. Hopefully, the families served by 
    family daycares will have a happier and better 
    future. ¢

    Lawrence Expressway Planning 
    - Dan Collen  
    [Editor’s note: I wrote to the County Roads and 
    Airports department to ask about the planning 
    process for improvements to Lawrence 
    Expressway. I’ve published the response from 
    Dan Collen here:]  
     
    The County of Santa Clara is developing a 
    planning document to assess the various 
    expressway needs and to develop potential 
    projects and priorities to address those needs.   
    Traffic capacity is one of the areas of need 
    under consideration .  We will also assess other 
    areas of need such as pedestrian and bicycle 
    provisions, landscaping, sound walls, 
    infrastructure maintenance and rehabilitation, 
    and transit accommodation.   In addition to the 
    technical analysis, the study includes an 
    extensive collaborative planning process 
    with city and public participation playing key 
    roles in the process.  
     
    We have really just begun the Study and are 
    still in the data gathering phase.   As a starting 
    point, we have compiled previous planning 
    documents and pro posed projects, and are 
    coordinating with Sunnyvale city staff to get 
    information on city development plans and the 
    city general plan.   Potential grade separation 
    projects at Sandia/Lakehaven and Tasman are 
    listed in past planning documents (e.g., the 
    T2000 Countywide Transportation Plan adopted 
    in the late 1980's).   As part of this Study, we 
    will take a fresh look at these previously 
    proposed grade separation projects, and any 
    other proposals that may be developed, such 
    as at -grade intersection improvement s.  We 
    are also holding discussions with the Santa 
    Clara Valley Transportation Authority (VTA) 
    about their transit plans related to the 
    expressways and will continue to work with 
    them to identify improvements to 
    accommodate transit use.  
     
    We welcome and enc ourage public comment.   
    Our web site is now operational 
    (www.expressways.info) and includes an e -
    mail link for public input.   We also have a 
    project hotline (408 -544-2476, ext. 333) for 
    taking comments. As a rule, we will not be able 
    to respond to each ind ividual e -mail and 
    telephone comment but we are compiling the 
    comments and using them as we proceed with 
    the Study.   We also plan to have several 
    community meetings during the course of the  
     study.   Over the next month, you will see a 
    great deal of infor mation added to the web site 
    starting with the results of our data collection 
    work.  We will also post community meetings 
    on the web site and announce them on our 
    project hot line as they are scheduled.   We will 
    add you to our contact list to keep you 
    informed when community meetings are 
    scheduled so you can notify your readers.  
     
    The draft plan will be brought forward for 
    discussion this summer and an additional 
    public review period is planned in the Fall.   The 
    Final Plan is scheduled for adoption at the en d 
    of 2002.   Attached is our Project Fact Sheet 
    which provides more information about the 
    Study process and schedule.  
     
    Dan Collen, Project Manager 
    County Roads and Airports Department
