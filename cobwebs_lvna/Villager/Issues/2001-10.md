    Neighborhood News
    Michael Jenesse, LVNA President
    "Service is the result of the outflow of the energy of Love
    from the Heart, informed by the Intelligent Mind - a mixture
    of Intelligent Action motivated
    By Love." -Alice Bailey
    Like so many others I was in shock of the events that
    happened on September 11, and like so many others
    scrambled to find some way, some action to respond to that
    event.  It wasn't until a friend's message came with a simple
    and beautiful reminder that I knew a way that I could
    respond.  She reminded me: to keep doing what we are
    doing, but to do it with a greater awareness and
    consciousness.  "That as we go through the coming days, and
    we come into contact with another, kissing a loved one or
    hugging another, know that while we are touching them, we
    are also touching one of those in grief, maybe more."
    We move on in honor of those that gave up their lives that
    we as a community, a nation, a world that we might live.
    Our daily actions at times may have seemed so mundane yet
    with all the gratitude that we can muster we must move on
    so that those lives are not a waste.
    Candidates forum
    Elections are fast approaching.  The League of Women
    Voters approached us (of the LVNA) to sponsor an evening
    in which the League would set up a public forum of the
    candidates seeking to be elected to the city council and the
    school board.  We agreed to give them the space normally
    allotted for our general meeting.  The event is scheduled for
    Wednesday, October 10 at Fairwood Elementary School
    located at 1110 Fairwood Ave.  The meeting will be in the
    school auditorium/cafeteria. The program will begin
    promptly at 7pm and go until 9pm.  There will be some time
    after the meeting to personally approach and greet the
    candidates.  This is a rare and fun opportunity to meet those
    that seek to represent us in our city government.  We are
    excited to have this chance to provide this opportunity to our
    neighbors and the public.

    Diversity
    Finally, the beauty of our community, our neighborhood is
    the diversity.  Our neighborhood is blessed with a diversity
    of people and their cultures, and the ability of the residents
    to live in harmony.  The diversity is what feeds our
    neighborhood, and we ask all to remember to care for one
    another so that there isn't any type of racial or ethnic
    backlash with in our community. This is the responsibility of
    each and every one of us.  Thank you in advance for your
    support and care.
    Please if family, friends, or you have received any type of
    threats, please contact the Department of Public Safety by
    calling 911.  As the Director of Public Safety Irwin Bakin
    stated at last month's meeting, the department is taking all
    threats seriously and will pursue and prosecute any person
    that has made a threat. He further stated, "we are doing are
    part to ensure the safety and stability of the community."
    We look forward to seeing you at next month's meeting,
    until then do take care.

    Register to Vote Now!
    On November 6 there will be an election for the Sunnyvale
    City Council and the Sunnyvale School District. To vote in
    that election, you must be registered already, or you must
    mail a voter registration card by October 22.  To help
    increase the voter turnout in Lakewood Village, we are
    enclosing a voter registration card with this month's
    newsletter. If you need more, contact Elana Anderson 7456028. You can also get more registration cards at libraries
    and post offices. Just fill out the card and drop it in any
    mailbox.
    You are eligible to vote if you are a California resident, a US
    citizen, at least 18 years old, not in prison or on parole for a
    felony, and have not been judged incompetent by a court.
    Registration is permanent in California.  So, even if you do
    not vote in every election, your name stays on the voter rolls.
    Voters need to reregister only if they move, change their
    name, or wish to change their party.
    The Registrar of Voters will send you a voter card to confirm
    your registration within several weeks of the day you mail
    your registration card.  If you do not receive that
    confirmation card, you should call the Registrar at 299VOTE and ask about your status. All registered voters
    should get sample ballots in the mail several weeks before
    the election. The sample ballot will have information about
    what you can vote on. The sample ballot will also tell you
    where you must go to vote on Election Day.
