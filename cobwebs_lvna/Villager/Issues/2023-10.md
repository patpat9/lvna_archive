    Because the artificial turf fields will be fenced off (similar to the fencing at
    Fair Oaks Park), our park will lose its sense of open space. Instead, it will be
    effectively divided into three parts: (1) two grass fields on the west side, zoned
    for U-10 soccer; (2) the artificial turf fields in the middle, zoned for baseball,
    softball, soccer, and cricket; and (3) the rest of the park on the east side.                             
    The next step in the renovation plan is for the City Council to review and approve or reject the PCP. This is
    currently planned for the Oct 24 City Council meeting, which will take place at 7:00pm online (sunnyvale-ca-
    gov.zoom.us/j/96111580540) and in person (Council Chambers, City Hall, 456 W Olive Ave). We need people from
    Lakewood Village to voice their opinions on this topic. Email City Council at council@sunnyvale.ca.gov. If you
    can, join the Oct 24 meeting in-person or over Zoom! Be sure to note that you are providing “Public Comment on
    the Lakewood Park Renovation” and identify yourself as a resident of Lakewood Village.
    Traffic is also likely to increase as sports clubs use the artificial fields for games. Given that Lakewood Park
    has no dedicated parking lot, people attending games will have to park on the streets, therefore reducing the
    amount of street parking available to residents during games. Additionally, per Page 6 of the Sunnyvale
    Athletic Field Use and Reservation Policy, the following will be prohibited on the artificial turf: food; drinks
    (other than water); dogs and other pets; tents and staked umbrellas; metal cleats; high-heeled shoes; bicycles
    and scooters; chairs with straight legs; and more. The meeting included a presentation by city staff; public
    comment, which focused almost entirely around whether or not to include the artificial turf; and questions
    from the commissioners to city staff. The full recording of the PRC meeting can be found at bit.ly/455cXgr.

    Pet Safety Reminder
    Please keep dogs on leash and make sure that fences are secured so dogs can't escape from yards.
    Dogs that escape may get lost in our neighborhood - or worse. Loose dogs may bite kids, adults, or
    other dogs, which can result in anything from citations to the dog owners to euthansia of the dog.
    In a recent tragic event in our neighborhood, a dog escaped from her yard and killed a cat. The dog
    is now staying at an animal shelter, where her fate remains unclear. Please also make sure that your
    dogs (and cats) are microchipped with accurate contact information. Microchips are a huge help in
    reuniting lost pets with their owners. Please keep your pets safe!
