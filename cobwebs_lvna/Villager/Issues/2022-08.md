    - Lakewood Park Tree Walk -
    Tree #4: Saucer Magnolia
    In April, we highlighted Tree #1, the London Plane tree.
    From there, Tree #2 was the Italian Stone Pine, visible from
    the same spot as Tree #1. We then walked northeast,
    stopping at a concrete bench on your left for Tree #3, the
    Crape Myrtle. The fourth tree on SUFA (Sunnyvale Urban
    Forest Advocates) Lakewood Park Tree Walk is the Saucer
    Magnolia.
    By this point, you should be mostly at Lakechime Drive,
    having approached it from the south. If you look to your
    
    right, you should see the playground. Just outside the playground fence will be a medium
    height, skinny tree, which is our Tree #4, the Saucer Magnolia.
    The Saucer Magnolia gets its name from its flowers, which are large and (at least
    according to Google, saucer-shaped). The tree blooms in early spring, with pink flowers
    that appear before the leaves do, and it is common in both the USA and the British Isles.
    In fact, it’s one of the most popular horticulture magnolias and, unlike other magnolias,
    the saucer magnolia is relatively tolerant to both alkaline* soils and wind.
    Its Latin name is Magnolia × soulangeana, and the tree was bred in France in 1820 by a
    retired cavalry officer from Napoleon’s army, Étienne Soulange-Bodin. Monsieur
    Soulange-Bodin was well-known for his work establishing professional horticulture in
    France in the early 1800s. The genus is named after Magnus von Lagerström. A Swedish
    merchant and director of the Swedish East India Company, he sent many plants to Carl
    Linnaeus (who is known as the father of modern taxonomy).
    Want to plant one in your yard? Plant it in full sun or partial shade (preferably full sun).
    The saucer magnolia likes rich, well-draining soil - although, once established, it is
    reasonably drought tolerant.
    *What’s “alkaline”? It comes from the pH scale. A pH of 7 is neutral. pH of less than 7 is
    acidic, and a pH of more than 7 is basic. An alkaline substance has a pH of more than 7,
    meaning it is more basic than neutral. You may have heard arguments that alkaline water
    is better than plain tap water because alkaline water can neutralize acid in the
    bloodstream. (Whether or not this is true is something to research after your tree walk.)
    Alkaline soil is called “sweet soil”. It has a lot of magnesium, sodium, and calcium. It is
    also less water soluble than acidic or neutral soil, which means that it’s more difficult for
    plants in alkaline soil to access the soil nutrients. Plants growing in alkaline soil are often
    nutrient deficient and have reduced growth.
