    The Holiday Parade was held on December 1 Ith at 10:00 a.m,
    The step off was a little late, but about 300 plus neighbors

    minds. A special thank you to the officials who joined in our

    Councilman Jim Roberts; Sunnyvale School Board members
    Linda Killian, Pamela Kittler, Bill McDowell, Ellen
    McHenry, and Peggy Quillinan; Parks Director John
    Christian, Parks Commissioner Butch Johnson; Chief Regan
    Williams and Officer Bob Isaacs of Neighborhood Resource.

    GEOFF CARTER (733-0502)
    Recently, in Lakewood Village, there has been “much ado
    about something.” If you've been reading the flyers, “The
    L.V.N.A. Newsletter,” and “The Underground Chronicle”
    delivered on your doorstep, over the past few months, you
    may have an understanding of what it’s all about. In order to
    better appreciate where we are and where we want to go in
    the future, it is important to consider where we have been. So
    
    please bear with me as we consider the saga of the bylaws of
    the L.V.N.A.
    
    The Lakewood Village Neighborhood Association (otherwise
    known as L. V.N.A.) was started some three odd years ago. As
    a new organization we needed a set of governing rules to
    work and live by. The leadership “borrowed” a set of bylaws
    from the defunct Lakewood Village Residents Association
    (LVRA) as a starting point for their own set of bylaws. These
    bylaws were worked on for a while but were never approved
    by the general assembly of the L.V.N.A. nor by its board of
    directors.
    
    The fact that we had no bylaws came to a head this past
    August when the president of the L.V.N.A., Mr. Matt
    Castrigno, was asked to resign for, among other things,
    failure to take action on getting the bylaws approved. Mrs.
    Debbie Nixon took over as president with a mandate from the
    general assembly to bring the bylaws forward for discussion
    and approval.
    
    At the September General Assembly meeting a set of bylaws
    were presented for approval, but not discussion. The general
    feeling of those present were to not approve the document
    without first having the opportunity for public review and
    debate. They failed to pass.
    
    Nothing about bylaws was on the agenda for the October
    General Assembly meeting. The meeting in November was
    devoted to public review and discussion of a newly revised set
    of bylaws. Much discussion on many issues took place. Pastor
    George, of the Victorious Life Christian Center, was present
    at this meeting. In an effort to help our neighborhood retain
    its strong, cohesive, work-together attitude that has made this
    neighborhood association the envy of the valley and which
    had garnercd the respect of city government, he volunteered
    to publish the bylaws presented so that every resident of
    Lakewood Village could have a chance to study them. Look
    for them to be delivered to your door right after the first of the
    year with the Victorious Life newsletter.
    
    Please read the bylaws and be prepared to discuss them at the
    next meeting on Thursday, January 19th. You are invited to
    bring your comments and concerns forward so that they can
    be considered. We want the bylaws to reflect the “will of the
    people” who live in Lakewood Village.
    
    Anyone interested in joining the bylaws committee to
    complete this task should contact Mr. Mel Anderson or Mrs.
    Debbie Nixon. We invite you to show your support for the
    neighborhood by taking an active role in this very important
    process.
    
    The deadline for submissions to next month’s newsletter is
    February 4th. We attempt to print all submissions as space
    permits. However, we cannot verify information. Call the
    Editor at 245-8382 if you have any questions or for
    
    decorations, and several sign boards. We are in dire need of
    
    storage space, if you can help us out in any way, please let us
    know.
