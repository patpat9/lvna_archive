    President’s Column 
    -Ursula Syrova, LVNA President 
    Summer Picnic on Saturday, September 17! 
    Everyone who lives in Lakewood Village is invited to 
    the annual picnic on Saturday, September 17 at the 
    Lakewood Park picnic area. The picnic will start at 
    11:30 a.m. and wrap up by about 3 p.m. The LVNA 
    will be providing music, hamburgers, veggie burgers, 
    hotdogs, grilled vegetables, chili, chips, and cold 
    drinks. Folks who come are encouraged to bring a 
    dessert. Planned activities for the kids include face 
    painting, clowns, and a piñata. Sunnyvale Public Safety 
    has also been invited to bring a fire truck for the kids 
    (and adults!) to check out. For the adults, items donated 
    by local merchants will be raffled off as prizes. 
    This year the Community Child Care Council of Santa 
    Clara County (the 4C Council) will also be on hand 
    with their Info on Wheels van. The group specializes in 
    providing information about the health and well-being 
    of children five years old and younger. This includes 
    information on: resources for children that are available  
     
     
     
     
    in the county, nutrition, subsidized child care programs, 
    and referrals for children with special needs. The staff 
    of Info on Wheels will be giving away little gifts to the 
    children, and they will be on hand to answer questions. 
    The picnic is a great way to come out and meet your 
    fellow Lakewood Villagers. September 17 is also 
    National Citizenship Day, so what better time to 
    celebrate being a good citizen by eating some free food 
    and chatting with your neighbors.   
     
    Call for Picnic Volunteers! 
    Every good picnic needs some good grill masters! If 
    you can spare a little extra time to help set up, grill, or 
    clean up, we would greatly appreciate it! Please call 
    one of the board members to volunteer or come to the 
    next general meeting on Thursday, September 8, at 7 
    p.m. at the Lakewood Park building. 
     
    Rebuilding Together:  Guest Speaker Julianne Felix 
    at September General Meeting 
    Please come to the LVNA general meeting on 
    Thursday, September 8, 2005 at the Lakewood Park 
    building at 7 p.m. to hear Julianne Felix discuss the 
    Rebuilding Together Peninsula project. This group 
    assists low-income families or non-profit organizations 
    with renovation projects. From their Web site at 
    www.rebuildingtogetherpeninsula.org: “Rebuilding 
    Together Peninsula does everything from yard clean-up 
    and painting to skilled repairs of plumbing, windows, 
    electrical systems, flooring as well as roof repair and 
    replacement.” The deadline to apply for Rebuilding 
    Together services will be coming up in October, so 
    now is the time to find out more about the project.

    September in Your Yard  
    and Garden 
    •For the best selection, purchase spring 
    bulbs as soon as they come on the 
    market. Shop for native plants, trees, 
    and shrubs. Do not buy or plant frosttender plants such as citrus and 
    bougainvillea (wait until next spring). 
    •Continue to weed. Add young, seedhead-free weeds 
    and excess summer growth to the compost pile. 
    Remember to keep the pile as damp as a wrung-out 
    sponge for faster composting. 
    •As always, keep after the snails. Use a strong spray 
    from the hose to wash away aphids. Ants are likely to 
    become a problem soon. Discourage them by picking 
    up fallen fruit and sealing off cracks that would lead 
    them into the house.  
    •Hold off fertilizing most plants in preparation for 
    winter, but do fertilize all flowering plants that are 
    about to bloom. Fertilize roses with a low-nitrogen  
     
     
    formula or one that has no nitrogen at all. Do not 
    fertilize most antique roses. 
    •Pinch off dead flowers. Of course, if you wish to save 
    seeds for planting in the future, you will want to leave 
    the entire flower head, particularly the swollen ovaries 
    where seeds form. 
    •Postpone transplanting fall- and winter-flowering 
    plants into the bedding plot during a heat wave. Hot 
    weather is hard on newly transplanted plants. If you do 
    transplant, shade them for a few days with the 
    temporary shelter of an umbrella or a wooden shake. 
    Stake tall, sprawling flowers. 
    •Harvest vegetables as they ripen. Pick them early in 
    the day. Plant the vegetable garden with beets, broccoli, 
    cabbage, carrots, cauliflower, garlic, leeks, onions, and 
    shallots. In the hottest areas wait until next month to 
    put in new crops. 
    •Keep the garden well watered, particularly citrus trees. 
    Soak all newly transplanted plants. 
    These tips are from the book Northern California Gardening: A 
    Month-by-Month Guide, by Katherine Grace Endicott, 
    published by Chronicle Books, San Francisco, copyright 1996, 
    and are used with the publisher’s permission. 
    Reminder: The SMaRT Station on Borregas Drive, off 
    Caribbean Drive, has mounds of compost and mulch 
    that are free to Sunnyvale residents. Take your own 
    container and shovel.

    Get Rid of Your Unwanted Vehicle—For Free 
    Sunnyvale residents who have an unwanted vehicle 
    that is taking up parking space may have the vehicle 
    disposed of at no cost during Sunnyvale’s Fall Cleanup Tow-away program.  
    Throughout September, residents may call the 
    Sunnyvale Department of Public Safety (DPS) and 
    request a pick-up appointment. Requests will be sent 
    to a city-approved towing company, which will call 
    residents directly to arrange for tow-away of the 
    vehicle. Vehicle owners must sign over ownership and 
    turn in the vehicle’s pink slip to the approved towing 
    company. Vehicles must be towable, with four 
    inflated tires, and have all debris and garbage 
    removed. 
    Vehicles removed during the spring clean-up do not 
    qualify for a tax deduction. For additional information 
    on the program and to schedule vehicle pick-up, 
    contact Michelle Tani in DPS at (408) 730-7104, TDD 
    (408) 730-7501. 
     
     
     
     
    LVNA Meetings 
    -Skip Gottschalk, LVNA Secretary 
    August 11 General Meeting 
    Columbia Neighborhood Center Manager Linda Kim 
    and MayView Community Health Center Nurse 
    Practitioner Milan Chavarkar described the Columbia 
    Neighborhood Center health clinic program and its 
    role in the community.  
    City Council member Julia Miller talked about some 
    city and neighborhood issues. Currently in her last 
    term on City Council, she will be running for Foothill 
    School District Trustee. 
    Curtis Black, interim director of the Sunnyvale Parks 
    and Recreation Department, described the proposed 
    Community Garden project. Lakewood Village and 
    other areas in the city are being considered for the 
    program. (A meeting at a proposed area in Fairwood 
    Park was conducted on August 15.) 
    August 25 Board Meeting 
    The board planned activities and food for the LVNA 
    Picnic on Saturday September 17 at Lakewood Park. 
    Board members discussed upcoming events and guest 
    speaker recommendations for the general meetings.  
    The board agreed on applying to the Union Bank of 
    California for the LVNA checking account.

    Info on Wheels at the Annual  
    Lakewood Village Picnic 
    Families living in the Lakewood Village 
    neighborhood will be able to access community 
    resource information at the annual picnic on 
    September 17, 2005. The Info on Wheels van, a free 
    traveling resource for families with children prenatal 
    through age five, will be at the picnic. 
    Info on Wheels provides services in English, Spanish, 
    Mandarin, Hindi, and a variety of other languages, and 
    offers the following free services: 
    •Child care provider background check information 
    •Child care referrals 
    •Child identification cards 
    •Community resource information 
    •FIRST 5 Santa Clara County programs and services 
    information 
     
     
     
     
     
     
    •Information and referrals for children with special 
    needs 
    •Nutrition information 
    The primary goal of Info on Wheels is to increase 
    awareness of and access to community resources and 
    information, services, and other activities available to 
    families. The Info on Wheels project partners with 
    Parents Helping Parents, Sunnyvale Community 
    Services, City of Palo Alto, City of Mountain View, 
    City of Los Altos and City of Sunnyvale.  
    For more information, call Info on Wheels 
    Coordinator Jorge Ceballos at 1-800-943-4044 or visit 
    www.4c.org.
