    Support Our Neighbors
    Residents feel that there needs to be additional support from the city about neighborhood
    upkeep. This should include regular walks/drives through the city to identify and address
    safety issues, abandoned vehicles, overgrown vegetation, etc. The suggestion from the city
    was to work with the Neighborhood Preservation Dept on this.
    Due to Covid, the City Council paused tree trimmings for two years. This allowed the City to
    responsibly manage its budget and avoid laying off any employees. As a result, tree trimmings
    are behind schedule. City Council recently set aside $1.2M to help catch-up on tree trimming.
    This is an issue across Sunnyvale, and Council expects it to take about two years to make up
    for the two year gap from covid. Submit tree maintenance requests through Access Sunnyvale.
    Information on how to submit a request will be on the next page.
    Sidewalk repair requests should be submitted through Access Sunnyvale. The city does
    patching and grinding of damaged sidewalks year-round. However, sidewalk replacements are
    only done in the summer, and the City contracts out this work to a third-party (since the work
    is only done in summer, it’s not practical for the City to have a full-time staff for this work)
    Regarding the unhoused camp that is no longer in Lakewood Park, the City Council changed
    the ordinance to prevent overnight camping and funded HomeFirst to provide outreach to the
    unhoused residents living there. Valley Water is now planning to do something similar with
    the unhoused residents living along Calabazas Creek

    Transportation Updates from SEE (Sunnyvale for Equity in Education)
    LVNA VILLAGER - AUGUST 2023

    On July 13th, SEE met with Fremont Union High School District to review responses to
    concerns brought up at the May 20th Community Meeting on Transportation of High School Youth
    Bus Pass Eligibility and Cost: The district clarified student eligibility for bus passes. Students north of Central
    Expressway receive free bus passes regardless of income. Students between Central and El Camino Real receive
    free bus passes if they qualify for free or reduced lunches. If they don't qualify, they can purchase bases for
    $15/month. Students south of ECR can purchase bus passes at full price ($35/month).
    Communication: Communication with families about bus transportation will improve. This includes creating a one-
    page guideline that describes types of passes offered, eligibility, application
    and pick-up process, and VTA resources for route planning. There will be a simplified, district-wide form in Spanish
    and English to sign up for bus passes. The district will meet with staff at each school to make sure everyone
    understands and applies the district policy. VTA will attend Freshman Orientation at Fremont HS to provide an
    overview to students on how to ride the bus, plan routes, etc.
    Tardy Policy: The District will try to conduct a pilot program that will excuse students who are tardy because the
    VTA bus is late. This is complicated, which is why they want to test ways to do it.
    VTA Service Update: VTA updates service twice a year in August and December. For the August update, VTA will be
    changing the bus schedule so it arrives at Fremont five minutes earlier (8:15) to give more buffer time in case of
    delays. They will also convert one of the two extra service buses they run to an express-like bus (#255) that runs
    from Lakewood directly to FHS (without going through SNAIL and downtown).
    Other: Parking passes at Fremont High School will be distributed with some consideration for distance from
    residence to school and not just student seniority. We discussed a school bus service, safety on buses, students
    having to walk 20 to 30 minutes to a bus stop, and the challenges with having to get a bus pass monthly vs. an
    annual pass. There needs to be further discussion with the City of Sunnyvale regarding improved bike/ped
    infrastructure and the City's shuttle study issue. FUHSD Trustee Sinks is very interested in pursuing other options
    to serve Sunnyvale students, especially in extracurricular activity participation.
    New Partnership: On July 14, SEE partnered with Livable Sunnyvale and Sunnyvale Community Services to provide
    information about education inequity to Spanish-speaking Community Leaders and to support those who wanted to
    speak or write to the school board. On July 18, this community spoke compellingly to the FUHSD Board during
    public comments. After the comments, trustees spent about 20 minutes asking Superintendent Clark about issues
    that the community brought up, including bullying, racism, the discrepancy in academic scores, language barriers,
    inequitable services, transportation barriers, and lack of support for students. SEE is enthusiastic that a broader
    range of community voices is emerging.
    By Peggy Brewster & Lauren Ledbetter, SunnyvaleEquityInEducation@gmail.com, https://bit.ly/joinSEE
    
    Again, it’s been too many months since we did an entry from the SUFA (Sunnyvale Urban Forest Advocates)
    Lakewood Park Tree Walk. We covered five trees in 2022: the London Plane tree, the Italian Stone Pine, the
    Crape Myrtle, the Saucer Magnolia, and finally the Purple Leaf Plum. We covered Tree #6, the Aleppo Pine, in
    February, and now we are onto Tree #7, the maples.
    Start today at the corner of Lakechime Drive and Silverlake Dr. Look south (towards the playground). On your
    side of the stone wall - a bit past the yellow “pedestrian crossing ahead” sign - you will see the tall, not-so-
    straight Tree #6 Aleppo Pine. Keep going a bit farther south (but not as far as the picnic tables) until you see
    the two maples, spaced a bit apart.
    There are roughly 132 species of maple trees, many of which are native to Asia. Maple syrup can come from any
    time of maple tree, although sugar trees are the most commonly used species because their sap has the highest
    sugar content. It typically takes 40 gallons of sugar maple sap to yield one gallon of syrup.
    Anyone from Canada will easily recognize the maple leaf, which is on both the Canadian coat of arms and the
    Canadian flag. Their leaves are distinctive with multiple lobes. The sugar maple leaf has five fatter lobes with
    smooth edges, while the red maple leaf has three fatter lobes with saw-toothed edges. The maple seeds
    (sometimes called “helicopters” or “whirlees”) are also distinctive. When they fall, they spin, which helps the
    seeds travel a longer distance before landing on the group. In fact, the US Army developed a new airdrop
    supply carrier during World War II, which was based on the maple seed and could carry up to 65 pounds of
    supplies. The seeds are even edible, either raw or roasted.
