    Avoid Auto Vandalism. In the past six months there 
    have been 15 reported cases of auto vandalism in the 
    Lakewood area. Items taken have been radios, 
    sunglasses, and laptops. Cars are less likely to be 
    burglarized if they have an auto alarm system, do not 
    have items in clear view inside the car, and are locked 
    anytime when unoccupied. 
     
     
     
     
    April in Your Yard and 
    Garden 
     
    When the garden soil is dry enough 
    to work in without the soil clinging 
    to the shovel, dig in lavish quantities 
    of compost or other organic 
    amendments. This is particularly 
    important in the vegetable garden. 
    •Before digging into the spring gardening season, clean 
    and repair garden tools; sharpen the blades of pruning 
    tools and shovels; service the lawn mower. 
    •When spring-blooming bulbs fade, leave the dying 
    foliage intact to draw nutrients down into the bulb for 
    next year’s bloom. To cover the fading bulb foliage, set 
    out six-packs of lobelia and sweet alyssum. 
    •Begin weeding while the weeds are young and the 
    ground is still wet. 
    •In preparation for the coming hot, dry season, apply a 
    mulch. 
    •Take cuttings for new plants from azaleas, carnations, 
    chrysanthemums, geraniums, and succulents. 
    •Fertilize roses and crape myrtles. Fertilize azaleas, 
    camellias, and rhododendron when they finish 
    blooming. If you did not fertilize them last month, 
    fertilize citrus, flowering plants about to bloom, trees, 
    shrubs, and cool-season lawns. 
    •Prune spring-blooming plants as they finish blooming. 
    Prune hydrangeas, fuchsias, and bougainvilleas. 
    •Plant out summer bulbs. Set out summer-flowering 
    plants. 
    •Watch out for snails and earwigs, both of which 
    devour seedlings. Use a strong spray from the hose to 
    wash away aphids. Wrap fruit tree trunks with a sticky 
    tape to control ants. Wrap low so that birds, particularly 
    hummingbirds, will not be attracted. 
     
    These tips are from the book Northern California Gardening: A 
    Month-by-Month Guide, by Katherine Grace Endicott, 
    published by Chronicle Books, San Francisco, copyright 1996, 
    and are used with the publisher’s permission. ■

    Get Rid of Your Unwanted Vehicle―For Free
    City News 
    Sunnyvale residents who have a vehicle taking up 
    parking space may have the vehicle disposed of at no 
    cost during Sunnyvale’s Spring Clean-up Tow-away 
    program. Between April 1 and April 30, residents 
    may call the Sunnyvale Department of Public Safety 
    (DPS) and request a pick-up appointment.  
    Tip-a-Cop Breakfast To Feature Sunnyvale Public 
    Safety Officers and Special Olympics Athletes 
    This year's Tip-a-Cop event will be breakfast on 
    Wednesday, April 19, 7 to 10:30 a.m., at Coco's 
    Restaurant, 1206 Oakmead Parkway. Tip-A Cop is 
    one of the activities in the Northern California 
    Special Olympics Law Enforcement Torch Run 
    campaign. Officers pair up with restaurant wait staff 
    to help serve food, refill water glasses, and bus tables. 
    "Tip" envelopes are available at each table for 
    customers to tip their law enforcement server in 
    addition to the tip they leave for the regular wait staff. 
    Call 408-730-7199 for more information. 
    Towing will take place in May. Requests will be sent 
    to a city-approved towing company, which will call 
    residents directly to arrange for pick-up of the 
    vehicle. Vehicle owners must sign over ownership 
    and turn in the vehicle’s pink slip to the approved 
    towing company. Vehicles must be towable, with 
    four inflated tires, and have all debris and garbage 
    removed. 
    Special Olympics staff and athletes will be on hand to 
    talk about their program and benefits. All tips 
    collected by officers benefit Special Olympics, a 
    nonprofit organization that provides sports training 
    and competition opportunities for developmentally 
    disabled individuals. 
    Vehicles removed during the spring clean-up do not 
    qualify as an IRS tax donation. For additional 
    information on the program and to schedule your 
    vehicle pick-up, contact Michelle Tani in DPS at 
    (408) 730-7104, TDD (408) 730-7501. ■ 
     
    Sunnyvale Public Safety held its first Tip-a-Cop in 
    1999 and has held one or two events each year since.

    Fairwood School News 
     
    -Mary Jane Cabildo 
    -Haley Cross 
     
     
    The Fairwood Parent and Teachers Group (PTG) met 
    March 28 at 6 p.m. Thank you to all who attended.  
    Oh what a day it was! Opening day (April 1) for 
    Lakewood PONY Baseball was awesome. The teams 
    looked great in their uniforms. Now the 16 teams are 
    out on the field playing―we have to put in a lot of 
    games for these 175 kids, so we have games almost 
    every day of the week, at the Lakewood ball fields 
    near the Lakewood Park Building. Come out and 
    watch your kids and friends play. Great food is 
    available from the Snack Shack. We are also looking 
    for more volunteers to staff the Snack Shack. ■ 
    Kindergarten registration began April 3. Children 
    must be five years old on or before December 2, 
    2006 to be eligible for kindergarten in the fall. To 
    enroll your child you must provide proof of residency 
    that includes ALL of the following: Current utility 
    bill, (NO cable bills), valid rental, lease, or sales 
    agreement, picture identification such as a driver’s 
    license or passport, and proof of immunizations. 
    Verification of date of birth must also be submitted: a 
    birth certificate, baptismal certificate, passport, 
    statement by local registrar or county recorder 
    certifying the date of birth, or a notarized affidavit of 
    a parent or guardian. If you have any questions, 
    please call the Fairwood office at 523-4870.

    enjoy learning about the forest, meadow, and stream 
    communities through the use of games and other 
    activities. Friendships made during the week may last 
    a lifetime. During the week, classroom teachers will 
    be available to help students adjust to being away
