    Parade at Risk—Estimated Cost Is $2000—
    That LVNA Does Not Have 
     
    December is coming up fast and I am in the 
    process of planning the annual Lakewood Village 
    Holiday Parade, which is scheduled for 
    December 11, 2004.  However, this year the 
    Lakewood Village Neighborhood Association is 
    facing some high fees we haven’t had to pay 
    before, and so the entire parade is jeopardized.  
    Before last year, the only expenses the LVNA 
    had for the parade were the snacks and drinks 
    for the party afterwards at the park.  Last year, 
    because of budget cuts, the City of Sunnyvale 
    decided to charge us to cover costs they had 
    previously provided for free.  First they wanted 
    to charge us a $100 fee for an Unenclosed Use 
    Permit.  This they decided to waive because it 
    was the first year they were charging it and we 
    hadn’t known about that cost in advance.  Then 
    they wanted us to pay for city employees to put 
    up barricades.  Thanks to the efforts of 
    Lakewood resident and former parade planner 
    Craig Crawford, we had a matching grant from 
    the city to cover costs of the parade, so the 
    barricade fees were covered.  This year, 
    however, we have no grant and no fee waiver, 
    and the city also wants to charge us for the 
    overtime expenses of the public safety officers 
    needed to manage the road closure and traffic 
    directing needed for the parade.  We are also 
    faced with a new insurance premium.  
    Previously, insurance coverage was included in 
    the annual membership fee we pay to the 
    United Neighborhoods of Santa Clara County 
    organization.  We had to let them know about  
     
    big events, but no additional premiums were 
    charged.  This year is far different: the 
    insurance company that UNSCC uses has 
    increased its fees dramatically.  We now face an 
    unknown--possibly $200 or more--insurance 
    premium for events such as the picnic and 
    parade.  Altogether, the costs we face this year 
    for the parade are about $2000.  This is an 
    amount the LVNA simply cannot cover.   
      
    LVNA vice president Thom Mayer has talked to 
    Mayor John Howe and City Manager Amy Chan 
    about the estimated charges, but no decision 
    has been made yet about how, or if, city officials 
    will help find a way to cover these costs for us.  
    The Lakewood Village Holiday Parade is a 
    tradition in Sunnyvale, and city officials are 
    major participants.  As far as I know, it is also 
    the city’s only major parade.  The mayor and 
    the city manager have both acknowledged that 
    it is an important holiday tradition that serves 
    the city through community building, so we 
    hope that they will find a way to help us.  The 
    major cost is the overtime pay for the public 
    safety officers, estimated at $1260.  Even if that

    Annual UNSCC Conference Was Great! 
     
    Last year I took the opportunity to attend the 
    10th anniversary annual conference of the 
    United Neighborhoods of Santa Clara County 
    (UNSCC).  It was a most informative day of 
    seminars on issues affecting neighborhood 
    associations.  Even though faced with a severe 
    budget crisis and the loss of their one paid 
    employee, UNSCC again organized a great event 
    that was again highly informative. This year I 
    attended a workshop on conflict resolution and 
    learned about local resources available to help 
    bring conflicts to a favorable resolution.  These 
    include Project Sentinel and the Santa Clara  
    County Office of Human Relations Dispute 
    Resolution Program.  If you have a conflict with 
    a neighbor (or anyone else, for that matter) that 
    you need help with, you can contact these  
     
    organizations.  Project Sentinel can be reached 
    at 408-720-9888.  The Santa Clara County 
    Office of Human Relations Dispute Resolution 
    Program phone number is 408-792-2300. 
     
    I also attended a seminar on neighborhood 
    planning, which urged local residents to stay 
    informed about plans their city may have and 
    how those fit in with the vision a neighborhood 
    may have for itself.  The final session I attended 
    was on the woeful situation of the insurance 
    coverage available for neighborhood association 
    events.  Ed Rast of the UNSCC has done an 
    outstanding job of wading through the insurance 
    information and grilling the insurance company 
    itself to better understand the whole process 
    and how to protect the member neighborhoods 
    without leaving ourselves open to unnecessarily 
    large premiums.  The UNSCC provides a muchneeded service by bringing together people who 
    are involved with neighborhood associations so 
    we can learn about issues that affect us all. 

    Criminal Incidents in Park 
    Safety in Tense Situations 
    - Faith Rogers 
    The fatal shooting of two men near Lakewood 
    Elementary School and Lakewood Park on 
    October 12 and an assault on a man walking in 
    the park in the evening of October 7 point out 
    the need for taking measures to keep yourself 
    and the neighborhood safe.  
     
     
     
     
    Helped by information from neighborhood 
    residents, Sunnyvale public safety officers 
    arrested six suspects, less than 24 hours after 
    the shootings. Police investigation indicated that 
    the murders resulted from a domestic dispute, 
    not drug dealing or gang activity. The action of 
    residents, who reported hearing gunshots and 
    later answered the questions asked by police, 
    made the fast arrests possible.  
     
    The other criminal incident, the assault, 
    occurred when a man walking his dog 
    confronted a group who were drinking beer and 
    painting graffiti on a concrete table and trash 
    cans. He told them to stop. The man was 
    punched and kicked. Such incidents are not 
    common in Lakewood Village, but this assault 
    should remind us all to take some commonsense precautions: 
     
    ¾ Be alert and aware of people and activities 
    around you when on the street or in a park. 
    ¾ Walk with another person, if possible. 
    ¾ Avoid walking after dark. 
    ¾ Carry a cell phone. 
    ¾ Report suspicious or illegal activity. 
     
    Don't confront people who are acting suspicious 
    or doing something illegal. Instead, call 911 if 
    it’s an emergency, such as a person with a gun. 
    Be prepared to give the location of the 
    emergency — street address or street or 
    highway area. If the situation is not an 
    emergency, call 730-7100 to reach Sunnyvale 
    Public Safety. Other numbers to use are the 
    drug hotline, 245-3784, and the graffiti hotline, 
    730-7680. 
     
    If you see people doing something illegal, try to 
    observe as many details as you can, and be 
    prepared to help the police in their 
    investigation. 

    November in Your Yard 
    and Garden 
    ¾ Finish planting spring bulbs. 
    ¾ Shop for native plants and set 
    them in the garden. 
    ¾ Complete fall cleanup in the 
    garden. 
    ¾ Despite the drop in the temperature, 
    composting continues. Add disease-free 
    leaves and chopped-up, dead annuals to the 
    compost pile. Remember to keep the pile as 
    damp as a wrung-out sponge for faster 
    composting. 
    ¾ Keep after the snails. 
    ¾ Watch out for ants invading the house from 
    the garden. 
    ¾ Wrap vulnerable, young tree trunks, 
    particularly citrus trees, with paper or burlap 
    to protect them from freezing. 
    ¾ Water plants as needed. 
    ¾ Stabilize plants that might be harmed by 
    autumn wind. 
    ¾ Prune wisteria and clematis to control size 
    and encourage new flowering wood in 
    spring. Lightly prune forsythia. 
    ¾ Water roses but withhold all fertilizer. When 
    the ground freezes, mulch the rose bed 
    heavily. 
    ¾ Finish transplanting fall- and winterflowering plants into the bedding plot. 
    ¾ Harvest what is left of the summer 
    vegetables before the rain and cold destroy 
    them. Continue to plant the winter vegetable 
    garden with cool-season crops such as 
    beets, broccoli, cabbage, carrots, 
    cauliflower, onions, parsnips, and turnips. If 
    you have put the vegetable garden to rest 
    over the winter, cover it with manure so that 
    the manure will age properly over the 
    winter. 
    ¾ Flush your watering system to remove 
    mineral deposits. Soak all newly 
    transplanted plants until the rains come. 
     
    These tips are from the book Northern California Gardening: A 
    Month-by-Month Guide, by Katherine Grace Endicott, published by 
    Chronicle Books, San Francisco, ©1996, and are used with the 
    publisher’s permission. See http://www.lvna.net/Resources 
    /Reading_List/reading_list.html  
    LVNA Meetings 
    – Skip Gottschalk, LVNA Secretary 
    October 13 General Meeting 
    1. Captain Mark Stivers, from Sunnyvale Public 
    Safety, provided information on the double 
    homicide that took place October 12 outside of 
    Lakewood School. Captain Stivers said that it 
    appears to have started as a domestic dispute. 
    Arrests have been made in this case.  
    There was also a recent assault in Lakewood 
    Park on a Lakewood Village resident by a group 
    of men who were observed putting graffiti on 
    park property. As of October 30, no arrests have 
    been made in this case. If anyone has 
    information regarding this incident please 
    contact Sunnyvale Public Safety, 730-7100.  
    2. Michele Bromstead, newly assigned 
    Community Outreach Coordinator, described her 
    role and responsibilities in assisting with 
    neighborhood events and activities. 
    3. Val Rivera, from the Sunnyvale Housing 
    Division, provided a briefing on the Housing 
    Rehabilitation Program. This program can assist 
    low-income families, the disabled, and senior 
    citizens. 
    4. Jeff Goldman, from the city Planning 
    Department, provided a briefing on Sunnyvale’s 
    Consolidated Plan. This is a five-year plan that 
    covers the city’s housing and community 
    development needs. It deals with four major 
    federal funding programs and sets priorities for 
    use of funds and grants. 
    5. Open forum – Voter information pamphlets 
    were available. Fairwood School is participating 
    in a contest for recycling old phone books. 
    Drainage, trash, and tree removal issues were 
    discussed. 
      
    October 28 Board Meeting 
    1. Faith Rogers was nominated for LVNA 
    treasurer. The nomination was approved. LVNA 
    bank account activity and Villager advertising 
    accounts were reviewed.  
    2. The LVNA Annual Christmas Parade was 
    discussed. Because of the city’s current budget,

    Parade is in jeopardy unless funding 
    assistance is received. 

    Mom’s Night Out at Lakewood 
    Elementary School 
    -Beth Hayward 
    Lakewood Elementary School 5th graders will 
    hold their first annual Mom’s Night Out on 
    Friday, December 3, 2004 in the school cafeteria 
    from 7 to 9 p.m. 
     
    Proceeds will benefit the 5th Grade Science 
    Camp fund. We want to make sure all of our 5th 
    graders are able to attend camp this year. Come 
    prepared to shop for fun things! Various vendors 
    will be happy to help you with some seasonal 
    shopping. Home-baked goodies will be available 
    for purchase, and child care will allow you to 
    shop and enjoy some holiday cheer. 
     
    We are looking for artisans or vendors who 
    would like to set up a booth for our shopping 
    night; all we ask is that you come prepared to 
    sell and that you give 10% of your income that 
    night to the Science Camp fund. Please contact 
    Beth Hayward at (408) 734-4246 or Lori 
    Abrahams at 522-8272 if you are interested in 
    selling your products. We look forward to seeing 
    you all on December 3! 
     
    If you aren't interested in shopping but would 
    like to make a donation to the Lakewood 
    Elementary Science Camp Fund, please let us 
    know. Science Camp is an incredible experience 
    for the 5th graders–one that lasts a lifetime. For 
    some families, the financial commitment is more 
    than they can manage alone, and we don't want 
    any of our children to miss out because of the 
    cost. We welcome your contributions and 
    appreciate your support. 

    Community Meetings on Consolidated Plan 
    The City of Sunnyvale is developing a housing and community development plan for 2005-2010 – the 
    "Consolidated Plan." This plan will guide the City in spending federal housing and community 
    development funds. What are the needs in Lakewood Village? The city needs your input. 
     
    What are the needs and possible solutions, and 
    where should federal money be spent over the 
    next five years (2005-2010) in the areas of: 
    Community Facilities and Services 
    Economic Development 
    Homeless Facilities and Services 
    Housing 
    Public Infrastructure Improvements 
    Neighborhood Preservation 
    Rental Housing Preservation 
     
    Examples of Current Spending: 
    Services for Homeless, Seniors, Youth and 
    People with Disabilities 
    Community Centers 
     
    Housing Improvement Programs 
    (Rehab, Paint, Accessibility) 
    Sidewalk Accessibility 
    1st-Time Homebuyer's Assistance 
    New Affordable Rental Housing 
     
    At the meetings, residents will be encouraged to complete survey forms about community development 
    needs. Copies of the survey forms are available in several languages on the City's website at 
    http://sunnyvale.ca.gov/housing
