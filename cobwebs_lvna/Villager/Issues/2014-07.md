    Every park in Sunnyvale is just about
    completely renovated once every 18
    years and this year is Lakewood Park's turn. Unfortu-
    nately, the status of the Branch Library is gumming
    up the works. The new library is tied up in litigation
    having to do with the sale of the Raynor Activity Cen-
    ter property, and no progress is possible until the Ie-
    gal chatlenges are fully litigated. ln the meantime, the
    Parks people face a dilemma. They would like to plan
    Lakewood Park's renovation, taking into account the
    final design and placement of the library building.
    Without knowing where (or even if) the library is to be
    constructed, they are having trouble planning the rest
    of the park area work.
    Scott Morton wants to describe the situation and ask
    for our input as to how the City should proceed.
    There are basically three options: 1) plan and
    undertake the renovation assuming the library
    Iitigation will be resolved quickly and the building will
    be built as planned; 2) plan and renovate only the
    western half of Lakewood park at this time; or 3) skip
    the Lakewood Park renovation this year and do Fair
    Oaks Park instead, pushing Lakewood back to next
    year. Each choice has risks, and each choice will
    impact our neighborhood significantly. Come to the
    meeting, hear Scott's presentation and then let him
    know what you think the City should do.
