    By Fred Fowler
    It wasn't long ago that Sunnyvale voters
    chose to enact a new set of regulations
    governing guns and ammunition within the
    city limits. Placed on the November ballot
    as "Measure C", the new regulations
    required allfirearms to be stored under
    lock and key, all lost or stolen weapons be
    reported to the Sunnyvale Public Safety
    department, purchaser and transaction
    information for all ammunition sales be
    recorded and that ownership of
    ammunition magazines with a capacity of
    10 rounds or more to be prohibited.

    Sunnwale in Gun Battle with
    the NRA
    By Fred Fowler
    (Continued from page 1)
    Sunnyvale voters made their will known
    in an unmistakable way: Measure C
    passed by a2 to 1 margin, with "Yes"
    garnering more than 66% of the vote.
    As could have been predicted, the gun industry and its
    representatives have reacted with a pair of lawsuits. On
    December 10th, the US Firearms company (a Sunnyvale
    gun dealer located near the intersection of Mary Avenue
    and El Camino Real) filed suit to invalidate the ammunition
    sales reporting and the lost/stolen firearm reporting
    requirements of the law. On December 16th, six
    Sunnyvale residents filed suit to lift the restrictions on
    ammunition magazine capacity. Both lawsuits claim the
    restrictions conflict with California law, Federal law and the
    United States Constitution
    The fact that a Sunnyvale business and six
    Sunnyvale residents are the "plaintiffs" for
    these lawsuits should not fool anyone into
    believing that larger forces are not
    involved. The US Firearms lawsuit has
    another plaintiff, namely the National
    Shooting Sports Foundation (NSSF). The
    NSSF is an industry association
    representing firearm dealers and gun
    shows nationwide. One of the ironic things
    about the NSSF is the location of its
    headquarters. They are based in NeMown, Connecticut
    and their building is only 3 miles away from the site of the
    Sandy Hook elementary school. The NSSF has retained a
    high-powered law firm from San Diego (Wright &
    L'Estrange) to plead its case. They have asked for an
    injunction to stop the law from taking effect while the suit is
    litigated.
    The six Sunnyvale residents suing about ammunition
    magazine capacity have an ever bigger set of backers.
    The attorneys handling their case are from the law firm
    Michael & Associates, of Long Beach, California. The
    home page of the firm's web site lists "Firearms Law" as
    one of their principle areas of practice, and they list both
    the National Rifle Association and the California Rifle and
    Pistol Association as major long-time clients.
    The Michael & Associates website states in part:
    ... challenging a law's constitutionality, advocating to
    invalidate or change a law, ... our lawyers have been
    there. We know where to find answers when illogical,
    ill-conceived and poorly implemented laws do not
    provide them. We know the state and local
    requirements and politics. Our network of professional
    relationships includes politicians and political staffers,
    as well as staff at regulatory agencies administrating
    firearms laws ...
    Clearly, the gun industry has brought out the "heavy
    artillery''to challenge Measure C.
    One of the tactics used in these kinds of cases is to
    make the prospect of court fights so expensive those
    cash-strapped municipal governments give up without a
    fight. It is worth noting that in both lawsuits the plaintiffs
    are asking the City of Sunnyvale to pay all of their legal
    costs.
    The day the first lawsuit was filed, Sunnyvale got
    some welcome help. Sunnyvale
    patterned Measure C after some "model legisla-
    tion" created by an organization called The Law
    Center to Prevent Gun Violence (LCPGV), which
    is a non-profit organization promoting "smart" gun
    laws. The same day the initial suit was filed, the
    LCPGV announced that one of its members - the
    San Francisco based law firm Farella Braun &
    Martel LLC - would defend the city in court, free of
    charge. Farella Braun & Martel is a respected firm
    that has been involved in a number of high profile
    cases, including the successful challenge to Cali-
    fornia's "Proposition 8" restrictions on same sex mar-
    riages.
    The fact that Sunnyvale will be getting high-level "pro
    bono" legal help takes the threat of high legal costs out
    of the equation for the city. lt will likely cause the cases
    to be heard in their entirety and not "settled out of
    court".
    For the next few months (or even years), Sunnyvale will
    be on the front lines of the legal battle over "gun con-
    trol". Measure C set the stage, and even though the
    plaintiffs are local the battle will be fought out in court
    by national organizations and high level lawfirms.
    It will be interesting to watch. I

    Neighborhood Crime Corner
    On December 8th
    Sunnyvale Depaftment of Public Safety
    hosted the annual Rides-for-Toys that
    took place at the Toys-R-Us parking lot
    in Sunnyvale.
    The Rides-For-Toys gives community
    members an opportunity to donate toys
    and go for a ride on a fire engine. The event ran from
    10:00 a.m. to 1:00 p.m. and families
    from all over Sunnyvale donated more
    than 200 new toys while enjoying the
    fire engine rides and taking pictures
    with Public Safety Officers.
    The toys were delivered to Sunnyvale
    Community Services for distribution to
    Sunnyvale families in need this holiday
    season. Four Sunnyvale Fire Engines were used to
    provide rides. PSO Rego entertained the crowd with
    holiday songs while PSO Barba provided safety
    education to participants as they waited in line.
    Sunnyvale DPS family members Darren Chetcuti, Michael
    O'Brien, and Christopher Dunson helped collect all the
    toys and stacked them onto an antique fire engine.
    Our Sunnyvale Explorer Volunteers ensured people
    safely got on and off the engines and made sure
    everyone wore their seat belt. The event would not have
    been successful without the support of all the many
    volunteer participants which included: DPS Explorers
    Post 417, DPS family volunteers, Sunnyvale Community
    Service volunteers, Photographer Dave Thompson and
    Len Williams of the Santa Clara County Fire Associates.
    A very special thanks to Public Safety Specialist Dori
    Fontaine who worked behind{he-scenes to make this a
    fun and successful event. O
