    Picnic Date Changed To October 7 
    The date for the annual picnic, usually held in September, 
    had to be changed this year. The new date is Saturday 
    October 7, 2006. I am confident that this date will work 
    out fine and we will have a lovely early fall picnic. Please 
    come to our next general meeting on September 14, 2006 
    at 7 p.m. in the Lakewood Park building—we will need 
    some volunteers to help on picnic day with set-up, 
    grilling, and clean-up. 
    If you are new to the neighborhood, be sure to go to the 
    picnic—it’s a great way to meet your neighbors. We 
    invite people to bring a dessert or some other dish to 
    share. The LVNA provides burgers, veggie burgers, hot 
    dogs, grilled veggies, chips, chili, and beverages. We will 
    have some fun activities for the kids—a piñata and 
    probably face-painting, and perhaps a fire truck for the 
    kids to check out. We will have a raffle for prizes as well. 
    Come to the general meeting in September for more 
    details.■

    Community Gardens Host Fall Harvest Festival 
    The new Sunnyvale Community Gardens will hold a 
    Fall Harvest Festival on Sunday, October 1, from 1 to 4 
    p.m. The event will include tours of the gardens, 
    entertainment, activities for kids, food, music, and 
    vegetable tasting. 
    The organic gardens, more than 100 separate beds, are 
    near the corner of Olive Avenue and Charles Street. 
    The garden beds, leased by Sunnyvale residents, are 
    now in full production. The Sunnyvale Sustainable 
    Gardening group, which got the community gardens 
    started and includes several master gardeners, is part of 
    the Sustainable Community Gardens (SCG) non-profit 
    organization. 
    Check www.SunnyvaleCommunityGardens.org for 
    more information.■

    September in Your Yard  
    and Garden 
     
    ☼For the best selection, purchase 
    spring bulbs as soon as they come on 
    the market. Shop for native plants, 
    trees, and shrubs. Do not buy or 
    plant frost-tender plants such as 
    citrus and bougainvillea (wait until next spring). 
    ☼Continue to weed. Add young, seedhead-free weeds 
    and excess summer growth to the compost pile. 
    Remember to keep the pile as damp as a wrung-out 
    sponge for faster composting. 
    ☼As always, keep after the snails. Use a strong spray 
    from the hose to wash away aphids. Ants are likely to 
    become a problem soon. Discourage them by picking 
    up fallen fruit and sealing off cracks that would lead 
    them into the house.  
    ☼Hold off fertilizing most plants in preparation for 
    winter, but do fertilize all flowering plants that are 
    about to bloom. Fertilize roses with a low-nitrogen 
    formula or one that has no nitrogen at all. Do not 
    fertilize most antique roses. 
    ☼Pinch off dead flowers. Of course, if you wish to 
    save seeds for planting in the future, you will want to 
    leave the entire flower head, particularly the swollen 
    ovaries where seeds form. 
    ☼Postpone transplanting fall- and winter-flowering 
    plants into the bedding plot during a heat wave. Hot 
    weather is hard on newly transplanted plants. If you do 
    transplant, shade them for a few days with the 
    temporary shelter of an umbrella or a wooden shake. 
    Stake tall, sprawling flowers. 
    ☼Harvest vegetables as they ripen. Pick them early in 
    the day. Plant the vegetable garden with beets, broccoli, 
    cabbage, carrots, cauliflower, garlic, leeks, onions, and 
    shallots. In the hottest areas wait until next month to 
    put in new crops. 
    ☼Keep the garden well watered, particularly citrus 
    trees. Soak all newly transplanted plants. 
    These tips are from the book Northern California Gardening: A 
    Month-by-Month Guide, by Katherine Grace Endicott, 
    published by Chronicle Books, San Francisco, copyright 1996, 
    and are used with the publisher’s permission.■

    Lakewood School News 
    -Annette Grasty, Lakewood School Principal 
    Our wonderful Parent-Teacher Organization (PTO), 
    is now working hard to become a member of the 
    national PTA. Please support us by becoming a 
    member. Look for more information in the student 
    folders and at Back-to-School night. 
    We welcome three new staff members to Lakewood 
    School: Miss Laura Smith, kindergarten; Mrs. Kathi 
    Brager, first grade; and Mrs. Shannon Gordon, fifth 
    grade. 
    Our school district is implementing a new Wellness 
    Policy this year. We will be making an effort to 
    promote healthy lifestyles and eating habits by no 
    longer offering students treats or snacks that are more 
     
     
     
    than 35% fat, 35% sugar, and 175 total calories. We 
    will re-open our Student Store in the fall, but we will 
    no longer sell ice cream. Please help us to promote 
    student wellness by sending your child to school with 
    healthy snacks like fruit, whole grains, and 
    vegetables. Students may not bring items such as 
    soda and candy to school. 
    If you purchase school lunches for your child, please 
    note that the cost is now $2.50 for lunch and $1.25 
    for breakfast; the reduced price will remain 40 cents. 
    Thank you for supporting learning at Lakewood 
    School!■

    Free Wireless Access at the Library 
    Getting on the Web just got easier for patrons of the 
    Sunnyvale Public Library. MetroFi, Inc. is now 
    providing free wireless Internet access at the library. 
    MetroFi, which is already providing free wireless 
    access to residents of Sunnyvale and in the downtown 
    business area, installed three transmitters atop 
    streetlight poles in the area surrounding the library. 
    MetroFi is currently working with city staff to deploy 
    additional transmitters to bring free wireless access to

    Sunnyvale Public Safety Opens Recruitment 
    The Sunnyvale Department of Public Safety (DPS) is 
    looking for success-oriented men and women. DPS 
    has opened recruitment for Public Safety Officers-InTraining (PSOIT) beginning Friday, September 1 and 
    continuing through Friday, September 29. 
    Sunnyvale’s Public Safety Officers are fully trained 
    as police officers and firefighters and perform both 
    duties on a rotating basis. After hiring, PSOITs attend 
    a police academy, fire academy, and EMT academy, 
    and complete a field training program. Sunnyvale 
    offers officers a benefits package that includes 
    comprehensive medical, optical, and dental coverage, 
    education incentives, bilingual pay, and more. During 
    academy training, officers receive a salary of 
    $72,990. Once on the job, salaries can rise to a top 
    level of $105,019.
