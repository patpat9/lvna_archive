    Background 
    Concern over this issue goes back to 1983 
    when development of the Mission College area 
    was still just a proposal.  A traffic signal at 
    Wildwood and Lawrence has long been 
    suggested as a solution, and the developers of 
    the Mercado Center had even agreed to 
    contribute money toward that traffic light 
    project.  However, solid plans for the traffic 
    light (which would involve re-routing Wildwood 
    to connect directly wi th Lawrence) have met 
    with many problems over the years.  Currently, 
    there is no money budgeted by the City of 
    Sunnyvale for the light (which would cost more 
    than $3 million to construct), and the time to 
    take advantage of the developer contribution 
    has run out.  Another option that has been 
    considered over the years is to close Wildwood 
    Avenue at the city limits with Santa Clara 
    during the problem times of the day or week.  
    This would keep Mercado traffic from heading 
    our way, but would also keep us from getting 
    in or out of the neighborhood that way during 
    those times.  There are pros and cons to 
    consider for any proposal to address this 
    problem, but the important thing right now is 
    to get the city to agree to do something
     and 
    allocate some funding. 
     
    The city did get a traffic study in the 
    neighborhood completed in March 2002; it 
    included a couple of neighborhood meetings for 
    feedback.  Cars were counted and the problem 
    areas identified, but we need to keep 
    reminding them that this problem is still here—
    and will probably get worse.  Hearing from the 
    people who are directly affected is the best

    Neighborhood Preservation Specialist 
    Some of you may remember that the City’s 
    Community Development Department met with 
    the Lakewood Village Neighborhood Association 
    Board in May of 2002.  The meeting was held 
    to discuss a program designed to improve the 
    overall maintenance, appearance and safety of 
    the properties in Lakewood Village. At meeting 
    conclusion, the City and the LVNA Board 
    developed the “Pilot Program.” 95 property 
    owners and residents of Lakehaven Dr., 
    between Lawrence and Meadowlake Dr. 
    and all of Lakenoll Dr. worked with City staff 
    to enhance their neighborhood.   
     
    The Pilot Program has produced great results; 
    Highlights include the following:  
     
    • 53 properties were improved by code 
    violation corrections including, but not 
    limited to: 
    Garage conversions 
    Roof and patio extensions 
    Animal control violations 
    Weeds 
    Inoperable vehicles 
    Overgrown vegetation  
    • Approximately 220 gallons of paint was 
    distributed to the Pilot Program households 
    for free.   
    9 out of 95 houses were painted.   
    • 5 property owners installed new driveways 
    or expanded their old driveways to prevent 
    vehicles from parking unpaved surfaces. 
    • 2 property owners removed their chain link 
    fences. 
    • 12 dumpsters were filled during a City 
    sponsored “Dumpster Day.”   
    This “Dumpster Day” brought the 
    Community together in helping to 
    revitalize a property where the property 
    owner was disabled.  
    • Rebuilding Together
    , a non-profit, volunteer 
    program designed to provide free repair 
    and renovation services to elderly, disabled 
    and low-income homeowners selected a 
    house in the Pilot Program for 
    rehabilitation. 
    Some of the work performed included 
    painting the inside and outside of the 
    house, installation of a new fence, the

    replacement of a broken window and 
    minor repairs inside and out. 
    • SSA Landscape , a contracted landscape 
    company, developed 6 front yard landscape 
    designs and 3 park strip landscape designs. 
    Landscape grants will be made available 
    to landscape approximately 10 front 
    yards and 14 park strips.

    If I were in charge of Lakewood Village, 
    I would address the lack of parking and 
    safety around Lakewood Park because it 
    is the hub, not the downfall, of the 
    Village. 
     
    Too many times I have passed Lakewood 
    Park, found myself stuck in the traffic 
    of party-goers in their cars and had no 
    space to park my own car near my home.  
    We cannot prevent all events from being 
    held in the park, so the solution is to 
    create more parking.  A 20 space lot 
    between the fence and picnic tables 
    where the dumpster currently is, and 
    expanding the lot next to the pool would 
    alleviate the problem.  People attending 
    parties, baseball games and the pool 
    would all be grateful. 
     
    Crucial items that are missing in the 
    community are two stop signs on the 
    intersection of Silverlake and Lakedale 
    next to Lakewood Park.  Children are 
    constantly crossing Silverlake from the 
    park, but often large, parked cars block 
    drivers from seeing them.  Some drivers 
    also enjoy speeding on such a long, 
    uninterrupted street.  We cannot wait 
    until a tragic accident finally awakens 
    us.  Stop signs would allow pedestrians 
    to cross safely into the park and 
    discourage speeding.  A bonus would be 
    the much-wanted increased police patrols 
    because they would need to enforce the 
    new signs. 
     
    If the Village does increase parking and 
    adds a stop sign, the costs may prompt 
    some people to want to cancel the annual 
    Holiday Parade.  I think the parade 
    would actually be beneficial to us 
    because revenue could be raised at the 
    picnic after the parade.  By charging $5 
    per adult and $3 per child (ages 4-12) 
    and having the City chip in, we would 
    eventually raise enough money to pay off 
    these new and much needed additions.

    SUNNYVALE, Calif. – With the 
    start of the new school year,  
    Sunnyvale Department of Public Safety (DPS) 
    officers have increased enforcement of traffic 
    safety laws around schools. 
     
    X continued in next column 
    X continued from previous column 
     
    Lt. Walt Lee of the DPS Traffic Safety Unit says 
    Sunnyvale officers have been doing this for 
    more than a half-dozen years. “Our goal is to 
    educate drivers, bicyclists and pedestrians while 
    we also enforce traffic regulations,” Lee said. 
    “All our patrol officers will be involved in this 
    campaign and we will be working around all 
    schools, both public and private, in Sunnyvale.” 
     
    The Sunnyvale effort coincides with the 
    countywide Operation Safe Passage. Many drop-
    off sites at schools cannot handle a large 
    number of cars arriving at the same time, and 
    this can lead to congestion and violations for 
    double-parking and jayw alking. In order to 
    reduce traffic congestion around schools, 
    parents are encouraged to use carpooling or, 
    where appropriate, have their children walk to 
    school in groups. 
     
    As part of the public education program, traffic 
    advisories will be distributed at all schools for 
    students to bring home to their parents. Special 
    advisories will be distributed at schools with 
    unique traffic issues. 
