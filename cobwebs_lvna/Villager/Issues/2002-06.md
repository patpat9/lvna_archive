    A Victory for Wetlands Restoration 
    - Editor 
    Many of you know that a large portion of the 
    waterfront of the south San Francisco Bay has 
    been used for commercial salt production in 
    evaporation ponds since the 1850’s. Cargill 
    Corporation, the agribusiness company that  
    currently owns the ponds, has agreed to sell 
    16,500 acres to the government so that they 
    can become a public wildlife refuge for birds, 
    fish, and other species.  
     
    Some 8,100 acres in Fremont, Alviso, 
    Sunnyvale, and East Palo Alto, will be added to 
    the fede rally funded Don Edwards San 
    Francisco Bay National Wildlife Refuge. Another 
    7,000 acres near Hayward and Union City, and 
    1,400 acres near the Napa River, will be owned 
    by the State of California. Cargill will continue 
    to operate 11,000 acres of ponds near  Newark. 
     
    Because all of the ponds will need to be 
    gradually diluted to match the salinity of the 
    Bay and some of the ponds will need to be 
    filled with Bay mud from dredging operations, 
    it will take many decades before wildlife and 
    plants can return.  
     
    Since 1850, nearly 80% of San Francisco Bay’s 
    wetlands have been lost to diking, filling, and 
    development. These 16,500 acres represent 
    more than 10% of that lost acreage, and their 
    restoration will provide habitat for wildlife, help 
    filter pollution, and improve water quality. ¢

    New Trail and Bridge for Calabazas 
    Creek 
    - Editor 
    In April, the City of Sunnyvale recommended 
    approval of funding for the Calabazas Creek 
    Trail Project. The project will provide for the 
    construction of a missing link to the City's 
    bicycle route network. It consists of a 
    connection to the Hetch -Hetchy path on the 
    Fairwood side of Lakewood Village, a crossing 
    of Calabazas Creek with a new trail bridge, and 
    extension of the trail northerly along the creek 
    levy to the Highway 237 vicinity, ultimately 
    connecting it with Baylands Park. When 
    completed, improvements will include signs, 
    fencing, gates, lights, trail surface, and a new 
    bridge across the Santa Clara Valley Water 
    District floodway. ¢

    President’s Column 
    - Craig Crawford 
    President, LVNA 
     
    This has been a busy and productive month in 
    the LVNA. Several major items are in the 
    works: 
     
    Traffic 
    For several years we have been plagued by 
    traffic from the movie complex cutting through 
    the neighborhood. Santa Clara could care less 
    if their development impacted Lakewood. 
    Sunnyvale did a traffic study recently and 
    indeed found that we are impacted by the 
    development. City staff prepared a report and 
    a recommendation to alleviate the traffic 
    problem. At May’s city council meeting several 
    alternatives to help with traffic were put to a 
    vote. The Wildwood traffic light at Lawrence 
    Expressway alternative passed the City Council 
    overwhelmingly. Manny Valero  and Tim Risch 
    cast the opposing votes. Tim had fiscal 
    concerns that were valid and real; Manny 
    wanted to give the traffic diversion gate 
    alternative a try, which was also a valid choice. 
    All in all the council was clear in understanding 
    that we have a pro blem with the traffic from 
    the movie complex invading the neighborhood 
    and creating an unsafe condition. Lakewood 
    residents were out in force at the council 
    meeting, several residents were able to speak 
    to the council about the traffic problems and 
    why the y wanted the traffic signal alternative. 
    Mayor Fred Fowler, a Lakewood resident, used 
    his vast fiscal knowledge to find the money for 
    the traffic signal and he used his 
    communication skills to bring real meaning to 
    the debate. Good job, Fred! The City staf f’s 
    report stated that the diversion gate was the 
    best option to control the traffic. This was 
    clearly not what the council felt. I seemed to 
    see city staff frown when this alternative 
    passed. Oh well, that’s politics! 
     
    Neighborhood Improvement Pilot Program 
    Lakewood is being surveyed this month to find 
    out what code enforcement issues are 
    important to residents. A pilot program will be 
    developed out of these responses. If you want 
    the pilot program used on your block be sure 
    to note that on the survey. The  results of this 
    survey are important to our neighborhood, so 
    send in the city provided stamped response as 
    soon as possible. This will be a neighborhood -
    friendly program; it will bring up the quality u 
    u of life that is so important to Lakewood. 
    Don’t wait, send in the survey now!  
     
    Dumpster Weekend 
    The dumpsters are coming to Lakewood on July 
    20th and 21st. This is a wonderful event that is 
    an annual happening in Lakewood. If this is 
    your first Dumpster weekend, let me explain. 
    The city puts huge trash dum psters at several 
    locations around Lakewood for residents to get 
    rid of the items that are no longer useful, 
    broken or just plan junk, and it’s free of 
    charge. It is a chance to make your property 
    look better by trimming the overgrown shrubs 
    that have been  growing out of control. Get rid 
    of the old bricks and concrete that you have 
    been piling in the back yard. Old appliances 
    that you have been meaning to get fixed but 
    realistically never will, get rid of them now. If 
    you have neighbors that can use a good yard 
    cleanup, let them know that this is the time to 
    do it. If some of our neighbors are not 
    physically able to do the cleaning, give them a 
    hand and you will be rewarded with a feeling 
    that you are helping someone that needs your 
    help. ¢

    Hetch-Hetchy Greenbelt to be 
    Renamed and Dedicated 
    - Editor 
    The City of Sunnyvale will be dedicating and 
    naming the Hetch -Hetchy Greenbelt with a 
    ribbon cutting ceremony on Monday, June 10 
    from 12 – 12:30 PM at the intersection of 
    Meadowlake Dr. and the Greenbelt, across the 
    street from Lakewood Elementary School in 
    Sunnyvale. The section of the Hetch -Hetchy 
    right-of-way in Sunnyvale will be named the 
    “John W. Christian Greenbelt”, after a former 
    City of Sunnyvale Parks and Recreation 
    Director who was instrumental in the vision 
    and implementation of changing the Hetch -
    Hetchy easement from a bare area to a 
    greenbelt with landscaping and amenities.   Mr. 
    Christian enthusiastically promoted the 
    greenbelt concept during his tenure with the 
    City from 1988 until his death in 1996. 
     
    The Hetch -Hetchy right -of-way in Sunnyvale is 
    a fairly linear piece of land that is 80 feet  wide 
    and 2.7 miles long that extends east to west 
    across the city.   The land is owned by the City 
    of San Francisco and is leased and maintained 
    by the City of Sunnyvale.   It serves as a 
    pedestrian and bicycle greenbelt that links 
    Fairwood Park with Orchard Gardens Park. 
     
    “The Greenbelt in Sunnyvale is an excellent 
    example of two agencies, Sunnyvale and the 
    San Francisco Public Utilities Commission, 
    working together to provide the public with a 
    recreational facility that combines form and 
    function,” said Sc ott Morton, Sunnyvale Parks 
    Supervisor. u 
     
     
     
     
    u The City has been working on this project 
    since late 1996 when the City Council 
    authorized staff to pursue an open space 
    agreement with the San Francisco Public 
    Utilities Commission for development and 
    maintenance of the 2.7 mile trail segment 
    within Sunnyvale city limits. 
     
    The ribbon cutting ceremony will be followed 
    by a reception with light refreshments.  ¢

    High Speed Internet Access in 
    Lakewood Village - Brian Smithson 
    My experience of trying to get high speed 
    Internet access in our neighborhood was that it 
    is difficult or impossible. I was e ventually able 
    to get a microwave -based wireless service, but 
    that service is no longer available to new 
    subscribers. 
     
    I would like to hear from others who have tried 
    to get DSL, cable modem, or other high -speed 
    Internet services in our area. If you tried – 
    whether or not you were successful – please 
    take a moment to send me a quick email about 
    it. If there is as much of a problem as I 
    suspect, then I plan to survey the 
    neighborhood to gather some statistics about 
    Internet access demand and perhaps convince  
    the local telephone, cable, and wireless 
    companies to properly provide services to 
    Lakewood Village. 
     
    Send your email to internet_survey@grot.com 
    and let me know what you tried (DSL, cable, or 
    other), which companies you contacted, and 
    whether or not you were successful. ¢
