    Community Emergency 
    Possible Neighborhood Fire Station 
    Closure  
    - Jim Lane 
    In a conversation with the Sunnyvale Fire 
    Chief, it was confirmed that the Fire 
    Department is in the planning stages of 
    reorganizing and possibly consolidating some 
    of the fire stat ions in Sunnyvale.  A major 
    concern is that Fire Station #6, located at 1282 
    Elko and Lawrence Station Road, could be 
    closed. 
     
    If Fire Station #6 were closed, there would be 
    an increase in the response time to our 
    neighborhood.  The distance from Fire Stat ion 
    #6 to the intersection of Lawrence Expressway 
    and Sandia/Lakehaven (about the center of our 
    neighborhood) is approximately one mile.  The 
    distance from the same intersection to the fire 
    station located at Wolfe and Arques (Fire 
    Station #4) is approxima tely 2.2 miles.  This 
    may not seem like a significant distance, but if 
    you take into consideration the traffic 
    congestion coming down Lawrence Expressway 
    and the additional red lights, there is little 
    doubt in the event of a life threatening fire or 
    medical emergency, where minutes make the 
    difference, the additional response time will 
    cost lives and property damage.  
     
    Fire Station #6 is also our primary coverage in 
    case of a disaster.  During an event such as an 
    earthquake, where roads and overpasses are 
    rendered too dangerous for travel, it is likely 
    we would be cut off from help.  
     
    Back in 1961, it took Lakewood Village 
    residents a year and a half of knocking on 
    doors, getting petitions signed, phone calls, 
    and meetings with the City of Sunnyvale to get 
    Fire Station #6 built.  The neighborhood is now  
    composed of over 1,600 homes in Lakewood

    As we all remember, and for those who don’t, the 
    ground work for the station began back in October 1961.  
    “Actually,” Bob Caswell said, “the ground work f or the 
    station began with a promise to my oldest son, Robert 
    Jr., shortly after the fire in which we almost lost Robert.  
    If it hadn’t been for Public Safety Officer Hanson and 
    Mr. John Zamora plus friends and neighbors, we would 
    not have saved him. “ I al so,” he continued, “promised 
    the people of Lakewood Village that if they would 
    support me I wouldn’t let them down.  I feel now that 
    my promise is complete. 
     
    In November 1961 things looked pretty gloomy for the 
    station.  Caswell’s committee began to circul ate a 
    petition which later was to be presented to the City 
    Council.  During this era past president of the LVRA, 
    Ray Harvey, began obtaining statistics on the time, 
    distance and accessibility.  Many long months of hard 
    work faced the committee with quite a few hardships 
    along the way.  Finally, with approximately 1,600 
    signatures plus all their data, the Committee went before 
    the Council.  Ray Harvey presented the arguments, in 
    behalf of The Village, and a motion was presented.  The 
    decision went four to th ree against the motion and it was 
    decided that the City Manager’s Office was to study the 
    problem and discussion was tabled.  After their own 
    findings had been compiled, the issue came before the 
    Council once again.  At this time Caswell presented 
    further facts and final arguments.  Once again the issue 
    became a motion.  The motion was not only to build 
    Lakewood’s station, but it included the construction of 
    two other stations to be constructed simultaneously.  The 
    motion again went four to three, this time  in favor. 
     
    It was a victory well appreciated by both Caswell and 
    Harvey, not to mention the whole Village. 
     
    Construction began in the fall of 1962 and reports were 
    made and kept up to date by the Committee.

    Mayor Fowler Responds 
    - Frederik Fowler, Mayor 
    (excerpted from email to the Editor) 
     
    My understanding of the situation is that Public 
    Safety is looking at Fire Station #5 (Lockheed ) 
    rather than #6 (Lawrence/Elko).  The concern 
    is that with all of the major development that 
    is anticipated in Moffett Park, there may be a 
    future need for more equipment to be 
    stationed there.  The Public Safety department 
    is working on a request for Cou ncil 
    authorization to spend some money to look 
    into the question.  That request hasn't come to 
    us yet, and probably won't until December. 
     
     
    The wording of the memo is vague, and may 
    be why it might have gotten misconstrued a 
    bit.  As far as I'm concerned, Fire Station #6 is 
    one of the last ones I would allow to be closed.  
    They are the first responders to medical issues 
    in both Lakewood Village and all of the Mobile 
    Home parks, where a large population of young 
    children and retirees live -- the very people 
    who need quick medical responses the most.   
     
    Chief Bakin told me over the phone that the 
    objective was to decrease response times for 
    all concerned, and to make sure that the right 
    equipment was located in the right place to be 
    effective.  
     
    I'll let you kn ow if there are further 
    developments. ¢

    City Manager LaSala Responds 
     
    - Robert LaSala, City Manager 
    (from a memo to Mayor Fowler) 
     
    CITY OF SUNNYVALE 
    OFFICE OF THE CITY MANAGER 
     
    October 15, 2002 
    TO:  Mayor Frederik Fowler 
    FROM:  City Manager Robert S. LaSala 
    SUBJECT: Information Regarding Fire Station 6 
     
    At the pancake breakfast last Saturday a question was asked 
    Greg Kevin if the Public Safety Department was looking to 
    close Fire Station 6.   Apparently a citizen had heard and 
    told you that at some time  in the future the station would 
    close.   
     
    This rumor probably resulted from the Public Safety 
    Department’s initiation of a non -routine item and proposed 
    Council Study Issue for this fiscal year to obtain funding to 
    conduct a “Fire Station Location and Res ource Allocation 
    Study”.  The study is being requested in light of the 
    increase in development in the Moffett Park area over the 
    last few years and possible additional changes with the 
    Moffett Park Specific Plan.  Because of this increase there is 
    a need to ensure that the City will continue to provide 
    emergency fire and EMS services that meet the goals 
    established in the Fire Services Sub -Element and Fire 
    Services Program outcome measures.   Although this increase 
    is occurring in the Moffett Park area the s tudy will include 
    the area north of Highway 101. 
     
    This project will evaluate: 
    •  fire station location(s)  
    •  resource allocation (equipment & personnel)  
    •  current and estimated future response times to 
    emergency fire and EMS calls for service 
     
    If the study is fund ed at the completion of it the Public 
    Safety and Community Development Departments and my 
    office will discuss the findings and recommendations.  
    Council will be involved at that time to make a decision on 
    any significant options.  ¢

    An Update on Code Enforcement 
    Activities in Lakewood Village 
     
    The month of October was very exciting for 
    both Neighborhood Preservation and the 
    Department of Pub lic Safety.  During the 
    September meeting with the residents of the 
    Lakewood Village Pilot Program, Neighborhood 
    Preservation received a tip regarding numerous 
    chickens at two properties in the 
    neighborhood.  Based upon the information 
    provided, Neighborhood Preservation contacted 
    the Department of Public Safety’s Animal 
    Control Unit and on October 9, 2002, Animal 
    Control and the police obtained a warrant and 
    searched the two houses in the Pilot Program 
    area for illegal activities.   During the search, 
    Public Safety and Animal Control discovered 
    evidence of illegal cock fighting and drugs.  The 
    outcome of the search resulted in the 
    euthanization of 154 chickens and roosters, the 
    arrest of the property owner and his son for an 
    illegal cockfighting ring, anima l cruelty, and 
    drug possession.  Neighborhood Preservation 
    staff is currently in the Abatement process to 
    remove all of the illegal sheds, debris, junk,
