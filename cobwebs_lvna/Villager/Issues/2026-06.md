    Village Vistas: Meet the Majestic Deodar Cedar
    LVNA VILLAGER - JUNE 2026
    PAGE 4
    Hello neighbors! Welcome to this month's Village Vistas column. Today, we're turning our attention to one of
    the most stunning evergreens lining our streets: the Deodar Cedar. While you can spot these graceful trees
    throughout the neighborhood, be sure to check out the magnificent cluster of them growing right in
    Fairwood Park.
    Here are a few fascinating facts to share on your next walk around:
    Timber of the Gods: The name "Deodar" is derived from the ancient Sanskrit word devadāru, which
    beautifully translates to "wood of the gods."
    Towering Heights: While they typically grow between 40 to 80 feet in neighborhoods like ours, in their
    native Himalayan habitat, these incredible trees can reach towering heights of up to 250 feet!
    Unique Upright Cones: Unlike most conifers that have hanging cones, the Deodar Cedar's barrel-
    shaped cones sit proudly upright on the branches and can take up to three years to fully mature.
    Built-In Antifreeze: Ever wonder how they stay so green in the winter? Their needles are packed with a
    carbohydrate-rich fluid that acts as a natural antifreeze, allowing them to thrive and photosynthesize all
    year long.
    Aromatic Defense: The inner wood produces a highly aromatic oil that naturally deters insects, making
    the tree incredibly resilient and a popular, durable choice for woodworking.

    What Issues Should You Report?
    Look around Lakewood Village and let us know about areas that need attention, including but not limited to:
    Sidewalks that are cracked or unsafe
    Potholes that need filling
    Overgrown trees in need of trimming
    Abandoned vehicles that are taking up space
    Neglected properties that impact our community’s appearance
    How to Submit a Report
    The more reports we get, the stronger our case! You can submit issues in two easy ways:
    1. Email us with specific details (exact addresses, descriptions, and photos if possible) at board@lvnasv.org.
    2. Report directly to the City via Access Sunnyvale (https://sunnyvale.dynamics365portals.us). This officially
    enters your complaint into the system. If you do this, let us know so we can help follow up!
    3. Attend a City Hall meeting on a Tuesday to voice your concerns in person—this can make an even bigger
    impact and catch their attention. If you choose this option, also file a claim on Access Sunnyvale to
    strengthen your case. More information about the meeting: https://www.sunnyvale.ca.gov/your-
    government/governance/city-council/council-meeting-information
    Together, we can make Lakewood Village a safer, cleaner, and better-maintained community. Thank you for
