    The “Hot August Night” That Wasn't 
     
    In the last newsletter there was a brief article about the 
    upcoming “Hot August Nights” car show that was  
    scheduled to happen on August 4th.  As it turns out, the 
    event didn't happen.  The decision to cancel it came after 
    the July edition of the newsletter came out, and there  
    wasn't a good way to let the neighborhood know it had 
    been called off.  I'm sorry for the confusion that resulted.   
     
    The Hot August Nights event is a complicated one to put 
    on.  A number of different factors have to come together 
    properly for it to take place.  First of all, permission needs 
    to be obtained from the manager of the Lakewood  
    Shopping Center in order to use its parking lot.  Next, a 
    city permit must be obtained several weeks in advance.  
    Next someone has to arrange for a band and a stage and 
    sound system in order to have music.  Someone has to 
    make sure there is electricity available to power the sound 
    system.  Someone has to make sure the LVNA is  
    protected by event insurance.  Someone has to arrange 
    for traffic barricades to mark off the parts 
    of the shopping center parking lot that 
    will be used.  Someone has to arrange 
    for a porta-potty to satisfy the city's large 
    event permitting requirements.

    chairs for the kid crafts area.  Someone 
    has to go call on all of the vendors in the

    what to expect, and to give them a

    West Nile Virus in our Area 
    -Phyllis Fowler 
     
    Last month Fred reported in 
    The Villager that we found a 
    dead raven in our front yard. It 
    looks like it was a warning.  
     
    In July, the Santa Clara  
    County Vector Control District 
    (SCCVCD) confirmed that 
    adult mosquitoes were  
    collected from the surrounding areas tested positive 
    with West Nile Virus (WNV). This area included Santa 
    Clara, San Jose and part of Sunnyvale. The zip codes 
    included: 94089, 95002 & 95054. The SCCVCD 
    fogged the areas from July 29th and ending that night.  
     
    The WNV infection is transmitted by mosquito bites 
    and does not cause symptoms in most people.  
    However, in some individuals it can cause fever,  
    headache, body aches, and in severe cases,  
    significant neurological damage or death. Adults older 
    than 50 years old and individuals with chronic medical 
    conditions are the most at risk for serious  
    complications.  
     
    Since the arrival of West Nile Virus in 2003, 4,800  
    people across California have contracted the disease, 
    177 of those cases were fatal. 
     
    Residents can take practical steps by eliminating any 
    standing water around your home.  
     
    Tips for preventing mosquitoes can be found at : 
    \http://t.co/cmFM9xyc6e  
     
    Contact the Vector Control District if you are being 
    bothered by  
    mosquitoes or know of a potential mosquito-breeding 
    source. For free assistance with mosquito control, 
    WNV, or other vectors, contact the District office by 
    call (408) 918-4770 or fill out a service request online 
    at SCCvector.org.  

    In addition, there was a volunteer who committed to take 
    on the task of coordinating with Goodwill and the 
    Sunnyvale School District to have them participate in 
    Dumpster Day again.  Another person began to work on 
    the task of getting us insurance coverage for Dumpster 
    Day, Hot August Nights and the Holiday Parade. 
     
    As time passed, our volunteers became busy 
    with their own work and let their LVNA tasks slip.  
    I ended up doing the coordinating with Goodwill 
    and the school district for Dumpster Day, and I 
    ended up having to arrange the necessary event 
    insurance.  I was busy, too, and I didn't have 
    time to coach the volunteers about fulfilling their 
    commitments – I just did the tasks myself in  
    order to get them done. 
     
    The last newsletter was put together at the end of June, 
    before the real state of things for Hot August Nights  
    became apparent.  I was focused on getting Dumpster 
    Day out of the way, and it wasn't until after Dumpster Day 
    had passed that I realized how little had been done to 
    prepare for Hot August Nights.  If it was going to happen, 
    I would have to once again do all of the tasks I had done 
    last year, plus a few more.  In addition, I would have to do 
    all of that work in just a few days.  There was no way it 
    could work. 
    I'm not blaming anyone.  People have lives and their 
    own priorities to deal with.  Every hour someone  
    volunteers for the LVNA is a gift to all of us.   
     
    For events like Hot August Nights, there has to be 
    someone who says “I will do whatever it takes to make 
    this happen”.  Someone has to be willing to step in and 
    take care of things when no one else will.   
     
    That's what I did last year, and the event 
    was a great success.  This year I am 
    breaking under the strain.  I couldn't be the 
    one with unlimited responsibility this time.  
    The event didn't happen as a result. 
     
    Some people have expressed  
    disappointment that Hot August Nights was 
    canceled, and want to know if it will be  
    rescheduled.  My answer to them is “of course it can 
    be rescheduled.  I can show you exactly what you 
    need to do to organize it.” 
     
    We'll see if anyone else is willing to make an unlimited 
    commitment.  All someone has to do is say “I will do 
    whatever it takes to make this happen.” 
     
    Will you?   
