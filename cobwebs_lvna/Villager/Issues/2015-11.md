    Next LVNA Meeting:  El Nino! 
     
    Greetings Neighbors.! 
     
    We've had a long hot summer that never seemed to 
    end.   I loved it!  Temperatures were in the 80's all 
    through September and October, with warm comforta-
    ble weather that lasted through  
    Halloween.    
     
    I'm a summer kind of guy.  Give me bright sunshine 
    and short sleeves and I'm happy.  I feel blessed that 
    2015 gave us such a long spell of such nice weather. 
     
    That nice weather does have its downsides, though.  
    As we all know, we've been living through one of the 
    longest droughts on record.  All of the reservoirs are at 
    historic low levels, with Anderson and San Luis at only 
    about 25% of normal capacity.  Earlier this year we 
    heard a presentation by the Santa Clara Valley Water 
    District about conserving water.  Many of us have been 
    wishing for rain.       
     
    There is an old proverb about “being 
    careful what you wish for”. It looks like El 
    Nino might answer our prayers for rain 
    and then some.  Meteorologists have 
    been predicting that this El Nino could 
    be the strongest since 1998 or even 
    1983.  Those of us who were here at the 
    time remember that Lakewood Village 
    suffered through some flooding during 
    those rainy periods.

    Sunnyvale: Lakewood Village Holiday 
    Parade celebrates 24 Years 
     
    Neighbors, families and friends be ready to line the 
    streets Dec. 5th to celebrate the 24th anniversary of a 
    treasured Sunnyvale tradition, the Lakewood Village 
    Holiday Parade. 
     
    The parade will kick off at about 11 a.m. at Lakewood 
    Elementary School led by the Fremont High Marching 
    Band. The parade will turn left on Meadowlake, and 
    then turn left onto Lakehaven. It will cross Lawrence 
    Expressway to Sandia and continue to Fairwood Park. 
    At the Park , Santa will greet children and cookies and 
    hot chocolate will be provided. Do you know anyone 
    who would like to be in our parade? It so, please 
    contact Pat at patjesse@sbcglobal.net.  
     
    Students from the Kids Learning After School program 
    will also pass out candy and collect new, 
    unwrapped toys to donate to Sunnyvale 
    Community Services.  Please be aware the 
    this route will be closed to traffic for about an 
    hour. 

    Home Safety Tips from ADT:  
     
    Keeping you and your family safety is a big deal. But  
    sometimes, it’s the little changes you make that add up to a 
    big difference. We’ve collected these residential security 
    tips to help every member of the family feel safe and  
    secure.  
     
    Here are home security tips to help  
    you and your family stay safe.  
     
    If possible, install a locked mailbox at your home and never 
    put outgoing mail containing personal information in an 
    unsecured out-box or in your mailbox for pick up. 
     
    Never leave notes on your door for service people or visi-
    tors when you are not home. It’s a signal to burglars that 
    the home is empty. 
     
    Landscaping is an important deterrent. If you live in a warm 
    climate, thorny bushes like roses and bougainvillea can 
    deter window break-ins. 
     
    Other visual deterrents are signs like “Beware of Dog” or 
    “Security Cameras in Use.” Security company yard signs 
    are also effective. 
     
    When traveling, always tell a trusted neighbor you are go-
    ing out of town and stop all newspapers and mail delivery. 
     
    Turn down the volume of telephones and answering ma-
    chines so burglars can’t hear when there isn’t an answer 
    indicating no one is home. 
     
    Don’t post pictures of your vacation on social media until 
    after you return home 

    Time to Pass the Torch 
     
    By Fred Fowler. 
     
    Hello again neighbors.  In a month 
    we will have had the LVNA parade 
    and the major work of the LVNA 
    year will be behind us.  It is not too 
    soon to start thinking about how 
    things will work when the next LVNA 
    year begins next March. 
     
    March is when the LVNA holds elections to fill its board 
    for the coming year.  I've been elected LVNA President 
    five times: 1996, 1997, 2010, 2014 and 2015.  As I write 
    this, I'm thinking back to all of the different people I've 
    worked with during those years. I remember all of their 
    names and faces.  I'm sad to count how many of them 
    are no longer around, either because they have moved 
    away or they have passed away. 
     
    The LVNA has always been blessed with selfless people 
    who have taken on challenges to help the neighborhood 
    and make it a better place to live.  As “old hands” drop 
    out or move away, new people with new ideas step  
    forward.  The structure of the LVNA board fosters that 
    kind of turn over, as no board member may serve more 
    than three consecutive years before having to drop off 
    and make room for new blood.. 
     
    I'm afraid I have not been able to do as good of a job as 
    LVNA president as I would have liked to this year.  My 
    father passed away in February and I've had to travel 
    back east to help settle his affairs.  I've also started out 
    on a new career and a new business, and that has taken 
    a great deal of my time and energy.  Last year I had 
    much more time to devote to LVNA matters, and the 
    association prospered.  This year I've had to deal with 
    other things, and the association has suffered as a  
    result. 
     
    I think it is time that I made room for the next person to 
    take up the title of President and lead the LVNA through 
    its next big adventure.  For that reason, I won't be  
    running for LVNA president again next March. I've had 
    my turn (again) at leading the organization, and it is time 
    to pass the job on to someone else. 
     
    If anyone is interested in replacing me and has  
    questions about the job, please let me know. I’d be  
    happy to let you pick my brain. I'll give you all of the  
    “secrets” I know about how to cope with the many  
    challenges and take advantage of the many strengths of 
    our neighborhood. 
     
    I would also like to invite any people interested in  
    serving to attend a board meeting or two. The board  
    setting is the best place to learn about how the LVNA 
    works. Let me know of your interest and I'll make sure  
    you get an invitation. 
     
    Being President of the LVNA is a very rewarding  
    experience. I've enjoyed it five times now. It’s time for 
    someone else to have the pleasure. I hope that you 
    (whoever you are) will enjoy the job as much as I have.  
    49ers Visit Lakewood School! 
     
    Reading Partners and the 49ers teamed up in the name 
    of literacy at Lakewood Elementary School. On Sep-
    tember 28th , a group of San Francisco 49ers players 
    spent their afternoon with Lakewood Lions teaching 
    them the Importance of reading and learning. 
     
    Michael Wilhoite, Marcus Martin, Garrison Smith, Nick 
    Easton, Tony Jerrod-Eddie, and Bradley Pinion  
    surprised a group of second and third graders with a 
    special read aloud session. The players read and acted 
    out If you Take a Mouse to School by Laura Numeroff 
    to the children's delight. 
     
    They then broke out into one-on-one sessions where 
    they practiced reading and reviewing the sequence of 
    events, story structure and character response events. 
    Students then ended the day with high fives and an  
    autographed book to chosen by the player to take 
    home . 
