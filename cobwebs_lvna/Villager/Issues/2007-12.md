    Parade Kicks Off 2007 Holiday Season 
    -Ursula Syrova 
    A wonderful variety of groups made the Lakewood 
    Village Holiday Parade and party great fun again this 
    year. The parade set out from Lakewood School at 11 
    a.m. on December 1 and, with the help of Department of 
    Public Safety officers, crossed Lawrence Expressway 
    and ended at Fairwood Park. Spectators along the route 
    were showered with candy from the passing participants 
    and had a chance to donate new toys for Sunnyvale 
    Community Services. Many returning groups helped us 
    celebrate, as well as some new faces.

    neighborhood.

    Lakewood Pony Baseball Gears Up for Another 
    Great Year 
    Hello and Happy Holidays from everyone at Lakewood 
    Pony Baseball. As the holidays approach, we like to 
    focus on family, friends, and good deeds. Players from 
    Lakewood Pony Baseball rode again this year in the 
    Holiday Parade (see photo above), always fun for the 
    participants and their families. 
    Lakewood Pony Baseball is gearing up for its annual 
    registration drive. Each Saturday in January from 10 
    a.m. to noon, the LPB Board of Directors gathers at the 
    Lakewood Activities Center and collects registrations 
    for the upcoming season. Registration forms are 
    currently available online at 
    http://www.lakewoodponybaseball.com. 
    This year, we’ll raise funds for the kids of LPB in a 
    new and exciting way. During the week before 
    Christmas, December 17–24, Lakewood Pony Baseball 
    will have a booth at the Borders Book Store at the 
    intersection of El Camino Real and Mathilda Avenue. 
    Volunteers will help wrap presents purchased by 
    shoppers at Borders Books in exchange for a small 
    donation. We are looking for volunteers to help in this 
    drive. Time slots are still available. For information, 
    call Amanda Wigylus at (408) 991-3478 or e-mail 
    Sammie Davis at sammi31195@yahoo.com. Please 
    come and help out any way you can. Buy a book and 
    have it wrapped by the hard-working volunteers. Show 
    them how much you appreciate the time they have 
    donated to a good cause. 
    Thank you, and have a wonderful holiday!

    December in Your Yard  
    and Garden 
    ☼Be sure to get in the last of the spring-
    blooming bulbs this month. Remember that 
    wet soil is difficult to dig properly. Be careful not to 
    plant the bulbs too deep. 
    ☼Water plants as needed.  
    ☼Watch out for frost. 
    ☼Direct water from downspouts to avoid damage to the 
    garden. 
    ☼Save wood ashes from the fireplace as a source of 
    potash for the compost pile. 
    ☼Request seed and nursery catalogs from mail-order 
    companies. 
    ☼Set out large-sized transplants of winter-flowering 
    plants into the bedding plot, using such plants as 
    calendula, Iceland poppy, primrose, and stock. 
     
     
     
     
     
     
    ☼Apply dormant spray to deciduous flowering plants 
    and fruit trees. 
    ☼Prune pines, magnolias, junipers, and red-berried 
    plants for holiday greens. 
    ☼Cut back chrysanthemums as they finish blooming. 
    ☼Hold off fertilizing most plants in preparation for 
    winter. 
    ☼Stabilize plants that might be harmed by wind. 
    ☼Harvest winter vegetables as they ripen. 
    These tips are from the book Northern California Gardening: A 
    Month-by-Month Guide, by Katherine Grace Endicott, 
    published by Chronicle Books, San Francisco, copyright 1996, 
    and are used with the publisher’s permission.
