    President’s Column 
    - Craig Crawford 
    President, LVNA 
     
    As we enter into spring, our focus turns to the 
    outdoors and fresh air . Take this time to look 
    at your yard and house, and set your priorities 
    for repairs and maintenance. For me, I want to 
    pressure-wash the outside of my house and 
    touch up the paint where it is showing it’s age. 
    This should only take a weekend or so, and it  
    will make everything look bright and fresh. We 
    used to call this spring -cleaning, but his 
    tradition has all but been lost in our instant 
    gratification society. I remember my great 
    grandmother pulling the furniture and rugs out 
    of each room and washing eve rything down. I 
    wish I had the energy to do this, but I must 
    confess that I don’t. Set a goal this spring to 
    tackle one improvement project to make your 
    place look good, after you finish, step back and 
    admire your work and a smile will cross your 
    face for a job well done. 
     
    I made a bicycle ride around Lakewood last 
    week to admire the flowers and shrubs that are 
    blooming, and I have good news to report. I 
    only saw a few garbage cans left out in the 
    front of Lakewood homes (which violates city 
    code). What a c hange from two months ago 
    when two streets had forty houses with 
    garbage cans left out. Is it that people care 
    more, or is it the threat of getting a citation? I 
    don’t know, but I want to believe that people 
    are starting to care more. Congratulations to 
    all of Lakewood for a job well done! ¢

    May LVNA Meeting Guest Speaker

    President, LVNA 
     
    For our May meeting, we have a very special 
    guest spe aker. Pam Kelly will be talking about 
    Santa Clara County Children’s Health Initiative. 
    Santa Clara County is the first county in the 
    nation to commit to providing health coverage 
    for its estimated 71,000 uninsured children. 
    Through a groundbreaking compreh ensive 
    health insurance package and aggressive 
    outreach program called the Children’s Health 
    Initiative, over 26,000 of those uninsured 
    children have applied for coverage since the 
    program inception on January 2, 2001. And the 
    rate of applications is stead ily increasing, 
    showing the great need for children’s health 
    insurance in the State’s fourth largest county.  
     
    The Children’s Health Initiative (CHI) reaches 
    out to uninsured children through three health 
    plans: Medi-Cal, Healthy Families, and the new 
    Healthy Kids program, which insures children 
    whose families earn up to 300% of federal 
    poverty level and are not eligible for Medi -Cal 
    and Healthy Families. Healthy Kids is the 
    essential component in helping reach  
     the goal of making health insurance 
    acces sible to all children in the County. 
     
    Eligibility requirements for Healthy Kids are 
    simple, a child must: 
     
    Ø Be 18 years of age or under. 
    Ø Live in Santa Clara County. 
    Ø Have a family income at or below 300% of 
    the Federal Income Guidelines, For 
    example, a famil y of four earning up to 
    $54,000 a year can insure each child for 
    only $6 per month. 
    Ø Not be eligible for Medi -Cal or Healthy 
    Families. 
     
    If you are in need of Health insurance for your 
    children, or you know of someone that has the 
    need for this program, plea se come to the 
    meeting at 7:00pm on May 8 th at the Lakewood 
    Park Building. There is nothing more important 
    than having your children insured. The cost of 
    hospital stays without insurance is enough to 
    lead to financial disaster for all but the 
    wealthiest of  us.  If you can’t make it to the 
    meeting, here are some telephone numbers

    This Month in Lakewood History… 
     - Brian Smithson 
     
    39 years ago:  In 1963, the president of the 
    LVRA (predecessor to today’s LVNA) 
    proclaimed the month of May to be 
    “Lakewood Beautiful” month. Maybe we 
    should do that again?  
     
    39 years ago:  Also in 1963, the LVRA’s 
    “Lakewood Park Committee” raised 
    enough money to pay for the City to 
    plant larger, more mature trees in 
    Lakewood Park as it was being 
    developed. 
     
    35 years ago:  In May 1967 a “Miss Lakewood 
    Village” beauty contest was held. 
    Entrants were judged for poise, talent, 
    and beauty. No formalwear was 
    required, but they did need to display 
    themselves in one -piece bathing suits. 
    I’m not kidding about this. Wow, times 
    have changed...  
     
    Did you know? 
     
    Lakewood Village was built in a series of 
    development stages, and each one was given a 
    “unit” number according to the order in which 
    houses were built in each region. Here they 
    are: 
     
    Ø Unit 1: Sandia Avenue to Wildwood 
    Avenue, east of Lawrence Expressway.  
    Ø Unit 2: Lakehaven Drive to Lakewood 
    Drive, west of Lawrence Expressway.  
    Ø Unit 3: Lakehaven Drive to Lakebird Drive, 
    west of Lawrence Expressway.  
    Ø Unit 4: Palamos Avenue to Sandia Ave nue, 
    east of Lawrence Expressway.

    Bicycle Laws 
     
    Q: What is the law regarding wearing 
    bicycle helmets?  
     
    A: California state law mandates that 
    anyone under the age of 18 must wear a 
    helmet while riding a bicycle.  However, I 
    recommend that everyone riding a bike wear a 
    helmet, it could save your life! 
     
    Q: Is it ever legal to drive in a bike lane? 
     
    A: It is not legal to drive in a bike lane 
    except to prepare for a turn within a distance 
    of 200’ from an intersection, to park wher e 
    parking is permitted or to enter or leave the 
    roadway.  
     
    Bike Safety Tips 
     
    Ø Protect your head, wear a helmet.  
    Ø Make sure your bicycle is adjusted properly  
    Ø Always check brakes before riding 
    Ø See and be seen, wear bright colored 
    clothing 
    Ø Avoid biking at night  but if you must be 
    sure your bike has reflectors and a light, it’s 
    the law 
    Ø Stay alert. Keep a lookout for obstacles in 
    your path 
    Ø Go with the flow. Travel in the same 
    direction as vehicles  
    Ø Check for traffic. Always be aware of the 
    traffic around you 
    Ø Learn the rules of the road. Obey traffic 
    laws 
    Ø Don’t flip over your bicycle. Wheels should 
    be securely fastened 

    Fairwood Neighborhood Traffic 
    Study 
    - Craig Crawford 
    President, LVNA 
    Non-neighborhood traffic and traffic congestion 
    at the Lawrence Expressway / Lakehaven -
    Sandia intersection have been issues for many 
    years. Dating back to the early 1980’s, 
    concerns about development in the City of 
    Santa Clara causing traffic impacts in the 
    neighborhood have been discussed and 
    debated. The City of Sunnyvale has considered 
    various roadway improvement and traffic 
    enforcement strategies to address potential 
    problems. 
     
    At this time development in the south Great 
    America area of Santa Clara has largely 
    stabilized. The city has conducted a technical 
    assessment of traffic conditions to determine 
    the extent of traffic problems and the need for 
    further improvements. A comprehensive data 
    collection effort has taken place, and various 
    improvement alternatives explored. 
    Alternatives include realignment of Wildwood 
    Avenue at Lawrence Expressway and 
    installation of a new traffic signal, 

    Neighborhood Preservation: Weeds 
    in your Neighborhood 
      
    Hello, I’m Richard Gutierrez, your 
    Neighborhood Preservation Specialist.  I have 
    been circulating the Lakewood Village area and 
    have observed several prop erties with 
    overgrown vegetation.  Springtime is here and 
    weeds are becoming a problem. 
     
    Weeds, when mature, may bear seeds of a 
    downy or wingy nature.  These seeds take 
    flight and spread throughout the neighborhood 
    becoming a nuisance for everyone.  When 
    weeds and indigenous grasses attain such a 
    large growth they become a health and safety 
    problem.  Not only do weeds create a hazard 
    but they also decrease the appearance and the 
    value of your property. The City of Sunnyvale 
    requires residents to remove or destroy weeds 
    from their property. 
     
    Sunnyvale Municipal Code 8.20.010(a) states:  
    “No owner, agent, lessee or other 
    person occupying or having charge or 
    control of any building, lot or premises 
    within the city shall permit weeds to 
    remain upon the premises,  or public 
    sidewalks, or streets, or alleys between 
    the premises and the centerline of any 
    public street or alley.”  
     
    Please be aware if you are found in violation of 
    the SMC 8.20.010, you may be issued a 
    citation of $50.00 up to $500.00 for each day 
    the vi olation exists.  The weeds may also be 
    declared a public nuisance and can be abated 
    by the City.  
     
    In addition to removing the weeds from your 
    property, you can improve the appearance of 
    your home and neighborhood by installing 
    landscaping.  There are a num ber of drought 
    tolerant and low maintenance plants available 
    that could dramatically increase the 
    appearance of your home and neighborhood.  
    Your efforts to improve your property, and 
    keep Sunnyvale looking great, will not only be 
    appreciated by you, but a lso by your neighbors 
    and the City.  
     
    For more information, contact: 
    Richard Gutierrez 
    City of Sunnyvale
