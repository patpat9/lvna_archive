    It’s Parade Time! 
    The days are chilly again, the leaves are 
    turning colors and the noonday sun 
    stands low in the sky.  We all know what 
    that means.  It's Parade Time in       
    Lakewood Village! 
    Come one, come all on Saturday, December 4th to watch the 
    latest edition of the Lakewood  Village Holiday Parade wind 
    its way through our neighborhood streets.  The Lakewood 
    Elementary School Color Guard will lead it off at 11:00am 
    from the corner of Lakechime and Meadowlake in front of 
    Lakewood School.  They will be followed by the combined 
    forces of the Fremont High School and Columbia Middle 
    School marching Bands, a big bunch of awesome classic 
    cars, an antique 1930's garbage truck, a fire engine, police 
    cars, and of course the big man himself, Santa Claus.  There 
    will be lots of marchers of all kinds, from celebrities to school 
    kids, all proudly strutting their stuff for the community.  At the 
    end of the parade (in the Fairwood Park picnic area) there will 
    be the usual reception where everyone can enjoy some hot 
    cocoa and cookies and the fellowship of living in the best 
    neighborhood in Sunnyvale.  You won't want to miss it! 
     
    Last year we estimated that about 
    4,000 people either watched the 
    parade from the street or attended 
    the reception or both.  This year we 
    expect the turnout to be even larger.  
    Times were hard this time last year, 
    and they aren't much better now.  
    We needed the parade then and we 
    need it now to give us a     respite 
    from all of the bad economic news 
    we've been hearing.  We need to be 
    reminded that even though times are 
    tough, we've all seen tough times 
    before and if we work        together

    By Fred Fowler

    Holiday Lighting Contest  
    (continued from page 1) 
    The rules of the contest are simple.  Get 
    your creative juices flowing and show us 
    all what you can do!  Decorations can be 
    hand-made or store bought.  They can be 
    small and modest or huge and           
    spectacular.  It doesn't matter.  All that is 
    important is that they are expressive and 
    sincere.  They need to convey the spirit of 
    the neighborhood and of this special time 
    of year.  Many  people in Lakewood Vil-
    lage have taken up this challenge in past years and produced 
    memorable results.  I expect this year's entries to be even 
    better than before. 
    The LVNA always arranges for a celebrity judge to pick the 
    winners in the contest.  The neighborhood is divided into four 
    pieces, separated east-west by Lawrence Expressway and 
    north-south by Lakehaven/Sandia avenue.  On a specified 
    night the judge is escorted through the neighborhood by 
    some LVNA officials and the winner of each section is      
    chosen.  There is also a “grand champion” selected for the 
    entire neighborhood as a whole.   
    The results are published in the January newsletter. 
    We haven't identified the “celebrity judge” as of yet, but the 
    date of the judging has been set.  To be eligible to participate, 
    your decorations must be powered on and available for     
    review on the evening of Sunday, December 19th.  Judging 
    will take place on that night only, rain or shine.  If you want to 
    take part in the competition, be sure to be ready! 
    The Lighting Contest marks the last event of the LVNA year.  
    Things start off in March with the election of new  officers, 
    move on to July when Dumpster Day takes place, proceed to 
    September and the LVNA Picnic, and end up in December 
    with both the Holiday Parade and Lighting   competition.  
    When the contest is over the LVNA will “hunker down” for the 
    rest of the winter, waiting for spring to start the cycle       
    Hopefully it will continue this way for many years to come 
    To those of you who are about to start decorating, I would like 
    to say “thank you very much!”  Your efforts help give       
    Lakewood Village both a “personality” and a “soul” that is not 
    very common among communities in Silicon Valley. All of our 
    lives are richer because of the community we live in, and 
    our community is nothing more than a bunch of ordinary 
    people who occasionally get together to do things for the 
    benefit of all. 
    A philosopher once said “it is better to light one small    
    candle than to curse the darkness”.  We go one better.  We 
    light up a whole neighborhood!  Thanks for doing so, and 
    may the most creative decorations win! 

    A Rocky Start For The New Villager 
    by Fred Fowler 
     
    Hello folks.  As I reported in the last edition of The Villager, we 
    are now using our own LVNA equipment to print and fold the 
    newsletter, saving us money and letting us expand the size of 
    the publication.  We even have the capability to print things in 
    color!  I wrote a long article in the November Villager where I 
    explained all of the new capabilities and asked you to check out 
    the color pictures and advertisements that were now part of the 
    publication. 
    Of course, I wrote the article before we had printed anything 
    with the new equipment.  As it turned out, the first issue we 
    produced had some “issues” that we had to scramble to 
    overcome.  One of the ironic things that happened was that 
    some of you received a newsletter that wasn't printed in color at 
    all!  Not only that, but the newsletter itself was delivered very 
    late.  Clearly, things didn't go as planned. 
    The story of the first expanded issue of The Villager is a story 
    of “problems overcome” and “lessons learned”.  There were 
    several surprises (and catastrophes) that almost derailed things 
    altogether.  In the end everything worked out and all 1,700 
    issues were printed and delivered.  I don't mind telling you, 
    though, that the first edition was an ordeal for Phyllis and I.  We 
    had a heck of a time with it.  Luckily we now know what to 
    expect and the next run of The Villager should go much more 
    smoothly. 
    THE FIRST SURPRISE 
    The first surprise we got was discovering that the printer arrived 
    with no toner cartridges in it.  Phyllis and I unpacked the printer 
    and set it up the Wednesday before the newsletter was to be 
    distributed.  I went to print some test pages, and nothing came 
    out!  Phyllis checked online and found some cartridges we 
    could afford, and had them shipped overnight to us.  They 
    arrived Friday afternoon.  On Friday evening I loaded the 
    cartridges and printed a few pages successfully.  Everything 
    seemed ready for the next day.

    More Arson  
    (Continued from page 1)      we can keep watch over our 
    neighborhood better than anyone else can.  The police can find and 
    arrest criminals, but they can do it much quicker if we see the 
    criminals in action and tell the police who to look for. 
    Is this kind of vigilance still necessary now that the arsonist 
    has been “caught?”  The facts speak for themselves.  Chief 
    Don came to talk to us on Wednesday night, and on the 
    following Saturday night there were MULTIPLE fires set in the 
    brush along the Greenbelt.  Someone had walked down the 
    path and set fires as he/she went.  It is unclear whether the 
    culprit is the same one who set the summer fires or a different 
    person altogether.  All that is clear is that the threat of arson 
    along the John Christian Greenbelt is far from over. 
    I heard about the fires when a neighbor who lives near the 
    Greenbelt came to see me the following 
    morning.  He was (understandably) quite 
    upset and worried about what to do.  I 
    suggested that he get some of his 
    neighbors together to form a 
    neighborhood watch group.   
    I promised that if he formed the group, I 
    would go to the first meeting and bring 
    Sunnyvale Neighborhood Resource 
    Officer Jim Davis with me.  I talked with 
    the neighbor about the possibility of 
    mounting inexpensive hidden “Web 
    Cam” cameras in his backyard foliage in 
    order to keep an electronic watch on 
    things.  In the end, there were many things that he could do to 
    help protect his home from fires set by capricious and 
    irresponsible people. 
    During the talk that Chief Don gave us, I asked him to pretend 
    he had a “magic wand” he could wave  to create the perfect 
    Public Safety department he wanted.  I asked him what that 
    department would be like.  He then answered that he wouldn't 
    change anything about how the city is currently patrolled, or 
    about how the fire stations are staffed.  He said what he would 
    change is to greatly increase the staff he has who are 
    dedicated to crime prevention and youth outreach.  He has 
    enough officers to respond to the crimes that take place at the 
    moment.  The ongoing budget cuts that have been made over 
    the years have cut back the department's ability to head off the 
    causes of future crimes.  Chief Don has the resources to 
    respond to crimes after they happen.  My impression of his 
    answer is that he doesn't think he has enough resources to 
    prevent those crimes in the first place.  If he is right, we'll be 
    facing more crimes in the future like the Greenbelt arson, 
    rather than less. 
    What can we do?  The answer is still the same as it was last 
    summer.  We as a community have a great deal of power to 
    keep our neighborhood safe.  That power comes from our 
    numbers and our watchfulness.  The police can't be 
    everywhere in our neighborhood at once.  If we work together, 
    though, we CAN be everywhere at once.  Together we can 
    see what is going on.  We can tell the police what is 
    happening and help them get it taken care of. 
    I'll make the same promise to all of you that I made to our 
    neighbor living near the John Christian Greenbelt.  If you 
    get a meeting of your neighbors together to organize a 
    watch group, I'll attend the meeting and bring a 
    police officer with me.  Together we'll learn how to 
    watch efficiently and communicate with the police 
    effectively.  If we do then we'll be taking the safety 
    and security of our neighborhood into our own 
    hands. 
    Lakewood Village has done this kind of thing before.  
    In the 1990's we were plagued with incessant graffiti, 
    and the neighborhood organized to fight it.  It took 
    years of effort, and a good deal of self sacrifice, but 
    in the end, we won.  I like walking by the side and 
    back of the Lakewood Shopping Center and looking 
    at those broad white walls with no tags on them at 
    all!  Back in the 90s most of us would have thought that 
    would have been impossible.  The fact that the walls are 
    clean now speaks to the power we have as a community.  
    We can stop crime if we convince the criminals that we will 
    not tolerate it.  We can get others to respect our 
    neighborhood if we show them how much we respect it 
    ourselves  
    When the graffiti stopped in the neighborhood, some of 
    the local kids told us it was because they finally realized 
    that someone cared.  The same will probably be true for 
    the other types of crime we're experiencing.  If the 
    miscreants realize that we as a neighborhood actually 
    care enough to do something about it, they will go 
    elsewhere.  It worked before.  It can work again.  
    Let's give it a try.

    LESSONS LEARNED 
    And so the first edition of The 
    Villager that was produced on 
    LVNA printing equipment has passed into history.  It was 
    an experience that I will not forget any time soon.  Lots 
    and lots of things went wrong, and I hope that Phyllis and I 
    never have to go through a similar ordeal in the future. 
    As bad as the production process was, though, there are 
    still a great many good things that have come out of it.  
    The finished product was just beautiful!  The extra space 
    is serving its purpose in providing more room for 
    neighborhood news, and  our advertisers are anxious to 
    convert their advertisements to color (and pay us extra for 
    printing them that way!)   All in all, as bad as the printing  
    process was, the overall idea looks like it is a success. 
    The first run of the new Villager was definitely one to 
    remember.  Like all “birth” experiences, it was painful and 
    messy.  I'm sure we'll have more problems as we go through 
    the “terrible twos” and other growing pains.  After a while, 
    though, the process will learn how to stand on its own and 
    grow.  Before long this “child” will be something we can all point 
    to with pride.  It will be a neighborhood resource that will pay 
    many dividends in the years to come. We've seen the first step. 
    The rest of the journey will be exciting indeed. 
