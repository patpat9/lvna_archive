    Greetings Neighbors! 
     
    May is finally here!  After a cold and 
    windy March and April, May has arrived with warm and sunny 
    weather.  At long last we're enjoying the mild and comfortable 
    climate the south bay is supposed to be famous for.  Speak-
    ing as a guy who does not like dark and cold, all I can say is 
    that it is high time we had some bright and warm days! 
     
    Even though it feels like spring has only now gotten started, it 
    won't be long before summer comes this way.  By the time 
    you are reading this there will only be about four weeks of 
    school left for this year.  School lets out for the summer on 
    June 11th, and after that kids will have lots of free time until 
    the next school year starts in August. 
     
    For many of our children those summer days are carefree 
    and happy times.  For others, though, summer break can 
    bring fresh challenges for themselves and their families. 
     
    Some of our kids come from households where parents and 
    older siblings all work – sometimes with two or even three 
    jobs each.  Those kids end up spending their summer days in 
    empty houses with no one to watch them 
    or even help them figure out what to do 
    with their time. 
     
    Some of those kids depend on the 
    school lunch program to get enough to 
    eat.  When there is no school for them to 
    go to, they can go hungry.

    30th Annual Hands On The Arts 
     
    When: Saturday, May 16th  
    Time: 10 A.M. - 4 P.M. 
    Where: Sunnyvale Community Center 
    550 East Remington Drive 
     
    Hands on the Arts is an all-day children's art festival. 
    This event features more than 30 workshop booths 
    where children can try their hands at different art 
    forms. Professional artists lead workshops using a 
    wide variety of arts and craft materials and  
    techniques, as well as music and dance. Many of the 
    workshops introduce children to unique art forms from 
    different cultures, such as Indian rangoli, Chinese silk 
    knotting, Japanese brush painting and Mexican  
    folklorico dancing. All supplies are included in the 
    nominal per-child admission fee. (Adults and  
    non-participating children are welcome at no charge.) 
     
    The festival’s outdoor stage features family-friendly 
    entertainment all day and roving performers add to the 
    festive atmosphere. There is also a small food court 
    near the stage selling a variety of healthy, kid-friendly 
    foods. Picnics are also welcome.   

    Don’t Fall Victim to Common Scams 
     
    Reprinted from  
    “Beyond the Badge” 
     
    An aggressive and sophisticated 
    phone scam targeting taxpayers, the 
    elderly, and citizens in general has 
    been making the rounds throughout 
    the country. Telephone callers will 
    claim to be employees of the IRS, PG&E, Cable 
    Company, a local or foreign law enforcement  
    agency, or other collection agency. These con artists 
    can sound very convincing when they call. They will 
    use fake names and bogus identification badge  
    numbers. They may know a lot about their targets 
    and they usually alter the caller ID to show the  
    agency which they claim to be calling from. 
     
    Victims are told they owe money and it must be paid 
    promptly through a pre-loaded debit card or wire 
    transfer. If the victim refuses to cooperate, they are 
    then threatened with arrest, deportation or  
    suspension of a business or driver's license, or  
    termination of services.  In many cases, the caller 
    becomes hostile and insulting. 
      
    Victims may also be told they have a refund due to 
    try to trick them into sharing private information. If the 
    phone isn't answered, the scammers often leave an 
    "urgent" callback request. 
      
    It is important to note that the IRS, a law  
    enforcement agency, or any reputable business will 
    NEVER: 
     
     Call to demand immediate payment, nor will the 
    agency call about taxes owed without first having 
    mailed you a bill. 
     
     Demand that you pay taxes without giving you 
    the opportunity to question or appeal the amount 
    they say you owe. 
     
     Ask for credit or debit card numbers over the 
     Require you to use a specific payment method for your 
    taxes, such as a prepaid debit card. 
     
     Ask for credit or debit card numbers over the phone. 
     
     Threaten to bring in local police or other law             
    enforcement groups to have you arrested for not     
    paying. 
     
    If you suspect you've been the victim of this scam, please 
    contact Sunnyvale Department of Public Safety.  
    The Non-Emergency line is (408) 730-7100. 
     
    Otherwise, we urge you to be on the lookout and help 
    spread the word to prevent others from falling victim to 
    these scammers. 

    A Little Free Library Project 
     
    A Little Free Library is a "take a book, return a book" 
    gathering place where neighbors share their favorite  
    literature.  
     
    This Little Free Library offers a way to share good things 
    to read—favorite books from your childhood or books you 
    would recommend to friends; books that teach, intrigue 
    and engage you.  It is basically a box full of books where 
    anyone may stop by and pick up a book 
    (or two) and bring back another book to 
    share. 
     
    Many people build their own Little Free 
    Library, often as a group or classroom 
    project. Others purchase a kit or  
    completed library from   
    LittleFreeLibrary.org. At LFL’s website 
    you can also find tips, blueprints and 
    measurements for building your Library. 
    (While you are there, check out the 
    YouTube tutorials!) 
     
    These tiny neighborhood libraries embody the essence of 
    a public library on a micro scale. To help spread this idea, 
    the Friends of Sunnyvale Library will award up to five 
    grants per year towards purchase or construction of Little 
    Free Libraries in Sunnyvale. The grants will be up to $200 
    each to eligible applicants.  
    To be eligible, your Little Free Library (LFL) 
    should: 
     
    - be sturdy and waterproof * 
    - have an official charter number * 
    - be registered on the LFL world map * 
    * Information found at LittleFreeLibrary.org. 
    - have some indication of Friends of the 
    Sunnyvale Library sponsorship  
     
    FOTSVL sponsorship can be  
    indicated by a sign, a plaque,  
    labels on the books in your library, 
    bookmarks, etc. 
     
    If you have established a  
    LittleFreeLibrary in Sunnyvale and 
    would like to apply for a grant from 
    the Friends, please send a  
    completed application form with the 
    official charter number, a picture of 
    your LFL, and receipts of related 
    expenses (copies are OK)  
    by mail to: 
     
    Friends of the Sunnyvale Library 
    665 W Olive Ave 
    Sunnyvale CA 9406 
     
    Or by email attachment to fotsvl@gmail.com.  
     
    Sorry, only one application per residence.  
