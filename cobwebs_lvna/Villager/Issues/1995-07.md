    Public Safety Corner Communty Services Bureau Summer Crime Prevention Tips Did you know that summer is traditionally a time we experience more criminal activity than usual? Don't let yourself be a summertime victim of crime. Please take the time to follow a few simple precautions while on your trip. Camping Tips Auto - Is vour auto parked where it is well lit, can be readily observed, and is patrolled regularly by park staff? Valuables - Have you locked your auto and locked your valuables out of sight in the trunk before you reached your campsite? Camping Gear - Do you secure your valuable camping gear (stove. lantern, etc.) at night or when you leave during the day? Emergency Numbers - Do you have the emergency numbers for the area you are visiting? On The Road Cash - Never carry or display large amounts of cash. Use traveler's checks or charge cards. Checks, Credit Cards - Keep traveler's checks, credit card, and telephone numbers handy to report a loss or theft. Surroundings - Always be aware of where you are and don't advertise plans, routes, or valuables. Breakdowns - Raise the hood, attach a white cloth to the antenna, stay with your car, and ask others to call for help. If you see a stranded motorist, don't stop to help but go to the nearest phone and call for help Stalker - If you feel you're being followed, stop at the nearest service station. market, or anywhere there are people around and call the police. If you feel unsafe to get out of the car, honk your horn and flash your lights and remain locked in the car.     A Community's Concerns Addressed Geoff Carter. Editor On June 28ff, twenty-three of our neighbors met together With Chief Regan Williams, Cdr Don Olson, and Capt Don Johnson at the Lakewood Park building to discuss a serious problem somewhat unique to isolated pockets within our community. The problem discussed was Gangs, Vandalism, and Graffiti. Our good Chief pretty much tried to pooh-pooh the fears of our residents by reciting city-wide statistics. He also outlined results of the cases generated on one block of one street in our neighborhood. He tried to show crime in Sunnyvale is actually down in most regards, and Lakewood isn't one of the hot spots of activity in the city. I don't think the citizens in attendance were placated in the least. Of the three representatives from the Department of Public Safety, Cdr Olson was the only one who seemed to listen and brought forth a suggestion for finding a solution to the problems, Granted, I don't think much Of the idea, but at least it was something. "What, if anything can we do to turn this neighborhood terrorism around?" we asked. Cdr Olson suggested that we form a committee of about four residents and our Neighborhood Resource Officer to study the problem and come up with recommendations. This smacks of a typical bureaucratic stonewall, study the problem, indeed! We know what the problem is and where they live. But hey, at least Cdr Olson is willing to talk to us and get something stalled. When you make a call to Public Safety to report an incident, ask for an "Event Number". Keep a log of your calls and the associated event number, then when we get together with Public Safety, we will be armed with specifics that can be addressed, looked into, and answered. Remember... "Case Numbers" are what go into the statistics that Chief Regan was quoting. They were generated by "Event Numbers" that required further investigation and ended up with prosecution. Not all events become cases. And, from our point of view, the events are the most visible and of most concern to us. Being the optimistic sort of person that I am, I think that we may eventually get somewhere with this... I do, however reserve the right to modify my opinion as the solution and execution thereof unfolds.     At the Last L.V.N.A. Meeting Geoff Carter, Editor The L. V.N.A. General Assembly meetings continue to be very informative. Over thirty people were in attendance; if you weren't among them, then you missed out. Vicky Ebstien from the Green Circle Program came and told us about the diversity training they conduct at our schools. Green Circle is a national program that has served our county since 1981, helping our children develop positive social skills through appreciation and respect for human differences. Landon Noll addressed the assembly regarding the city's budget process. He pointed out that although the city is able to continue most levels of service without increasing taxes, we can't expect this to last very much longer. I'll bet the decision by Lockheed Martin to consolidate some of their satellite building operations in Sunnyvale was most welcome news for the bean counters at city hall. Kalvin Fourtano outlined some of the many programs available for our youth. Look for their monthly newsletter "Fresh " at selected locations around the city. I, for one, was surprised there was so many diverse activities out there for our youth. Now if only we could entice them off the street corners and away from their hoodlum buddies and get them to join in the fun. After the meeting there was a bake sale where donated items were sold at reasonable prices. We are grateful for the donations of Rita Donnough, Lea Crawford, Elena Anderson, Phillis Fowler, and Geraldine Carter. In all, we raise $46.50 that is going directly into the picnic fund for the end of summer neighborhood picnic. This will be a regular feature, so feel free to bring your favorite desert recipe and donate it to the neighborhood We would also encourage you to bring your sweet tooth and pocketbook to help support this worthy cause.
