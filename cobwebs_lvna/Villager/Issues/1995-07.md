    Geoff Carter, Editor
    
    On June 28", twenty-three of our neighbors met together
    with Chief Regan Williams, Cdr Don Olson, and Capt Don
    Johnson at the Lakewood Park building to discuss a serious
    problem somewhat unique to isolated pockets within our
    community, The problem discussed was Gangs, Vandalism,
    and Graffiti
    
    Our good Chief pretty much tried to pooh-pooh the fears
    of our residents by reciting city-wide statistics. He also
    outlined results of the cases generated on one block of one
    street in our neighborhood. He tried to show crime in
    Sunnyvale ts actually down in most regards, and Lakewood
    isnt one of the hot spots of activity in the city. I don’t think
    the citizens in attendance were placated in the least.
    
    Of the three representatives from the Department of
    Public Safety, Cdr Olson was the only one who seemed to
    listen and brought forth a suggestion for finding a solution to
    the problems, Granted, | don’t think much of the idea, but at
    least it was something. “What, if anything can we do to turn
    this neighborhood terrorism around?” we asked. Cdr Olson
    suggested that we form a committee of about four residents
    and our Neighborhood Resource Officer to study the problem
    and come up with recommendations. This smacks of a
    typical bureaucratic stonewall, study the problem, indeed!
    We know what the problem is and where they live. But hey,
    at least Cdr Olson is willing to talk to us and get something
    started.
    
    When you make a call to Public Safety to report an
    incident, ask for an “Event Number”. Keep a log of your
    calls and the associated event number, then when we get
    together with Public Safety, we will be armed with specifics
    that can be addressed, looked into, and answered.
    Remember... “Case Numbers” are what go into the statistics
    that Chief Regan was quoting. They were generated by
    “Event Numbers” that required further investigation and
    ended up with prosecution. Not all events become cases.
    And, from our point of view, the events are the most visible
    and of most concern to us.
    
    Being the optimistic sort of person that I am, I think that
    we may eventually get somewhere with this... | do, however
    reserve the right to modify my opinion as the solution and
    execution thereof unfolds. Look for follow-up information at

    At the Last L.V.N.A. Meeting
    
    Geoff Carter, Editor
    
    The L.V.N.A. General Assembly meetings continue to be
    very informative. Over thirty people were in attendance, if
    you weren't among, them, then you missed out.
    
    Vicky Ebstien from the Green Circle Program came and
    told us about the diversity training they conduct at our
    schools. Green Circle is a national program that has served
    our county since 1981, helping, our children develop positive
    social skills through appreciation and respect for human
    differences.
    
    Landon Noll addressed the assembly regarding the city’s
    budget process. He pointed out that although the city is able
    to continue most levels of service without increasing taxes, we
    can’t expect this to last very much longer. I’Il bet the
    decision by Lockheed Martin to consolidate some of their
    satellite building, operations in Sunnyvale was most welcome
    news for the bean counters at city hall.
    
    Kalvin Fourtano outlined some of the many programs
    available for our youth. Look for their monthly newsletter
    “Iresh” at selected locations around the city. 1, for one, was
    surprised there was so many diverse activities out there for
    our youth. Now if only we could entice them off the street
    corners and away from their hoodlum buddies and get them to
    join in the fun.
    
    After the meeting there was a bake sale where donated
    items were sold at reasonable prices. We are grateful for the
    donations of Rita Donnough, Lea Crawford, Elena Anderson,
    Phillis Fowler, and Geraldine Carter. In all, we raise $46.50
    that is going directly into the picnic fund for the end of
    summer neighborhood picnic. This will be a regular feature,
    so feel free to bring, your favorite desert recipe and donate it
    to the neighborhood. We would also encourage you to bring,
    your sweet tooth and pocketbook to help support this worthy
    cause.
