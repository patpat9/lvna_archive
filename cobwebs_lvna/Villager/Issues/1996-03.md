    Linda Kilian
    
    The Sunnyvale School District needs millions of dollars
    to renovate its schools. We’re not talking about glamorous
    changes either. We're talking about new roofs, wiring,
    plumbing, dry rot, termite damage, bathroom tile, and the
    like,
    
    After a year of visiting school sites, talking with
    administrators, and surveying the community, the Board of
    Education of the Sunnyvale Elementary School District
    placed a bond measure on the June 4" ballot. This was a
    difficult decision, because passing a bond measure is not easy.
    To win, a two-thirds majority of voters must vote in favor of
    the measure. One “no” vote cancels out two “yes” votes.
    
    If passed, the bond would raise $34 million. All of this
    money would be spent locally on our schools. None of the
    money could be spent on salaries.
    
    Lakewood School would receive up to $3.5 million to
    upgrade its facilities. Like most other schools in Sunnyvale,
    Lakewood is almost 40 years old. Time has taken its toll, and
    Lakewood needs a new roof, upgrades to the electrical system,
    repairs to rusted plumbing fixtures and pipes, a new fire
    alarm system, remodeled restrooms, new playground
    equipment, and more,
    
    The Sunnyvale School District has been able to renovate
    and reopen three schools in recent years by selling a surplus
    school site. However, as our student population continues to
    grow. selling other properties is not an option. Says
    Sunnyvale School Superintendent Doris Wilson, “When
    voters understand the condition of our schools, we feel they
    will sce this bond measure is a good thing for the schools, for
    the city, and for the property values of our homes.”
    
    The bond campaign organization, Friends of Sunnyvale
    Schools, is being co-chaired by Linda Kilian and Betty
    Verstegen. Area resident and Board member, Jessica Lee,
    will be organizing volunteers in the Lakewood area. If you
    have any questions about the bond measure or are interested
    in volunteering, call Friends of Sunnyvale Schools at (408)
    450-1100, G3

    After reading the February issue of the L.V.N.A.
    newsletter I also felt compelled to speak my opinion. I tend
    to agree with the L.V.N.A. President, in that I am very
    concerned with the quality of life for me and my family. |
    don't understand why some people "have a right" to live in
    our parks or shopping centers because they "grew up here."
    Since I didn't "grow up here", I don't have rights? What
    about the right to take my two young children to the park
    without frightening them with the sounds of a screaming,
    fowl mouthed man in the restrooms! Anyone who thinks this
    person is keeping undesirables out of the park should come
    look at the fear in my toddler’s face when they see this man.
    I thought our parks were created for children, not vagrants.
    
    When we walk to the shopping center, and see the
    "burrito lady" come out of the Lakewood Lounge or the liquor
    store, do you think she was buying food with the money she
    begs for? I doubt it. Again, my concern is for the safety and
    happiness of my children. I would like them to grow up in a
    community that fosters a secure and friendly atmosphere.
    
    I have also spoken with some teens in the neighborhood,
    who say they are intimidated by these people. When they
    come home from school they'd rather not pass by them.
    Those of you who don't feel threatened by the presence of
    these “harmless” vagrants probably don't see the world from a
    child’s point of view.
    
    These “harmless” people have been turned out on our
    streets by politicos and medicos trying to deal with lack of
    funds for properly institutional care. Maybe those of you who
    disagree, should join forces and actually DO SOMETHING
    for these people. Create an "adopt a homeless person"
    campaign. Offer them a room in your home, or clothes and a
    shower to help them clean themselves up so they can find
    work. Get them a roof over their heads, (yes, even a
    Lakewood home!). Anything, but don't try to convince me
    they are welcomed by the neighborhood while they are living
    on our streets!
    
    I feel attention should return to helping remove these
    folks from the street, and doing whatever is necessary to help
    them become productive in the community and to be
    welcomed in Lakewood Village. os
