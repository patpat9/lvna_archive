    RECIPE CORNER
    ZUCCHINI WALNUT MUFFINS
    Have a recipe that you love? 
    Share it with us at editor@lvnasv.org
    02     LVNA VILLAGER | MAR 2020
    1. Preheat oven to 350°F.
    2. In a large bowl, mix eggs, sugar, vanilla extract, and 
    melted butter. When fully combined, incorporate grated 
    zucchini. 
    2. In a separate bowl, combine all of the dry ingredients 
    (minus the walnuts): flour, baking powder, baking soda, 
    salt, cinnamon, nutmeg, and ginger. 
    3. Stir dry ingredients into the bowl with the zucchini until 
    fully combined. Do not over-mix.
    4. Fold in chopped walnuts. You could also consider adding 
    ingredients like cranberries or chocolate chips. 
    5. Place muffin liners inside of your muffin pan. Evenly fill 
    muffin cups with batter.
    6. Bake at 350°F for about 25-30 minutes, or until a 
    toothpick is free of batter when inserted into center of 
    muffin. 
    2 eggs
    1/2 CUP brown sugar
    1/4 CUP white sugar
    3 TSP vanilla extract 
    3 CUPS grated zucchini 
    1/2 CUP unsalted butter, melted
    31/2 CUPS all-purpose flour
    Note: Depending on the moisture content of your zucchinis, it 
    could change the consistency of your muffins. If there is a lot of 
    moisture after grating, let zucchini shreds sit in strainer to drain 
    a little. Also, I used some yellow squash when I made this recipe 
    because I didn’t have enough zucchini on hand, which is why there 
    are flecks of yellow in the muffin picture to the left.
    1 TSP baking powder
    1 TSP baking soda
    1/4 TSP salt 
    2 TSP ground cinnamon
    1/2 TSP ground nutmeg
    1/2 TSP ground ginger
    1 CUP chopped walnuts
    yields approx 12-16 muffins

    LVNA MEETING RECAP
    LVNA MEETING ON FEB 12
    Anne Karoly, Partnership Specialist at the U.S. Census Bureau, provided an overview of the census and why it’s so 
    important for everyone to participate. Besides being legally mandated by the constitution, there are 2 high-level 
    reasons why ALL people residing in the U.S. must be counted, regardless of citizenship:
    Government Representation: Headcount directly determines the number of seats each state gets in the House 
    of Representatives. California currently has 53 seats, as a result of the population count from the 2010 census. 
    That number could change, depending on the results of this year’s census. Additionally, local-level changes are 
    made every 10 years, pursuant to census information, such as redrawing maps for school districts and the Board of 
    Supervisors.
    Federal Funding: Demographics from the census are used for allocating $675B in federal grants, between states, 
    for transportation, medical, social services, libraries, and many others.
    The results of the census are completely confidential, even from law enforcement and other government agencies. 
    The records will not be released until 72 years after they’re collected (2092 for this year’s census data).
    Deborah Abbott, a U.S. Census Bureau Recruiter, discussed the temporary U.S. Census Bureau positions that are 
    available every 10 years. To qualify, you must be 18+, have a social security number, email address, and be a U.S. 
    citizen. There are also some other positions available for non-citizens, such as interpreters.
    In our area, the pay is $27 - $33 per hour. Field hours are flexible and based on your preferred schedule. The 
    primary working season is May through August. If you’re interested in getting out there to help conduct census 
    surveys, call the Sunnyvale office at (408) 541-3400.
