    Lakewood Villagers Enjoy Picnic Food and Fun 
    Thank you to everyone who came out for free food and 
    fun at the annual Lakewood Village picnic! We 
    estimate that about 160 people came--definitely more 
    than last year.  
    The kids enjoyed face painting (thank you, Phyllis and 
    Fred Fowler!), a piñata, and a visit from a fire truck 
    (thank you, Sunnyvale Public Safety!). Everyone 
    enjoyed the music (thank you, Gary Fernandez!) and 
    the food. As always, the help of many volunteers made 
    it all possible. The grills were expertly managed by 
    Skip Gottschalk, with lots of help from Jason Kenney 
    and Chris. Judy Fernandez handled the raffle tickets 
    and sign-in area.  
    We would have run out of hotdogs if Lakewood Dental 
    had not kindly donated several packs, including buns. 
    InStyle salon contributed hair products for the raffle, 
    Coco’s donated three pies, and Lake Vista Liquors 
    supplied ice. Many thanks to those local businesses.  
    The LVNA also thanks the Community Child Care 
    Council of Santa Clara County for sending the Info 
     
     
    Van to the picnic and providing health resource 
    information targeted to families with small children. 
    Many people visited the Info Van tables and came 
    away with great information, toys, and stickers.  
    Sarah and Art Daugherty and Annie Gottschalk were 
    on hand to help with everything, including cleanup. Jim 
    Dougherty did a great job on cleanup also. Thank you! 
    And thank you also to those who brought food to share 
    with everyone. I had a great time getting to meet folks 
    from around the neighborhood, and I hope everyone 
    else who attended did too.

    October in Your Yard  
    and Garden 
    •Purchase spring bulbs for the best 
    selection.  
    •Shop for native plants and set them in 
    the garden. 
    •Purchase fall foliage plants in the 
    nursery; the fall color of seed-grown plants varies 
    greatly even in the same variety. 
    •Continue to weed. Add young, seedhead-free weeds 
    and excess summer growth to the compost pile. 
    Remember to keep the pile as damp as a wrung-out 
    sponge for faster composting. 
    •Keep after the snails. 
    •Use a strong spray from the hose to wash away aphids. 
    •Water plants as needed. 
    •Stabilize plants that might be harmed by autumn wind. 
    •Water roses but withhold all fertilizer. When the 
    ground freezes in colder areas, mulch the rose bed 
    heavily. 
    •Continue to pinch off dead flowers. When frost comes 
    to the garden, move fuchsias and begonias grown in 
    pots to a sheltered, warmer location. 
    •Finish transplanting fall- and winter-flowering plants 
    into the bedding plot. Soak all newly transplanted 
    plants until the rains come. 
     
    •Harvest vegetables as they ripen. Plant the vegetable 
    garden with cool-season crops such as beets, broccoli, 
    cabbage, carrots, cauliflower, onions, parsnips, and 
    turnips. If you put your vegetable garden to rest over 
    the winter, cover it with manure so that the manure can 
    decompose and age over the winter. 
    •Flush your watering system to remove mineral 
    deposits. 
    •Rake up leaves. Continue to pull up dying annuals and 
    vegetables. Add garden debris, except seed-bearing 
    weeds and diseased plants, to the compost pile. 
    •Pick up fallen branches, pots, or other debris from the 
    garden to reduce the number of over-wintering sites for 
    snails, slugs, insects, and fungal spores. Continue to go 
    through the garden and examine plants for black spots, 
    bright orange rust pustules, white powdery mildew, 
    rotted stems or branches, large, irregularly shaped galls 
    near the soil, or other signs of infection. Pull up the 
    infected plants and rake up infected leaves and dispose 
    of them. Do not put infected material into the compost 
    bin. Bring a trash can with you into the garden so you 
    can immediately dispose of infected material without 
    spreading disease by dragging it through the garden. 
    •After cleaning up the garden, rinse gardening tools 
    with a solution of one part bleach to three parts water. 
    Then coat your tools lightly with oil to keep them from 
    rusting. 
    These tips are from the book Northern California Gardening: A 
    Month-by-Month Guide, by Katherine Grace Endicott, 
    published by Chronicle Books, San Francisco, copyright 1996, 
    and are used with the publisher’s permission.■

    Complaints Bring Responses 
    Several Lakewood Village residents have complained 
    about the motor home parked in the shopping center 
    and the activities of people behind the Valero gas 
    station and around the entrance to the greenbelt path. 
    LVNA board members contacted the Sunnyvale 
    Department of Public Safety about these matters. The 
    following were received from the Lakewood Village 
    Neighborhood Preservation representative and the 
    Department of Public Safety. 
     
    Neighborhood Preservation staff worked with Public 
    Safety regarding concerns about a motor home parked 
    at the Lakewood Village shopping center used for 
    human habitation. Public Safety informed 
    Neighborhood Preservation that they spoke with the 
    occupants of the motor home and was informed that 
    they are living in the motor home because they are 
    “keeping an eye on the property” for Mr. Yee, the 
    property owner. 
    Staff checked with Planning regarding a section in the 
    Sunnyvale Municipal Code that states, “The 
    occupying of a motor home is permitted in a nonresidential zone when it is demonstrated for security 
    purposes.” Planning advised staff that human 
    habitation of a motor home is allowed in a situation 
    like this; however, a miscellaneous plan permit is 
    required and conditions must be adhered to by the 
    property owner and occupants of the motor home. 
    Based on this information, staff contacted Mr. Yee 
    and advised him that a permit is required through the 
    Planning Division to have people living in a motor 
    home on his property. Mr. Yee said that he was not 
    aware of the permit requirements and was doing the 
    owners of the motor home a favor by allowing them to 
     
    stay on his property in return for them keeping an eye 
    on the property at night. Mr. Yee informed staff that 
    he did not want to apply for a permit and that he 
    would contact the owners of the motor home and give 
    them written notice that they are no longer allowed to 
    stay in the motor home on his property overnight. 
    Parking the motor home during the daytime does not 
    constitute a violation. 
    Staff is waiting for a copy of the notice sent to the 
    motor-home occupants from Mr. Yee stating that the 
    occupants of the motor home are no longer allowed to 
    reside on the property of the Lakewood Village 
    shopping center. A copy of the notice will be 
    forwarded to Public Safety so they can monitor the 
    situation during the evening hours. 
    Public Safety staff responded that the concerns have 
    been passed on to one of the swing-shift lieutenants, 
    who is responsible for the Lakewood Village beats. 
    His officers have contacted the RV's occupants and 
    discussed concerns with them. He has also asked his 
    officers to give special attention to the transients who 
    are causing the problems at the shopping center 
    behind the gas station. Public Safety says that the 
    officers will give these concerns their immediate and 
    serious attention and strictly enforce the laws at their 
    lieutenant’s direction. The lieutenant will also work 
    closely with neighborhood preservation about these 
    issues. 
    Public Safety added “Thank you for bringing these 
    issues to our attention. Without this type of assistance 
    we have no way of knowing about many of these 
    types of problems and it greatly assists us.”■

    Get Rid of Hazardous Waste Safely 
    Lakewood Village and other Sunnyvale residents can 
    safely dispose of unwanted hazardous items on the 
    third Saturday of each month. Saturday, October 15 is 
    the next hazwaste dropoff date at the SMaRT station. 
    A wide variety of household materials are not safe for 
    disposal in the main trash stream and require special 
    handling and disposal. Paints, stains, insecticides, 
    flammable or combustible liquids, pool chemicals, 
    compressed gas barbecue tanks, and even fire 
    extinguishers fall under the general heading of 
    hazardous waste. 
    Hazardous materials can be dropped between 8 a.m. 
    and 1 p.m. on October 15 at 164 Carl Road, near the 
    SMaRT station, just north of the intersection of 
    Borregas Avenue and Caribbean Drive. 
    There are some restrictions on materials to be dropped 
    off. There is a limit of five gallons or 50 pounds of 
    material per vehicle; materials should be carried in 
    their original, non-leaking containers and should be in 
    boxes or bags you do not want, as no containers will 
    be returned. 
    For more information on the drop-off events, call the 
    City of Sunnyvale at 408-730-7262 (TDD 408-7307501).■

    Einstein’s Ideas To Be Featured at the Sunnyvale 
    Public Library 
    In celebration of the 100th anniversary of Albert 
    Einstein’s special theory of relativity, the Sunnyvale 
    Public Library has been named one of 20 libraries 
    nationwide to serve as an outreach site for the NOVA 
    television special, Einstein’s Big Idea (Tuesday, 
    October 11 on PBS television). The special is based 
    on David Bodanis’s book E=mc2: A Biography of the 
    World’s Most Famous Equation. 
    Events at the library include the following. For six 
    other events, check at the library or see the Web site 
    einstein.inSunnyvale.com.  
    Gravity Probe B: Testing Einstein’s Universe, 
    Saturday, October 15, 2 p.m. NASA’s Shannon Range 
    will give an interactive presentation on this 
    sophisticated experiment designed to test Einstein’s 
    general theory of relativity. The world’s most precise 
    gyroscopes are measuring whether time and space are 
    warped by Earth’s presence, and if the Earth’s rotation 
    drags space-time around with it. For students 6th 
    grade and up and adults. 
    Einstein Big and Small, with comedian and physicist 
    Norm Goldblatt, Tuesday, October 18, 7 p.m. A 
    popular lecture exploring the very large and the very 
    small. Scientists and nonscientists alike deal with 
    numbers much smaller and much larger than can be 
    comprehended directly. Norm Goldblatt will acquaint 
    you with both these worlds, microscopic and cosmic, 
    to bring an increasing awareness of the power of the 
    human intellect and of the success we have had in 
    extending the range of our normal senses to the far 
    regions of the physical universe.  
    Book Discussion: A Short History of Nearly 
    Everything by Bill Bryson, Thursday, October 20,    
    7 p.m. Bryson’s book is a highly readable account of 
    scientific knowledge. From atoms to Earth to the 
    universe, and the rise of civilizations, Bryson 
    describes the seminal discoveries in nearly every 
    branch of the sciences.■ 
     
     
    Halloween Safety Tips  
    • Make sure your yard is clear of such things as 
    ladders, hoses, dog leashes, and flower pots that can 
    trip the young ones.  
    • Pets get frightened on Halloween. Confine them to 
    protect them from cars or inadvertently biting a trickor-treater.  
    • Battery-powered jack o’lantern candles are 
    preferable to a real flame.  
    • If you do use candles, place the pumpkin well away 
    from where trick-or-treaters will walk or stand.  
    • Make sure paper or cloth yard decorations won’t be 
    blown into a flaming candle.  
    Parents: 
    • Make your child eat dinner before setting out.  
    • Children should carry quarters (or a cell phone) so 
    they can call home.  
    • Ideally, young children of any age should be 
    accompanied by an adult.  
    • If you buy a costume, look for one made of flameretardant material.  
    • Older children should know where to reach you and 
    when to be home.  
    • You should know where they're going.  
    • Although tampering is rare, tell children to bring the 
    candy home to be inspected before consuming 
    anything.  
    • Look at the wrapping carefully and toss out anything 
    that looks suspect. 
    Tips are from Children’s Safety Zone Web site■
