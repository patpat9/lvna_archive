    The kick-off meeting fo r the Lakewood Village 
    Neighborhood Enhancement Program was held 
    on October 23rd at the Lakewood Park Building. 
    Neighborhood Preservation’s Rich Gutierrez 
    and Christy Gunvalsen led the meeting that 
    was attended by a room full of several dozen 
    residents. The scope and purpose of the 
    program was introduced, participants from the 
    previous Pilot Program shared their 
    experiences, and Rich and Christy described 
    what to expect and how to participate in this 
    program. The next step in the program is that 
    Rich will survey every home in the program 
    area and begin contacting residents as needed. 
    The next Neighborhood Enhancement Program 
    meeting will be on November 20
    th. 
     
    The Neighborhood Enha ncement Program is a 
    partnership between the City of Sunnyvale, the 
    LVNA, and residents and property owners, and 
    its purpose is to improve the overall 
    maintenance, safety, and appearance of 
    Lakewood Village.  Our neighborhood was 
    chosen because a 2001-2 study of the 55,000 
    single family residences in Sunnyvale showed 
    that 20% of the homes in Lakewood Village 
    had two or more code violations, in contrast to 
    5% city-wide. Code violations can be anything 
    from minor issues, like trashcans left on the 
    street more than 24 hours after pick-up day, to 
    major issues, like illegal construction or unsafe 
    occupancy. The program is paid for by Federal 
    funds, not from the regular City budget. The 
    City receives $2 million from Housing and 
    Urban Development, and $85,000 is earmarked 
    for programs like this one. 
     
    This program is the follow-on to the Pilot 
    Program that was started in 2002. The Pilot 
    Program covered 95 homes around Lakehaven 
    and Lakeknoll Drives, and is generally 
    considered to have been a success by the City 
    and area residents alike. A report on the 
    results produced by the Pilot Program was 
    published in the September issue of The 
    Villager, and is available on the web at 
    http://www.lvna.net/Villager/villager.html.

    In today’s tough times, lots of 
    neighborhoods have cat 
    burglars, but not too many of 
    them are actually cats! 
    Lakewood Village is the home 
    to one such cat.  
     
    Many of you living near Fairwood / Tucson / 
    Torrance know Dooley, a cat that is often seen 
    walking his owner around the block. Over the 
    past several months, Dooley has developed a 
    habit of stealing merchandise from the 
    neighbors, usually in the wee hours of the 
    morning. Generally aro und 2:00 AM, Dooley’s 
    owner is periodically treated to yet another 
    gift, quite often accompanied by a loud howl to 
    proudly announce another delivery.  
     
    What kind of gifts? Well, ANY cat can bring his 
    owner dead animals, but in Dooley’s case, the 
    gift list looks something like this: 
     
    • Over 20 stuffed animals 
    • A dozen slippers 
    • Over 50 socks 
    • Gloves (gardening, kitchen, bike racing) 
    • Hats & caps 
    • Toys (balls, balloons) 
    • Car polishing pads, towels, rags 
    • And much, much more… 
     
    Note: Friends of 
    Dooley’s owner have 
    suggested that he train 
    Dooley to look for things 
    that are shiny and/or 
    green! However, since 
    Dooley’s only motivation 
    for stealing appears to 
    be love for his owner, 
    training him would 
    probably do no good. 
     
    Dooley’s owner has gone door-to-door and also 
    posted signs in a futile effort to locate the 
    owner(s) of these items.

    On October 18, three 
    representatives from the Lakewood Village 
    Neighborhood Association attended the United 
    Neighborhoods of Santa Clara County (UNSCC) 
    Fall conference.  This conference marked 10 
    years since the organization formed with the 
    purpose of bringing county neighborhood 
    groups together in the spirit of education, 
    advancement, and cooperation.  The Lakewood 
    Village Neighborhood A ssociation is a member 
    of the UNSCC, paying yearly dues.  One 
    example of the benefits of that membership is 
    that the events we put on as a neighborhood 
    can be insured through the UNSCC.  The 
    UNSCC emphasizes education and cooperation-
    -for instance organizing a set of cleanup tools 
    that associations can share, or education about 
    issues that stretch beyond one neighborhood, 
    such as BART extension plans or traffic 
    calming.   
     
    This year’s conference provided a great chance 
    to meet other people in volved in neighborhood 
    associations, and it featured seminars on 
    various issues of importance to neighborhoods 
    everywhere.  One set of seminars addressed 
    reaching out to people of different cultures and 
    nationalities.  Another set taught us how to ask 
    for money, how to form a 501(c)3 corporation, 
    and how to write grant proposals.  Yet another 
    addressed how to better reach out to and 
    interact with local government officials.  More 
    information about what was offered at the 
    conference can be found at the UNSCC website 
    at www.unscc.org.  It was wonderful to see 
    how many people care about improving their

    Do you miss the Sunnyvale 
    Bookmobile? Has your age or a 
    physical disability confined you to 
    the house? If the answer  is "yes", then the 
    Sunnyvale Public Library has a program for you.  
     
    Special Outreach Services (SOS) provides 
    library materials once a month to residents who 
    are home bound. You can receive books, books-
    on-tape, large print books, picture books, 
    compact discs, and non-feature videos.  
     
    You discuss your reading preference and 
    interests with a member of the library staff. 
    Then this employee or a volunteer selects the 
    materials that will be delivered to you. 
    Deliveries are made on a regularly scheduled 
    day, and last month's materials are picked up.  
     
    Does this sound like something that you would 
    like to do? All you need to do to enroll is call 
    408-730-7306. Someone at this number can 
    also answer your questions. 
