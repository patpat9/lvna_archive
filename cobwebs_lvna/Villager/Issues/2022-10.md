    November 8, 2022 Elections
    The November 2022 elections are coming up quickly. This is a reminder on how to register to vote
    if you haven’t already, and then a quick review of some of the topics that will be on the ballot.
    Register to vote:
    California offers online voter registration. Go to https://covr.sos.ca.gov/ to complete your Voter
    Registration Application. The application is available in English, Spanish, Chinese, Hindi, Japanese,
    Khmer, Korean, Tagalog, Thai and Vietnamese. The deadline to register to vote is October 24. To
    be able to register to vote in California, you must:
    -
    Be a United States citizen and California resident
    -
    Be 18 years or older on Election Day (November 8, 2022 this year)
    -
    Be NOT serving a state or federal prison term for a felony conviction
    -
    Be NOT currently found mentally incompetent to vote by a court
    If you want to register using a paper voter registration application, please call (800) 345-VOTE
    (8683) to request one. Paper applications must be postmarked or hand-delivered to the county
    elections office at least 15 days before the election.
    If you can’t get your voter registration application submitted by October 24, you can also do same
    day registration (called Conditional Voter Registration). See
    https://www.sos.ca.gov/elections/voter-registration/same-day-reg for more information.
    Not sure if you are registered to vote? Go to https://voterstatus.sos.ca.gov/ to check your
    registration status.
    See more information here: https://www.sos.ca.gov/elections/voter-registration

    - September LVNA Meeting Recap -
    Our September LVNA general meeting was held in person (and on a Thursday!). Our guest speaker
    was Mayor Larry Klein. Since we did not record the session, here are some quick highlights from
    the meeting:
    -
    Mayor Klein is the first directly elected mayor in Sunnyvale. Prior to the last election, the
    mayor of Sunnyvale was selected by the City Council
    -
    The Lakewood Library is moving forward and has reached the final design stage. We are
    waiting on state approval and hoping to start construction in Summer 2023. The library
    should open by the end of 2024. More information can be found at
    https://www.sunnyvale.ca.gov/business-and-development/projects-in-sunnyvale/infrastruct
    ure-projects/lakewood-branch-library
    -
    The City is putting $200M into renovating our parks. The Fair Oaks Park was the most
    recent one to be renovated. Lakewood Park is next. This fall, the city will start soliciting
    community input on what the new Lakewood Park should be.
    -
    The Village Center plan will provide a new Village Center at the current Lakewood
    Shopping Center. More information can be found at
    https://www.sunnyvale.ca.gov/business-and-development/projects-in-sunnyvale/long-range
    -planning-initiatives/village-center-master-plan
    -
    The Moffett Park plan is being evaluated as a mix of retail space, office space, housing
    (including affordable housing), and a large grocery store. The plan focuses on the land
    bounded by Moffett Park Dr, Caribbean Dr, and Enterprise Way. More information can be
    found at https://www.moffettparksp.com/project-overview
    -
    The Sunnyvale Block 15 project near Mathilda Ave and Iowa Ave with provide affordable
    housing units. It is expected to open in Fall 2023 and will prioritize housing for people with
    intellectual or developmental disabilities. More information can be found at
    http://www.sunnyvaleblock15.com/
    -
    The City is aware that there are unhoused people living at Lakewood Park. By law, the City
    cannot force them to move without providing them an alternative place to stay. Sunnyvale
    has only one homeless shelter, which serves the entire Santa Clara County population
    (meaning that Sunnyvale unhoused residents do not get priority). Covid substantially
    reduced the number of available beds, and the shelter has not returned to full capacity yet.
    The City also cannot build housing; it has to work with developers and non-profits to get
    this housing. On the advice of the city attorney, the City is working to revise a local
    ordinance to enforce no camping in the park. Once the ordinance is passed**, there is a 60
    day public comment period before it can take effect. Once the ordinance takes effect, the
    City will work with local non-profits (e.g., HomeFirst) to provide the unhoused residents a
    housing option. Unhoused residents who do not choose the housing option will be told to
    move on (although there is no clear place for them to move to). Proposed changes to the

    - Obituaries for
    Gladys and Bob Bemis -
    [Editor’s note: We have not typically run obituaries in The Villager. However, when a Lakewood resident reached out to ask
    us to include a note on the passing of Gladys and Bob Bemis, we appreciated the chance to honor their contributions to our
    neighborhood. We will consider future obituaries on an individual basis. Please reach out to vp1@lvnasv.org to discuss.]
    The Lakewood Village neighborhood lost two
    long-term residents recently. Gladys Bemis, age 104,
    died peacefully in her home in August and her
    husband, Bob Bemis, age 96, passed away shortly
    thereafter in September.
    Bob was born in Webb City, MO as the oldest of 9
    surviving children. He served in the Navy for 20 years
    with a high rank of Chief Petty Officer. Gladys had
    been a resident of Lakewood Village since the
    beginning, moving into her home in 1958. In 1968,
    Gladys and Bob purchased the Grog and Vittle, a beer
    and pizza place. They renamed it the Lakewood
    Lounge and served daily
    lunch specials until it was
    sold in the 1970s.  Bob was a
    member of the Sunnyvale
    Elks Lodge, and held
    various officer positions
    including Exalted Ruler and
    was the Lodge Secretary
    until 2013. Bob loved to
    cook and was even a
    certified sommelier. He
    enjoyed watching baseball and he won a car in 1968 at
    a Fan Appreciation Day for the San Francisco Giants.
    Gladys also owned the
    Lakewood Beauty Salon for
    a short time. She was active
    with the Sunnyvale
    Emblem Club, CPO Wives
    Club at Moffett Field, Bay
    Area Waves Group, and
    volunteered at the VA Palo
    Alto for over twenty years.
    Gladys was an avid
    gardener and loved
    hummingbirds.
    Gladys and Bob are survived by their daughter Cynthia
    Kahakui and grandson Roland Kahakui.  Donations in lieu of
    flowers can be made at womensmemorial.org

    - Request for Help -
    [Editor’s note: Need help from your neighbors? Let us know and we will post your request in the Villager.]
    Dear Neighbors,
    It is packing shoe boxes season to support Operation Christmas Child. Operation Christmas Child
    is an international effort run by Samaritan’s Purse, a nondenominational evangelical Christian
    organization led by Reverend Franklin Graham. My family participates in this project every year,
    and it brings our community together. Each box is packed with items like school supplies, personal
    care items, and quality toys. These shoebox gifts are sent to children around the world. For many
    children, the shoebox gifts become a tangible expression of God's love for them. If you would like
    to participate, please buy some suggested items below or come help us to pack the shoe boxes on
    November 12 (Saturday, 10am-12pm) at the park building in Lakewood Park. Last year, we packed
    89 shoe boxes as a community to bless children around the world. How many will we do this
    year??
    This year, we will be packing boxes for kids
    ages 2-4 years old. Here are some suggested
    gifts (all items must be new):
    ●
    school supplies (paper, colored paper,
    crayons, markers, color pencils, erasers,
    sharpeners, glue stick, rulers, watercolor
    set, pens, coloring books, notepads,
    stickers, etc)
    ●
    personal care items (comb, toothbrush,
    washcloth, cup, water bottle, bowl, plate)
    ●
    clothing and accessories (shirt, pants,
    dress, shoes, socks, sunglasses, tote bag,
    hair bows/clips, bracelets)
    ●
    toys (ball, slinky, jump rope, games,
    puzzle, doll, stuffed toy, car, etc
    We also need donations of regular adult-size shoe boxes.
    Thank you for your generosity and support. If you have any questions, please contact me at
