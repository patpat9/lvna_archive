    Fires After-Hours at Lakewood Park 
    According to our neighborhood resource officer, 
    Jennifer Anton, there have been several instances of 
    people burning fires at night in the grills and fire pits at 
    Lakewood Park, sometimes using newly planted park 
    trees as kindling. NO activities are allowed in the parks 
    after dark, and the destruction of the tree saplings is 
    vandalism. If you see any fires or after-dark park 
    activity, please call 911 and alert the Department of 
    Public Safety.

    on a mailing list for future meeting notifications, call 
    the Parks Division at 408-730-7506 or contact the 
    Superintendent of Parks at cblack@ci.sunnyvale.ca.us.

         June in Your Yard  
         and Garden 
     
    ☼ Weed. Do not let the weeds 
    form seedheads. Be sure to knock 
    down tall dry grasses because they 
    create a serious fire hazard. 
    ☼ Add young, seedhead-free 
    weeds to the compost pile. 
    Remember to keep the pile as 
    damp as a wrung-out sponge for 
    faster composting. Turn the pile occasionally. 
    ☼ Watch out for snails and earwigs, both of which 
    devour seedlings. 
    ☼ Use a strong spray from the hose to wash away 
    aphids. 
    ☼ Wrap fruit tree trunks with a sticky tape to control 
    ants. Wrap low so that birds, particularly 
    hummingbirds, will not be attracted. Fruit will be 
    ripening soon and the ants will go after the fruit. Water 
    plants as needed.  
    ☼ Insulate the garden soil from hot, dry weather by 
    applying a two-inch-thick mulch. 
    ☼ Fertilize plants if you see signs of pale, lackluster 
    growth. Yellow leaves with green veins often indicate 
    an iron deficiency, which can be corrected with 
    chelated iron.

    June in Your Yard and Garden, from page 3 
    ☼ Fertilize the lawn. Fertilize all flowering plants 
    about to bloom. Fertilize roses. 
    ☼ Pinch off dead flowers, particularly off lilacs and 
    other late-spring flowering shrubs. Make sure you get 
    the entire flower head when dead-heading annuals—
    including the swollen ovaries where seeds form. This 
    will encourage new flowers as the plant rushes to 
    reproduce itself. 
    ☼ Transplant summer-flowering plants into the 
    bedding plot. Hot weather is hard on newly 
    transplanted plants. Shade them for a few days with the 
    temporary shelter of an umbrella or wooden shake. 
    Stake tall sprawling flowers such as delphiniums and 
    dahlias. 
    ☼ Continue to plant the vegetable garden. Thin 
    vegetable seedlings that were sown too close together 
    by snipping them with scissors. Harvest vegetables as 
    they ripen, picking them early in the day.

    And Don’t Plant These 
    Hottentot fig (iceplant with yellow flowers), Vinca 
    major (periwinkle), licorice plant, scarlet wisteria, 
    broom, pampasgrass, and cotoneaster. 
    California Invasive Plant Council information notes 
    that these plants are among those that have escaped 
    from gardens and are displacing native species and 
    changing natural processes in the San Francisco Bay 
    area. The Web site www.cal-ipc.org offers alternatives 
    to the plants listed above and gives details about the 
    problems these “pest plants” create when they go wild. 
    Some Lakewood Village residents know, for example, 
    that rats favor ivy growth. ■

    Is Our Tap Water Safe? 
    Chloramine, a water disinfectant that combines 
    chlorine and ammonia, has caused skin irritation, 
    asthmatic symptoms, and other health problems for 
    some residents of Sunnyvale and other Bay Area cities, 
    according to Citizens Concerned About Chloramine 
    (CCAC). At an informational meeting in Sunnyvale on 
    May 23, CCAC handed out fact sheets and copies of 
    newspaper items (one was from The Sunnyvale Sun of 
    March 29, 2006) documenting problems with 
    chloramine. The San Francisco Public Utilities 
    Commission changed the disinfectant treatment to 
    chloramine from chlorine in February 2004. 
    After receiving complaints and inquiries from people 
    about the health and environmental effects of 
    chloramine, State Assemblyman Ira Ruskin has  
     
     
     
     
     
     
    introduced Assembly Bill 2402, preliminary legislation 
    to strengthen existing water-treatment regulations. The 
    bill would mandate studies to identify potentially 
    dangerous drinking-water disinfection byproducts and 
    to identify and develop alternatives to chemical 
    disinfectants. The Board of the Bay Area Water Supply 
    and Conservation Agency found that public health 
    agencies could not produce studies on the effects of 
    chloramines on humans. 
    For more information about the legislation, go to 
    www.assembly.ca.gov/acs/acsframeset2text.htm. To 
    learn more about Citizens Concerned About 
    Chloramine, see www.chloramine.org or call CCAC 
    president Denise Johnson-Kula, 650-328-0424. ■
