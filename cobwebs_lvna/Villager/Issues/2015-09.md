    Greetings neighbors! School is back in session and the 
    summer months are behind us. It will be fall soon, and

    junk to the  
    Smart Station for recycling. In addition, we

    usable goods to  
    Goodwill of Silicon Valley.

    second time they worked with us during our 
    neighborhood clean-up event.  Once again 
    our donation total was

    money to print this newsletter for a year, 
    so our donations to Goodwill will help us 
    for a long time to come. The Dumpster 
    Day partnership between the neighbor-
    hood and Goodwill is here to stay.

    check, I visited the Goodwill

    Making College Affordable for Lakewood 
    Village Students 
    By Ro Khanna 
     
    For the past 15 years, my student loans have been a 
    burden.  I was aware that just one financial stumble 
    like a job loss or a medical mishap, and my credit 
    would be ruined.  Despite having worked for President 
    Obama and teaching at Stanford, these loans have 
    also made it difficult for me to buy a house in our area. 
     
    I don’t want my kids and their friends to have this  
    challenge. I don’t want the kids in a middle class  
    neighborhood, Lakewood Village, to feel like this. 
    I  have visited Fremont High school many times. I am 
    particularly inspired by the AVID program which is  
    targeted at those who are first in their families to go to 
    college. I want these kids at Fremont High to be able  
    to have the higher education they deserve without  
    worrying of a lifetime of debt! 
     
    It is time for a University of California sys-
    tem with room for Californians. They didn’t 
    name it the University of Out-of-State and 
    we should act like it too. 
     
    Less than 50 years, the University of  
    California charged no tuition.  Today, over 
    a dozen European countries charge either 
    no tuition or only a tiny fee to attend  
    college. We can do it too. 
     
    To a large extent, the skyrocketing tuition 
    public university students pay is due to 
    drastic cuts in state support.  We need our state  
    leaders to consider new funding sources to roll back 
    tuition increases and create more spaces for  
    students.  The UC’s also should explicitly prioritize  
    local students.  Finally, we need more oversight on 
    spending.  In today’s age, university presidents  
    shouldn’t be living in taxpayer-funded mansions while 
    their students can hardly pay the bills. 
    This is a problem too big for state action alone.  We need 
    new vision in Washington. The federal government can 
    play a key role in encouraging high-quality online courses 
    and textbooks to be available at a fraction of what a  
    traditional lecture class or textbook would 
    cost. As for the students and families  
    already struggling under the burdens of 
    onerous student loans, these borrowers 
    should be allowed to consolidate their 
    debt into federal loans. These federal 
    loans will have lower interest rates.  
    Finally, the federal government should 
    provide subsidies to Universities in  
    exchange for capping tuition, much like 
    many European countries do. 
     
    I couldn’t afford to get my education  
    without student loans. I don’t regret my 
    choice to go into debt to get my  
    degree- education is one of the most powerful keys to the 
    universe- but I don’t want my children or any of America’s 
    children so burdened.  Let’s do more than giving lip service 
    to our kids at Fremont High. Let’s actually fight for policies 
    so they can afford higher education. 
     
    Ro Khanna is a Lecturer in Economics at Stanford  
    University, and he is a member of the Sunnyvale Rotary 
    Club. He can be reached at:  
    rokhanna1@gmail.com or www.rokhanna.com

    Street Light Repairs 
     
    By Maria Dolores Singson-Tamez  
     
    I was in Dick`s market and a neighbor  
    approached me and asked me to pass this 
    on. A streetlight on Greenlake Drive was 
    busted. So they called the City and left a 
    voice mail. They were called back two days 
    later and were told to take note of the  
    number that is imprinted in the metal post . 
    So they had to go back and write all the  
    numbers and report them to the city. 
     
    I am not sure what phone number they called to get help. 
    However, most people don’t know that it is our duty/part to 
    call the City. This is because the city doesn`t inspect the 
    streetlights at night. 
     
    The City of Sunnyvale maintains over 9,000 street lights 
    illuminating the City streets after dark. Almost 15 percent of 
    these lights are the new Light Emitting Diode (LED) lamps. 
    The City has begun replacing the traditional low pressure 
    sodium lamps (LPS) with the LED lights. The LEDs provide 
    a softer, more even, illumination with truer color. LEDs are 
    primarily installed along the busier streets, where there was 
    significant saving by replacing higher wattage lights. LEDs 
    are also being considered to replace the 70-watt LPS lights 
    currently serving most  
    residential areas. 
     
    Public Works staff performs periodic night time surveys 
    of street lights on the City’s major streets, inspecting and 
    repairing any street light outages discovered. On  
    neighborhood streets, the City relies on residents to  
    report street light outages. 
     
    About 10 percent of street lights in Sunnyvale are owned 
    and maintained by PG&E. These are usually identified 
    as street lights mounted on wooden poles. PG&E also 
    supplies power directly to some City owned lights. The 
    City is not authorized to make repairs to PG&E street 
    lights or supply lines that provide  
    electricity to City owned lights. City staff reports these 
    issues to PG&E.

    Burglary Update: A Suspect Has Been Identified 
     
    By Fred Fowler 
     
    Hi neighbors.  If you recall, I wrote about an incident that 
    affected Phyllis and me a few months ago. We went out 
    for pizza on a Saturday evening in March, and when we 
    came home we found that our house had been burgled.  
    The thieves got away with several computers, cell 
    phones, IPod, and some jewelry, and left us a big mess 
    to clean up. 
     
    We called Sunnyvale DPS immediately and their crime 
    scene investigator arrived a few minutes later. He  
    examined everything very carefully, noting the position 
    and orientation of various items that had been picked up 
    and subsequently dropped by the burglars. He was able 
    to get quite a good number of readable fingerprints from 
    the items and other surfaces in the house. 
     
    A few days ago Phyllis and I got some good news,  A 
    Sunnyvale detective called us to let us know that some of 
    the fingerprints had been matched.  A suspect has been 
    identified and tied to the robbery by the fingerprints that 
    had been left behind.  The subject has been arrested. 
     
    We will probably never get our stolen belongings back, 
    but Phyllis and I are glad that someone who either  
    committed or took part in the crime has been caught. If 
    others were involved, the other fingerprints found at the 
    scene will eventually lead to their arrest as well. If they 
    are ever fingerprinted for any reason, they will be tied to 
    the burglary and will be caught.   
    1101 Prescott Little Free Library 
     
    Dear Neighbors, 
    We wanted to let you know about our 
    Little Free Library that we launched in 
    August on the Fairwood side of  
    Lakewood Village. The “Little Free  
    Library’ is a community movement where home  
    owners construct or purchase a weather-proof  
    bookshelf type “library” place in or adjoining their yard 
    and off free books for the community to borrow. The 
    motto is “borrow a book, return a book” – but if  
    someone loves the book they borrowed, they can re-
    turn a different book? We have often been frustrated 
    at how inconvenient the Sunnyvale library is to our 
    neighborhood, and we thought it would be fun to share 
    our love of books with our neighbors and the Fairwood 
    Explorer Elementary students. 
     
    We’d love to have you visit our little library on  
    Blazingwood and Prescott at 1101 Prescott Ave. we 
    have a Facebook page if anyone is interested. We are 
    planning to post featured kids’ books weekly. In the 
    meantime, you can see how we built our little library.  
    www.facebook.com/1101prescottlittlelibrary   
     
    -Brenda
