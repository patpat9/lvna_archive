    Pilot Program Survey Results 
    - Richard Gutierrez, Neighborhood Preservation 
          As many of you know, the City’s Community Development Department met with the officers of 
    the Lakewood Village Neighborhood Association to  discuss a pilot program designed to enhance the 
    quality of life in our neighborhood. The objective of the program is to improve the overall 
    maintenance, appearance and safety of properties in Lakewood Village.   The program will attempt to 
    establish a par tnership between residents and City staff to identify property maintenance priorities, 
    and then address the highest priorities through education, assistance and (as a last resort) code 
    enforcement.  
     
    The Pilot Program, a cooperative between the Lakewood Vi llage Neighborhood Association and the 
    City is taking shape.  On June 20, 2002 the LVNA Board met with the City to discuss the specific 
    locations where the Pilot Program would be most beneficial to the Lakewood Village residents.  The 
    Board and the City mu tually agreed to conduct the Pilot Program in the areas of Lakehaven Drive 
    between Lawrence and Meadowlake Drive and all of Lakenoll Drive.   This area is great for 
    Lakewood Village because it is highly visible and is one of the main gateways into our commu nity.  
    This is also an area where a relatively large number of respondents to the survey indicated a desire to 
    participate in the Pilot Program. 
     
    The LVNA Board distributed 1,700 surveys (one to each home) in the June newsletter, 249 surveys 
    were completed  and returned to the City to calculate the results.  Residents of Lakewood Village 
    ranked specific code enforcement issues as “not important”, “important”, or “very important”.  The 
    results indicate to the City what we, as a community, consider important a s well as not 
    important.  
     
    The code enforcement issues below are ranked from highest to lowest, in the “very important” 
    column.  For example, Garbage, debris, furniture, etc. stored in public view, was ranked #1 in the 
    very important category.  (71% of the  respondents indicated that this issue was very important.  And, 
    only 5% indicated that this issue was not important.)  Weeds ranked #6 in the category of very 
    important and fences exceeding the City’s height limitations was ranked #17; only 19% of the 
    respondents indicated that this issue was very important.

    The Right to Be…  
    James L. Daugerty 
    I was just reading over the last two copies of 
    the Villager, our own neighborhood newsletter. 
    It is a means to talk to our neighbors and 
    express our ideas and personal co ncerns about 
    where we live, and as a forum for leadership 
    and change. It is democracy at one of our most 
    basic levels. 
     
    And so I write to my fellow Villagers about a 
    problem for us that seems to be evolving and 
    gaining strength. I am asking you for guidanc e 
    and your opinions, so please respond. 
     
    My problem seems to be with the pro -active 
    nature of our City government and certain 
    people in our LVNA. I do very much believe in  
    u continued in next column  
     
    u continued from previous column  
     
    looking after our nei ghborhood and making 
    sure the properties we live in are kept in 
    reasonably good shape. It enhances the value 
    of the property if it’s ours or not, and enhances 
    the comfort level of the neighborhood that 
    permits us to take pride in our community, and 
    satisfy the needs of public safety. Beyond that, 
    we come to a point where what we do and how 
    we live is our business and no one else’s. It is 
    our inherent right to be left alone.  
     
    It seems that this pro -active philosophy, if 
    allowed to gain strength, may divide u s as 
    neighbors. We may no longer ask our 
    neighbors to move their cars, clean up their oil 
    spils, turn down their overly loud stereos, 
    control their pets, take down their Christmas 
    lights, or whatever the problem may be. It’s 
    easier to stay anonymous and se cretive, and 
    just turn  ‘em into the City. 
     
    There is an element of fear here, too. If I turn 
    in my neighbor, then sooner or later he’s going 
    to find something on me to turn me in for. 
    Rare is the neighbor that is perfect and never 
    does anything wrong. And so we become 
    apprehensive, angry, and intolerant of each 
    other. 
     
    So let’s be neighbors to our neighbors and help 
    out when we can. We can be pro -active among 
    ourselves. By taking care of our neighborhood, 
    and showing civil respect to others before there 
    is a problem, so this problem that I speak of 
    will disappear on it’s own. ¢

    This Month in Lakewood History… 
     - Brian Smithson 
     
    44 years ago:  In 1958, the first LVRA Picnic 
    was held at Blackberry Farms. At the 
    time, there was no Lakewood Park. 
     
    44 years ago:  Also in 1958 , a fence was 
    established at Havenwood to block 
    access to Calabazas Creek, which was a 
    hazard for children. 
     
    39 years ago:  In 1963, it was predicted that 
    homes in Lakewood Village would sell
