    Greetings Neighbors!   
     
    By the time you read this, the Super Bowl 
    will be over.  With luck, all of the  
    disruptions it brings will things of the past 
    and we will all have survived.  After our day in the 
    national limelight, things can get back to normal and 
    our neighborhood can again focus on important 
    things closer to home. 
     
    One important thing about Lakewood Village is the 
    fact that there are two fine schools in our  
    neighborhood, one on the Lakewood side and one 
    on the Fairwood side.  This month our February  
    general meeting will focus on two initiatives centered 
    on those schools. 
     
    First of all, Lakewood School and Sunnyvale  
    Community Services (SCS) are partnering on a new 
    food program for North Sunnyvale.  The SCS will be 
    opening and running a new food distribution point at 
    the Lakewood School to make it easier for North 
    Sunnyvale residents who can't travel 
    easily to get access to SCS's nutrition 
    programs.  We've invited a representa-
    tive of the program to come and pre-
    sent the  
    details for us.  By the way, they will 
    also be describing volunteer  
    opportunities for Lakewood residents 
    who want to help distribute the food.   
    It should be very interesting. 
     
    Second of all, the LVNA has been  
    contacted by the “Reading Partners” 
    program.  Cont. on Page 2)

    (Continued from page 1) 
     
    Reading Partners is an AmeriCorps 
    program that puts domestic “peace 
    core” type volunteers into schools to 
    help with literacy programs.  The  
    Reading Partner people provide  
    tutoring and other services to help kids get the 
    most out of their formative educational  
    experiences.  They would like to visit the LVNA, 
    talk about their mission and perhaps recruit some 
    neighborhood volunteers to help.   
     
    They say it is better to light a candle than to curse 
    the darkness.  Now that the artificial hoopla of the 
    Super Bowl is over we need to get back to  
    focusing on some important realities.  In our  
    wonderful community there are still people who 
    are hungry and kids who need help learning to 
    read.  Let’s come to the LVNA meeting at the 
    Lakewood Park building on Wednesday, February 
    10th at 7:00pm and learn what we can do about it.  
    We can't solve these problems, but we can do 
    something to help!  As long as we try, we can 
    make a difference. 
     
    I'll see you there.  

    Changes at Instyle Salon 
    A Review by Phyllis Fowler 
     
    For those of you who have 
    not visited Instyle recently, 
    Lynh has a new manicurist!  
     
    Her name is Tina. She has 
    twenty years’ experience in 
    doing nails. She owned a 
    salon in San Jose and is now working Lynh.  
     
    I went there on Wednesday and had a manicure 
    and pedicure. Tina did a very nice job. Lynh has a 
    new line of gel polish that has the matching color 
    for your feet! This time I decided to try two of the 
    new colors, a bright pink on my toes and a red for 
    my hands. (Next week I will get my toes the 
    matching red, I like it better.) One of things I love 
    about the gel polish is that it lasts two weeks and 
    still looks good.  
     
    If your haven’t been to Instyle lately, come on 
    down and meet Tina and receive a free gift.  
    Better yet, get your nails and hair done!  
     
    Instyle is open Monday through Saturday, from 
    10:00am to 7:00pm. Call for an appointment at 
    (408) 734-2035. Walk-ins are also welcome. 

    Robbery – El Camino & Pastoria 
    Monday February 1 @ 7:47am 
    The victim was standing at a bus stop with his bicycle. Three 
    unknown suspects approached the victim, threatened to 
    physically assault him, and stole his bicycle. 
     
    Robbery – 800 Blk. Iris 
    Tuesday January 12 @ 8:39pm 
    As the victim was getting out of his car, he was approached by an 
    unknown suspect who pointed a gun at him and demanded 
    money.  The suspect fled the area on foot. 
     
    Fire – 1300 Blk. Kifer 
    Tuesday December @ 9:33am 
    DPS Fire personnel responded on a water flow alarm.  Upon 
    arrival, crews found smoke in the building and upgraded the call 
    to a first alarm assignment.  It was determined a bucket full of 
    lithium batteries had caught fire, which activated the sprinkler 
    system.  The fire was extinguished and nobody was injured as a 
    result of the fire.   
     
    CPR—1000 Blk. The Dalles 
    Monday January 4 @ 10:00pm 
    Fire personnel responded on an EMS call where the patient was 
    experiencing chest pain.  While on scene the patient went into 
    cardiac arrest.  Personnel performed CPR along with county 
    paramedics.  The patient received one shock from an AED and 
    was transported to the hospital.  The patient regained a heartbeat 
    and was released from the hospital four days later. 
     
    Residential Burglary/Robbery – 1100 Blk. Borregas 
    Monday January 11 @ 6:30am 
    The victim was awakened by his dog barking.  The victim then 
    discovered two unknown suspects had entered the apartment by 
    unknown means and were in the process of stealing a 
    computer.  The victim confronted the suspects and was struck in 
    the head with a metal object.  The victim suffered non-life 
    threatening injuries.  The computer was recovered but the victim’s 
    wallet was stolen.  
     
    Residential Burglary – 200 Blk. Twinlake 
    Monday January 11 @ 8:43pm 
    An unknown suspect entered the residence through an unlocked 
    kitchen window and ransacked the residence.  At the time of the 
    report it was unknown what was stolen 
     
    Attempted Robbery – Dartshire & Flicker 
    Monday January 11 @ 9:12pm 
    Two victims were walking in the area when they were approached 
    by two suspects.  One of the suspects pointed a gun at the victims 
    and demanded money.  The victims did not have any money and 
    the suspects fled to a waiting vehicle. An officer responding to the 
    area located a vehicle matching the description and stopped the 
    vehicle in the area of Fremont & Mary.  One adult and three 
    juveniles were in the vehicle.  Officers located two loaded 
    handguns in the vehicle.  All four suspects were arrested.
