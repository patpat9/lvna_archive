    - Celebrating Earth Month -
    Friday, April 22 is Earth Day, which means that all of April is Earth Month. Here are some ways to
    get involved:
    -
    Roadmap to Sustainability: The City of Sunnyvale is sponsoring a Roadmap to
    Sustainability competition. After calculating your personal carbon footprint, there are a
    variety of challenges and activities to keep you busy each month. Submit your photos by
    May 8 at 5:00pm for a chance to win prizes. Find more information here:
    https://sunnyvale.ca.gov/news/displaynews.htm?NewsID=723&
    -
    Go vegan (for a day or two or more): Meat production (much of it factory farmed) has a
    huge environmental impact. Raising livestock accounts for more than half of all the
    greenhouse gasses that come from food production, leads to deforestation (to grow animal
    feed and to farm the animals), and is water-intensive. No need to give up meat completely,
    but use April as an excuse to try a vegan meal (or two or three). A quick Google search will
    turn up endless options (many of which are actually good).
    -
    Join the Sunnyvale Sustainability Commission: This commission is responsible for
    providing the Council with guidance on achieving the City’s sustainability goals and uses the
    Climate Action Playbook. There’s a position open now, and applications are being accepted.
    Applications are due by 4:00pm on April 28. See
    https://sunnyvale.ca.gov/government/boards.htm for more information
    -
    Save water: With another low-rain winter on records, water levels continue to be extremely
    low. Try a new way to reduce water - try watering your lawn only once a week, take a
    shower every other day (or take a shorter shower), and turn the water off when brushing
    your teeth.
    -
    Get a free DIY Earth Day kit: California State Parks Foundation has partnered with REI to
    provide free Earth Day kits. The kits include gloves, a trash bag, an REI coupon, and more -
    all intended to help you clean up your local community. You have to register in advance. See
    https://donate.calparks.org/event/diy-earth-day-climate-action-with-rei/e390279 for more
    information.
    For people looking for other activities, Saturday, May 21 is the National River Cleanup Day. You can
    find more information about this and other events by calling 408-730-7717 or viewing this flier:
    https://sunnyvale.ca.gov/civicax/filebank/blobdload.aspx?blobid=23701

    - Meet Your Board Member -
    Cortney Jansen
    Cortney is the first vice president of the
    Lakewood Village Neighborhood Association.
    Question: Tell me a bit about yourself and
    your family.
    Cortney: My husband and I have two kids and
    two cats. We moved to the Bay Area in 2011 -
    California is the sixth state we’ve lived in. My
    husband is an engineer and I’m a project
    manager.
    Question: When did you move to the
    neighborhood?
    Cortney: We moved to this neighborhood in
    early 2013.
    Question: When in the neighborhood do you
    live?
    Cortney: We live near Fairwood Elementary.
    Question: Name one thing you see out of your
    kitchen window.
    Cortney: A North Country Wind Bell. I lived in
    Maine for several years, and this company
    makes metal windchimes whose chimes match
    the sounds of actual Maine buoys. I can no
    longer remember which one I have (Bar
    Harbor Bell maybe), but they make the best
    sound in the wind.
    Question: Something that is in your front
    yard?
    Cortney: Green grass! Last year we planted
    drought-tolerant American-West-native grass
    (blue grama), which gives us a nice lawn
    without any real need to water it.
    Question: A sound you hear frequently in the
    neighborhood?
    Cortney: Airplanes - in our backyard, we can
    see the planes flying out of SJC.
    Question: What is something you like about
    living in Lakewood/Fairwood? And something
    that has changed since you moved to the
    neighborhood.

    Cortney: We have such a great location. We
    are close to Fairwood Park, which is great for
    the kids. We’re close to 101 and 237, and it’s
    relatively easy to get to SF, Santa Cruz, and
    East Bay. We’ve lived in Sunnyvale since we
    moved to California, and we love how close it
    is to everything. Something that’s changed?
    They completely redid the “big kids” side of
    Fairwood Park since we moved here. I totally
    can’t remember what used to be there, but
    the ropes course that they installed is great.
    Question: What are your favorite parks/things
    to do in Sunnyvale and/or the Bay Area?
    Cortney: I’ve been making it a focus to
    explore the state parks near us. So far, we’ve
    done Wilder Ranch State Park (SP), Robert E
    Wooley SP, Henry Coe SP, Henry Cowell SP,
    Castle Rock SP and Mt Diablo SP. In fact, my
    photo is from when my husband and I did a 7
    mile loop around Mt Diablo last month.
    Anyhow, next up on my list are Butano SP and
    San Bruno Mountain SP. We also really enjoy
    The California Academy of Sciences, which we
    recently went to for the first time since covid
    started. And we enjoy any opportunity to walk
    along the water - either the bay or the ocean.
    Question: Tell me a bit about your hobbies.
    Cortney: Since covid started, we’ve spent a lot
    of time exploring local parks (in addition to
    the state parks mentioned above), hiking in
    the hills, and playing at the beach. We’ve also
    gotten into Legos - we all enjoy building them
    and the kids enjoy taking them apart. Before
    covid, we did a lot of rock climbing, wine
    tasting, and travel, but those have all been
    paused the last two years.
    Question: Any great stories to share about
    living in the neighborhood?
    Cortney: Hmm..the best I can think of is when
    we took our kids trick or treating a few years
    ago. There was someone walking on the other
    side of the street, and they were dressed in a
    full-size T-Rex dinosaur costume. My husband
    and I looked at our kids, and we both thought
    that they were laughing at the costume. Well,
    we were completely wrong - our kids were
    terrified of the dinosaur costume. We had to
    pick them both up, stop trick-or-treating right
    there, and go home. Ok, so maybe not a great
    story, but in hindsight, it’s funny.
    Question: How did you get involved with the
    LVNA initially?
    Cortney: I attended one LVNA meeting many
    years ago - it was in person and they were
    talking about traffic control on Lawrence. Then
    I didn’t attend for several years. A little over a
    year ago, I reached out to talk about some
    ideas on content for the Villager. One thing led
    to another, and then I found myself as vice
    president. It’s been really nice to work with
    the other board members. Once thing we
    struggle with, though, is community
    engagement. We have the monthly general
    meetings, but they have low turnout. We
    recently did a survey to understand what
    people in the neighborhood are concerned
    about, and we are working on processing that
    information now. If you haven’t already done
    the survey, please do - you can find it in the
    printed March Villager or online at
    LVNASV.org/tiny/rTX3.
