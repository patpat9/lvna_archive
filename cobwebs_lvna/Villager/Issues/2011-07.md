    Hello Neighbors, 
     
    For our June meeting, the 
    LVNA invited of the City of 
    Sunnyvale Staff to discuss 
    our recent survey. Due to problems with our printer, we were 
    unable to get the newsletter out before our meeting. With that 
    being said, we had a very low turnout.  
    Our meeting included three guest speakers to address our 
    neighborhood concerns including traffic, pool and firehouse 
    closures, and expansion of services.  
    Our first guest speaker was Heba El-Guendy, from the Traffic 
    and Transportation department. Heba explained the different 
    types of calming measures the City can use to slow down the 
    traffic. The different types are: V-calm Signs, Speed Trailers, 
    Lawn Signs, Middle Line, Traffic circles and speed humps. 
    The board will continue to work with her to see if we can get 
    this issue resolved. 
    Our second guest speaker was Nancy Stewart from the 
    Parks and Recreation  
    Department. Nancy talked about the City cuts that will directly 
    affect Lakewood pool and Firehouse 6 that 
    service our   neighborhood.  
    Thank you to the neighbor’s who went 
    down to the June 14th City Council      
    meeting.  They spoke to ask the City to 
    defer the budget cuts that would close the 
    Pool and  
    reduce the manning of firehouse 6 (located 
    at Elko and Lawrence.) 
     
    Our last speaker was Planning              
    Commissioner Bo Chang.

    Fourth of July Trivia 
    By Phyllis Fowler 
     
    I recently found these trivia facts about Fourth of July and I 
    thought I would share them with you. Enjoy! 
    In 1777, thirteen gunshots were fired, once at morning and 
    again as evening fell, on July 4 in Bristol, Rhode Island. 
    Philadelphia celebrated the first anniversary with an official 
    dinner for the Continental Congress. The celebrations 
    included toasts, 13-gun salutes, speeches, prayers, music, 
    parades, troop reviews, and fireworks. Ships were decked 
    with red, white and blue bunting. 
    In 1779, July 4th fell on a Sunday. The holiday was 
    celebrated on Monday instead! 
    1781, the Massachusetts General Court became the first 
    state legislature to recognize July 4th as a state 
    celebrations. 
    In 1791, the first recorded use of the name ―Independence 
    Day‖ occurred. 
    In 1820, the first Fourth of July celebration was held in 
    Eastport, Maine which remains the largest in the state. 
    In 1870, the U.S. Congress made Independence Day an 
    unpaid holiday for federal employees. 
    In 1936, Congress changed Independence Day to a paid 
    federal holiday.

    DUMPSTER DO’S AND DON’T’S 
    DO: 
     
    Distribute items in dumpsters evenly. 
    DO: 
     
    Place furniture into dumpster, Use another bin if the first one is full 
    DON’T: 
    Overfill the dumpsters. They can not be serviced if overfilled!!! 
    DON’T: 
    Dump auto parts/refrigerators/air conditioners/construction deris/business waste. 
    DON’T: 
    Dispose of syringes in bins. Place them into an approved sharps container and drop off 
     
     
    at the SMaRT Station, 301 Carl Road at no additional cost. (look for the red metal 
     
     
    container) 
    DON’T: 
    Put paint, motor oil, or other household hazardous waste into Dumpster. For information 
     
     
    about Household Hazardous Waste, please call (408) 730-7262 
    DON’T: 
    Dispose of electronic devices, small appliances, televisions or computer monitors, 
     
     
    fluorescent lights, or mercury thermometers. These items may be recycled at the  
     
     
    SMaRT Station seven days a week by Sunnyvale residents at no cost. For information, 
     
     
    please call (408) 730-7262. 
     
    Household hazardous waste drop off days are on the 3rd Saturday of each month from 8:00am to 
    1:00pm (except December) at 164 Carl Road, just west of the SMaRT Station.  
    The Next Hazardous Waste Drop Off Event: July 16th

    Reprinted from “Beyond the Badge: 
     
    The Department of Public Safety encourages parents to keep 
    their children and family safe this summer by properly securing 
    their firearms. The latest statistics show that there are more than 
    270 million privately owned firearms in the United States today. 
    Approximately 40% of Americans own at least one firearm and of 
    those gun owners, more than 74% own more than one gun. The 
    most recent data from the US Center for Disease Control and 
    Prevention identifies that more 
    than 3,000 children and teens die 
    from gunfire in the United States 
    each year. There were more 
    kindergarten age children killed 
    by accidental gunfire last year 
    than Police Officers in the line of 
    duty. 
    The Department of Public Safety 
    has partnered with Project 
    ChildSafe to ensure safe and 
    responsible firearm ownership. 
    Residents of Sunnyvale can stop by Public Safety to obtain a free 
    cable-style gun lock. These locks will fit almost any firearm to 
    provide a gun owner the ability to safely store their firearm. 
    Let’s reduce the these tragic statistics and take advantage of this 
    free program. Thank you. 
