    Although autumn officially began on September 23rd, you may 
    have noticed the Lakewood Village trees finally catching up. With 
    rain shortly behind, it’s important to stay on top of the leaves to 
    prevent downspout clogs and street drainage problems.
    The green bin is your friend when it comes to excess foliage, 
    but there are plenty of other options for utilizing your trees’ hard 
    work and your yard will thank you for it with an abundance of 
    produce and/or greenery next spring.
    Organic Mulch
    Mow over leaves with a grass catcher to chop and collect them 
    for a nutrient-infused organic mulch. Sprinkle it on garden beds, 
    in your lawn, or in a homegrown compost pile/bin.
    Insulation
    Whole or chopped leaves can be used as insulation for the soil 
    and around delicate plants during the cold winter months.
    Lasagna Garden
    Build your lasagna garden, a minimal-effort gardening technique, 
    with a few layers of whole or chopped leaves

    1. Peel sweet potatoes and cut into approx 1 inch cubes. 
    Place sweet potatoes in saucepan. Add water to cover. 
    Bring to boil. Reduce heat and cook until fork tender, 
    approx 15 minutes. Drain potatoes and return to pan. 
    Mash until smooth. Cool to room temperature.
    2. Preheat oven to 350°F. In a bowl or standing mixer, 
    cream together room temp butter and brown sugar 
    (and white sugar, if desired). Add eggs and mix well.
    3. Add evaporated milk, vanilla extract, flour, cinnamon, 
    nutmeg, cloves, and ginger. Mix well until smooth and 
    fully combined.
    4. In a separate bowl, combine chopped pecans and 
    flour until the pecans are coated. Add maple syrup 
    and melted butter. Mix until you can’t see white flour. If 
    white flour is still present, feel free to add a little more 
    butter. Set aside when fully mixed.
    5. Roll out pie crust and carefully place into pie dish (10.5 
    inch diameter). Push crust up against pie dish and 
    make it smooth. Trim off excess dough, if needed.
    6. Pour sweet potato filling into pie dish. Dollop pecan 
    mixture over filling until pie is covered. 
    7. Bake in 350°F oven for 55-60 minutes. Let cool for 1 
    hour prior to serving.
    1 pie crust (store bought ok)
    1 LB sweet potatoes
    4 TBSP butter room temp
    2 eggs room temp
    3/4 CUP evaporated milk
    1/4 CUP brown sugar
    1/4 CUP white sugar (optional)
    1.5 CUPS chopped pecans
    2 TBSP flour
    1 TSP vanilla extract
    2 TBSP flour 
    1 TSP ground cinnamon 
    1/2 TSP ground nutmeg
    1/4 TSP ground cloves
    1/4 TSP ground ginger
    1.5 TBSP maple syrup
    5 TBSP melted butter

    LVNA MEETING ON OCT 9
    Sunnyvale Election System –
    Jaqui Guzman, Deputy City Manager for Sunnyvale, provided an overview of Sunnyvale’s current “at-large” 
    election system and outlined the reasons for moving towards a district-based system. Cities with at-large voting 
    systems have increasingly been targeted for litigation under the California Voting Rights Act (CVRA), which 
    prohibits any voting practices that have the potential to dilute the votes of racial minorities.
    Sunnyvale has proactively decided to pursue a district-based voting system to avoid a lawsuit under CVRA, which 
    would cost between $3M and $6M. No city has ever won one of these litigation cases.
    Under the new district-based system, Sunnyvale will be divided into 6 districts, each voting for their district’s City 
    councilmember. Sunnyvale’s mayor would still be elected through an at-large vote.
    For more information, and to view the finalists for district maps, please visit SunnyvaleElections.org

    Manuel Aguilar and Anthony Hernandez, from the International Brotherhood of Electrical Workers (IBEW), spoke

    Anthony spoke about their involvement with youth groups and connected with other meeting attendees for
