    FACTOID
    We’re introducing a new recurring column in the Villager – Factoid. 
    Each month will feature a fun, interesting, or even obscure fact about something relevant to our neighborhood. 
    Given the ongoing wildfires and likelihood for more to come as climate change continues to push them towards 
    an inevitability each year, this month’s fact is on air quality ratings and, more specifically, why different sources 
    can have varying results. Thanks, Cortney, for the great suggestion!
    Air Quality Index (AQI) is the Environmental Protection Agency’s metric for reporting air quality. In simple terms, 
    it’s a measurement for the amount of “stuff” in the air that isn’t healthy for us to breathe. Some of this “stuff” 
    is comprised of things you could actually see under a microscope, such as smoke and dust, and is typically 
    classified by its size; PM2.5 refers to particulate matter less than 2.5 micrometers in diameter, and PM10 is for 
    particulate matter under 10 micrometers (one one-hundredth of a millimeter). Other “stuff” included in the 
    measurement could be completely invisible, such as Carbon Monoxide (CO) and Sulfur Dioxide (SO2). As you 
    can imagine, higher numbers for AQI mean more pollution in the air.
    We’re in the midst of poor air quality as a result of the nearby wildfires and wind blowing that smoke into our 
    neighborhood. A couple popular tools for checking the air quality are AirNow.gov and PurpleAir.com. If you 
    happen to check both, you may have noticed they frequently differ, often enough to be the difference between 
    safe and unhealthy levels of pollution. Why? There are essentially 3 variables that lead to the differences.
    Technology: AirNow gathers most of its information from EPA monitors, which have an array 
    of different sensors and methods for detecting various pollutants in the air. These monitors 
    are frequently calibrated to ensure a tight level of precision. PurpleAir’s monitors are 
    designed to detect particulate matter by measuring the size of those particles with lasers. 
    These are commercially available for anyone to purchase and then register on the website as 
    a new monitor location. These differences in technology, as well as the underlying purpose 
    for each type of system, can lead to differences in precision for a given type of pollutant.
    Location: There are many more PurpleAir monitors around the bay than AirNow monitors. 
    Since PurpleAir monitors can be purchased by anyone and placed anywhere, a diesel truck 
    driving by one of them could easily change the reading for a little while in that immediate 
    area. PurpleAir would provide a much more accurate representation of the air quality for

    RECIPE CORNER
    Eggplant Parmesan
    1.	 Cut eggplants into 1/2 inch thick slices. Place all of the slices 
    onto 2 baking trays lined with paper towels. Generously salt both 
    sides of the eggplant slices and let them sit at room temperature 
    for approximately 45 minutes to an hour. This will help draw out 
    moisture from the eggplant. Afterwards, use a clean paper towel 
    to wipe both sides of the eggplant to remove excess salt and 
    liquid. 
    2.	 Preheat oven to 425 degrees. In a shallow bowl, scramble 2 eggs. 
    In another shallow bowl, add the panko breadcrumbs. 
    3.	 Grab an eggplant slice and dip it into the eggs (coat both sides). 
    Then move eggplant into the breadcrumbs (coat one or both 
    sides). Place eggplant onto a baking tray (breadcrumb side 
    face-up if only one side is coated) lined with parchment paper 
    or silicone baking mat. Repeat this step with all eggplant slices. 
    Sprinkle Italian seasoning onto the tops of each slice.
    4.	 Spray the slices with a cooking spray and place in preheated 
    oven for about 30-35 minutes. Remove trays from oven.
    5.	 Spoon a layer of marinara onto each eggplant slice, followed by 
    a thin layer of mozzarella. Return tray to oven for about 5 more 
    minutes. Once cheese has melted, take out of oven and top with 
    freshly chopped basil or parsley. Serve alongside your favorite 
    pasta. 
    1-2 eggplants
    1 cup panko breadcrumbs
    2 eggs
    Italian seasoning
    marinara sauce
    mozzarella cheese
    handful of fresh parsley or basil 
    salt
