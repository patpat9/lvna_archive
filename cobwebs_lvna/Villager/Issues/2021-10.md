    MEET YOUR BOARD MEMBER
    LYDIA FOTIADES
    Lydia is the president of the Lakewood Village Neighborhood Association. 
    Q: Tell me a bit about yourself and your family.
    Lydia: I married my husband two years ago. We’ve 
    been together for 13 years. It’s the two of us, and 
    we have two cats (Apple and Banana). Those are 
    the names from the shelter. We tried to find better 
    names, but gave up after two weeks and kept their 
    original names.
    Q: When did you move to the neighborhood?
    Lydia: We moved to this neighborhood at the 
    beginning of 2016. 
    Q: Where in the neighborhood do you live?
    Lydia: I live near Lakewood Park
    Q: Name one thing you see out of your kitchen window.
    Lydia: My neighbor’s fence and the top of their patio.
    Q: Something that is in your front yard?
    Lydia: There’s not much in my front yard…
    Q: A sound you hear frequently in the neighborhood?
    Lydia: Laughter from kids at the park, and music from people having picnics at the park. It’s the sound of people 
    having a good time, which makes me happy.
    Q: What is something you like about living in Lakewood/Fairwood? And something that has changed since you 
    moved to the neighborhood.
    Lydia: I like that our neighbors are low-key and friendly. Not much has changed since we moved here. 
    Q: What are your favorite parks/things to do in Sunnyvale and/or the Bay Area?
    Lydia: I love to go for bike rides to Shoreline Park and back. It’s about 20 miles roundtrip, and sometimes when 
    I get to Shoreline Lake, I’ll do a run around the lake, which is about two miles. I love the bike trails in this area! I 
    can take the greenbelt and get all the way to Shoreline Lake from there. I barely have to go on any actual roads 
    to get there. I can get to Levi Stadium by bike trail and Mercado on my bike. I love the accessibility that we have 
    here – I can even bike to the salt ponds.
    Q: Tell me a bit about your hobbies.
    Lydia: Lately, I enjoy running, but this has been recent since covid. Before covid, I hated running. But now I’ve 
    been forced into it since it’s the only form of cardio I can do with covid around, but I’ve started to really enjoy 
    it. I also like taking drives, like driving Skyline Boulevard. You start in Saratoga and end up in Foster City (and 
    then come back down 280). It’s a great chance to listen to music and clear my head, similar to what I get from 
    running. I also love road trips and will do long drives around the Bay Area.

    Lydia: Most of the stories are just related to what I’ve already talked about – I love having Lakewood Park nearby

    Lydia: No one else was interested, and I didn’t want LVNA to dissolve completely. I’ve always said that, if anyone

    to stay involved and active. There are many reasons to be on the board – change things, etc. I just want to be 
    involved in the social event, and to help with dumpster day, holiday events, etc.

    FREE SCHOOL MEALS FOR ALL 
    CALIFORNIA SCHOOL CHILDREN
    Many of us may not be aware that, in addition to all the other ways covid has disrupted our lives, the pandemic 
    resulted in significant changes to how school meals are funded. Previously, school districts across the United 
    States were reimbursed by the USDA (United States Department of Agriculture) for school meals as part of 
    the National School Lunch Program (NSLP); reimbursement amounts were on a sliding scale based on the 
    student’s free or reduced-price meal (FRPM) status. In the 2019-2020 school year, 63% of California’s students 
    (3.9 million children) participated in the NSLP. With the onset of the pandemic, the USDA policy changed to 
    reimburse schools for all school meals (regardless of the income of a student’s family). Earlier this year, the USDA 
    announced that it will extend the broader reimbursements through the 2021-2022 school year. While that was 
    wonderful news to families across the US, it left many wondering what would happen in the 2022-2023 school 
    year and beyond.
    In California, access to free meals for students will continue - the Universal Meals Program was included in the 
    state budget that the California State Legislature and Governor Gavin Newsom signed in June. The funding will 
    cover school meals in the 2022-2023 academic year, and it comes from larger-than-expected projected revenues 
    (in other words, surplus tax dollars collected from California residents) to provide $650 million annually to 
    reimburse schools for the meals.
    Why is feeding our students so important? There is a proven link between hunger and learning - kids who are 
    hungry do not learn as well as kids who are not hungry. As Alia Wilson, the Communications Coordinator at 
    the Sunnyvale School District (SSD), says, “When we take care of students’ basic needs, they are free to focus 
    on learning and growing and having fun!” The SSD covers Sunnyvale’s 8 elementary schools and two middle 
    schools, and the district is providing free meals to all students for the 2021-2022 academic year through the 
    Seamless Summer Option program provided by the USDA.
    We spoke with Alia to learn more about these programs and how they help SSD and its students. The 
    conversation below is edited for clarity and length.
    Q: What meals do students get?
    Alia: We provide students breakfast, lunch, snack, and dinner.
    Q: Do families still have to fill out paperwork to qualify for the free meals?
    Alia: Yes, we still ask families to fill out the FRPM paperwork, even though they do not have to pay for the 
    meals. The paperwork helps them qualify for other financial assistance, such as p-ebt cards (you can find more 
    information about p-ebt cards here: https://www.cdss.ca.gov/home/pandemic-ebt). Additionally, SSD funding 
    that we get from the state is based on how many students qualify for FRPM. Getting families to complete the 
    paperwork is also important because it will help us with funding in the future if or when free lunches end. 
    Q: How many free meals are served each day?
    Alia: Across our whole district (10 schools), about 26% of students are socio-economically disadvantaged. Under 
    the old rules, these students would have qualified for FRPM. Under the new rules, all of our students get free 
    meals. We serve about 4184 free school meals each day. 
    Q: How many food service workers work in SSD?
    Alia: Districtwide, we have 29 food service workers, 2 delivery drivers, and 2 office support staff members.

    Q: Tell me a bit about the food students get.
    Alia: Our food services contract is with Sodexo, and all students receive the same meal. They get breakfast, a hot 
    lunch, a snack, and a frozen supper entree to take home. The after-school program receives a hot supper entrée 
    and snack. We get locally grown produce when available. You can find more about our partnership with Sodexo 
    here: https://sunnyvalesd.sodexomyway.com/ 
    Q: Anything else to add?
    Alia: Yes, we are so happy to be able to provide this important and needed resource for our families!

    MEET YOUR NEIGHBOR
    SARAH OWENS
    Q: Tell me a bit about yourself and your family.
    Sarah: My husband Conley and I have been married 
    for 11 years, and we have 7 children! Our oldest is 
    10, and the youngest is 15 months old. Conley is a 
    software engineer at Google and a part-time pastor 
    at the church in Lakewood Village (Silicon Valley 
    Reformed Baptist Church). I am able to stay at home 
    and have been homeschooling our kids for the past 
    5 or so years.
    Q: When did you move to the Lakewood/Fairwood 
    neighborhood?
    Sarah: We bought a house in Lakewood Village 
    in late 2018; before that we had been renting in 
    Mountain View.
    Q: What street do you live on?
    Sarah: Lakewood Dr.
    Q: Name one thing you see out your kitchen window.
    Sarah: In our floor plan, the kitchen doesn’t have 
    windows (haha). But out of the living room window 
    we can see our lemon and orange trees! My husband 
    and I are originally from Virginia, and I was very 
    surprised when we moved to CA to see how many 
    homes had fruit trees in the yard.
    Q: Something that is in your front yard?
    Sarah: A very nice-smelling gardenia plant. I didn’t 
    know what it was called until our kids did a botany 
    field trip last year and recognized it. I now see 
    them in other people’s yards also, and when they’re 
    blooming you can smell them all the way from the 
    sidewalk.
    Q: A sound you hear frequently in the 
    neighborhood?
    Sarah: Definitely roaring traffic noises from 101, since 
    our yard is right up against it. It took us a couple 
    months to get used to the noise, especially at night, 
    but now we hardly notice it.
    Q: What is something you like about living in 
    Lakewood/Fairwood? 
    Sarah: I most appreciate being close to our church, 
    which is right down the street, also in Lakewood Village. 
    Mountain View was not that far away, but being this 
    close has let us become even more connected with the 
    community.
    Q: And something that has changed since you moved to 
    the neighborhood?
    Sarah: I’ve seen more Little Free Libraries pop up, I think, 
    which is a cute and nice trend.
    Q: What are your favorite parks/things to do in Sunnyvale 
    and/or the Bay Area?
    Sarah: We really love Lakewood Park! It’s easy to get to, 
    and our kids love the water feature when it is on. Even 
    during the pandemic when playgrounds were closed, we 
    were able to go play in the sand of the volleyball court.
    Q: Tell me a bit about your hobbies.
    Sarah: I’ve really enjoyed helping out with our local food 
    pantry this past year! We distribute food every Saturday 
    morning from 10-11am, giving out food that has been 
    donated from local grocery stores or even donated by 
    our neighbors. It’s been wonderful to meet people from 
    the community this way, and to see people coming to 
    donate and even help set up without being asked. If you 
    or someone you know needs food, or if you would like to 
    help, you can go to svrbc.org/food for more information.
    Q: Any great stories to share about living in the 
    neighborhood?
    Sarah: We like walking around and exploring -- our 
    favorite local find is Calabazas Creek east of Fairwood 
    neighborhood; the kids really enjoy pretending to swim in 
    it, even when it is only shin-deep (haha).
