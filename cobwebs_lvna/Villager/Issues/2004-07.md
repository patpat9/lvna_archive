    Big Events Coming Up 
     
    Dumpster Day 
    Dumpster Day will be one day only again this 
    year, on July 17.  Dumpsters will be dropped off 
    at various locations around the neighborhood 
    (see www.lvna.net for a map), and residents will 
    be able to drop off unwanted stuff all day.  
    There are certain restrictions on what can be 
    dumped, 
    though, 
    and 
    we 
    strongly 
    urge 
    everyone to pay attention to the posted signs 
    and respect what the Dumpster Monitors have 
    to say about what you are trying to drop off.  
    Certain items, like anything with engine oil or 
    Freon (used for engine cooling and in old 
    refrigerators), or latex paint, are considered 
    hazardous.  These items may not be put in the 
    Dumpsters.  If you have hazardous materials, 
    please drive the short distance to the SMaRT 
    Station at 301 Carl Road (off Caribbean Drive; 
    just go north on Lawrence Expressway)—less 
    than 2½ miles away!  July 17 is also hazardous 
    materials day at the SMaRT Station (8 a.m. to 1 
    p.m.), so you see how well that works out!  
     
    We urge all interested residents to attend the 
    Lakewood 
    Village 
    Neighborhood 
    Association 
    general meeting on July 14 at 7 p.m. at the 
    Lakewood Park building to learn more about 
    what can and cannot be put into the Dumpsters.  
    Please remember that the Dumpster Monitors 
    don’t make the rules; they just make sure 
    people follow the guidelines for safe dumping.  
    Sneaking up on the Dumpsters in the middle of 
    the night and dumping restricted items, or  
     
     
    arguing with the Monitors is both rude and 
    environmentally irresponsible.  This is a great 
    free service we are offered, so let’s not abuse 
    the privilege. 
     
    This year we will have a Dumpster Day Garage 
    Sale as well.  Every year a lot of stuff that is still 
    in good condition ends up at the Dumpsters, 
    and this year we will move those items over to 
    the area of parking lot between the two gas 
    stations on Lakehaven and sell them.  Proceeds 
    will go to the Lakewood Village Neighborhood 
    Association to help pay for the other events we 
    put on during the year like the summer picnic 
    and the holiday parade.  We encourage people 
    to come on by and check out what’s for sale!

    Keep Paint, Batteries, Motor Oil, 
    TVs, Computer Monitors, and Other 
    Hazardous Waste Out of Dumpsters 
    July 17 is the next Dumpster Day in Lakewood 
    Village, but don’t plan on putting paint, 
    pesticides, solvents, batteries, fluorescent bulbs, 
    or automotive fluids (oil, brake fluid) into the 
    Dumpster in your area. These items are 
    hazardous waste, so they need to go to the 
    Household Hazardous Waste Drop-Off Event Site 
    near the SMaRT Station and Recycling Drop-Off 
    Center off Caribbean Drive and Borregas Avenue 
    (it’s a short trip north on Lawrence Expressway 
    to Caribbean and Borregas). The site will be 
    open from 8 a.m. to 1 p.m. on July 17. 
     
    Televisions and computer monitors cannot be 
    put into the Dumpsters either. Sunnyvale 
    residents can take these items to the SMaRT 
    Station 7 days a week between 8 a.m. and 5 
    p.m. TV and computer monitors require a 
    special handling fee of $15 each. Call (408) 
    730-7262 for more information. 

    July in Your Yard 
    and Garden 
    • 
    Weed. Do not let the weeds form 
    seedheads. 
    • 
    Add young, seedhead-free weeds 
    to the compost pile. Remember 
    to keep the pile as damp as a 
    wrung-out 
    sponge 
    for 
    faster 
    composting. 
    • 
    Watch out for snails and earwigs, both of 
    which devour seedlings. 
    • 
    Plant bulbs that bloom in autumn. 
    • 
    Use a strong spray from the hose to wash 
    away aphids. 
    • 
    With fruit crops ripening this month, watch 
    out for ants. Wrap fruit tree trunks with a 
    sticky tape to control ants (wrap low so that 
    birds, particularly hummingbirds, will not be 
    attracted) and if necessary, spray. 
    • 
    Water plants as needed. 
    • 
    Insulate the garden soil from the hot, dry 
    weather by applying a two-inch-thick mulch. 
    • 
    Fertilize plants if you see signs of pale, 
    lackluster growth. Fertilize the lawn. Fertilize 
    all flowering plants that are about to bloom. 
    Fertilize camellias. Fertilize roses, except 
    most antique roses. In really hot areas, 
    roses will probably stop flowering until the 
    weather cools. 
    • 
    Pinch off dead flowers. Make sure you get 
    the entire flower head when deadheading 
    annuals—particularly the swollen ovaries 
    where seeds form. 
    • 
    Transplant summer-flowering plants, such as 
    cosmos, petunias, and zinnias that will 
    endure heat, into the bedding plot. Hot 
    weather is hard on newly transplanted 
    plants. Shade them for a few days with the 
    temporary shelter of an umbrella or wooden 
    shake. Stake tall sprawling flowers such as 
    dahlias. 
    • 
    Continue to plant the vegetable garden. Thin 
    vegetable seedlings that were sown too 
    close together. Harvest vegetables as they 
    ripen. Pick them early in the day. 
    • 
    Soak all newly transplanted plants. Prune 
    berry 
    vines 
    when 
    you 
    have 
    finished 
    harvesting. 
     
    These tips are from the book Northern California 
    Gardening: A Month-by-Month Guide, by Katherine 
    Grace Endicott, published by Chronicle Books, San 
    Francisco, ©1996, and are used with the publisher’s 
    permission.
