    Fairwood School Turns 50! 
    Fairwood Elementary School first opened its doors in 
    1956, so 2006 marked its 50th year. Some residents of 
    the neighborhood likely remember that the school closed 
    in 1982 because of declining enrollment, but it was 
    renovated and re-opened in 1997—and 350 children 
    immediately began attending. The school reports that 
    nearly 30% of the students who attend Fairwood School 
    have family members who attended Fairwood before 
    them. That impressive statistic shows how families stay 
    in this community over the long term.

      January in Your Yard and Garden 
    ☼Order now from seed catalogs. If you wait too long, 
    you may have to accept substitutions later in the season. 
    ☼Order bare-root roses and fruit trees from specialty 
    nurseries. They will usually stop shipping bare-root 
    plants by the end of March. 
    ☼Move garden plants brought indoors for the holidays 
    back outside if they will not be subject to frost or snow. 
    ☼Make sure you have adequate drainage for heavy rain 
    storms. 
    ☼Constant dry heat from heating systems stresses 
    indoor plants. Check them for signs of yellowing or 
    wilting; they may need more water and an occasional 
    misting. Fertilize indoor flowering plants that are in 
    bloom now. 
    ☼Watch out for frost. The weather is likely to be truly 
    cold this month. Be ready to cover susceptible plants or 
    to move them to a sheltered location. 
    ☼Prune roses, deciduous fruit trees, and ornamental 
    shrubs. Work from the inside of the plants toward the 
    outside. Remove diseased or dead branches as well as 
    branches crossing or rubbing each other. Then prune the 
    exterior for an attractive form. Prune spring-flowering 
    trees and shrubs after they have bloomed. 
    ☼If desired, spray deciduous shrubs and fruit trees with 
    a dormant spray to kill overwintering insects. 
    ☼Shop for bare-root plants and plant them when the soil 
    is dry enough to dig properly. 
    ☼Look for colorful bedding plants such as calendulas 
    and chrysanthemums in the nursery to give an instant 
    color boost to the entryway or window boxes. 
    These tips are from the book Northern California 
    Gardening: A Month-by-Month Guide, by Katherine Grace 
    Endicott, published by Chronicle Books, San Francisco, 
    copyright 1996, and are used with the publisher’s 
    permission. ■

    MicroRockets Win Pop Warner Championship 
    -Judy Fernandez, LVNA Vice President 
    The Sunnyvale MicroRockets football team had a 
    great season: 15 wins and 1 defeat. This record took 
    them to the Super Bowl of Pop Warner football in 
    Orlando, Florida, in early December. The Rockets 
    defeated the Waterbury Patriots of Connecticut with a 
    14 to 6 finale, to become the Division II Junior 
    Midget Champions. When the boys returned home, 
    Sunnyvale Mayor Otto Lee and the other City Council 
    members invited the team to receive a special award 
    on behalf of Sunnyvale on December 19. 
     
     
     
     
     
    Way to go Lakewood and Fairwood kids on the 
    MicroRockets team! We are very proud of you. 
    The mission of the program, Pop Warner Little 
    Scholars, Inc., is to enable children to benefit from 
    participation in team sports and activities in a safe and 
    structured environment. Pop Warner programs teach 
    fundamental values, skills, and knowledge that 
    children will use throughout their lives. It is the only 
    national youth sports organization in America that 
    requires its participants to perform adequately in the 
    classroom before permitting them to play.

    Free Online Homework Help Now Available 
    Through Sunnyvale Public Library 
    Puzzled by a tough homework assignment? Get out 
    your Sunnyvale library card. Students from grades 4–
    12 and college introduction-level can go to the 
    Sunnyvale Public Library or its Web site at 
    www.sunnyvalelibrary.org from their home PC to get 
    immediate, free help from qualified tutors. Live 
    Homework Help, a new service offered by the library, 
    is an online tutoring service from Tutor.com that 
    connects students to expert tutors in math, science, 
    social studies, and English via the Internet. Spanishspeaking tutors are available in math and science. 
    Log On and Learn. This free service allows students 
    to connect to an expert tutor either from the 
    Sunnyvale Public Library or from their home PC with 
    a library card, seven days a week from 1 p.m. to 10 
    p.m. Access to a Spanish-speaking tutor is available 
    Sunday through Thursday from 1 p.m. to 7 p.m. Live 
    Homework Help is easy to use; children and teens 
    simply go to the library’s Web site, click on the Live 
    Homework Help link and then enter their grade level 
    and the subject in which they need help. In just a few 
    minutes, students are connected to a tutor in an online 
    classroom for one-to-one help in math (elementary, 
    algebra, geometry, trigonometry, and calculus), 
    science (elementary, earth science, biology, 
    chemistry, and physics), social studies (American 
    history, world history, political science, and more), 
    and English (spelling, grammar, essay writing, and 
    book reports).  
    All tutors are certified teachers, college professors, 
    professional tutors, or graduate school students from 
    across the country. Every tutor has completed a 
    comprehensive training program and a third-party 
    background check. 
    A California State Library LSTA (Library Services 
    and Technology Act) grant of $22,500 allows the 
    Sunnyvale Library to offer this free service to students 
    in Sunnyvale.  
     
     
    Don't Dump Unused Medicines Down the Drain 
    If you flush unused or expired medicines down the 
    toilet or pour them into the drain, to keep them out of 
    the reach of children and pets, you are endangering 
    local water quality. New evidence shows that trace 
    pharmaceutical deposits are turning up in creeks, 
    rivers, and bays around the world. The Environmental 
    Protection Agency now describes pharmaceutical 
    waste as an environmental pollutant. 
    To help protect our creeks and Bay, take unused or 
    expired prescription drugs and medications to a 
    Household Hazardous Waste drop-off event for safe 
    disposal. These events are held the third Saturday of 
    every month (January 20 is the next one) from 8 a.m. 
    to 1 p.m. at 164 Carl Road (near the SMaRT Station, 
    off Borregas, which intersects Caribbean). Medicines 
    and pills should be transported in original containers, 
    but be sure to remove your personal information. 
    Proper disposal of pharmaceuticals keeps pollution 
    out of our waterways and leaves a clean environment 
    for fish and wildlife.
