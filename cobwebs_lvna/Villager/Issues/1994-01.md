    January, 1994
    
    Adult English Classes at Lakewood School
    
    by Pat Boyle, 744-1149
    
    Three different English classes are being offered at Lake-
    wood Elementary School this winter.
    
    Beginning English classes are available at the school on
    Monday and Wednesday mornings from 9:15 to 11:15 A.M. Sign
    in at the school office. Books for this class cost about $15.
    
    De Anza College is teaching an intermediate English class
    at Lakewood School starting on February 1. The intermediate
    classes will be held on Tuesday and Thursday evenings from
    7:00 to 8:30 P.M. The cost for 8 weeks is $65. Register before
    January 21 by calling 864-8817 and use a credit card to pay, or
    pay by mailing a check and registration form to De Anza. Forms
    are available at the Lakewood School office from 8:00 A.M. to
    4:00 P.M. Monday through Friday.
    
    In addition, the adult high school will teach evening ESL
    classes at Lakewood School beginning January 25. Register at
    the first class. Books will cost about $35. |
    
    Second Annual Holiday Parade
    
    Rain could not dampen the spirit of our parade. At about
    9:00 A.M. on December 11, parade participants began arriving at
    Fairwood School with puzzled looks on their faces. The Chief of
    Police said, "You aren't going through with this, are you?" Our
    response was, "We're Lakewood, and the show will go on!"
    
    At about 10:15, the parade started to move. It was still rain-
    ing, but the neighbors came out and showed their support. By the
    time the parade reached Lawrence, the rain had stopped. We
    continued marching to Lakewood Park.
    
    At the park, neighbors and City officials had refreshments,
    including coffee and hot cocoa. Our outgoing mayor Pat Castillo
    spoke about the spirit of Lakewood and her pride in living here.
    The new mayor, Frances Rowe, thanked Lakewood for inviting
    her and expressed her desire to be a part of the event next year.
    Councilmembers Stan Kawezynski, Landon Noll, and Jim Rob-
    erts thanked the neighborhood for their support.
    
    We also had a coloring contest for the children. Congratula-
    tions to our winners Stacey Jenkins, Virginia Dao, and David.
    The winners will receive prizes at the January meeting.
    
    Next year will be here before you know it. If you would like
    to get involved, please contact an L.V.N.A. board member.
    
    Finally, our special thanks go out to parade coordinator Pat
    Maloney for a job well done. a
    
    Letter from the President
    
    HAPPY NEW YEAR! I want to thank you ALL for
    organizing, supporting, and participating in
    our 1993 Parade and Holiday Lighting Contest.
    Our City was so supportive that many past and
    present City officials came out and celebrated
    the holiday with Lakewood Village residents.
    
    The next order of business is that the 1994
    L.V.N.A. election is coming up in March. New
    officers will take office immediately after
    the election. Current officers will stay on as
    assistants for three more months during the
    transition.
    
    The L.V.N.A. is the voice of our community and
    
    the channel by which we get things done. The
    L.V.N.A. needs you to volunteer as officers
    now. So volunteer yourself or recommend a
    neighbor by calling Paul Schaefer at 734-1680.
    
    On a different note, we have permission from
    the County to place two permanent "Welcome to
    Lakewood Village" signs on the sound walls at
    the intersection of Lawrence Expressway and
    Sandia/Lakehaven. We will discuss this
    proposition at our meeting on January 20.
    
    Jessica Wei Lee, L.V.N.A. President

    Holiday Lighting Contest Winners
    
    On December 15 Lakewood held its third annual lighting
    contest. The contest was judged by Councilmembers Stan
    Kawezynski and Landon Noll, Chief Regan Williams, John &
    Carolyn Christian from the Parks Division, Iris Fox of California
    Young World, and Teresa Faz from Neighborhood Preservation.
    
    The contest started at 6:00 P.M. There were so many homes
    lit that judging took about 2-1/2 hours. The judges had a difficult
    time choosing the winners, and they wood like to acknowledge
    all participating homes for a job well done.
    
    The Holiday Lighting Contest winners for 1993 were:
    
    Grand Prize:
    1319 Socorro
    
    First Place:
    Unit 3: 654 Greenlake
    
    Unit 1; 1254 Sandia
    
    Unit 2: 750 Lakemuir Unit 4: 1118 Pecos
    
    The Santa trophies will be awarded at the January meeting.
    Once again, our neighborhood shined for the City officials. They
    enjoyed themselves very much and talked about doing this event
    citywide. Way to go Lakewood—we did it!
    
    P.S. All the judges asked to return in 1994! |
    
    Flood Control Project Update
    
    In April of 1993, the Villager reported on a year-long flood
    control project to be built on Calabazas Creek. The project was
    to reduce the risk of flood to less than once every hundred years.
    
    The project has not begun yet, so Lakewood Villager Paul
    Schaefer called the Water District to find out why. Paul found
    out that there has been a delay in securing federal permits. How-
    ever, the permits are expected to be resolved in January so that
    work may begin in spring of 1994, a

    Now is the time to start thinking about nominating yourself or a
    neighbor for the 1994-1995 L.V.N.A. board. Nominations for

    The Sunnyvale City Council is currently seeking applicants to
    serve on the Law Enforcement Citizen Participation Commit-
    tee. The committee will serve as an advisory group to update the
    City's 5-year law enforcement policy and service document. Ap-
    plicants can represent businesses, schools, or the community at
    large. For more information, call 730-7533.
    
    The City of Sunnyvale has a program which allows residents
    who are over 60 or permanently disabled to purchase discount
    vouchers for taxi or van service. With the vouchers, local trips
    cost $2 for and extended trips cost $5 for up to 4 passengers. For
    program information and registration, call 730-7394.
