Lakewood Village took a big step to help the city keep our streets clean. Many people in the neighborhood made an extra effort on March 18 to move their cars; this made it easier for the city to clean our streets. Special thanks go to Gene Goldsmith, Rick Flovin, and Charlene Jolly for distributing the reminder notices. As you probably know, for the first six weeks (until April 15) we are trying to make an extra effort on Sandia, Socorro, and Torrence, by posting the orange reminders on cars parked on these streets. If this voluntary effort stays successful, we hope to try a similar effort on other streets in the neighborhood. Some people have called asking if they will get a parking ticket if their car is parked on these streets during the street sweeping days. No, you will not get a ticket. It is legal to park on the street at that time, but we are asking everyone to move their cars if at all possible, However, you may get a parking ticket if you violate any of the parking regulations that are listed on the street sweeping notice; for example, if you leave a car parked in one location for more than five days. The city has been helping us clean up our streets by increasing the enforcement of parking regulations that have been in place for a long time. This helps us all in the long run by making the neighborhood look better. This may also increase the market value of the houses - if it changes the value of the house by only 1%, it would be like putting $2,000 in your pocket! One item that was discussed at the recent LVNA board meeting was the issue of Lakewood residents who run a business out of their home. Lakewood is a residential neighborhood, and there are zoning regulations that limit the activities that can be carried out in a residential neighborhood. If you are running a business, you should of course have a business license from the City of Sunnyvale ($10/year). Sunnyvale is a pro-business city, so if you have any questions about your business you can call City Hall and they will bc Now that Sunnyvale Public Safety is increasing its enforcement efforts of parking regulations, if you do run a business from your home, you may want to know about Municipal Code SMC 10.16.160. It says "It is unlawful for any person to park any commercial vehicle (other than a pick-up truck, station wagon, or similar vehicle of a size similar to an ordinary passenger vehicle) more than five hours in any residential district except: (a) While loading or unloading property and time in addition to such five-hour period is necessary to complete such work; or (b) When such vehicle is parked in connection with, and in aid of, the performance of a service to or on a property in the block in which such vehicle is parked and time in addition to such five-hour period is reasonably necessary to complete such service. (Ord. 2435-93-1)." If you are parking a commercial vehicle on a Lakewood Village street you may want to make other arrangements. You should either park these vehicles in your driveway, or rent parking/work space in an industrial area. I have been told that it is possible rent such space for between $35/month and $200/month, depending on the location and how much space is needed. Such rent would be a business expense, and therefore would give you an income tax deduction, If you need help finding space, or have any other questions, feel free to give me a call at 734-5769. coco'S OAKMEAD PKWY.
