    Street Sweeping
    
    Tom Freeman
    
    Congratulations everyone! Lakewood Village took a
    
    big step to help the city keep our streets clean.
    
    Many people in the neighborhood made an extra
    effort on March 18 to move their cars; this made it easier for the
    city to clean our streets. Special thanks go to Gene Goldsmith,
    Rick Flovin, and Charlene Jolly for distributing the reminder
    notices. As you probably know, for the first six weeks (until
    April 15) we are trying to make an extra effort on Sandia,
    Socorro, and Torrence, by posting the orange reminders on cars
    parked on these streets. If this voluntary effort stays successful,
    we hope to try a similar effort on other streets in the
    neighborhood.
    
    Some people have called asking if they will get a parking
    ticket if their car is parked on these streets during the street
    sweeping days. No, you will not get a ticket. It is legal to park
    on the street at that time, but we are asking everyone to move
    their cars if at all possible. However, you may get a parking
    ticket if you violate any of the parking regulations that are listed
    on the street sweeping notice; for example, if you leave a car
    parked in one location for more than five days.
    
    The city has been helping us clean up our streets by
    increasing the enforcement of parking regulations that have
    been in place for along time. This helps us all in the long run
    by making the neighborhood look better. This may also increase
    the market value of the houses - if it changes the value of the
    house by only 1%, it would be like putting $2,000 in your
    pocket!
    
    One item that was discussed at the recent LVNA board
    meeting was the issue of Lakewood residents who run a
    business out of their home. Lakewood is a_ residential
    neighborhood, and there are zoning regulations that limit the
    activities that can be carried out in a residential neighborhood.
    If you are running a business, you should of course have a
    business license from the City of Sunnyvale ($10/year).
    Sunnyvale is a pro-business city, so if you have any questions
    about your business you can call City Hall and they will be
    happy to answer any questions you have.

    Now that Sunnyvale Public Safety is increasing its
    enforcement efforts of parking regulations, if you do run a
    business from your home, you may want to know about
    Municipal Code SMC 10.16.160. It says:
    
    "It is unlawful for any person to park any commercial
    vehicle (other than a pick-up truck, station wagon, or similar
    vehicle of a size similar to an ordinary passenger vehicle) more
    than five hours in any residential district except:
    
    (a) While loading or unloading property and time in
    addition to such five-hour period is necessary to complete such

    such five-hour period is reasonably necessary to complete such
    service. (Ord. 2435-93-1)."

    Another difficulty is that the Roads Commission and the
    County Supervisors hold their meetings DURING BUSINESS
    HOURS. Working people will have to make a special sacrifice
    if they wish to attend these meetings and give input. It will cost
    me money, but you can rest assured that I will be at both
    meetings. If you are an interested resident who does not have

    the meetings. You will be doing your neighbors a great service.
    
    I asked Ray about the chances of the Wildwood project
    winning County approval. Ray said that it was ‘touch and go’ at
    this point, but that a significant show of neighborhood support
    could make the difference. We need to show the commissioners
    and supervisors that the impending problems on Sandia are not
    just numbers on paper. The problems will affect real people,
    real families with children. The Wildwood intersection is a
    compromise between competing needs. We need to tell the
    powers-that-be that the Wildwood intersection, imperfect as it
    is, is the best solution for both the neighborhood and for the
    drivers on Lawrence Expressway. If nothing is done, the result
    would be disastrous.
    
    Please come to the meeting on the 9th, to hear Ray's
    presentation and to help us plan our strategy. Call me at
    747-1454 if you want to help. If you can't attend one of the
    County hearings, please write to the commissioners and/or
    supervisors. If you wish, you can send your letters to me and I
    will see that they are delivered.
    
    I'm optimistic about the chances of our success. I think we
    have a strong case to make, and the Supervisors will go along
    with us. If they do, we still won't be out of the woods: CalTrans
    has yet to give its approval to the project. We need to do this in
    a step-by-step fashion: first the County, then the State agency.
    We mustn't give up--I think it is fair to say that the future of the
    Fairwood side of the neighborhood is at stake. If we work
    together with Ray and the City Traffic Engineering Department,
    I think we stand a good chance of seeing this thing through.
    Lets resolve to do what we can to keep our neighborhood a
    place where ordinary people can live and raise their families in
    peace, where they don't have to worry about their kids being hit
    by speeding cars on the street. That's worth working for, isn't
    it? I'll be doing my part, and I'm sure all of you will, too.
