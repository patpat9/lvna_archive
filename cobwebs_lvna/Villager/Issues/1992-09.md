    Register to Vote Now
    
    Californians Must Register by October 5 to Vote in
    November Election
    
    As you probably know, a major election is coming up on
    November 3. This election is especially important because we
    have the chance to vote not just for a president, but for both Cali-
    fornia senators, as well as a U.S. congressman, a state senator,
    and a state representative.
    
    Voting protects your interests in two ways. First, you get the
    chance to choose the men and women who will represent your
    needs to the federal and state governement. Second, if a neigh-
    borhood like Lakewood has high voter turnout, we have more
    clout when we lobby an elected official, or ask him or her to
    come speak to our association.
    
    Although it sometimes seems like a single vote could not
    possibly make a difference, many elections throughout history
    have been won by just one vote per precinct. That means that
    every U.S. citizen over the age of 18 can make a difference just
    by exercising the right to vote. We should remember that voting
    is not just a right, but a privilege which all our ancestors sacri-
    ficed for, no matter where they came from.
    
    Voter registration forms can be picked up at the library or
    post office, or at the next L.V.N.A. meeting. You can also call
    Lakewood resident Linda Cheung, at 734-1661, and have one
    brought to your home. The forms take only a minute or two to
    fill out, and they are already addressed and stamped.
    
    See the other side of this newsletter for a list of candidates
    for state and national offices. |
    
    Misprint in Local Newspaper
    Valley Journal Misrepresents Information About L.V.N.A.
    
    People tend to believe much of what they read, especially in
    teputable newspapers. Unfortunately, even the best newspapers
    can make mistakes, misquote information, or misprint facts.
    Such was the case in the August 12 Valley Journal.
    
    Last month, the Valley Journal printed that the L.V.N.A.
    had spent $1200 on the August 1 teen B.B.Q. The officers of the
    L.V.N.A. wish to assure our members that the B.B.Q. expenses
    came in well under budget, at less than $250. In addition, we
    would encourage our readers to call Vicki Piazza at 730-8274 or
    any other L.V.N.A. board member to verify facts any time you
    tread something in a local paper which concerns or worries you
    as a resident of Lakewood. |_|

    Letter from the President
    
    As the L.V.N.A. nears the end of its first
    year, I would like to take the time to share
    with you some of our many accomplishments. For
    those of you that are new, I hope to explain
    what we do and how you can make a difference.
    
    When we first started, a survey revealed that
    neighborhood appearance and parking problems
    were key issues for residents of Lakewood vil-
    lage. We met with the city about these con-
    cerns, and the Partners Program came about.
    
    What an improvement everyone has made: houses
    painted, lawns mowed, pride in the community.
    I'm sure you have heard as well as I've heard
    the many compliments that residents and visi-
    tors have made.
    
    Partners is coming to a close, so now we need
    to move into a more proactive mode. We cannot
    afford to lose what we have gained and there
    is still a lot to do. New issues face our com-
    munity all the time, and we need to be pre-
    
    pared.
    
    Together we can build for a better future. I
    would like to see more of you get involved in
    the neighborhood association, even become
    block captains. One of the best ways to get
    involved is still to come to our meetings.
    
    Vicki Piazza, L.V.N.A. President
