    - Recap of “Overcoming
    Bigotry: A Sunnyvale Unity
    Event” -
    On Thursday, March 16, “Overcoming Bigotry: A Sunnyvale Unity Event” was held at Lakewood
    Elementary School. The event was hosted by the City of Sunnyvale and Supervisor Otto Lee, in
    collaboration with several partner organizations, including LVNA, Sunnyvale School District
    (SSD), Fremont Union High School District (FUHSD), and the Islamic Networks Group (ING).
    Over 70 people attended the event, including Sunnyvale Mayor Klein, our City Council
    representative Omar Din, and representatives from LVNA, SSD, and FUHSD. The meeting
    provided attendees with the chance to examine bigotry and explore solutions to strengthen our
    diverse community.
    The event opened with music from Triple Illusion, a student band from Fremont High School.
    After introductions, the meeting moved to a panel discussion on how stereotypes impact
    members of minority groups. The meeting also included time for smaller breakout groups
    within the audience, where community members shared personal experiences of bigotry and
    shared ideas on how to counter bigotry in our community. Here are some highlights:
    -
    Implicit bias is defined as the adoption of stereotypes (good and bad) about an entire
    group of people. These biases can be conscious or subconscious. Subconscious bias is so
    powerful that it can override our conscious values, especially in times of fear or stress
    -
    Even within minority groups, there is substantial diversity. For example, there are 2
    billion Muslims on Earths, covering a variety of ethnicities and histories. The word
    “Asian” is used to cover 48 different Asian countries. In other words, minorities are not
    the monolithic groups that stereotypes make them out to be.
    -
    Bigotry and hate can happen anywhere, including in the Bay Area. In March of this year,
    drawings of swastikas were written on the whiteboard outside a Jewish student’s
    dormitory room. From March 2020 to March 2021, roughy 11,500 anti-Asian hate crimes
    were reported to stopaapihate.org - and about 370 of those took place in Santa Clara
    County
    -
    Panelist Dorothy Hwang talked about how, with the increase in anti-Asian hate crimes,
    she was “afraid to leave my home and go to the grocery store”.
    -
    Panelist Shannon Rivers, a member of the Akimel O’otham (River People) Nation,
    reminder the audience that “we can’t keep hating each other…we need each other…you
    are a human being first.”
    -
    Panelist Karla Alvarez talked about the effects of systemic racism on our own community.
    For example, the top 3 zip codes in San Jose that were most impacted by covid were all
    in East San Jose, which are predominantly Latino/Latina communities. These inequities

    - Sunnyvale Equity in Education:
    Advocating for High School Students in North Sunnyvale -
    As you may know, most Sunnyvale public high school students attend high school as part of
    Fremont Union High School District (FUHSD). All of the FUHSD high schools are located south
    of El Camino Real. The only public high school in Sunnyvale that was north of El Camino Real,
    Sunnyvale High School, closed in 1981. A group of parents joined together last year to
    advocate for equitable school access in the FUHSD. The group, Sunnyvale Equity in Education
    (SEE), is advocating for our students in the short-term, mid-term, and long-term. SEE’s goals
    are:
    ●
    Short-term: Understand the burdens faced by students and families in North Sunnyvale
    since the closure of Sunnyvale High (survey and this listserve are starting point);
    advocate for transportation solutions that address those burdens (Sunnyvale city council
    study issue on shuttle & carpool options for FHS families)
    ●
    Mid-term: Elect a representative from North Sunnyvale to the Fremont Unified High
    School District Board
    ●
    Long-term: Advocate for bringing high school back to North Sunnyvale
    They are advocating at City Council meetings, FUHSD meetings, and more. To raise our voice,
    they need your help, especially from minority families and families who are not native English
    speakers. For more information, please see their Facebook page here
    (https://www.facebook.com/profile.php?id=100089514400981) or email
    sunnyvaleequityineducation@gmail.com
    For anyone who is a Fremont High School family, SEE is asking you to fill out a survey to better
    understand transportation options and needs from North Sunnyvale to Fremont High. Please fill
    out the survey here:
    ●
    Survey in English: Transportation to Fremont High School:
    https://docs.google.com/forms/d/e/1FAIpQLScYjh0L0Kr1mBB1W0yEn0FX_feY1PidKLpra
