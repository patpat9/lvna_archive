    New L.V.N.A. Board Takes Office
    
    The L.V.N.A. elections were held at the March meeting of
    the neighborhood association. The winners are as follows:
    
    President: Jessica Lee

    Secretary: Mary Magnie
    Treasurer: Bob Lacy
    
    All winners accepted the positions and have taken over the .
    
    responsibilities of their offices. However, Mary Magnie has
    since resigned due to personal obligations.
    
    In addition to the board listed above, three other residents
    have volunteered as "backup members." They are Judy Gauthier,
    Bill Kohrt, and Cliff Arnett. Former president Vicki Piazza will
    continue to be available as an advisor to the new board.

    board. She served as treasurer on last year's board. Jessica has
    one child at Lakewood Elementary and has done much work in a
    variety of not-for-profit organizations aimed at furthering com-
    munity involvement in government and community benefits.
    
    Debbie Bakker has lived in Lakewood for five years. Deb-
    bie has three preschool-aged children and works as an education-
    al consultant with elementary school reading programs. She has
    also worked as an L.V.N.A. volunteer, serving as block captain,
    walking flyers, serving on the parade committee, and more.
    
    Bob Lacy is originally from West Texas, but has been living
    in Lakewood for twenty years. Bob has four children and works
    as a microwave engineer. He enjoys gardening and sport events
    and doing Christian work with his church. |

    Lakewood Can Save Thousands of $$
    by Paul Schaefer, 734-1680
    
    The March meeting of the Lakewood Village Neighborhood
    Association featured Santa Clara Valley Water District Senior
    Civil Engineer, Timmy K. Yung. Yung described the flood con-
    trol project that will be built on Calabazas Creck (the eastern
    border of Lakewood Village), tentatively starting in July, 1993
    with an estimated completion in the fall of 1994,
    
    Flood control? In Lakewood Village? Thousands of dollars
    saved? (Answers to follow.)
    
    But first, this advice: Spend an hour a month at the meetings
    of the L.V.N.A. Present issues which you would like to learn
    more about to the L.V.N.A. board. They will try to schedule an
    expert at an upcoming meeting to address your concern.
    
    And now, the answers: There is a "1%" flood plain along
    Calabazas Creek, which translates to the statistical chance of the
    water overflowing the banks once every one hundred years. With
    the new flood control project, there will be even less of a statisti-
    cal chance. Regarding dollars saved, F.E.M.A. suggests that
    flood insurance will be unnecessary in Lakewood once the statis-
    tical chance of flooding goes below 1%. |
    
    Sunnyvale School Chief Fired
    
    As reported in the San Jose Mercury News on March 30, the
    trustees of the Sunnyvale School District voted to fire Gary
    Mills last month by a vote of 3 to 2. Mills has served as Superin-
    tendent of Schools for five years.
    
    Many teachers in the district feel that the board had no rea-
    son to fire Mills, and that this decision will cost the school dis-
    trict money. However, in a letter to Mills, the trustees were able
    to list 26 reasons why they were taking up the dismissal action.
    
    Mills has already left his position. At this time, no replace-
    ment has been announced. Ll

    Registration will be held from Monday, April 5, through Friday, April 16, 1993 during regular
    school hours for ail children eligible to enter kindergarten in September, 1993, and for those
    who have not been enrolled in kindergarten this school year, but who will be eligible to enter
    first grade in September, 1993.
    
    Some of our schools are planning to set up a special day for this purpose during which time
    all parents of these particular schools will be notified and invited to register their youngsters.
    In the majority of the schools, however, registration will be during the weeks listed above.
    
    During the month of August, all registrations of new children at all grade levels will take
    place at the Administrative Service Center, 830 W. McKinley Avenue, Sunnyvale, CA
    94086.
    
    The state legal age requirements are as follows:
    
    To be eligible for kindergarten in September, 1993, a chiid must have been born
    on or before December 2, 1988.
    
    To be eligible for first grade in September, 1993, a child must have been born on
    or before December 2, 1987.
    
    California law requires that all children enrolling in public schools have verification of
    immunization against polio, measies, diphtheria, whooping cough, tetanus, mumps,
    tuberculosis, and rubella (German measles). Exemptions permitted by law are for reasons
    of health or belief. A child may be exempted from the mumps and rubella immunization if
    a physician provides the school with a written statement that the child has had laboratory
    confirmed diseases.
    
    It will not be necessary to bring the youngster to schoo! in order to register him/her. If a
    parent is not able to register the child, a relative or friend may do so, providing he/sheis able
    to furnish the necessary information.
    
    Children presently enrolled in kindergarten in the Sunnyvale School District will automati-
    cally be registered in first grade.
