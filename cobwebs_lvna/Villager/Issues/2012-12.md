    The City of Sunnyvale and Specialty 
    Solid Waste & Recycling offer holiday 
    tree recycling from December 31, 2012 to 
    January 18, 2013. Specialty Waste makes 
    as easy as 1-2-3!    
     
    1. Remove all decorations, light strands 
    and tree stand 
    2. Cut tree into 4 foot or shorter lengths 
    3. Place tree near your trash bins  
     
    That’s all there is to it! 
     
     
    Specialty Solid Waste & Recycling is committed to 
    “going green”. They joined Sustainable Silicon 
    Valley in 2005. Their goal was to reduce carbon 
    dioxide (CO2) emissions 20% by the year 2010. They 
    are currently at 22% and have recently 
    installed waterless urinals, timers on 
    our lights, motion detector lights, and 
    Low-E windows with argon gas. They 
    have 37 trucks operating on 
    Compressed Natural Gas (CNG) and 
    the remainder of their diesel fleet 
    operates on 20% bio fuel.

    Also Collected at Curbside: 
     
     
    Used motor oil - pour into the jug, provided by 
    Specialty, or a non-breakable one-gallon container with a 
    screw top lid (e.g. rinsed plastic detergent container). 
     
     
    Oil filters - after completely draining excess oil from 
    filter into an oil pan, place filter in a heavy-duty zip-lock 
    plastic bag, seal the bag, and place the sealed bag at the 
    curbside on your next recycling day.  When the filter is 
    collected, a replacement bag will be provided.   
     
    The city provides one-gallon jugs for used motor oil, and 
    heavy-duty zip-lock bags for used motor oil filters. 
    Customers may use paper bags for excess recyclables 
    except oil and oil filters. To order oil jugs or filter bags call 
    the Sunnyvale Revenue Division at (408) 730-7400 (Limit: 
    one cart per address). 
     
    Corrugated cardboard - tie or 
    tape into bundles 30” x 30” x 6” or 
    smaller. 
     
    Paper grocery bags - flatten 
    bags and put inside another paper 
    grocery bag or bundle 
    with cardboard. White bags are OK.  
     
    Household batteries - 
    rechargeable batteries from cell 
    phones, laptops and cordless 
    power tools, AA, AAA, C, D, 9-
    volts, and button batteries (such as those found in 
    hearing aids). Tape battery terminals and put batteries 
    in a tightly sealed, clear-plastic bag and place the 
    bag on top of your recycling split cart.

    Holiday Tips From Sunnyvale 
    Public Safety 
     
    The Sunnyvale Department of Public 
    Safety is proud to serve you each and 
    every day. During the Holiday Season our 
    officers are especially vigilant to ensure this time of year is 
    safe and joyous. Unfortunately, the Holidays do attract 
    those to our neighborhoods that are intent on committing 
    thefts.  
     
    Here are helpful tips you and your family can use now and 
    throughout the year: 
     
       Lock ALL doors and windows and use a secondary                
    blocking device on those that slide open (i.e. small 
    wooden dowel rod).  
     
    Close and lock your garage door.  
     
    Lock all gates leading to the rear or side area of your 
    residence.  
     
    Secure all home tools inside your residence or 
    garage. 
     
    Download tracking applications on electronic devices. 
     
    Get to know your neighbors.  
     
    Invest in a home security system with an audible 
    alarm.  
     
    Photograph valuables and record serial numbers. 
     
    If you observe any suspicious people, vehicles, or activity 
    in your neighborhood, please contact Public Safety 
    immediately by dialing 911. Our dispatchers are standing 
    by to immediately send an officer to you.

    contact our Community Service Bureau at (408) 730-7140.

    Foam Food Container Facts: 
     
    Foam food containers are made from 
    non-renewable petrochemical 
    resources and typically end up in 
    landfills or as litter. While it can be 
    recycled, the recycling market is 
    diminishing. 
     
    Styrene is a suspected human carcinogen There is 
    evidence that it leaches into the hot food placed in it. 
     
    Bay Area jurisdictions have been tasked by regional 
    water quality officials with reducing the amount of foam 
    food containers and other trash pollutants in local 
    waterways. 
     
    Over 50 jurisdictions in California (20 in the Bay Area) 
    have already banned foam food take-out containers. 
     
     Less than one half of one percent (0.005) of foam food 
    containers used for food service are actually recycled. 
     
    Out of concern for their customers and nature, many 
    food vendors have already switched to high-quality, 
    affordable and readily available alternatives. 
     
    Perhaps it is time for our city to join others in banning 
    “food foam.”  
    City to Consider Foam Food 
    Container Ordinance  
     
    The City of Sunnyvale is considering an 
    ordinance that would make it unlawful for 
    food service vendors to distribute foam 
    food or beverage take-out containers.  
     
    Foam food containers are also known as, 
    "Styrofoam," and "Expanded Polystyrene 
    Foam (EPS). “Food foam can take hundreds of years to 
    biodegrade. It quickly breaks down into small pieces that 
    are lightweight and easily blows into our  waterways.  
     
    Unfortunately birds, fish and other wildlife mistake these 
    small bits for food. The foam can get lodged in their 
    stomachs, thus harming or killing them. 
     
    Examples of products that would be prohibited include: 
    plates, cups, bowls, lids, trays, hinged 
    containers and lidded containers. The 
    ordinance would likely exempt pre-
    packaged foods, such as eggs and 
    meat sold in retail stores. Take-out 
    containers still allowed for use would 
    include non-foam plastics, aluminum, 
    paper and containers that are 
    compostable. 
     
    Out of concern for their customers and nature, many food 
    vendors have already switched to high-quality, affordable 
    and readily available alternatives.
