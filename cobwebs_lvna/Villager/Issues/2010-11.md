    A New Look For The Newsletter 
    Greetings neighbors!  As you read          The Villager  this 
    time you may notice something different 
    about it.  After many years of existence as a 
    strictly black and white publication, our 
    neighborhood    newsletter has entered the 
    world of color!  We have color advertise-
    ments, color clip art, even color pictures!  It 
    is also bigger!  Instead of six pages we now 
    have eight!  We hope you like the new look 
    for           The Villager and that these chang-
    es will make it more       interesting, informative and useful to 
    read. 
    There is a story about how these changes came about.  It 
    involves a gutsy decision by the LVNA Board and some   
    generous help from the City of Sunnyvale.  You may think 
    that these changes are adding greatly to the costs of the 
    newsletter, but you would be wrong.  Making these changes 
    is actually saving us considerable money!  In fact, thanks to a 
    grant from the City of Sunnyvale we'll be able to publish our 
    improved and expanded newsletter virtually free for the next 
    12 months!  There is a great deal to explain, so let's get start-
    ed. 
    BEST COPY BEST PRINTING 
    For many years the LVNA has been using a fine local      
    company called “Best Copy Best Printing” to print and fold the 
    newsletter.  Best Copy has always given us a good price and 
    prompt service, but even with generous pricing, it has       
    become more and more difficult for us to pay their bill.  Once 
    per month the newsletter editor composes The Villager and 
    gives proofs to Best Copy.  Best Copy then runs the proofs 
    through their high-speed 11 x 17 inch copier and folds the 
    pages together with their folding machine. They produce 
    1,700 copies for us at a cost of about $420 per run.  $420 per 
    month adds up to more than $5,000 per year.  Publishing the 
    newsletter   (continued on page 3) 
    largest financial commitment made by the LVNA.

    A Talk With Chief Don Johnson 
    -Fred Fowler 
    As the days get shorter and the nights get longer our 
    thoughts turn to the holidays.  Dark cold nights can bring the 
    warmth of fellowship, but they can also bring less desirable 
    things.  Residential crime can increase, as well as the 
    danger of fire.  Bad people like the dark, and holiday warmth 
    can ignite our old wooden homes all too easily.  It is times 
    like these when we can appreciate especially well the work 
    of Sunnyvale's fine Public Safety department. 
    Do you have questions about our police force or our fire 
    fighting capabilities?  If so, then come to the Lakewood Park 
    building at 7:00pm on Wednesday, November 10th, to enjoy 
    a question and answer session with the LVNA's special 
    guest, Sunnyvale Public Safety Chief Don Johnson.  
    Chief Don will be joined by Lakewood Village's 
    Neighborhood Resource Officer, Jim Davis.  Together they 
    will be able to answer your questions and hear your 
    concerns about burglaries, the recent stand-off on 
    Velvetlake Drive, the recent reductions in public safety 
    staffing and the overall safety “climate” of the city.  It should 
    be a very interesting discussion. 
    And don't go away when the questions are over.  After we 
    hear from the Chief it will be time for final preparations for 
    the 2010 Holiday Parade!  November's meeting will be the 
    last one we have before the 
    parade steps off on December 4th!  
    Come help out with planning, 
    organizing the participants, the 
    food and drink at the end of the 
    route, and any number of important 
    details.  Our neighbor Wendy 
    Stanley is coordinating the event, 
    so please come prepared to pitch 
    in and help! 
    I'll see you there! 

    To pay for the printing costs of the       
    Villager, the LVNA traditionally has 
    sold advertising space in the         
    publication.  We've developed several 
    long-standing relationships with local advertisers who      
    together provide just enough money to print the copies we 
    distribute each month.  We pay a price for producing the 
    Villager this way.  In order to  generate enough advertising 
    income to pay for an issue of the newsletter we have to   
    devote almost half of its space to advertisements.  To pay 
    for a 6 page newsletter we have to set aside almost 3 pages 
    for ads.  That means that everything else – announcements, 
    calendars, news, etc., has to fit in the space left over.  There 
    is never enough space for all of the things that should be in 
    The Villager. 
    THE SUNNYVALE COMMUNITY GRANT PROGRAM 
    Back this Spring, the LVNA board pondered this situation 
    and decided to try to do something about it.  The City of 
    Sunnyvale had announced a grant program to support 
    “community building” efforts within the city, and the LVNA 
    Board decided that a newsletter improvement project might 
    be a good candidate for help.  The board reasoned that   
    The Villager. is a “community building” tool if there every 
    was one, and that with the City's help we might find a more    
    practical way to produce it.  The most obvious way to      
    improve The Villager. is to increase the amount of space in   
    it for community information.  To do so, (cont. next Column) 
    change The Villager. to make it less dependent on           
    advertising revenue.  The best way to improve The Villager. 
    (cont.) we would have to change The Villager to make it less 
    dependent on advertising revenue. The best way to improve 
    The Villager, would be to produce it in a way that did not cost 
    $420 per month. If we could find a way to reduce the costs then 
    the advertising space problem would solve itself. 
    The plan we hit on was a daring one. The reason we’ve used 
    Best Copy to produce the newsletter is that they have the 
    equipment needed to do the job. They have a high-capacity 
    printer and a folding machine that can create the pages and put 
    them into a finished form without much trouble. Their turna-
    round time is normally about two days, which means that the 
    job of printing and folding probably takes 4-6 hours or so. Their 
    investment in these two pieces of equipment puts them in the 
    newsletter printing business and has earned them thousands of 
    dollars each year. 
    What if WE got into the “newsletter printing business”? 
    A PRINTER AND A FOLDING MACHINE 
    Last Spring the LVNA board decided to investigate the possibil-
    ity of working with the City of Sunnyvale to find a way to print 
    and fold the newsletter ourselves.  We would need some kind 
    of printer, some kind of folding machine, and money for toner 
    and paper.  Based on this idea, the LVNA applied for a 
    Sunnyvale “community building grant”. 
    After the grant application process had run its course the City of 
    Sunnyvale offered the LVNA a bit of a challenge.  If the LVNA 
    would purchase the equipment needed, the City of Sunnyvale 
    would make $1,000 available to reimburse the LVNA for toner 
    and paper costs.  This money would be available for the 2010-
    2011 fiscal year, and if we kept good records and used the 
    money well there would be the possibility of applying for further 
    monies the following year.  In other words, if the LVNA came up 
    with the money to make a “capital investment” in printing equip-
    ment, the City of Sunnyvale would pick up the tab for the 
    monthly expenses required to produce the newsletter.  After we 
    paid for the equipment, our monthly newsletter expenses would 
    go from $420 to zero! 
    How much would the printer and the folding machine cost?  A 
    few hours searching the internet gave the answer.  Our news-
    letter is printed on 11 x 17 inch paper, folded in half to produce 
    an 8.5 x 11 inch “magazine” format document.  It turns out that 
    refurbished printers capable of handling that size paper are 
    available for roughly $500. (continued on  page 4)

    Newsletter Walkers Needed! 
    By Fred Fowler 
    Hello Lakewood Village.  Did you know that the 
    newsletter you are reading was delivered by one of 
    your neighbors?  There has been a  long tradition 
    of volunteerism in our neighborhood that produces 
    all of the things we enjoy here.  Dumpster Day, the 
    LVNA picnic, the Holiday Parade and the Holiday 
    Lighting Contest are all made possible by volunteer 
    efforts.  The Villager works the same way.  It is written, 
    published and distributed by volunteers. 
    My wife Phyllis and I are volunteer “newsletter walkers”.  
    Every month we take a stack of newsletter copies and 
    spend an enjoyable hour walking around our street and 
    putting them in our neighbors' doors. We used to spend 
    only 30 minutes at the chore when we only had about 60 
    houses to cover.  Recently we asked for more addresses, 
    and now we do 110 deliveries.  Each hour is precious in 
    Silicon Valley, of course, but we find we enjoy the fresh 
    air, the exercise, and the conversations we have with 
    people we meet.  It's fun! 
    Most of our newsletter walkers have been doing it happily 
    for many years now, but once in a while someone has to 
    drop out.  Recently we lost a few long time walkers and  
     
     
    have had two routes on the Fairwood side open 
    up.  Many thanks to Bobbie Lacy for her long 
    service walking the various courts along 
    Palamos Avenue!  Special thanks to Willy and 
    Carol Rodriques who walked several courts 
    along Sandia and Fairwood!  Those people 
    served their neighbors well, and now it is time for 
    them to enjoy a well earned rest.  It is time for 
    some new people to step forward to take their places. 
    Do you enjoy reading The Villager?  Do you have 30 
    minutes or so to spare once per month?  Do you live on the 
    Fairwood side of the neighborhood?  If so, please contact 
    Leah Lane, who is the volunteer coordinator for the 
    Fairwood side newsletter walkers.  She can be reached via 
    e-mail at leahrlane@aol.com.  If you live on the Lakewood 
    side, call Wendy Stanley at (408) 745-1331. Please contact 
    them if you are willing to give up 30 minutes a month for the 
    sake of your neighbors. 
    Phyllis and I find walking our route to be one of the simplest 
    and most enjoyable volunteer activities we do.  As I said, it's 
    fun!  Please contact Leah and tell her you want to give it a 
    try!  

    Editor’s Corner 
     
    By Phyllis Fowler 
     
    Now that Thanksgiving is almost here I thought I would 
    share one of my favorite recipes.  A few years Fred and I 
    had my family over for Thanksgiving.  With all the cooking to 
    do I wanted a simple side dish that I could put together 
    quickly and easily.  I found this southern style recipe on 
    foodnetwork.com  by Paula Deen. It was a huge hit, 
    especially with the guys!  (We don’t need to tell them it was 
    so easy, just take the praise and let them feel spoiled!) I will 
    warn you that this is rich!  I left out the Cheddar cheese and 
    reduced the butter to a third of a cup.  I also use fat free sour 
    cream, to help bring down the calories.  The guys never 
    noticed and there rarely are leftovers.  However, if you do 
    have leftovers, it heats up nicely in the microwave the next 
    day.  I hope you enjoy your holiday! 
     
    Corn Casserole 
    Recipe courtesy Paula Deen 
     
    Prep Time: 10 minutes  
     
     
    Cook Time: 55 min 
    Level: Easy   
    Serves 6 to 8 servings 
     
    Ingredients 
    • 
    1 (15 1/4-ounce) can whole kernel corn, drained 
    • 
    1 (14 3/4-ounce) can cream-style corn 
    • 
    1 (8-ounce) package corn muffin mix  
     
    (recommended: Jiffy) 
    • 
    1 cup sour cream 
    • 
    1/2 cup (1 stick) butter, melted 
     
    1 to 1 1/2 cups shredded Cheddar 
    Directions 
    Preheat oven to 350 degrees F.  
    In a large bowl, stir together the 2 cans of corn, corn muffin 
    mix, sour cream, and melted butter. Pour into a greased 9 
    by 13-inch casserole dish. Bake for 45 minutes, or until 
    golden brown. Remove from oven and top with Cheddar. 
    Return to oven for 5 to 10 minutes, or until cheese is melted. 
    Let stand for at least 5 minutes and then serve warm.

    Letter to the Editor 
    Good morning, 
     
    I'd like to introduce myself, what I do, and my experience in 
    Lakewood Village. My name is Rick Collette, my family and 
    I live on Fairwood, and have been here for a year and a 
    half.  When we moved here (we moved out of CA, then 
    back), we were planning on looking around Mountain View 
    for a home to buy.  Things have changed a bit.  I opened 
    my garage to a small group of my kids friends and started 
    teaching them a Hawaiian Martial Art called Kajukenbo.  
    Apparently word gets around, and I now have 7 kids, 
    including 2 of my own, who show up every afternoon, Mon-
    Thurs at 5:30 to learn "karate".  As a side; I did take out 
    special insurance to cover this, and I don't charge - its 
    donation only. Since doing this, I developed a bit of an 
    attachment to the neighborhood, and we are switching our 
    house hunting to the Lakewood Village area.  We read the 
    newsletter when it comes, and my wife thought maybe I 
    should shoot you an e-mail to say thanks. 
     
    We do appreciate the information it contains, and look 
    forward to it each time.  Thank you for the work that goes 
    into this. 
     
    Rick Collette
