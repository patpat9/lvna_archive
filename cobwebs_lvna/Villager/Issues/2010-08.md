    August Meeting - Flood Control 
     
    Greetings neighbors.  One of the problems 
    we face in our little corner of Sunnyvale is 
    flooding caused by storm water runoff.  
    Back in 1997 when I was first elected to the Sunnyvale City 
    Council the neighborhood's streets flooded several times   
    because of large rain storms in the Santa Cruz mountains.  
    Water would flow down the Calabazas Creek and the    
    Sunnyvale East and West channels and would overflow their 
    banks.  Water would also come up through the storm drains 
    because of defective “flap gate” valves linking those drains to 
    the flood control channels 
    When I joined the City Council, one of my first inter-
    governmental assignments was to sit on a “flood control    
    advisory committee” for the Santa Clara Valley Water      
    District.  I spent almost 8 years on that committee and ended 
    up becoming its chairman before I left public office.  During 
    that time I pressed hard for solutions to the flooding problems 
    of our neighborhood and of other neighborhoods like ours. 
    One of the solutions was to find a way to pay for a great deal 
    of “deferred maintenance” on the flood control system.  In 
    2000, the Water District board of directors placed “Measure  
    B” on the November ballot.  It was a bond measure designed 
    to finance a large number of projects to revamp and          
    modernize a great deal of the flood control infrastructure in our 
    valley.  Measure B was put before the voters and required a 
    2/3 majority to be passed.  It passed the 2/3 threshold by less 
    than 200 votes!  It was a narrow victory, but it was enough and 
    work commenced on the projects the following Spring. 
    Two of the projects on the list were ones that directly affect 
    our neighborhood.  Most of the problems with the Calabazas 
    Creek were fixed some years ago without need to use the 
    bond money,  North Sunnyvale still had 
    two problem areas, though.  One is the 
    Sunnyvale West Channel which flows 
    through the “Orchard Gardens” neighbor-
    hood west of us.  The other is the 
    Sunnyvale East Channel, which forms the 
    western border of Lakewood Village.

    Dumpster Day, Picnic, and Parade 
    -Fred Fowler 
    Hello neighbors.  The nice Summer weather is finally here and the 
    sun seems to be shining brightly on our neighborhood.  It is shining 
    brightly on the LVNA as well!  After a few years of declining activity 
    the LVNA seems to be “firing on all cylinders”.  We started off with a 
    record breaking parade last December and followed up with a highly 
    successful Dumpster Day last month.  We're catching our breath a bit 
    in August, but come September it will be time to stage our annual 
    picnic.  Not long after that we'll be in “parade mode” again.  They say 
    time flies when you are having fun, and this LVNA year is flying by! 
     
    DUMPSTER DAY 
    July 17th was Dumpster Day, and once again 
    everything went very smoothly.  Specialty 
    Waste delivered all of the debris boxes on 
    Friday and by Saturday afternoon we had 
    cleaned more than 100 tons of trash, refuse 
    and junk out of the neighborhood.  There 
    were a few glitches (such as the breakdown 
    of one of Specialty Waste's trucks), but overall it was another event 
    for the record books. 
    There were lots of volunteers involved and there isn't room to thank 
    them all here.  I would like to give some special recognition to a few 
    who stand out in my mind. 
    The first I want to thank are the friends and 
    family of Jim and Helen Lane.  Jim was 
    one of the mainstays of Dumpster Day for 
    many years and took on the role of 
    organizing the entire event more than 
    once.  Unfortunately Jim is no longer with 
    us.  The neighborhood lost Jim sometime 
    back, and it felt very strange for me to 
    work through a Dumpster Day without him.  He presence was felt 
    though, at the dumpster that was delivered to its usual place in 
    front of the Lane's house.  Helen Lane, her daughter and their 
    neighbors took care of it and made sure it was available for the 
    people in the southeast corner of Lakewood Village.  Many thanks 
    to them for keeping up the tradition. 
    The next set of volunteers I want to thank are Sarah Daugherty 
    and her daughter.  Together they took care of two dumpsters in 
    front of Sarah's home           (Continued on page 2)

    The reason I'm telling you all of this is that 
    after ten years of waiting, work on the     
    Sunnyvale East and Sunnyvale West channels 
    is about to begin!  It will cause some disruption 
    to our neighborhood when the big earth moving equipment 
    starts to work, but when the dust settles Lakewood Village will 
    be much safer from storm caused flooding. 
    The Santa Clara Valley Water District wants to make a   
    presentation to us about the upcoming projects so that we'll 
    know what to expect.  They want to apologize in advance for 
    the noise, dust, and other disrupting things we'll be exposed to.  
    They want to explain the benefits the revamped channels will 
    bring to Lakewood Village in terms of safety ad security.  
    They've requested time during our August meeting to make  
    the presentation. 
    Please come to the Lakewood Park building on Wednesday, 
    August 11th at 7:00pm to hear the Santa Clara Valley Water 
    District presentation on the upcoming flood control projects.  
    Bring your questions with you!  There will be several           
    representatives of the Water District there to make sure all of 
    them get answered.

    PICNIC TIME 
    Now that Dumpster Day is over it is time to start serious           prep-
    arations for our annual picnic.  We've set the date (September 25th) 
    and we've reserved the picnic area.  All we need to do now is get 
    the food together and figure out how to cook it. In years past the 
    LVNA has gone to various businesses and asked them to donate 
    the food we've needed.  We found many of them to be very        
    generous and have fed up to 300 people at our picnics   entirely with 
    donated food.  The Lion and Compass restaurant on Fair Oaks 
    once donated all the hamburger we used (35 pounds of it), an New 
    York Style Italian Sausage Company 
    off Tasman Avenue donated a whole 
    case of delicious sausage, and Coco's 
    on Lawrence and Oakmead donated half a 
    dozen of their pies.  Armadillo Willy's used to 
    give us a selection of meats and side dishes.  
    Black Angus gave us gift certificates to raffle off 
    as door prizes.  Even some of our business 
    friends that are not in the food industry donated 
    things “pot luck” style.  Lakewood Dental (one 
    of our long time supporters and newsletter advertisers) once donated a 
    large quantity of absolutely fabulous       marinated chicken  (it was a se-
    cret family recipe).  The list goes on and on. 
    When it is at its best, the annual picnic is an exercise in making        
    “stone soup.” We ask everyone involved to bring their own contribution   
    to the feast. It doesn’t matter if the contribution is large or small.  All that 
    matters is that it is heartfelt.  It is all about neighbors working together a 
    unique and special meal that everyone can share. 
    I'm sure that the picnic will be a great success again this year.  There is 
    only one problem we have to solve.  We need to find someone to take 
    responsibility for making it happen!  As I said, we have the date and the 
    picnic area. All we need is someone to coordinate the solicitation of    
    donations, figure out where we can store it all, and then get some cooks 
    and other helpers together.  Things will all have to be set up on the    
    morning of the 25th, the food cooked and served, and then the picnic area 
    will have to be cleaned up afterward.   (Continued next column)

    (Parade Preparations   
    Continued from page 3)  
    One thing I've wanted for many years is to 
    have more than one marching band in the 
    parade.  The Fremont High School Band 
    has done a wonderful job for us for many 
    years, and the date of the parade is actually 
    set by their schedule.  The Columbia Middle 
    School Band has joined forces with them to 
    provide a bigger group and a louder sound.  
    They are the highlight of the parade, and 
    they always lead the way down Lakechime, 
    Meadowlake, Lakehaven and Sandia.  The trouble is that once 
    they've passed by the parade gets very quiet.  I would love to have 
    one band at the front of the parade and another bringing up the 
    rear.  In order to accomplish this, someone has to start contacting 
    local schools to see if we can be fit into their calendar.  The time to 
    do so is now, as once school starts it will probably be too late. 
    In 1996 our parade almost didn't take place.  No one stepped 
    forward to organize it and I wrote an article in the Villager saying 
    that perhaps the parade was no longer important enough justify all 
    of the effort.  A few days after the newsletter was published I got a 
    call from a young mother with a 5 month old child.  She said that 
    she would take on the challenge and make the parade happen 
    because she wanted her child to have the chance to see parades 
    when he grew up.  She took on the challenge and made the parade 
    a great success!  Her name was Nan Dorio, and she received a 
    special commendation from the Mayor of Sunnyvale for her efforts. 
    If Nan could make a success of the parade while caring for a new 
    baby, then why couldn't you?  What is holding you back?  Please 
    give me a call at (408) 505-2813 and let me know who you are.  
    Once again, there is no need to wonder what to do – the LVNA has 
    been staging the parade for more than 30 years and we have a 
    large crew of experiences helpers.  All you have to do is step 
    forward.  We'll show you how to do the rest...
