    Smart Meters! 
    By Fred Fowler 
    Come to the Lakewood Park Building at 
    7:00pm on Wednesday, April 14th to 
    hear representatives from Pacific Gas 
    and Electric (PG&E) give a presenta-
    tion about the Smart Meters that are 
    currently being installed in Lakewood     
    Village. 
    The “Smart Meter” program is a new 
    initiative by PG&E to bring high technology tools to bear on 
    the job of delivering electricity and gas to northern California 
    users.  PG&E is reported to be spending more that $2 billion 
    to change out all of the old “analog” meters and replace 
    them with digital ones that can be read remotely through a 
    huge wireless communication network.  PG&E says the 
    benefits of this project are to reduce its information gather-
    ing costs while greatly improving accuracy at the same time.  
    They say that another benefit is that its customers (you and 
    I) will be able to access this same data through a PG&E 
    website, where we can watch trends in our own utility us-
    age, compare   current consumption to consumption in prior 
    months or years, and ultimately to understand better how 
    we are using electricity and gas.  If we consumers are better 
    informed about the energy we are using,  we can make bet-
    ter      decisions about the energy we should use and why 
    we should use it in that way. 
    The benefits of this technology sound good, but this project 
    has stirred up controversy in other parts of California.  There 
    have been newspaper  
    reports from places like  
    Bakersfield where residents 
    have   reported skyrocketing   
    energy bills after the Smart 
    Meters were installed.  There 
    have been questions about the 
    accuracy of the meters, the 
    utility rate structure that they 
    make possible, and whether  
    This is actually a good deal for 
    California rate payers.

    LVNA Election Results 
    At the March general meeting of the LVNA, our annual 
    election for LVNA officers was held.  Voting was by   
    secret ballot, with outgoing LVNA Secretary Sarah 
    Daugherty handling the counting of votes. The following 
    people were elected to the following offices: 
    Fred Fowler was re-elected President for the 2010-
    2011 association year.  Fred is beginning his second 
    term as president, as he was most recently elected in 
    November to fill the vacant 2009-2010 president's term. 
    Joan Simon was elected 1st Vice President for the 
    2010-2011 association year.  Joan is a newcomer to the 
    LVNA leadership and is starting her first term.  We    
    welcome her and wish her good luck with her new    
    duties. 
    Skip Gottschalk was elected 2nd Vice President for the 
    2010-2011 association year.  Skip is beginning his first 
    current term on the LVNA board, but he is hardly a    
    newcomer to the LVNA.  He and Annie Gottschalk have 
    been long-time volunteers for the neighborhood associa-
    tion, and Skip has served on the board several times in 
    the past.  I want to thank Skip for “coming out of retire-
    ment” to serve the neighborhood once again. 
    Beverly Woidyla was re-elected to her third and final 
    term as  Treasurer for the 2010-2011 association year.  
    Bev has kept the books diligently and has taken the lead 
    on coordinating several LVNA events.  We're glad to 
    have her back once again. 
    There was no candidate nominated for the position of  
    LVNA board Secretary, and that position remains open.  
    At the last LVNA board meeting, Fred Fowler was     
    appointed Acting Secretary.  Fred will serve as Acting   
    Secretary until another candidate is appointed by the 
    board and confirmed by a vote of the membership. 
    All LVNA officers serve terms of one year, starting in 
    March of each year and ending in March of the following 
    year.  Individuals may serve for up to three consecutive 
    years before term limits require them to spend at least 
    one year off of the LVNA board.    

    Calling All Volunteers Who Like Bookkeeping:  
    LVNA Audit Committee is Forming 
     
     
    By Fred Fowler 
    Greetings neighbors!  The LVNA is in a rebuilding phase this 
    year, with a new President (me), a new 
    board and a “back to basics” approach 
    that (with luck) will build the LVNA 
    back into the effective advocate for the 
    neighborhood that it needs to be. 
    Part of the “back to basics” task is to 
    go back and review our association 
    Bylaws to make sure we are covering 
    all the bases that the document requires.  For those of you 
    who may be   unfamiliar with bylaws, they are the equivalent of 
    the “constitution” for organizations like the LVNA.  They lay 
    down strict requirements that the organization must follow in 
    order to act in a lawful manner.  If the LVNA organization does 
    not  comply with its bylaws, then LVNA members can sue the    
    organization for failing to act in the members' best interests.  If 
    you would like to read the LVNA bylaws, they are available on 
    the LVNA website  http://www.lvna.net. 
    The bylaws have a number of requirements in them regarding 
    the way the LVNA’s money is handled.  Every six months the 
    treasurer must prepare a written report of the revenues and  
     
     
     
     
        
    Audit Committee cont. 
    expenditures affecting the association treasury.   In addition, 
    the president must recruit and convene an “audit committee” to 
    audit the association's books periodically to make sure the 
    Treasurer is doing a good job. 
    The bylaws state that the Audit committee must consist of at 
    least three members, and that they must be appointed by the 
    President (me) and confirmed by a vote of the membership. 
    So, I would like to ask YOU if you would like to be a member of 
    the audit committee?  Here is what the audit committee would 
    have to do: 
     Spend one weekend afternoon with the treasurer every  
    6 months. 
     Use that afternoon to review the LVNA’s bank state-
    ments to make sure the checkbook is in balance. 
     Make sure that the deposits are accounted for properly 
    and that the source of the deposits are properly   doc-
    umented. 
     Make sure that the checks and/or other withdrawals 
    made represent authorized expenditures approved by 
    the LVNA board, as recorded in the LVNA board   
    minutes. 
     Compose and sign a letter to the association reporting 
    the results of the audit.

               Tennis Court Troublemakers? 
                    by Fred Fowler 
     
    Last Fall the LVNA got some reports of people taking unfair 
    advantage of  the neighborhood tennis courts.  The posted 
    rules state that players should only use the courts for an 
    hour before giving other people a chance to play.  The re-
    ports the LVNA received last Fall were about groups playing 
    all morning without giving anyone else a chance. 
    Now that the weather is nice again we may see a new out-
    break of this kind of behavior.  The Sunnyvale Parks and 
    Recreation department would like to know if anyone gets 
    affected by players like these who break the rules.  If it hap-
    pens to you, please note down the date, time of day, tennis 
    court location, and any other relevant information and for-
    ward it to me.  I'll make sure it gets to the City of Sunnyvale, 
    and I'll follow up to find out what gets done about the re-
    ports.  Please send me details via e-mail.  My e-mail address 
    is fred@fmfa.com.  Please put the words "Lakewood Tennis 
    Courts" in the subject line.   
    Thanks!  
