    - Lakewood Park Tree Walk -
    Tree #1 (London Plane)
    Sunnyvale Urban Forest Advocates (SUFA) is a local group
    dedicated to all things trees. Their mission is to educate the public
    and City officials about urban forests and their benefits, advocate
    to preserve and expand the urban forest,, and participate in tree
    maintenance and planting. As part of their mission, they have
    curated several local tree walks - short walks with accompanying
    tree guides to teach the public more about our local trees. Guided
    walks are scheduled periodically, but their website includes
    information for residents to do their own, self-guided walks as
    well. The tree walk maps can be found here
    
    (http://www.sunnyvaletrees.org/tree-walk-maps/), and the tree guides here
    (http://www.sunnyvaletrees.org/tree-guide/).
    On April 9, SUFA hosted one of these tree walks at Lakewood Park. It was nearly two hours
    long, and the guides were full of information and stories. For those who were unable to join
    - or are unable to do the self-guided tree walks, we wanted to highlight some of our local
    trees. We will begin with the first tree on the walk.
    Begin at the north side of the park building and look to the east, towards the playground
    and Silverlake Dr. See those tall, deciduous* trees lining both sides of the walkway, planted
    in straight lines? Those are our Tree #1 - the London Plane tree, Latin name Platanus x
    acerifolia. Due to its tolerance for pollution, the London Plane tree is a particularly common
    urban roadside tree and was very popular in Victorian London. Today, London Plane trees
    account for over 10% of the trees in New York City, which now restricts when the trees can
    be planted. London Plane have distinct mottled trunks. Their large leaves are similar to
    maple trees, although sycamore leaves only have three main veins while maples have five
    or seven main veins. Mature trees, which can reach a height of 70-100 feet,  have
    wonderful displays of foliage. London Plane trees are a hybrid of two other trees - the
    Western Sycamore (Platanus occidentalis) and the Oriental Sycamore (Platanus orientalis).
    The London Plane and the Western Sycamore (also called the California Sycamore) can be
    distinguished mostly by their location - London Planes are planted almost exclusively in
    urban areas, while Western Sycamores grow naturally only in wet areas.
    Want to learn more? Check out these links:
    ●
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platanus_%C3%97_acerifolia
    ●
    https://www.canadiantreetours.org/species-pages/London_plane-tree.html
    ●
    https://calscape.org/Platanus-racemosa-(Western-Sycamore)
    * A quick refresher: Deciduous trees lose their leaves in winter. Conifer, or evergreen, trees
    keep their needles year-round.
    Courtesy to Sunnyvale Forest Advocates (SUFA) for allowing us to reproduce their website
    content in our Village. For more information, see their website at
    http://www.sunnyvaletrees.org/

    - Recap of the April General Meeting -
    The Villager typically does not include a summary from the prior month’s general meeting, since those meetings
    are recorded. However, since the April general meeting was not recorded, here is a quick summary of the
    conversation with our guest speaker, Officer Chen from Sunnyvale’s Department of Public Safety.
    On unhoused residents:
    -
    Sunnyvale only has one full-time shelter to
    support our unhoused residents. Due to covid,
    bed capacity has been reduced and current
    residents are allowed to stay as long as
    needed. This severely limits the housing
    options available for our unhoused
    community.
    -
    The City Council recently approved spending
    $500,000 to support homeless resources
    -
    The 2018 decision by the US Court of Appeals
    for the Ninth Circuit in the court case “Martin
    vs Boise” declared that cities cannot enforce
    anti-camping ordinances unless the city has
    enough shelter beds to provide a bed to
    everyone in its homeless population.
    California is under the jurisdiction of the Ninth
    Circuit court, which means this ruling applies
    to our state. Therefore, since Sunnyvale does
    not have enough such beds, the City cannot
    force unhoused residents out of their camps.
    -
    As part of the renovation of the Fair Oaks
    Park, the City did provide funding for a
    six-month motel program for the 31
    individuals who lived in the encampment at
    that pak. More details about this can be found
    here:
    https://sunnyvale.ca.gov/business/projects/fa
    iroaks.htm
    On abandoned vehicles:
    -
    Vehicles in Sunnyvale must be moved every
    72 hours. To report an abandoned vehicle, you
    can call the hotline anytime at 408-730-7150,
    email them at
    vehicleabatement@sunnyvale.ca.gov, or
    submit a report online here:
    https://sunnyvale.ca.gov/government/safety/
    services/abandoned.htm
    On the Department of Public Safety (DPS):
    -
    All offices in Sunnyvale DPS are cross-trained
    in police, firefighting, and EMT work
    -
    In the mid-2000’s, they had about 210
    officers. Today they have only 202 positions
    On sideshows:
    -
    In the past, only people who were
    participating in the sideshow could be
    arrested or cited. A recent law has passed
    that allows people who are on the sidelines
    observing to now be cited or arrested
    -
    If you see a sideshow in progress, call 911
    On reporting crime:
    -
    If there is a crime happening, call 911 first
    (even before posting about it on Nextdoor!)
    -
    Be good witnesses. If it is safe to do so,
    capture video, write down the license plate,
    record the description of the vehicle (color,
    make, shape, bumper sticker,etc) and suspect
    (gender, clothing, tattoos, glasses, hair color,
    etc)
    -
    If you have valuables (bikes, computers, etc),
    know the serial number for that item. If it
    gets stolen, call the non-emergency dispatch
    at 408-730-7180 right away, so the stolen
    item can be entered into their system
    On supporting our community:
    -
    Communication and visibility is key. Meet your
    neighbors!
    -
    Join the neighborhood meetings (LVNA’s are
    at 7:00pm on the second Wednesday of every
    month)
    Other references:
    -
    The app Nixel is a good way to follow DPS.
    They also have Twitter and Facebook
    -
    Crimes are also reported on the Community
    Crime Map at
    https://communitycrimemap.com/?address=S
    unnyvale,CA

    Water Restrictions
    With limited rain during this wet season, the drought continues into its third year. The period of
    January through March was the driest one on record for both Santa Clara County and California.
    Santa Clara County gets much of its water from the Sierra Nevada, and the annual snowpack
    measurement on April 1 recorded the fifth lowest amount of snowpack on record.
    LVNA gets our water through the City of Sunnyvale’s Public Works Department, and our water
    comes mostly from the Hetch Hetchy Reservoir in Yosemite National Park. The City is effectively the
    retailer to deliver the water to homes and businesses in our community. The City gets the water
    from Valley Water, the wholesale water and groundwater management agency that supplies water
    to much of Santa Clara County.
    As a result of the continuing drought, on April 12, the Valley Water Board of Directors voted to
    include additional water restrictions. New water restrictions are:
    1. Watering of lawns and ornamental landscapes in Santa Clara County is limited to no more
    than twice per week.
    2. Watering during the warmest part of the day is prohibited. If you water, please water in the
    early morning or evening - it’s better for the plants as well.
    If you notice someone wasting water, you can either talk to them directly or you can report it
    through the Valley Water website here:
    https://www.valleywater.org/droughtsaving-water/report-water-waste
    Additional information can be found at these websites:
    -
    https://valleywaternews.org/2022/04/19/valley-water-restricts-watering-in-santa-clara-coun
    ty-to-no-more-than-two-days-a-week/
    -
    https://sunnyvale.ca.gov/property/water/waterconservation.htm

    Primary Election on June 7
    California’s primary election is coming up on June 7. All registered voters will receive their
    vote-by-mail ballot automatically. Ballots will be mailed on or before May 9, and drop boxes to
    return your vote-by-mail ballot will be open starting May 10. In-person voting is also available and
    polls will be open 7:00am to 7:00pm on Election Day. If you need another copy of the Official Voter
    Information Guide, please call (800) 345-VOTE (8683). This election will cover one congressional
    office and several state offices.
    Congressional offices (two elections for one US Senate seat):
    -
    US Senate special election to fill the vacancy left when Kamala Harris was elected as Vice
    President of the United States; the winner will serve until the term expires on January 3,
    2023 (incumbent is Alex Padilla, who was appointed by Governor Newsom)
    -
    US Senate regular election for a full six-year term for one senator that starts on January 3,
    2023
    Note that both US Senate elections are for one Senate seat. The special election is for who will hold
    that seat up until January 3, 2023, and the regular election is for who will hold that seat after
    January 3, 2023. California’s other US Senate seat is currently held by Dianne Feinstein, whose
    term does not end until on January 2, 2025.
    Statewide offices:
    -
    Governor (incumbent is Gavin Newsom)
    -
    Lieutenant Governor (incumbent is Eleni Kounalakis)
    -
    Secretary of State (incumbent is Shirley N. Weber)
    -
    Controller (incumbent is Betty Yee)
    -
    Treasurer (incumbent is Fiona Ma)
    -
    Attorney General (incumbent is Rob Bonta)
    -
    Insurance Commissioner (incumbent is Ricardo Lara)
    -
    Board of Equalization (one election each for Districts 1, 2, 3, and 4)
    -
    Superintendent of Public Instruction (incumbent is Tony Thurmond)
