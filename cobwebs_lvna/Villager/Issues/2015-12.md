    President’s Column 
     
    -Fred Fowler 
    Greetings neighbors!  I'm writing this 
    on “Black Friday”, the day after 
    Thanksgiving, and I'm recovering from 
    a wonderful Thanksgiving feast where 
    (of course) I ate too much – again.  
    Phyllis and I had a very nice visit with some of our  
    family, and the 2015 holiday season is now “officially” 
    underway. 
     
    So far that holiday season has brought a flood of  
    e-mails from every online and offline shopping  
    opportunity you can think of, all telling me how much 
    money I can save by spending  it on their goods rather 
    than someone else's.   The message is “Buy!  Buy 
    More!  Be Happy! Buy!”  Over the years, the meaning 
    of Thanksgiving seems to have become a day for  
    retailers to give thanks that the rest of us have a tradi-
    tion of gift giving around this time of year. 
     
    Of course, Thanksgiving means much more than that.  
    People remember that it is a time for families to get 
    together and think about all the good things they enjoy.  
    People often forget about the “community” aspect of 
    the holiday, though. 
     
    The first Thanksgiving, after all, was a 
    time when a community got together to 
    celebrate.  The Pilgrims in the Plymouth 
    colony in Massachusetts had toiled 
    through their first hard year in the new 
    world.  The first winter had seen half of 
    their number starve to death.  The  
    ensuing Spring and Summer had meant 
    back breaking work to get fields cleared, 
    get seeds planted and nurture the crops 
    through the growing season.

    First of all, I think we should remember how much neighbors 
    matter.  We live in a fast paced Silicon Valley world where 
    sometimes people can't take time to be with their own families 
    very much, let alone the folks who live nearby.  Nevertheless, 
    our neighbors are the people we share our lives with.  We 
    should be grateful for all of the good neighbors we have. 
     
    Second, I think we should appreciate the fact that we live in a 
    place with such a strong sense of community.  Living in  
    Lakewood Village means something.  We have strong  
    traditions and celebrations that are all our own.  We have a 
    good sense of our own identity.  We do things  
    together. 
     
    One of the big things we do each year is our Holiday 
    Parade.  The 2015 edition of the parade will step off 
    at 11:00am on Saturday, December 5th.  Once again 
    we will have the Fremont High School marching 
    band, a fleet of fancy cars and of course Santa Claus 
    marching their way from Lakewood School across Lawrence 
    Expressway to Fairwood Park.  We're the only neighborhood 
    in Sunnyvale (and perhaps all of Santa Clara County) that 
    does anything like this at all.  
     
    Why do we do this?  Because it helps us show the rest of the 
    world who we are. 
     
    I'm thankful that Phyllis and I live in Lakewood Village. We 
    moved here in 1995 –20 years ago! – and now there is  
    nowhere else in the United States I would rather call home. 
     
    Thanks neighbors!  Thanks for making this the best  
    community to live in in the city of Sunnyvale and beyond. 

    There was also the challenge of getting to know their 
    neighbors – the native people who had lived there for  
    generations and had a way of life that was very different 
    from the European newcomers.  
     
    The first Thanksgiving was a harvest festival.  The  
    growing season was over and the land had born its fruit.  
    There was finally plenty to eat.  The threat of starvation 
    had been staved off for at least another year.  
    Also, relations between the newcomers and the 
    original inhabitants of the land had gotten better.  
    What does one do in such circumstances?   
    Throw a party, of course! 
     
    We all know the story of that first Thanksgiving 
    feast.  The Pilgrims and the native people got  
    together, pooled their food stuffs and had a big banquet to 
    celebrate the occasion.  The colonists made pumpkin pie, 
    the Indians brought turkeys and the whole traditional menu 
    was established.   After eating themselves silly, everyone 
    fell into the traditional food coma and slept for the rest of 
    the day. 
     
    By the time our current crop of 6th graders graduate from 
    high school, we'll have the opportunity to celebrate the 
    400th anniversary of that first big party.  I think we should 
    stop and take a moment to think about what it means for us 
    here in Lakewood Village.

    Holiday Tips From Sunnyvale Public Safety 
     
    The Sunnyvale Department of Public 
    Safety is proud to serve you each and 
    every day. During the Holiday Season our 
    officers are especially vigilant to ensure 
    this time of year is safe and joyous.  
    Unfortunately, the Holidays do attract 
    those to our neighborhoods that are intent on committing 
    thefts.  
      
    Here are helpful tips you and your family can use now 
    and throughout the year: 
      
       Lock ALL doors and windows and use a secondary                
     
    blocking device on those that slide open (i.e. 
     
    small wooden dowel rod).  
      
    Close and lock your garage door.  
      
    Lock all gates leading to the rear or side area of 
     
    your residence.  
      
    Secure all home tools inside your residence or  
     
    garage. 
      
    Download tracking applications on electronic  
     
    devices. 
      
    Get to know your neighbors.  
      
    Invest in a home security system    
     
    with an audible alarm.  
     
      
    Photograph valuables and record 
     
    serial numbers. 
      
    If you observe any suspicious people, vehicles, or  
    activity in your neighborhood, please contact Public 
    Safety immediately by dialing 911. Our dispatchers are 
    standing by to immediately send an officer to you. 
      
    If you have additional crime prevention questions, 
    please contact our Community Service Bureau at  
    (408) 730-7140. You may also email an anonymous 
    (non-emergency) crime tip. 
      
    We wish you and your family a safe and happy holiday 
    season  
    Holiday Tree Recycling - "Treecycling" 
    The city provides an annual holiday 
    tree recycling program for all single family, 
    duplex, triplex, and mobile homes. During 
    this time, residents may recycle holiday 
    trees with the regular yard waste program by 
    placing them at curbside on the regular  
    garbage collection day. Trees may be placed in the 
    yard trimmings cart.  We also take flocked trees. 
    Please cut trees into lengths of four feet or shorter.  
    Remove nails and decorations.  
     
    Contact the City of Sunnyvale Recycling Program 
    at (408) 730-7262 or Specialty Solid Waste with any 
    questions (408) 565-9900. 
     
    Rethink Your Holiday Lights! 
    As the holidays approach, neighborhoods 
    will start to glow with festive light displays. 
    However, those decorations can often be 
    costly to you and the environment. Did you know  a light 
    display of only 6 strings (100 incandescent bulbs each) 
    costs #3.90 a day to operate? That adds up to $117 a 
    month! Switching to energy efficient LED lights will re-
    duce your daily cost to only 3.2 cents a day, or 96 cents 
    a month – that will surely fill your wallet with cheer. The 
    SMaRT Station at 301 Carl Rd in Sunnyvale accepts 
    holiday lights for recycling 8am-5pm, seven days per 
    week.
