    Pilot Project Update 
    - Craig Crawford  
    President, LVNA 
    The first meeting for the pilot project was held 
    at the Lakewood park building. It was a 
    disappointment, because the turnout was very 
    small. Many people expressed a desire and 
    positive opinion about the pilot project, but 
    they did not live up to expectations by showing 
    up at the meeting. We had one resident and 
    three landlords at the meeting. We had a good 
    exchange about how to get people involved.  
     
    A major issue is differences in language and 
    culture. The city said that when they go to the 
    door of a Lakewood residence, many times the 
    person answering the door does not speak 
    English, and the conversation stops there. 
    Letters went out to all the residences and 
    landlords in the pilot project area to let them 
    know of the meeting, but a  Vietnamese 
    gentleman who attended the meeting said that 
    for many immigrants, if they get a letter from 
    the city, it goes into the garbage. This is not 
    encouraging. 
     
    The chances of the pilot project being 
    successful and moving forward are reduced 
    without v olunteers coming forward and helping 
    with this neighborhood -enhancing project. 
    Please let Rich Gutierrez know if you can help 
    his number is 730 -7447. Call now. We 
    especially need people that can speak the 
    languages that are common in the 
    neighborhood that can carry the Pilot Program 
    message to those households in which English 
    is not the primary spoken language. ¢

    I Need To Come Clean 
    - Chet Vanek 
    The statute of limitations has, I believe, 
    expired by now so I can get this off my chest 
    without fear of going up the river.  
     
    It was about a year ago that the L.N.V.A. 
    Villager was nicely placed in my screen door by 
    our ever -friendly neighbor, Mrs. Rodriguez.  
    When I opened the paper I was greeted by a 
    full-sheet ad from the local (Mercado Center) 
    Ben and Jerry’s ice cream shop.  “Come in with 
    this ad,” it proclaimed, “and get a free sugar 
    cone.” 
     
    Ooh, a free cone.  I figured that sometime in 
    the nea r future my dear wife would again insist 
    that she and I take a healthful walk to the 
    Mercado Center.  I could get a free cone if I 
    agreed.  I do need a little reward to get me to 
    do “healthful” things — I had become immune to 
    whining doctors and friends, all  tremulously 
    intoning dire dirges about the impending health 
    debacle I was courting by living quietly, and 
    sumptuously.  But a whining wife, well some 
    things are inescapable.  The best one can do is 
    find a personal reason for doing what she 
    wants. 
     
    Sure enough, next evening my dearest decided 
    we must breathe fresh outdoor air and none of 
    my reminders about the hundred different 
    polychlorinated, fluoro biphenyl hydro what’cha 
    ma call-its that pervade modern fresh air would 
    dissuade her.

     “Oh, I have a coupon,” I said cheerfully, “for a

    and I pe rched atop the dining room chairs as 
    the large bugs crept under the front and back 
    doors until just yesterday as I was pulling out 
    the years old phone book affectionately known 
    as the roach killer, I have wondered why. Why 
    are these damn things everywhere?  Where do 
    they live? Where do they come from? Why

    8/16/2002 
    Dear Editor, 
     
    This is in re sponse to the LVNA President’s 
    Column from last month and “The Right To 
    Be…”  article from the previous month. 
     
    I very clearly stated that I do care about the 
    quality of life in Lakewood Village, and keeping 
    up the properties we live in, whether we own 
    them or not, and taking pride in our 
    community, and satisfying the needs of public 
    safety.  
     
    The first violations that Craig [Crawford] 
    mentioned definitely would involve public 
    health, and therefore public safety, and would 
    not be in the realm of “I can dow wh at I want 
    to on my property, so butt out”.  
     
    Barking dogs, crowing roosters, and loud 
    music, are within the realm of talking to your 
    neighbors. In response, your neighbors have 
    an obligation to listen to your complaint, and if 
    your compliant is legitimate, make an 
    adjustment in their lifestyle. If your request is 
    legitimate and is ignored, then yes, you have a 
    right to make a complaint to your public 
    officials. This is what they are there for, pro -
    active or not.  
     
    I must apologize for not explaining my 
    objection to pro -active more clearly. Pro -
    active, if I understand the term correctly, 
    implies that an agent of the government 
    actively seeks out violations rather than 
    reacting after a complaint.  
     
    u continued on next column  
     
     
    u continued from previous column  
    One case I have in mind that prompted this 
    article happened to a good friend of ours. This 
    couple had a large family and lived in a home 
    they are buying. Needing more room, they 
    converted their garage into extra bedrooms.  
     
    The way the story was told to me, so meone 
    from the City government was driving around 
    the neighborhood looking for violations, and 
    came across our friend’s house. This person 
    from the City entered the property and 
    demanded that the owner open the garage 
    door. The owner couldn’t, and he was c ited. 
     
    When it came to a hearing, the owner was 
    fined a nominal amount. One official 
    complained that the fine was not sufficient to 
    cover expenses, and so the fine was greatly 
    increased. This is policing -for-profit, and I am 
    opposed to it. As it turned out , the owner was 
    later allowed to submit plans that are 
    acceptable to make the rooms legal or

    Agreed, the owner should have done the

    the City government to go to this extent to 
    maintain a community standard that applies to

    Putting aside the issue of pro -active, I wrote an

    expressing themselves. As I said, this is

    served its purpose, even if I am later shown to 
    have over -reacted to the above story. Living 
    together and working together means giving up 
    a little of “doing our own thing” for the benefit 
    of the neighborhood we live in, and still have 
    our own lifestyle without undue interference.  
    - James L. Daugherty ¢

    cell phone? 
     
    A: All 9 -1-1 calls from cell phones are 
    forwarded to California Highway Patrol dispatch 
    centers, which receive over 6  million calls a 
    year statewide. Chances are that callers will 
    receive a recorded message stating that all 
    operators are busy. If have an emergency and 
    you can't get through to 9 -1-1 on your cell 
    phone, you can dial direct to the appropriate 
    agency. A list  of Sunnyvale’s neighboring cities’ 
    emergency dispatch lines follows. It is 
    recommended that you program your cell 
    phone with the numbers for CHP in the areas 
    that you frequently travel.
