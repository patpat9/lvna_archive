    April Meeting a Night to Remember  
    By Fred Fowler 
    For those of you who missed our April general meeting, it was 
    definitely one for the history books!  Two representatives from 
    Pacific Gas and Electric came to the Lakewood Park building to 
    give a presentation on the “Smart Meters” that are being in-
    stalled in our neighborhood.  More than 50 people from     
    Lakewood packed the meeting to hear the  details and ask 
    some pointed questions.  Everyone agreed that the            
    presentation gave quite a bit of valuable information and that 
    their time at the meeting was well spent. 
    There was just one peculiarity about the event.  It was held in 
    the dark! 
    Two weeks prior to the meeting, the City of Sunnyvale   began 
    repairs on an underground electrical cable that    supplies pow-
    er to the park building and the park's pathway lights.  When we 
    showed up on the night of April 12th there was no electricity 
    flowing anywhere near the park building.  One of our neighbors 
    pulled his car up to the doorway and volunteered the use of his 
    power inverter, but there just wasn't enough juice to power the 
    PG&E representatives' equipment.  The representatives made 
    their presentation the best they could. However, they just    
    happened to have a number of tiny LED based flashlights to 
    give away, (which proved to be very popular).  In the end, the 
    audience thanked the PG&E people for the information they 
    had given, for their patience and for being such good sports 
    about the situation.  It was unanimously decided to invite PG&E 
    back during our June meeting to give us the full  presentation 
    they had put  together. 
    As for the LVNA's May general meeting, the situation doesn't 
    look good.  The power is still out as of the date I am writing this, 
    and I've been told by the City of Sunnyvale that the power 
    “might” be back on in time.  There are no guarantees, however. 
    Because of the uncertainty about power, we’ve decided to can-
    cel the LVNA’s May general meeting on the 12th. Please stay 
    tuned, we’ll be back in busi-
    ness in June! I look forward to 
    seeing you then. 

    Neighborhood News 
    Hello my friends.  April was a good month for the neigh-
    borhood and the LVNA, and May looks like it will bring 
    another set of challenges and opportunities.  Things are 
    starting to “cook” and the pace of activity is starting to 
    build.  Lakewood Village has always had the reputation 
    of being a part of Sunnyvale where things happen.  
    We're active in our neighborhood, and we're not afraid to 
    roll up our sleeves and get our hands dirty  working for a 
    good cause.   
    Many other parts of the city watch what we do and   mar-
    vel at the things we can accomplish.   If our  newsletter 
    was a “real” newspaper, we'd have the third largest   
    circulation of any news publication in the city!  We     
    routinely stage large events requiring dozens of         
    volunteers.  When it comes time to get certificates of 
    recognition for our volunteers each year, the list of 
    names easily surpasses 100 people.  No place else in 
    Sunnyvale has so much “community spirit”.

    Looking for a Tribune of Trash or a  
    Duchess of Debris. 
    By Fred Fowler 
    Greetings neighbors!  The weather is finally getting nicer, and 
    we all know what happens when the weather gets warm.  
    Dumpster Day happens!  Our nice climate and the City of 
    Sunnyvale's contract with the Specialty Waste company gives 
    us a chance to spend a day cleaning out our closets, spare 
    rooms and back yards and getting rid of all the trash and junk 
    that has accumulated there over the years.  Dumpster day is a 
    magic day for the neighborhood.  There is a universal sense of 
    “out with the old, in with the new” as we get rid of vestiges of 
    the past.  It is a time to let go of old things and to de-clutter our 
    lives.  It is also a time when neighbors get out of their houses 
    and work together to spruce things up.  It is not too much of an 
    exaggeration to say that Dumpster Day is one of the reasons 
    that  Lakewood Village is one of the nicest places to live in 
    Sunnyvale. 
    Like all good traditions, Dumpster Day takes a lot of work to 
    stage each year.  It is one of the largest all-volunteer activities 
    in the City, and the amount of organization and coordination 
    required is quite significant.  On the Friday before the day 
    “officially” begins, about 20 dumpsters get delivered to various 
    parts of the neighborhood.   The LVNA is under strict           
    instructions to make sure the dumpsters do not get loaded 
    with hazardous waste such as TV sets, wet paint, cleaners, 
    and appliances containing Freon.  That means that the     
    dumpsters need to be watched.  We'll need between 20 and 40 
    volunteers to monitor the dumpsters just to make sure they stay 
    “clean” until the last one is taken away     (continued on page 3)

    Financial Literacy for Youth 
    Thursday, June 3, 4:00 p.m.-5:30 p.m. 
    NOVA, 505 W. Olive, Ste. 550 
    Are you concerned that your son or daughter doesn’t 
    listen to you about money? Are you worried that they 
    may not understand how to handle their money when they are on their 
    own? Here’s a workshop that can help! Financial expert Becky Jensen 
    of the Financial Literacy Project will talk with youth about how to identi-
    fy and work towards accomplishing their financial goals, stay out of 
    debt, budgeting, banking basics, and managing living expenses. This 
    workshop is for ages 13-21, but parents are welcome.

    send an e-mail to ChildCareResources@ci.sunnyvale.ca.us
