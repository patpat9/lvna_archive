    President’s Column 
    -Ursula Syrova, LVNA President 
    Vote me out! 
    What?  Yes, please do!  Elections for new board 
    members are coming in March.  Come out and get 
    involved--it would be fabulous to have some new 
    people on the board.  I’ve been president for two years 
    now.  One more year and I’m out due to term limits.  
    Skip, our secretary, has already served three years in 
    that position and won’t be running again.  We 
    desperately need new blood!  New ideas!  We need . . . 
    you! 
    There are over 1600 houses in Lakewood Village.  
    That’s a lot of houses.  That’s a lot of people living in 
    this community!  So . . . why is it so hard to find 
    volunteers to be on the board of the LVNA?  Maybe 
    “board” sounds intimidating.  Well, it’s really just four 
    or five people who meet monthly to organize the 
    monthly general meetings and to plan the major events 
    of the neighborhood. 
    What value do I find in volunteering for the LVNA?  
    Well, when I first moved here almost three years ago, 
    there was a copy of the Villager on our door.  I thought 
    it was so cool!  It had all this great information about 
    the neighborhood, local business, and local issues. 
    After a couple of months I decided to go to one of the 
    meetings.  There weren’t many people there, but 
    suddenly I had met some of the people in the 
    neighborhood.  One of them knew about the folks who 
    had previously owned our house.   Attending that 
    meeting gave me a way to start to plug in to the new 
    neighborhood we had chosen.  By getting involved  
     
    with the LVNA I have come to feel “settled” in this 
    neighborhood in a way I never felt in my previous 
    neighborhood.  I’ve met a lot of people and I now have 
    the feeling that I am giving something back to my 
    community.  As my daughter grows up, I have the 
    wonderful feeling that I am modeling good-citizen 
    behavior for her.  I am teaching her that it is a good 
    thing to get involved locally, to care about the 
    neighborhood we live in, and to give something back to 
    the community.  I don’t just take her to watch the 
    annual parade, I help plan it.  We don’t just go to the 
    picnic, we help coordinate it.  I no longer feel that I live 
    on a little island, just one house in an unknown 
    territory—I feel like I am part of a community. 
    We are coming up on election time for new board 
    members.  Why don’t you sign up?  Board members 
    plan the general meetings and work on coordinating the 
    several annual events that we have in the 
    neighborhood.  Call any of us board members, listed on 
    the back of the Villager, if you want more information. 
    It’s a good way to meet long-time Lakewood Village 
    residents and have a say in what LVNA does—or 
    doesn’t do.  This is your community. ■

    February in Your Yard and 
    Garden 
    • Purchase bare-root plants. Cover the roots 
    until you have the time and the weather is 
    right for planting. 
    • Order bare-root fruit trees from specialty nurseries. 
    They will usually stop shipping bare-root fruit trees 
    by the end of March. 
    • Send in orders to seed companies before they run out 
    of stock later in spring. 
    • Clean and repair garden tools. Sharpen the blades of 
    pruning tools and shovels. Service the lawn mower. 
    • Complete pruning of deciduous trees, shrubs, vines, 
    fruit trees, and roses. 
    • If desired and if deciduous plants have not leafed out 
    yet, apply a last application of dormant spray to kill 
    overwintering insects. It is particularly important to 
    use a lime sulfur spray for peach and nectarine trees 
    to prevent a fungus commonly known as peach leaf 
    curl that cases distorted leaves and scarred fruit. 
    • Select and plant azaleas, camellias, and 
    rhododendrons while they are in bloom. 
    • Fertilize spring-blooming bulbs as the foliage 
    emerges. Some growers recommend dried blood 
    meal; follow the directions on the package, sprinkling 
    the blood meal around the emerging foliage. 
    These tips are from the book Northern California Gardening: A Monthby-Month Guide, by Katherine Grace Endicott, published by Chronicle 
    Books, San Francisco, copyright 1996, and are used with the publisher’s 
    permission. ■

    Letters 
    Neighbors, we need your help. On 
    Sunday, January 22 at 4:00 a.m. our 
    Honda was hit head on. The vehicle 
    was parked on Meadowlake Drive, and 
    someone hit it and pushed it back 
    down the street about 500 feet, 
    right into a tree. The car was 
    totaled. The police followed the 
    other vehicle toward Fair Oaks 
    Avenue but then lost its trail. If 
    the driver had stopped, the police 
    said, it would have been just an 
    accident and we could have worked 
    out things with the insurance 
    companies, but because the driver 
    didn’t stop, it is a felony hit and 
    run. 
    The police need your help. If you 
    know who did this or have seen a 
    vehicle with the front end totally 
    damaged, please call the Public 
    Safety non-emergency number, 7307100. The case number is CR06-764. 
    We cannot let people get away with 
    this type of activity. They need to 
    be responsible for their actions and 
    accountable. It's just not right! 
    The only positive thing to come out 
    of this incident was that we met the 
    people who own and operate ABC Tow. 
    They live in the neighborhood and 
    advertise in the Villager. We called 
    them Monday morning and by that 
    afternoon the car was towed to the 
    junkyard. They are very nice people, 
    and prompt. I recommend them. Thanks 
    again, ABC! 
    - Fernandez Family □ 
     
     
     
    On a recent evening, a neighbor came 
    to our door with a lost dog. Because 
    the dog had no tag, our neighbor 
    walked the neighborhood while I 
    drove around to see if someone was 
    looking for a lost dog. Fortunately, 
    the owner was eventually found. Do 
    yourself a favor: even if your dog 
    or cat is considered an indoor 
    companion, put a collar and tag on 
    him or her with, at least a 
    telephone number to call if the pet 
    ever gets away from your home. 
    You’ll be glad you did! 
    Skip Gottschalk 
    Editor’s note: You might want to check with your 
    veterinarian or with Humane Society Silicon Valley 
    about getting a microchip implanted in your pet. It’s 
    tiny and is placed just under the skin. When a lost pet 
    is scanned, the number on the chip lets the vet or the 
    Humane Society check for the owner’s contact 
    information. ■

    Neighborhood Associations Quarterly Meeting  
    Speakers and topics included: Sunnyvale Sun 
    (www.svcn.com) Editor Sandy Simms, who talked 
    about how Sunnyvale's neighborhood associations can 
    partner with the local media to strengthen the 
    community; Sunnyvale Neighborhood Resource 
    Officer Greg Othon, who discussed home safety and 
    the importance of online news groups in maintaining 
    safe neighborhoods (pubsfty@ci.sunnyvale.ca.us); 
    Linda Kim, of the Columbia Neighborhood Center 
    (www.sunnyvale.ca.gov under Sunnyvale Facilities), 
    who described the health, education, and social and 
    recreation services the center provides; and Ed 
    Legorreta, of Project Sentinel (www.housing.org), 
    who talked about the free neighbor-to-neighbor 
    mediation and conflict management services of that 
    agency. 
    CitySkills Program 
    CitySkills is a basic training program that teaches how 
    to work with government to address issues. In the four 
    sessions (7 to 9 p.m. on Wednesdays, beginning April 
    5, 2006), participants will learn about city services 
    and government structure, how Sunnyvale is  
     
     
     
    managed, how to present your views to government, 
    city budgeting, having an impact on city decisions, 
    Sunnyvale’s “hot” issues, and conflict resolution. 
    March 20 is the application deadline for the spring 
    2006 session of CitySkills. 
    For information about this unique learning 
    opportunity, go to CitySkills.inSunnyvale.com (do not 
    type www) and download a schedule and application. 
    New Sunnyvale Bicycle Map 
    Looking for places to ride your bicycle in Sunnyvale? 
    Trying to find the quickest and safest bike route from 
    home to work? The City of Sunnyvale has unveiled a 
    new, improved Sunnyvale Bicycle Map to meet your 
    needs. The new map includes all bicycle lanes, routes 
    and trails, with updated graphics and colors. In order 
    to aid in route selection, all major streets have been 
    rated to indicate the recommended skill level for safe 
    bicycle travel. New information about neighborhood 
    pathway connections is also included.

    from the Sunnyvale Bicycle and Pedestrian Advisory
