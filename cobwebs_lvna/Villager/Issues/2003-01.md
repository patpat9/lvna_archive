    Leadership in the LVNA needed 
    - Craig Crawford 
    President, LVNA 
    The LVNA is looking for volunteers to fill board 
    positions this spring. If you are able to spend a 
    few hours of your time helping your 
    neighborhood to become a better place to raise 
    your family, then step up and take a leadership 
    position in the best neighbo rhood organization 
    in all of Sunnyvale.

    forward throughout the years without people to 
    run it. I have done my duty and have been the 
    President twice, and now I am stepping down.

    a person and father. I have become a fairly 
    competent public speaker, have sharpened my 
    organizational skills, made countless friends in 
    the neighborhood, and have a insight to how 
    city government does and does not work. If 
    this is a challenge that you are up to, give me 
    a call and I can talk to you about what it takes 
    to be the President.

    everyone enjoys, and no place to vent your 
    concerns or to hear neighbors with similar 
    concerns. Life in Lakewood Village would suffer 
    without the LVNA. Step up now and make a

    Sunnyvale Again Ranked 
    Among Safest Cities 
    - Sunnyvale Press Release 
     
    Sunnyvale, CA (12/17/02) – The City 
    of Sunnyvale was recently ranked as the 5 th 
    safest city in the United States and 3 rd safest 
    among cities between 100,000 and 500,000 in 
    population, according to the Morgan Quitno 
    Press, an independent private research an d 
    publishing company located in Lawrence, 
    Kansas.  The company specializes in reference 
    books and monthly reports that compare states 
    and cities in several different subject areas.  
      
    The Morgan Quitno Safest City Award is based 
    on a city’s rating for six ba sic crime categories:  
    murder, rape, robbery, aggravated assault, 
    burglary, and motor vehicle theft.  All cities of 
    75,000+ populations that reported crime data 
    to the FBI for the six crime categories were 
    included in the rankings. 
     
    “Our City Council, citiz ens, and all the 
    members of the Public Safety Department 
    deserve the credit for this continuing 
    accomplishment.  In our City, we are fortunate 
    to have a team of employees from various 
    departments that are committed to enhancing 
    the quality of life in our c ommunity,” said 
    Sunnyvale City Manager, Bob LaSala. 
     
    In fiscal year 2001/2002, Sunnyvale had its 
    lowest overall crime rate since records were 
    maintained in Public Safety, beginning in 1975.

    Flooding is 
    everyone’s problem 
     
    - Craig Crawford 
    President, LVNA 
     
    Rain, rain, rain ! It looks to be a wet winter. 
    This is a time to watch for local flooding on 
    your street. I came back from an outing 
    downtown and found my street was flooded, 
    the water was up in my neighbors front yard 
    and ri sing toward the front door. It was only 
    part of the street but close enough that it was 
    uncomfortable. The problem was the sewer 
    grate was clogged with leaves. This is a 
    common occurrence in Lakewood because 
    people won’t move their cars off the street 
    when the street sweeper is scheduled. Maybe 
    starting to ticket cars that are in the way of the 
    street sweeper is the answer. If people won’t 
    be responsible and do what is the right thing 
    and prevent a valuable city service to be 
    performed that could prevent someo ne’s house 
    to be flooded, then ticket away. Public safety 
    has to be involved and if it’s with tickets or 
    warnings to cars blocking the street sweeper 
    then so be it. Any ideas or alternatives out in 
    Lakewood land? We have put the street 
    sweeper schedule in the LVNA newsletter 
    several times but it is ignored. We have made 
    announcements at the monthly meeting to no 
    avail. Lets get public safety out to explain why 
    we are not able to get cars blocking the street 
    sweeper to have an enforcement nudge.  ¢

    Letters to the LVNA 
    Dear Editor,                  12/12/2002 
     
    I have visited Lakewood Village every 
    year and have immensely enjoyed the  
    Christmas light displays.  Now, I find 
    myself a new resident, and eagerly spent 
    many hours with my husband the last few 
    weekends decorating our home with 
    Holiday cheer.  Everything was fine for 
    a few nights, and then sections of the 
    lighting began to stop working.  
     
    Puzzled, my husband spent more h ours 
    trying to diagnose the problems, as  more 
    and more sections stopped working.  
    Finally, he discovered there were 
    multiple missing light  bulbs, as well as 
    cut wires. It looked very much like 
    vandalism. 
     
    Last night he replaced the bulbs and 
    repaired what he could, so once again, 
    our display looked nice.  Between 8:15 
    and 8:45 on 12/11, the vandals struck 
    again. About that time, I  observed 3 
    male juveniles walking up the street.  
     
    I am unsure whether we will even bother 
    to keep repairing our display, or  if we 
    will have one next year, either. It is a 
    sad thing to have a few ruin something 
    that pleased many (including  our 
    neighbors, my husband and myself).  I 
    wonder what kind of neighborhood this is 
    if the children are not taught to  
    respect other people's property.  
      
    Sincerely, New Resident (Unit II) 
     
    [Editor’s note: Report vandalism to Sunnyvale 
    DPS. They m ay not be able to solve your 
    particular problem, but the report wil l help 
    them establish patterns and assign patrols.  
    Don’t be discouraged, put up lights next year – 
    otherwise, the vandals will have won!]  ¢

    Sunnyvale’s Water 
    Pollution Control Plant 
    Unveils New Web Site 
    - Sunnyvale Press Release 
     
    Sunnyvale, CA (12/18/02) – In response to 
    frequent requests from the public for online 
    information about sewage treatment and storm 
    water pollution prevention, the Environmental 
    Division of Sunnyvale’s Water Pollution Control 
    Plant recently launched a website to provide 
    information that can readily be used by 
    everyone —  from elementary school students 
    to retirees.  
     
    “It is also useful to a highly skilled technical 
    audience looking for information on 
    understanding water issues,” said Gail Glen, 
    Environmental Outreach Coordinator for the 
    Water Pollution Control Plant. “In addition to 
    describing the plant’s operational pr ocesses, 
    the site also explains how the plant is 
    organized, funded, and regulated.”  
     
    In addition, specific tips on water pollution 
    prevention for businesses and residents are 
    included.  There are links to regional 
    coordination efforts with other organizati ons 
    engaged in protecting local creeks and South 
    San Francisco Bay. An extensive environmental 
    education section describing materials and 
    presentations available and tours of the plant is 
    also offered. 
     
    To access the site directly, go to 
    http://www.ci.sunny vale.ca.us/wpcp.  Or you 
    may use the “Search” button on the navigation 
    bar of the City’s home page.  For more 
    information about the new site, contact Gail 
    Glen at 730-7738 or gglen@ci.sunnyvale.ca.us.
