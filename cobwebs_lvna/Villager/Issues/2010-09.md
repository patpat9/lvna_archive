    What's Going On With   
    Downtown Sunnyvale? 
    By Fred Fowler 
    Sunnyvale enjoys a well earned 
    reputation for being a center of 
    excellence in city government.  It 
    has also been recognized often 
    for being a safe, comfortable and 
    livable community.  It wasn't too 
    very long ago that the President and Vice President of the 
    United States came to visit us in order to learn from      
    Sunnyvale how to improve the workings of Federal agencies.  
    Vice President Al Gore even visited Lakewood Village once, 
    in part because of our diversity and our strong sense of   
    community.  There are many reasons for all of us to take a 
    great deal of pride in this city that is our home. 
    Having said that, Sunnyvale also has its own set of           
    embarrassing problems.  The most visible of them is the 
    shambles that is our downtown.  For almost two decades now 
    the heart of our city has been a dilapidated ruin that, like the 
    proverbial “crazy aunt in the basement”, everyone knows is 
    there but no one knows what to do with.  There have been 
    many different plans for renovating the area, but despite good 
    intentions and hard work the fact remains that the area is a 
    half-finished construction site representing the failed dreams 
    of the latest developer who tried to tame that beast of a     
    project. 
    I was personally involved with the downtown for the eight 
    years I served on the Sunnyvale City Council (1997-2005), 
    and in those eight years the mall area went through three 
    changes of ownership and several changes in “concept”.  
    Each owner had his own idea for making “the project” go  
    forward, and each one ended up shipwrecked on the various 
    financial rocks they discovered along the way.  I was not   
    involved in selecting the current developer or approving the 
    current project, but I decided to find out what I could about 
    the developer's plans, his troubles, and where we can go 
    from here.  Here is what I discovered. 
    SAND HILL PROPERTIES 
    Shortly after I left the City Council, the city was approached 
    by a local developer who wished to acquire the old Town 
    and County center in the downtown just north of         
    Washington Avenue.  At that time, the buildings were 
    owned by a private landowner but the streets were owned 
    by the City of Sunnyvale.  The developer that was          
    interested in the Town and Country Center was “Sand Hill 
    Properties”, and it entered into negotiations with the City in 
    order to buy the streets and assemble a “super block” for 
    development.  The sale of the streets eventually went 
    through, and Sand Hill Properties now owns the entire area 
    that used to be the old Town and County Center. 
    During those negotiations, Sand Hill Properties became 
    aware of the problems the current owner was having with 
    the mall project “next door” across Washington Avenue.  To 
    make a long story short, Sand Hill Properties made a deal 
    with some “money men” to finance a takeover of the mall 
    project.  They teamed up with a financial company called 
    “RREEF” and bought out the previous developer's interest 
    in the downtown.  RREEF would supply the financing and 
    retain a 95% ownership stake in the final developed     
    property.  Sand Hill Properties would provide the            
    development expertise and retain a 5% share in the     
    completed project.  RREEF borrowed more than $400   
    million from a set of banks led by  (Continued Page 3)

    RREEF 
    RREEF is a specialized finance  
    company whose strategy is to      
    acquire and hold “big ticket” commer-

    around the world).  Most of their money is tied up in malls

    projects such as the Sunnyvale Downtown, RREEF 
    “mortgages” some of their property by borrowing against it 
    and using it as collateral.  The City of Sunnyvale knew this 
    and checked RREEF out carefully   before going ahead and 
    allowing the project take-over.  They made sure that the eq-
    uity that RREEF held in its other   properties was at least 
    double that needed to support the lending required for the 
    Town Center project plan. Everything checked out

    previous financiers of the mall, Lehman Brothers, actually

    One of the early casualties of this panic 
    was RREEF.  Remember, their strategy 
    was to develop projects using borrowed 
    money, with their other properties as 
    collateral.  They got hit by a “double-whammy”.  Borrowing 
    became nearly impossible almost overnight.  Also, the resulting 
    crash in the commercial real-estate market made their collateral 
    lose more than half its value.  Suddenly, the “money men” in 
    the Sand Hill Properties project could no longer supply any 
    money.  When that happened, construction on the downtown 
    ground to a halt. 
    Needless to say, when the money ran out and the work 
    stopped, everyone involved immediately “Lawyered up” and the 
    entire matter ended up in the courts.  As things stand now, the 
    project is being administered by a court appointed “Receiver” 
    whose job is to protect the property while the various suits are 
    litigated.  Wachovia's plan is to foreclose on its “mortgage” for 
    the property and push RREEF and Sand Hill Properties out of 
    the picture.  (I'm not sure if this has happened yet or not, but if it 
    does happen it will simplify the situation).  The City of 
    Sunnyvale is working to protect its interests in the downtown, 
    both through the agreements it had signed with Sand Hill 
    Properties and its ability to regulate the type of development 
    that is allowed. 
    WHERE DO WE GO NOW? 
    Right now the ball is in the court of the Wachovia Bank and the 
    court appointed Receiver.  There is no “entrepreneur” with a 
    “grand vision” involved anymore, and it will probably be a long 
    time before any “new” developer steps forward.  I have heard 
    rumors that Sand Hill Properties is looking for a new “money 
    man” to partner with in order to buy the property out of 
    foreclosure and resume the project, but so far those are just 
    rumors with no visible substance to them.  We'll see if they 
    prove to be true or not. 
    There is some progress though (at last!)  About three months 
    ago the Receiver came to the City and let them know that a 
    major tenant was interested in leasing one of the office 
    buildings.  City agreements would have to be modified in order

    At this point the downtown is only partly completed, and it is 
    likely to stay that way until the economy turns around.  No 
    one will pay for completing the unfinished office, retail and 
    residential units until they can be leased for a profit.  The 
    real estate market for all three types of property is at an 
    historic low.  It will be a while before that reality changes. 
    The best we can hope for is success for the “piece-meal” 
    approach being taken by the Receiver.  It may not be 
    possible to complete the downtown in one grand sweeping 
    project.  It may be possible to complete it little by little, as 
    individual tenants express interest in pieces of the property. 
    A piece-meal approach may not be a bad thing.  After all, 
    that is how most downtowns grew in the first place.  City 
    centers were not historically constructed all at once.  They 
    were build piece by piece, and by different “entrepreneurs” 
    with different “visions”.   
    The City has been trying to fix the downtown areas since  
    the original Town Center Mall was opened in1978.  After 32  
    years the job is still not finished.  There is reason for 
    optimism, but at this point it looks like we'll have to wait a  
    few years more before we have the kind of “heart” for our 
    city that we want. 
    Our “crazy aunt” is still there.  She is better than she was, 
    but she is still a burden and an embarrassment to the 
    community.  Let’s hope that she recovers soon, so she can 
    come out of the basement and help us entertain the guests 
    that come to the heart of our city. 
