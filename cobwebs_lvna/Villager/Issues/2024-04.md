    FUHSD Trustee Election Update
    It has been just over one year since the Fremont Union High School District (FUHSD) voted unanimously to move from
    at-large to by-trustee elections. In March, the the Committee for Trustee Area Districting (CTAD) selected 3 potential
    district maps and drafted proposed election sequence(s) for each map. District maps will divide the district into five
    areas. Per federal law, each area must have nearly equal populations (less than 10% total variance). District maps are
    based on total population (instead of voting population or FUHSD families) because the school district serves all
    families who live in the area, not just those who are of voting age or already have students attending a FUHSD school.
    The three maps are online here: https://tinyurl.com/MapScenario5, https://tinyurl.com/MapScenario6, and
    https://tinyurl.com/MapScenario7. 
    This will be the first time that there will be a trustee who lives in North Sunnyvale on the FUHSD board, so it’s
    important that our voices are heard regarding which map we prefer. Please review the maps and share your feedback
    (including simply stating which map(s) you like) supports the move to by-trustee area elections and helps ensure equal
    representation on the FUHSD board. Email input can be sent to trusteeareas@fuhsd.org  The final map hearing will be
    at the April 24 FUHSD board meeting.
    For more information, visit https://www.fuhsd.org/newsroom/trustee-areas, view the CTAD Informational Presentation
    at https://tinyurl.com/CTADPresentation, or view the March 20 CTAD presentation to the FUHSD board at
    https://tinyurl.com/CTADMarch20

    Sunnyvale Schools to Get New Playground Surface Materials
    At its March 14 board meeting, the Sunnyvale School District began the process of approving contracts for
    site improvements at its schools over the summer (Agenda Items 10.B and 10.C). The site improvements
    include:
    Installing digital marquee signs (San Miguel, Lakewood, Cumberland, and Columbia)
    Replacing the rubber play matting understand existing play structures with artificial turf (Bishop,
    Cumberland, Columbia, Fairwood, Lakewood, San Miguel, and Vargas)
    Applying standard and colored asphalt slurry on top of the blacktop (Columbia, San Miguel, and Vargas)
    Adding a shade structure (San Miguel)
    The projects are divided into two contracts: (1) Agenda Item 10.B for San Miguel, Lakewood, Cumberland, and
    Columbia, which will require electrical work to install the digital marquee signs; and (2) Agenda Item 10.C
    was for Bishop, Fairwood, and Vargas, which already have digital marquee signs and do not require electrical
    work.
    Several community members spoke out in opposition to the selection of artificial turf (AT) as the new
    playground surface material. Their primary objections to AT were its contribution to climate change; its use
    of PFAS chemicals, which have been linked to several negative health effects; and artificial turf’s contribution
    to microplastics and environmental pollution. The community members recommended engineered wood fiber
    (EWF) instead of AT. EWF recommended by multiple organizations - Safe Healthy Playing Fields (SHPFI) and
    TURI (Toxic Use Reduction Institute), among others - as a safe, ADA-compliant material for playground surface
    materials. Representatives from SHPFI and the Plastic Prevention Pollution Committee of the Loma Prieta
    Chapter of the Sierra Club also called in to speak against AT and in favor of EWF.
    The board discussion focused mostly on safety, ADA-compliance (particularly surfaces that are wheelchair-
    accessible), the need for cooler outside areas at our schools, and the role that plastics should play in our
    lives. Despite evidence presented by community members to support using EWF instead of AT, the board still
    viewed AT as the only ADA-compliant playground surface material. The discussion about reducing the
    number of hot surfaces at school properties covered many important details, but it did not include mention
    of State Bill 499, which stalled last year and would have required schools to "remove harmful surfaces such
    as asphalt, concrete, rubber, and synthetic turf and replace them with cooler surfaces." 
    The board voted to follow the staff recommendation. For Item 10.B (which includes Lakewood), all bids were
    rejected. The project will be rebid (the two contractors with the lowest bids did not go through the pre-
    qualification process, so the rebid gives them time to get pre-qualified). For Item 10.C (which includes
    Fairwood), the contracts were approved by a 4-1 vote (board member Eileen Le voted in opposition, due to
    the selection of AT as the playground surface material). 
    Item 10.B will be presented again at a future board meeting (likely April 4) for approval. Construction for all
    schools is planned for this summer.

    Update on Artifical Turf at Fremont and Homestead High Schools
    On Tuesday, February 27, the FUHSD board voted unanimously to replace six artificial turf (AT) fields (three at
    Fremont High School (FHS) and three at Homestead High School (HHS)) with more artificial turf fields. This
    was done in spite of several letters from community members highlighting the negative environmental,
    health, and safety impacts of AT. Several organizations - including Santa Clara County Medical Association,
    Sierra Club's Loma Prieta chapter, Safe Healthy Playing Fields (SHPFI), Sustainable Silicon Valley, and PEER
    (Public Employees for Environmental Responsibility) - wrote letters in support of natural grass. Supervisor
    Otto Lee wrote a letter in favor of natural grass, and Mothers Out Front wrote a letter in favor of natural
    grass, which was co-signed by Silicon Valley Youth Climate Action, 350 Silicon Valley, Climate Action
    California, and Elders Climate Action.
    The agenda was structured such that discussion on artificial turf (Agenda Item 14.1) started at 10:20pm. Due to
    the late hour, the board limited public comments to one minute each and cut off public comments before
    everyone had spoken. 
    The presentation by district staff compared old, traditional natural grass to modern AT. There was no
    mention of the third option - modern, organically managed natural grass fields. The presentation claimed
    that maintenance for AT fields is less than for natural grass fields, despite clear evidence from SHPFI and
    others that the maintenance of AT is comparable to the maintenance of natural grass fields. The presentation
    also highlighted concerns that natural grass uses fertilizers and herbicides, which contribute to water
    pollution. The presentation did not address the fact that AT fields are a substantial source of water pollution,
    both as a source of microplastic contamination and as a source of PFAS chemical contamination. 
    Construction on the fields is planned to start the day after graduation, which meant that the district waited
    until the last minute to approve the contract. The fields are used by multiple sports teams during the school
    year, which means the fields must stand up to heavy use. There was no information presented on whether or
    not modern, organically managed natural grass fields are compatible with heavy use, and performing the due
    diligence of contacting natural grass experts for accurate information would have delayed the project
    schedule. Because the replacement of the fields at FHS and HHS is already at least two years overdue, further
    delays could have required field closure due to safety concerns.
    Bond money will finance the construction. The cost of the total project (which will replace a total of 12 AT
    fields with more AT) is approximately $23,000,000. No financial information regarding the costs for just the
    FHS and HHS fields was presented in Tuesday’s board meeting. The new AT fields only last about 10-12 years,
    so they will need to be replaced again in approximately 2035 with an expected cost of $20-$30 million dollars
    in future bond money.
    There are six more fields in FUHSD that will need replacing (two each for Cupertino High, Lynbrook High, and
    Monta Vista High). The demand from the community is that, prior to determining the material used for these
    six fields, the board must require an updated presentation comparing modern, organically managed natural
    grass options with modern AT.

    Recap of Jan 10 General Meeting (Cont.)
    Wondering about the FoodCycle program? In 2021, 65% of Sunnyvale
    residents participated in the program, which diverts 5000 tons of food
    per year from the landfill. FoodCycle is going to be even more
    important now. Recent state law SB1383 requires communities to reduce
    the amount of organics going into the landfill by 75% by 2025. All of the
    food that goes into the FoodCycle bin is used to generate electricity.
    Some final food for thought:
    There’s a reason the order is “reduce, reuse, recycle.” Want to reduce
    your environmental footprint? First, reduce how much you buy.
    Second, reuse what you buy. And only then should you recycle it.
    Use the “Sunnyvale Recycles Right” app on your phone or the “How
    to Get Rid of Anything” feature on the City’s website to check if
    something is recyclable.
    Remember, “when in doubt, throw it out - and remember the Nice
    Nine!”, a digital copy is here: http://tinyurl.com/SunnyvaleRecycling
