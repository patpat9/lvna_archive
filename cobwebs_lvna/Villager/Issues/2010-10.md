    Q&A with Sunnyvale Neighborhood Preservation  
    Winter is coming fast and it may be time to think about      
    weatherizing your home.  This kind of chore is never easy and 
    can also be quite expensive – especially if you are on a fixed 
    income or just don't have much money to spare.  Tough times 
    like these make it difficult to do the kind of simple maintenance 
    that can help protect your home and make it last. 
    It may surprise you to find out that the City of Sunnyvale has 
    some programs that can help.  The city has set aside a fairly 
    good sized chunk of cash for “paint grants” – free money to 
    help people paint their homes.  There is also some Federal 
    Housing and Urban Development money available for very low 
    interest loans to help with minor (or even major) repairs.  As 
    with all government programs, the paperwork involved in   
    applying for and receiving this help can be daunting.  Still, if 
    you can find your way through the maze there is a great deal 
    of money available just for the asking.  As a matter of fact, 
    during the fiscal year just ended several hundred thousand 
    dollars of it went begging!  The city has set out to educate its 
    citizens (meaning you and me) on how we can take advantage 
    of the aid that is available. 
    Do you have a leaky roof?  Are you worried about termites 
    getting into your walls through cracks in your house's paint?  
    Then come to the Lakewood Park building on Wednesday,  
    October 13th at 7:00pm to hear Sunnyvale Housing       
    Rehabilitation Specialist Richard Gutierrez discuss the 
    various Sunnyvale programs that can help.  Rich's job is to get 
    this help to the people who need it, and he is very practiced at 
    getting people through the stack of forms required by the    
    Federal government to qualify for the assistance.  Come hear 
    Rich talk about what help is available, who can qualify, and 
    what kinds of projects are realistic candidates for funding. 
    We're also hoping that our new Sunnyvale Neighborhood 
    Preservation Specialist Chris Koelbel can join us on     
    Wednesday night.  Chris recently rotated into the position 
    when our old friend Jhoanne Navarro-Tran rotated out to take 
    care of the southern third of the city.  We hope that Chris will 
    engage in a little Q&A with us about the new rules for street 
    tree pruning and the upcoming changes in the nuisance    
    ehicle parking regulations. 
    It should be an interesting discussion!  I'll see you there! 

    It was a Beautiful day for a picnic! 
    By Fred Fowler 
    Greetings, neighbors.  A few days ago we all got to enjoy the 2010 
    installment of a time honored Lakewood tradition – the annual LVNA 
    Picnic.  It was a beautiful day.  We had LOTS of food.  Many neigh-
    bors came out to eat, drink, and reacquaint themselves with other 
    neighbors they hadn't seen since last year.  It was a time to be happy 
    and enjoy our special community, to celebrate the end of the summer 
    and prepare for the fall.  Once again, a little Lakewood magic was at 
    work.  A good time was had by all who took part. 
    This particular picnic has had special meaning for me.  For the first 
    time since I moved into Lakewood Village I actually played a central 
    role in the organization and staging of the event.  In years past I had 
    organized volunteers for Dumpster Day, helped pull together the  
    Holiday Parade, and recruited judges for the Lighting Contest.  
    Somehow someone else had always handled the picnic               
    arrangements. 
    Back in 1996 and 1997 when I was first president of the LVNA the 
    job was handled by the selfless efforts of Mel and Elena Anderson.  
    Elena especially set an example with her fearless solicitation of   
    donations from local businesses.  I was pleased to recognize her 
    efforts in a small way by giving her an Award of Excellence when I 
    was Sunnyvale's Mayor.  Elena set an example and also set a    
    standard that is tough to live up to.  These days she is not feeling 
    very well and not up to any volunteer work.  When Wendy Stanley 
    volunteered to handle the picnic logistics this year as long as     
    someone else would deal with the food, I decided that I would take 
    up the challenge myself, for Elena's sake.  If Wendy could find     
    people to cook it and serve it, I would take responsibility for making 
    sure there was food on the table. 
    It turned out that Wendy and I weren't the only ones who took      
    personal responsibility for making sure the picnic took place.  At the  
    LVNA meeting about 10 days prior to the picnic two other key      
    volunteers stepped forward.  One was Leah Lane (Jim and Helen 
    Lane's daughter).  Leah said that her late father always made sure 
    there was a big cake at the picnic, so she would make sure one was 
    there as well. 
    The other key volunteer turned out to be former LVNA Secretary Sa-
    rah Daugherty.  She said she wasn't afraid to go around and ask for 
    donations from various local businesses.  She said she would help 
    me with that chore.  In the end she earned the title of “professional 
    scrounger”, and provided some of the food and most of the raffle 
    prizes.  She did this by simply going to various local companies and 
    asking them to give something.  She kept after them until they did!  
    She certainly proved that she was in Elena's league.  We all owe 
    her a great debt of gratitude. 
    BEGGING FOR FOOD 
    My own efforts got off to a late start.  My load at work has been 
    very heavy lately and it had been hard to find any spare time.  I 
    finally got started just one week before the picnic was supposed to 
    take place!  I followed that up by taking a day off from work on the 
    following Tuesday.  I was afraid that I had set aside so little time 
    that there would be no chance of success.  I was afraid that there 
    would not be enough food to go around. 
    As it was, the day was saved by the extraordinary generosity of 
    almost two dozen Sunnyvale businesses.  All I had to do was to 
    say that the Lakewood Village is a community of good people..  
    The LVNA organizes the picnic to make sure everyone has the 
    chance to enjoy a nice community luncheon during these tough 
    times.  We do this by asking for help from local businesses.  If a 
    business can't help, that's OK.  If a business CAN help, they will 
    earn our undying gratitude and a big “Thank You” in the next LVNA 
    Villager newsletter.

    there were actually a great many of them that were very glad to

    complete list of who they are and what they gave. 
    The Lion and Compass Restaurant at 1023 North Fair Oaks.  
    They gave us 100 hamburger patties.  It turned out that they gave 
    us all the hamburger we needed.

    The day started out as the usual “mad rush” to get everything together 
    in the right place at the right time.  Food had to be picked up from the 
    donors. Charcoal fires had to be lit – not too soon and not too late.  
    Tables had to be set up.  Plates, cups, serving tools, cooking utensils 
    all had to be marshaled and set out.  It was a scramble to get ready. 
    Then the people started to come.  First in ones and twos, then in threes 
    and fours.  At one point just before 11:30am I wondered if many people 
    would even show up for the picnic at all.  (I needn't have worried!) 
    At noon we had a treat.  Earlier we had been contacted by the director 
    of the Lakewood School Color Guard, asking if they could take part 
    somehow.  They were promptly invited to open the picnic with a 
    presentation of the colors!  They did their job magnificently, to the tune 
    of the Star Spangled Banner.  After their formal advance, salute and 
    retirement the feasting got underway.  They and their parents joined in 
    the meal. 
    At 12:30 we had another treat.  This time it was the Peninsulaires 
    Barbershop Harmony Chorus that did the honors.  They are a group of 
    men who enjoy singing and performing together, and they treated us to 
    a performance of many “old favorites” arranged for close and intricate 
    harmony.  Their songs set a wonderful mood for the entire picnic!  
    When they were done singing they sat down and joined in the meal. 
    Before long every seat at every table was packed.  Old and new friends 
    were exchanging news and opinions between bites of delicious food.  
    The main food table itself seemed to be groaning under the weight of all 
    the bounty that had been assembled.  In addition to all of the items 
    Sarah Daugherty and I had “scrounged”, many neighbors brought 
    contributions of their own.  It was a magnificent feast indeed! 
     
    THE END OF THE STORY 
     
    And then it was over!  Friends said their goodbye's, the clean-up crew 
    tidied things up and the (very few) leftovers were taken away.  By about 
    3:30pm there was no trace of the picnic that had been.  It had vanished 
    like a cloud, almost as if it had never happened. 
    There is an artist named “Cristo” who creates strange works of art, such 
    as the “Running Fence” that stretched across half the width of 
    California about twenty years ago.  His pieces are not meant to last.  
    He claims that the “art” of the pieces lies in the experience of making 
    them, not in the resulting end product. 
    I don't particularly like the works of Cristo, but I understand what he 
    means.  Putting the picnic together was like that.  The experience of 
    finding food for 100 or more people was a very rich and rewarding one 
    for me.  Watching the food and all of the other “moving parts” of the 
    event come together and mesh so well had a bit of “art” to it.  We pulled 
    it off!  There is a very satisfying feeling in being able to say that. 
    Now all we have to do is to start planning for next year....
