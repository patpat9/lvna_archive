    President’s Column 
    -Michael Majchrowicz 
    The next Lakewood Village Neighborhood Association 
    event is less than a month away! Our annual Holiday 
    Parade, on Saturday, December 1, is the only one in 
    Sunnyvale. We can take pride in our parade! 
    There are many opportunities for volunteers and 
    participants. Please attend the next LVNA meeting on 
    November 14 at 7:00 p.m. to discuss the parade and 
    offer help, if you can. 
    Our LVNA meeting attendance has been increasing, and 
    I encourage more of you to come. These meetings help 
    us work toward improving our neighborhood. For those 
    who have recently moved into Lakewood Village, come 
    to the monthly meetings to meet neighbors and to learn 
    who has answers to your questions about Sunnyvale and 
    our neighborhood. ■

    Lakewood Village Holiday Parade! Participants will

    CFL Bulbs Will Save Money for LV Residents 
    -Ursula Syrova 
    Compact fluorescent light bulbs (CFLs) from Pacific 
    Gas & Electric (PG&E) went to many Lakewood Village 
    residents on October 27, 2007. The 32 volunteers who 
    delivered the free bulbs weren’t able to cover the whole 
    neighborhood, but 277 bulbs were distributed. The 
    volunteers included 11 Villager walkers. 
    According to PG&E, a compact fluorescent bulb will 
    last ten times as long as an incandescent bulb and uses 
    significantly less energy, so one bulb will save you $47 
    over its lifetime. The volunteer distributors just saved 
    the Lakewood Village residents who took the free bulbs 
    more than $13,000! Every year those 277 bulbs are used 
    instead of incandescent bulbs, 41,550 pounds of carbon 
    dioxide will be kept out of the environment, and this 
    reduction will help to combat global warming. 
    Cool Cities Sunnyvale, the Volunteer Center of Silicon 
    Valley, Acterra, and the Lakewood Village 
    Neighborhood Association formed the partners for this 
    bulb giveaway. If you are interested in learning more 
    about any of these groups, please contact Ursula at 408738-3092 or vicepresident1@lvna.net. ■

    November in Your Yard and 
    Garden 
    ☼•Finish planting spring bulbs. 
    ☼Shop for native plants and set them in 
    the garden. 
    ☼Complete fall cleanup in the garden. 
    ☼Despite the drop in the temperature, composting 
    continues. Add disease-free leaves and chopped-up, 
    dead annuals to the compost pile. Remember to keep 
    the pile as damp as a wrung-out sponge for faster 
    composting. 
    ☼Keep after the snails. 
    ☼Watch out for ants invading the house from the 
    garden. 
    ☼Wrap vulnerable, young tree trunks, particularly 
    citrus trees, with paper or burlap to protect them 
    from freezing. 
    ☼Water plants as needed. 
    ☼Stabilize plants that might be harmed by autumn 
    wind. 
    ☼Prune wisteria and clematis to control size and 
    encourage new flowering wood in spring. Lightly 
    prune forsythia. 
    ☼Water roses but withhold all fertilizer. When the 
    ground freezes, mulch the rose bed heavily. 
    ☼Finish transplanting fall- and winter-flowering 
    plants into the bedding plot. 
    ☼Harvest what is left of the summer vegetables 
    before the rain and cold destroy them. Continue to 
    plant the winter vegetable garden with cool-season 
     
     
     
     
    crops such as beets, broccoli, cabbage, carrots, 
    cauliflower, onions, parsnips, and turnips. If you have 
    put the vegetable garden to rest over the winter, cover 
    it with manure so that the manure will age properly 
    over the winter. 
    ☼Flush your watering system to remove mineral 
    deposits. Soak all newly transplanted plants until the 
    rains come. 
    These tips are from the book Northern California Gardening: 
    A Month-by-Month Guide, by Katherine Grace Endicott, 
    published by Chronicle Books, San Francisco, copyright 1996, 
    and are used with the publisher’s permission. ■

    PTA To Sponsor Waste Collection Event on 
    November 17 
    To raise money for classroom supplies, field trips, 
    and other expenses, the Lakewood School ParentTeacher Association will collect recyclable waste on 
    Saturday, November 17 at the school. The recycling 
    company will give the Lakewood School PTA 
    money based on the weight of all the items you 
    bring on November 17. You can help the school and 
    help the environment too. Please share this 
    information with your employers, friends, and 
    family. 
    Items Accepted: Computers, monitors, televisions, 
    printers, DVD players, VCRs, keyboards, power 
    cables, cell phones, video cameras, stereos, MP3 
    players, fax machines, projectors, scanners, insulated 
    wire, electrical wire, copper, brass, iron, tin, 
    aluminum, stainless steel, radiators, transmissions, 
    washers, dryers, refrigerators, vacuum cleaners, 
    small appliances … if you can plug it in, you can 
    bring it in! Newspapers, books, paper, and cardboard 
    will also be accepted. 
    Take your items to Lakewood School, 750 
    Lakechime Drive, on Saturday, November 17, 
    2007, between 10 a.m. and 3 p.m. ■

    dog Chaz:

    take time out of your day to make 
    sure that our dog was safe. We are

    Just a friendly reminder that it's 
    so important to keep your pet on a 
    leash while walking it in the 
    neighborhood. No matter how much 
    you think your sweet baby is 
    trained, there are so many 
    distractions that can happen, and 
    there have been many tragedies over 
    the years that back up the fact 
    that no one is exempt. Also, there 
    are still quite a few pets that 
    seem to roam freely. Everyone has 
    had the occasional accident, where 
    their pet gets out of the yard, but 
    there are numerous offenders that 
    can be seen on any given day 
    roaming the streets. That is just 
    an accident waiting to happen. The 
    law requires pets to be either 
    enclosed in a yard or on a leash. 
    Also, using a "pooper scooper" is 
    not just a courtesy, it's also a 
    requirement. 
    The Tosh Family on Tucson Avenue
