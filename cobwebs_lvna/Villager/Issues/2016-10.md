    City of Sunnyvale Expanded 
    Smoking Ordinance  
    Effective Oct. 1st  
     
    The City of Sunnyvale has updated 
    its smoking law to provide additional 
    protection from the harmful effects 
    of secondhand smoke by expanding no smoking   
    areas. 
     
    The new ordinance goes into effect October 1, 2016 
    and prohibits smoking: 
    •  in all outdoor dining areas, anywhere food or drink is 
    consumed;  
    •  within 25 feet of building doors and windows;  
    •  within 25 feet of service areas such as transit stops;  
    •  at public events such as farmers’ markets;  
    •  on South Murphy Avenue between Washington  
    Avenue and Evelyn Avenue; and  
    •  in all public parks, including golf courses.  
    The ordinance also expands the smoking prohibition to 
    multi-family housing properties with two or more units 
    including apartments, condominiums and townhomes; 
    multifamily no-smoking areas now include: 
    •  multi-family housing common areas (indoors and 
    outdoors);  
    •  within 25 feet of multi-family housing doors and  
    windows; and  
    •  inside units, including shared balconies and patios.  
     
    Smoking remains the leading cause of preventable 
    death in the United States and exposure to 
    secondhand smoke is responsible for an estimated 
    41,000 heart-disease related and lung cancer deaths 
    each year. According to the Centers for Disease 
    Control, the only way to fully protect nonsmokers is to 
    eliminate smoking in all homes, worksites and public 
    places.  
     
    The City is committed to creating a healthier  
    community for all residents and visitors, and 
    Sunnyvale joins more than 100 California  
    municipalities that require smoke-free outdoor areas 
    and nearly 40 communities that require smoke-free 
    multi-family housing within some or all units. An online 
    survey was conducted by the City 
    at OpenCityHall.inSunnyvale.com, nearly 90 percent 
    of respondents favored outdoor dining and around 
    business entryways as smoke-free areas. The  
    smoke-free ordinance updates also will raise the City’s 
    grade from a ―D‖ to a ―B‖ in the American Lung  
    Association’s State of Tobacco Control Report. To 
    help educate the community about the updated law, 
    businesses and multi-family housing are required to 
    post ―no smoking‖ signs. A limited number of signs are 
    available from the City by calling 408-730-7610 
    or emailing np@sunnyvale.ca.gov. 
    For more information about the law, visit  
    Smoking.inSunnyvale.com.

    Animal Assisted  
    Happiness (AAH) was 
    founded at the home of 
    Peter Higa and Vicki 
    Amon-Higa in January 
    2009 as a place for  
    children with special needs to interact with animals 
    and find happiness. It began with Vicki and Peter’s 
    barnyard animals. ―We began by the inspiration of our 
    mini horse, Lollipop, going to see Riley Church who 
    could no longer leave her home given a battle with 
    cancer.‖ Peter said. Lollipop went into Riley’s living 
    room and spent 1 ½ hours giving her love – the  
    inspiration for AAH. 
     
    What began by creating our first smile for Riley has 
    grown to have over 29,173 smiles for kids with needs 
    with over 1,399 explicit visits as of September 2016. 
    Our registered volunteers now number over 1,170 
    who have donated more than 13,311 hours to help us 
    create these smiles. We now have three programs:  
    our AAH Mobile Barnyard Friends which visits more 
    than 50 schools/programs/hospitals and events for 
    kids with special needs, our AAH Barnyard visits at 
    the AAH Happy Farm located in Sunnyvale where 
    families can visit with their children to ―experience a 
    smile only animals can bring: and our new program of 
    Vocational Education for kids with Special Needs 
    to learn and contribute to our AAH Happy Farm.  
     
    Our AAH Happy Farm was built in the fall of 2015 
    with the help of 300 AAH Volunteers who donated 
    over 200 hours over five weekends. More information 
    can be found at: 
    www.animalassistedhappiness.org
