    August in Your Yard 
    and Garden 
     
    ☼Continue to weed. Unfortunately as 
    long as it’s the growing season, it is 
    also the weeding season. Add young, 
    seedhead-free weeds and excess 
    summer growth to the compost pile. 
    Remember to keep the pile as damp as 
    a wrung-out sponge for faster composting. 
    ☼Keep after the snails 
    ☼Use a strong spray from the hose to wash away 
    aphids. 
    ☼Pick up fallen fruit and vegetables (particularly if 
    they are planted near the house) to discourage ants 
    from forming colonies near your dwelling. 
    ☼Insulate the garden soil from the hot, dry weather by 
    applying a two-inch-thick mulch. 
    ☼Fertilize plants if you see signs of pale, lackluster 
    growth. Yellow leaves with green veins often indicate 
    an iron deficiency that can be corrected with a chelated 
    iron. Fertilize the lawn. Fertilize all flowering plants 
    about to bloom. Fertilize roses, except most antique 
    roses. 
    ☼Pinch off dead flowers. In particular pick off spent 
    blooms from crape myrtles to bring on extra flowers in 
    the fall. Make sure you get the entire flower head—
    particularly the swollen ovaries where seeds form. 
    ☼Hold off transplanting fall- and winter-flowering 
    plants into the bedding plot. Hot weather is hard on 
    newly transplanted plants. If you do transplant, shade 
    them for a few days with the temporary shelter of an 
    umbrella or wooden shake. Stake tall sprawling 
    flowers. 
    ☼Harvest vegetables as they ripen. Pick them early in 
    the day. 
    ☼Continue to monitor your watering system. Soak all 
    newly transplanted plants. 
    These tips are from the book Northern California 
    Gardening: A Month-by-Month Guide, by Katherine 
    Grace Endicott, published by Chronicle Books, San 
    Francisco, copyright 1996, and are used with the 
    publisher’s permission.■

    How 911 Works in Sunnyvale 
    Martin Dale, the lieutenant in charge of the Sunnyvale 
    Department of Public Safety Crime Prevention Unit, 
    responded to a Lakewood Village resident’s concern 
    about calling 911 as follows. 
    “The question of appropriate use of 911 is an ongoing 
    debate throughout this nation. The answer I provide 
    may very well not apply to other areas of this state, let 
    alone other parts of the nation. Sunnyvale is blessed 
    with a state-of-the-art emergency communications 
    system staffed by award-winning dispatchers. We also 
    live in a community where we have not, at least to this 
    point in the history of our city, been so overwhelmed 
    with incoming emergency calls so as to have ‘true’ 
    emergencies be bypassed or put on hold.  
    “From the Sunnyvale Department of Public Safety, our 
    message is this: if you have an immediate need for 
    police, fire, or emergency medical personnel, call us 
    via 911. Our dispatchers prioritize the calls for the level 
    of response warranted. Yes, that means that the person 
    calling in a robbery in progress may be calling at the 
    exact time someone else is dialing 911 about a 
    neighbor’s barking dog. However, those calls would 
    not be dispatched nor responded to at the same priority.  
    We encourage the use of 911 for immediate police, fire, 
    or medical needs. If the need is not immediate, please 
    call the Desk Officer at 730-7110. The Desk Officer 
    will create a case file for a responding officer or will 
    forward the call to Dispatch for a higher priority 
    response.”■

    Helmets Required for Children 
     
    It’s just common sense to want to ensure your child’s 
    safety, but if for some reason you don’t think your 
    child needs a helmet when riding a bike, skateboard, 
    inline skates, or a scooter, be aware of the following: 
    According to California Vehicle Code Section 21212, 
    bike helmets are required for anybody under the 
    age of 18 when riding a bike, skateboard, inline skates, 
    or scooter. 
    For a first citation for riding without a helmet, the 
    juvenile is required to attend a juvenile traffic diversion 
    class with his/her parent or guardian. Cost of the class 
    is $7. Upon successful completion, the citation is 
    dismissed. If a juvenile fails to attend the class or 
    receives a second citation, the citation is forwarded to 
    the Juvenile Traffic Court for fine assessment. 
    So please, for your children’s safety, have them wear 
    helmets when riding bikes, skateboards, inline skates, 
    or scooters. Helmets can protect children from brain 
    injury, and having your children wear them could save 
    you some money! Guess who’s liable for the fine?■

    Letter 
    Dear Editor, 
    Please remind all of the neighborhood 
    to be considerate of their noise 
    levels, especially during the summer. 
    We need to remember that our houses 
    are very close to each other and that 
    the record heat causes some of us to 
    have all our windows open. In these 
    conditions, noise travels a lot 
    farther than we might expect. 
    Every time my neighbors turn their 
    stereos or their TVs up, or have a 
    late night loud party, all of their 
    noise goes directly into my bedroom. 
    This makes it impossible to get any 
    sleep. Every time my neighbors turn 
    their stereos up in the afternoon on 
    the weekends, it comes right back 
    into my back yard, where I am 
    enjoying a quiet respite from a hard 
    week at work. 
    With some of my neighbors, it is not 
    an issue: they have turned their 
    sound down upon request. With some of 
    my other neighbors, who do not speak 
    English, I have to keep calling the 
    police.  
    Thank you for listening. 
    A long-time Lakewood Village resident 
    Editor’s note: Some Lakewood Village residents 
    apparently don’t realize that loud music, TV, and 
    other noise disturb their  neighbors. If you can, please 
    remind the offenders that they are violating city noise 
    ordinances.■

    City News  
    Council Agendas Now Available Earlier 
    In an effort to allow for more community input to 
    Sunnyvale City Council deliberations, meeting agendas 
    are now available a day earlier. Historically Council 
    meeting agendas were posted in public places, on the 
    city’s Web site―www.sunnyvale.ca.gov―and on 
    KSUN-15 by the end of the Friday preceding a 
    Tuesday meeting. The information will now be 
    available late Thursday. 
    Council established a six-month pilot program to test 
    the earlier posting, after which it will be evaluated and 
    considered as an ongoing practice. It had been brought 
    to Council’s attention that the Friday postings did not 
    leave businesses, neighborhood groups, and concerned 
    individuals enough time to research a topic coming 
    before Council. Additionally, Council wanted to ensure 
    there was enough time for community members to get 
    together with each other to jointly address Council on 
    an issue. The new, earlier posting will provide the 
    agenda three full business days, plus the weekend, prior 
    to a regularly scheduled City Council meeting. 
    The Sunnyvale City Council meets at 7 p.m. most 
    Tuesdays in the City Council Chambers, 456 W. Olive 
    Avenue  
     
    Council Policies Available On Web 
    The City of Sunnyvale has posted its City Council 
    Policy Manual on its Web site, making this reference 
    tool easily available to the public.  
    The manual is a collection of policies established by 
    the City Council which provides guidelines to assist in 
    carrying out the City’s General Plan. Policies related to 
    land use and transportation, community development, 
    environmental management, public safety, socio- 
    economic programs, cultural programs, and planning 
     
     
    and management are all detailed in the Council Policy 
    Manual. 
    The Council Policy Manual can be found by visiting 
    the City’s Web site―www.sunnyvale.ca.gov―and 
    then clicking on the City Council link near the top of 
    the page. The manual is listed in the Council Related 
    Documents section and includes the Code of Conduct 
    for Elected Officials and the Code of Ethics.■ 
     
    Sunnyvale Mentors Wanted 
    Friends for Youth, a 27-year-old agency serving at-risk 
    youth residing in San Mateo and northern Santa Clara 
    counties is seeking mentors for kids in Sunnyvale. This 
    program creates and supports one-to-one friendships 
    between youth-in-need and caring adults. Training and 
    ongoing support, in addition to group activities each 
    month, are provided.  
    Mentoring is a rewarding and enjoyable way to make a 
    positive impact in the life of a young person simply by 
    sharing your time and having fun. Currently, there are 
    more than 50 young people on the waiting list who 
    actively wish to have a mentor.  
    If you are interested in becoming a mentor, please 
    contact (650) 482-2867 or send an e-mail to 
    volunteer@friendsforyouth.org.■
