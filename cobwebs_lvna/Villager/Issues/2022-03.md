    The annual LVNA board member elections are this coming 
    Wednesday, March 9th, 7:00 – 8:00 PM. Elections will again 
    be virtual this year with the meeting credentials appearing 1

    Are you passionate about our neighborhood? Do you enjoy 
    meeting new neighbors? Would you be overjoyed to help 
    build a tight-knit (Lakewood) Village for yourself, your friends, 
    and the next generation who’s currently growing up right 
    outside your door or even in your own backyard? Perhaps 
    you should consider running for one of the five LVNA board 
    positions. We have 2 members who will be stepping down this 
    year. The board meets once per month to discuss ongoing 
    activities, in addition to the monthly general meeting where 
    we invite guest speakers to discuss topics of interest for the 
    neighborhood (currently still virtual due to COVID) and the few 
    seasonal events each year. The level of commitment is up to 
    each individual – as these are all completely volunteer roles. All 
    of the incumbent board members are happy to shuffle around, 
    so, any of the 5 roles are available for the 2022-2023 term 
    (March 2022 – March 2023). The only requirements are that you 
    are 18+ and live in the neighborhood (renters included).
    President, 1st VP, 2nd VP, Treasurer, and Secretary 
    Want to help, but not interested in a board position? Our Villager editors will also be stepping down after 2.5 
    years so the role of Villager Editor is open to anyone interested (no age requirement). At a minimum, the role 
    consists of proof-reading the content and compiling it all into a single document. The sky is the limit though 
    so the previous editors have helped write some of that content, design a new layout, take photos for fun 
    backgrounds, and send it out to the Villager subscribers each month. The board is happy to help with these other 
    aspects though so you have as much creative freedom as you’d like.
    Want to partner up with a family member or friend? Great! Are you a high school student interested in writing or 
    media design and trying to increase your community service hours to meet a graduation or Scouts requirement? 
    This is your chance to feed a couple ducks with a single loaf of bread! (See how much fun this role can be? You 
    can make up your own idioms if you don’t like killing birds with stones.)
    Any of these roles are a great opportunity to help actively drive change for our community and make a positive 
    impact on our neighborhood. If interested, please email the board and let them know you’re on board (puns – 
    love it)!  -   board@lvnasv.org

    SUPPORT FOR OUTDOOR CATS
    Kitten season is already here! Santa Clara County has over 150,000 community (also known as free-roaming 
    or feral) cats. How to manage all of these cats? Humane Society of Silicon Valley (our local animal shelter) and 
    many other organizations support the trap-neuter-return (TNR) program. Cats are spayed or neutered (“fixed”) 
    and vaccinated. Eligible cats go up for adoption, while feral cats are returned to the exact spot where they were 
    trapped. This keeps the community cat population healthier, reduces the number of community kittens born, 
    and controls the spread of disease.
    If you are feeding outdoor community cats, please make sure the cats are fixed and each cat has a left ear 
    clipped. Humane Society Silicon Valley (HSSV) offers FREE spay/neuter, vaccinations, flea treatment, microchip, 
    and deworming for Sunnyvale feral and community cats. 
    Go to bayareacats.org/hssv to get started on how to trap your cats.
    A few helpful reminders:
    •	
    No appointments needed for Sunnyvale cats. Once you’ve trapped the cat, bring it to HSSV intake between 
    9:00am and 6:00pm (7 days a week). If you are unable to transport, call Sunnyvale Animal Control (408-730-
    7178) and they can transport the cat for you.
    •	
    For a TNR with same-day pick-up, schedule an appointment by emailing HSSV at medicalcenter@hssv.org
    Why does this matter? Without TNR, kittens suffer from worms, flea anemia, diarrhea, lung infections, eye 
    infections, predators, and could even be killed by other cats. Due to repeated pregnancies and the demands 
    of nursing kittens, mother cats are often underweight and overworked. Tomcats fight and are at risk of getting 
    severely injured, spreading diseases like FIV and feline leukemia, and being hit by cars. Spaying/neutering cats 
    also keeps feral populations smaller; a typical female cat who is not spayed will have 2-3 litters per year with up 
    to 8 kittens per litter.
    Help keep our community cat populations healthy and safe! If you have questions, file a TNR request for 
    guidance at bayareacats.org/tnr. If you need help with spaying/neutering your outdoor cat, please email 
    bayareatnr@gmail.com.
    Thank you for doing your part!
    ADDITIONAL RESOURCES FOR PET OWNERS
    Do you have pets but are struggling to feed them due to financial insecurity? HSSV offers a Pet Pantry program 
    to help pet owners continue to care for their pets. There is also support to spay/neuter your pet. Find out more 
    information here: https://www.hssv.org/pet-pantry/ 
    There are several reasons to get your pet fixed: health benefits include reduced cancer risk and fewer behavioral 
    issues, and community benefits include reduced dog bites and less pressure on animal shelters. There used 
    to be several free or low-cost options for Santa Clara County residents to spay/neuter their pet, but current 
    options are very limited due to covid. For more information on spaying/neutering your pet, please contact your
