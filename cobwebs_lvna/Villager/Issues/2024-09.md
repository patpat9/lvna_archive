    Library Bond Measure
    The November 5 ballot will include a vote for a $290 million bond measure to construct a new Main Library in downtown
     Sunnyvale. This bond measure does NOT impact the Lakewood Library branch project, and it requires a two-thirds majority in 
    order to pass.
    The current Sunnyvale Public Library (665 W Olive Ave) was built in 1960. The building has ongoing plumbing, wiring, and roof
     issues that require frequent, costly maintenance. The current wiring also places limits on upgrading the information technology 
    to meet community needs.
    As a result of renovations, the current library is now about 60,000 square feet, twice the size of the original library. In this time,
     the population of Sunnyvale has more than tripled. In the last year, customers checked out over 1,700,000 physical and digita
    l items, and Library programs were attended by roughly 55,000 people. However, the current library no longer meets the needs 
    of the community, and it has limited space for children’s programming, community group meetings, cultural programs, study are
    as, etc. 
    The bond measure would finance construction of a new library, which would be 120,000 square feet and three stories tall. The
     increased space would support 30% more space for the library collection and four times more space for dedicated children an
    d teens areas. The construction of the new Main Library is part of Phase Two of the 2018 City Civic Center Master Plan. 
    If the bond passes, the estimated initial cost would be $27.47 per $100,000 of assessed (not market) value, applicable to both
     residential and commercial properties. The rate would decline over the lifetime of the bond. The City has a AAA bond rating, s
    o the City can use the bond to borrow at very low interest rates (lower than other forms of borrowing). The bond is supported 
    by the leading members of the Sunnyvale Chamber of Commerce, argue that libraries are good for business, help companies f
    ind valuable information, and create places for cultural engagement with community members. 
    For additional information (including the text of the bond measure, a Q&A, and a fact sheet), visit the City’s “Library Bond

    Measure Y - Renewal of Sunnyvale School District Parcel Tax
    Also on the November 5 ballot is Measure Y, which is a vote to renew an existing $59 per year parcel tax for the
     Sunnyvale School District (SSD). SSD supports 8 elementary schools (including Fairwood Elementary and Lakewoo
    d Elementary) and 2 middle schools (including Columbia Middle School). 
    The parcel tax was first approved in 2011. A “yes” vote on Measure Y votes to renew parcel tax for another eight
     years. Measure Y only passes if two-thirds of votes support it. The tax is $59 per parcel (or dwelling unit) per year. The $59 parcel tax is also less than the nearby school districts such as Palo Alto ($905), Mountain View Whisman ($191), and Saratoga ($68) .
    The parcel tax raises about $1.1 million dollars annually to support classroom programs.
     The parcel tax funds help the district keep class sizes smaller, support qualified 
    teachers, and maintain computers and technology at each school.
    An Independent Community Oversight Committee of local residents monitors and
     reviews all parcel tax expenditures to ensure funds are spent for the benefit of loca
    l students. No money from the parcel tax is spent on district administration or pensions. 
    People with exemptions from the current parcel tax will be exempt from this parcel tax
     automatically. There are also exemptions available for anyone who is at least 65 years 
    old and/or disabled, and owns and resides in their home.
