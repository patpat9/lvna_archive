    Celebration and appreciation through recognition 
     
      Welcome back as we begin a new year with the LVNA. 
    Last month (March) brought to a close a successful year for 
    the LVNA.  It was our chance to reflect on the previous 
    year's events and an opportunity to recognize and honor all 
    of the people, institutions, and companies that made it 
    possible. So we ended this last year in celebration and in 
    appreciation. The city Parks Department joined us at our 
    meeting last month.  They asked to join in with us as we 
    celebrated the volunteers, and as they wished to share in the 
    celebration of the completion of the Hetch Hetchy 
    Greenway. Curtis Black, Superintendent of Parks, and Jack 
    Mastilock, Senior Parks Leader were on hand with a slide 
    presentation 
    that 
    documented 
    and 
    highlighted 
    the 
    renovations that were completed on the Greenway.  They 
    brought with them the food and refreshments for the 
    evening.  Thank you to them. We then held a very informal 
    ceremony to hand out certificates of appreciation to the 
    volunteers that made this year possible.  Without the 
    volunteer support, there would be no newsletter, picnic, 
    parade or forum to meet with our city representatives.  
    Thank you to our silent champions both at large. Thank you. 
     
    LVNA Board Election Results 
     
      Then it was time for elections for the New Year.  The 
    elections happen once a year.  It’s an open nominee ballot, 
    meaning that anyone with an interest can run to become a 
    board member.  The term of the board members run for one 
    year with a two-year term limit. The elections this year saw 
    only one newcomer, John Mayer, as our new 1st Vice 
    president.  Elena Anderson, 2nd Vice President; Tom 
    Freeman, Secretary; Bob Lacy, treasurer; myself (Michael 
    Jenesse), President.  We all return for a second term. We 
    want to thank Rick Flovin who steps down from the board 
    after 3 years of active service.  Thank you Rick for your 
    help, support, and encouragement over this past year, I have 
    really appreciated it. 
     
    Dick's Supermarket update 
     
      Now, we begin again and we continue. First with an update 
    on Dick's Supermarket.  As you may recall, a Mr. Metz of 
    the Starlite Ballroom was very interested in leasing the 
    Dick's Supermarket space after its close in March.  In order 
    for him to lease the space, he had to have a Special 
    Development Permit from the city, because his business did 
    not meet the zoning ordinance for the type of retail space 
    (see Feb. newsletter) that Dick's is listed under. Initially, the 
    Director of Community Development denied his request for 
    the special permit.  Mr. Metz decided to appeal the denial 
    before the City Planning Commission.  On March 12th, Mr. 
    Metz's appeal was heard by the planning commission.  The 
    planning commission upheld the Director of Community 
    Development's denial of the Special Development Permit. 
    Mr. Yee, the owner of the Dick's Supermarket space, used 
    the planning commission meeting to announce that he is 
    currently in negotiations with an ethnic market to have them 
    lease the space. We have no further information about this 
    development, but we will be watching it closely and will be 
    updating any information as soon as possible.

    April Meeting: City staff and survey results 
     
      This brings us up to the present time.  We have a very 
    exciting meeting this month. City Staff will be joining us 
    with the results of the survey that went out with the 
    December 2000 newsletter.  The survey had a list of 
    ordinance issues and asked that you rank each issue in level 
    of importance. Our neighborhood survey coincided with a 
    greater survey that was being done citywide at that time. Our 
    guest that evening will be: Ernie Bakin the Director of 
    Public Safety, Bob Paternoster the newly hired Director of 
    Community Development, Marvin Rose the Director of 
    Public Works, and Christy Gunvalsen the Supervisor of 
    Housing and Neighborhood Preservation. They will be 
    speaking about the results of the two surveys and what the 
    overall plan of action will be.  Traffic Engineering and 
    traffic calming measures joining them will be Joe Avila of 
    Traffic Engineering to talk about traffic calming measures.  
    He will present information about the steps it takes to get 
    certain traffic calming measures into place.  This is an 
    excellent opportunity to meet with key city officials, and it is 
    an invaluable opportunity to find out what it takes to institute 
    traffic calming measures. This is an opportunity to find out 
    firsthand what the challenges are for the city and for us the 
    residents of Lakewood Village to institute these type of 
    measures. From what I understand, it takes a lot of effort to 
    get some of these measures into place, for example, round-abouts or speed humps.  This is a very key and rare 
    opportunity to get involved in a cause that has definite safety 
    ramifications for the residents, especially with the children. 
    Traffic calming has been a hot issue with the LVNA for 
    several years. The reason is the amount of red tape it takes to 
    wade through to get a result.  We are asking all to please to 
    come out and join us on this evening to find out what it is 
    going to take to get some changes.  We need your help and 
    support. If there ever was an issue that needs a lot of support 
    to see it through this is one of them.  We need your help.  
    The results of this opportunity will be to make the village a 
    much safer area for all of us: both the residents of the 
    neighborhood and those that are passing through.  And this 
    is a key opportunity to bring about a change or the next step 
    without having to use a crisis to do it-someone having to get 
    hurt or worse die in order for it to happen.  
     
    Next Meeting 
     
     Please we ask you join us on Wednesday evening, April 
    18th at Fairwood Elementary School. Maybe together, we 
    can bring about a change for greater safety, where it is 
    needed first, in this place where we call home. We will be 
    meeting in the cafeteria/auditorium at the front of the school.  
    The school is located at 1110 Fairwood Ave.  The meeting is 
    scheduled from 7pm to 8pm. See you there.

    Dear Editor, 
     
    We need an article in an upcoming issue of the LVNA 
    Villager about a problem that persists here in Lakewood 
    village, i.e., dog owners who allow their dogs to run around 
    without a leash.  Taking my daily walk is something I enjoy 
    doing every day, and it's something I feel I have a right to 
    do.  After all, I live here and pay taxes here.  I also deliver 
    the LVNA Villager once a month.  Every now and again, I 
    get followed by an unleashed dog.  Also, whenever I 
    approach a house on my LVNA paper route having a large 
    aggressive dog that's clearly not being controlled by the 
    owner, I skip that house and move on to the next one.  My 
    personal safety has to come first.  Last Saturday, I was 
    walking down Palamos Ave., when a dog ran up to me from 
    across the street, jumped up and bit me on the arm.  I called 
    Sunnyvale Public Safety straight away and filed a complaint.  
    The owner of the dog (1163 Palamos Ave.) immediately 
    admitted that her dog had gotten out of the yard.  I told her 
    that was absolutely no excuse, and that one of these times 
    she could end up getting sued.  I wasn't seriously hurt, thank 
    goodness. Mind you, I won't include Palamos on my walking 
    route at any time soon.  It's just a little bit too scary!  If one 
    is going to have a pet, one must do so in a responsible and 
    neighborly manner.  Also, the city has leash laws, which 
    need more stringent enforcement.  In the meantime, I'll 
    probably take my next walk in Lakewood Village armed 
    with pepper spray and a cell phone, although I would prefer 
    to carry only a radio and my keys.  
     
    Gloria Burd 
    1078 Blazingwood Drive 
    Sunnyvale 94089

    PARKING HINTS 
     
    Remember to follow City Parking 
    regulations.  Failure to follow any of the 
    following Parking regulations could result 
    in you receiving a Citation and/or having 
    your vehicle abated.  
     
    θ SMC 19.48.260 - Vehicles must be parked on a 
    permanent paved surface.  The entire vehicle 
    must be parked on a permanent paved surface, 
    not just the tires. 
    θ SMC 9.24.180 - Inoperable vehicles parked on 
    private property can only be out in public view 
    for 72 consecutive hours; after that they must 
    be stored in a garage or a carport out of public 
    view. 
    θ SMC 10.16.130 - Vehicles parked on the street 
    must be licensed and operable, and cannot be 
    left in one location for more than 72 hours (3 
    days). 
    θ 22500(e) + 22500 (f) V.C. -  Vehicles should 
    be parked so they do not block or partially 
    block the public sidewalk or a neighbor’s 
    driveway. 
    θ SMC 
    9.26.030(s) 
    + 
    SMC 
    19.48.260 
    + 
    4000(a)V.C. - Boats must be licensed and 
    stored on a trailer which is operable and 
    registered.  Boats must also be covered and 
    parked on a permanent paved surface. 
     
     
    Please tell all of your 
    friends and neighbors to remember to 
    move their cars off the street on “Street
