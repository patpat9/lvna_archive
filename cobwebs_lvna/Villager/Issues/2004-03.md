    “Flooding may be closer than you 
    think” 
    - Brian Smithson 
     
    Perhaps you’ve heard the advertisements by the 
    Santa Clara Valley Water District. Or you’ve 
    lived here long enough to remember flooding 
    during the El Niño winter of 1997-8, or the 
    Alviso area flooding back in the 1980’s. If you’ve 
    lived here for a very long time, you might 
    remember 
    the 
    Christmas 
    1955 
    flood 
    in 
    Lakewood Village. Or perhaps during a recent 
    storm, you simply wondered “has it ever flooded 
    in Lakewood Village?”. The answer is yes, and 
    some parts of Lakewood Village are subject to a 
    small risk of flooding in the future. 
     
    How much risk, and where? 
     
    Take a look at the map on the next page. The 
    shaded areas of Lakewood Village have been 
    determined to be a 1% flood risk, which is also 
    known as a 100-year flood zone. What that 
    means is that there is a 1% chance each year 
    that flooding of more than one foot of water will 
    occur, or in other words, that such a flood will 
    occur once every 100 years. 
     
    The unshaded areas are much less likely to be 
    flooded and are considered to be a 500-year 
    flood zone, or a 2/10% risk. 
     
    If you live in or anywhere near the shaded area, 
    visit this web site to determine the exact risk for 
    your particular address: 
     
    http://www.valleywater.org/Water 
    /Watersheds_streams_and_floods 
    /Flooding_in_the_valley 
    /Is_your_property_at_risk.shtm 
     
    Only a 1% chance? Once in 100 years? 
     Why should I care? 
     
    Don’t be lulled into complacency by “once in 100 
    years”, because you never know which year in 
    the next 100 years will have a flood. It could be 
    next year. And even though the chances of a 
    flood are small, the damage can be great. Look 
    around your home and imagine what one foot of 
    mud and water would damage or destroy. Now 
    imagine two feet, or three feet. Also consider 
    that fast moving floodwaters can be very 
    dangerous for you, your children, and your pets.

    X continued from previous column 
     
    What should I do? 
     
    Here are a few of the most important points 
    about flood safety: 
     
    Before a flood: 
    ¾ Determine your property’s risk. Refer to the 
    web page listed earlier and look up your 
    exact home address. Identify the nearby 
    creek and your home’s elevation. 
    ¾ Check your insurance coverage. Most 
    homeowner’s and renter’s policies do not 
    cover flood damage. If you are in a 1% risk 
    area, consider getting flood insurance. 
    ¾ Educate yourself and your family. Make 
    plans. Put together emergency kits. The 
    Water District web site has more information 
    about this. 
     
    During a storm: 
    ¾ Stay tuned into local radio or television for 
    flood news and alerts. Check nearby storm 
    drains. Report problems to the Water 
    District. 
    ¾ Plug showers, tubs, and basins with stoppers 
    to help prevent sewer backflow into your 
    home. 
    ¾ Avoid unnecessary water usage, such as 
    laundry or bathing. 
     
    If a flood appears likely: 
    ¾ Collect what you’ll need in the event that 
    you need to evacuate: warm clothing, 
    prescription medicines, and other supplies 
    you might need for 24 hours or more. 
    ¾ Turn off your home’s electricity and gas 
    main only if instructed to do so. 
     
    Where can I get more information? 
     
    The best local resource for information is Santa 
    Clara Valley Water District’s web site, which you 
    can find at http://www.HeyNoah.com/. I’ve 
    taken some of the high points from that web 
    site to use in this article, but please look at the 
    web site for all of the details. In particular, 
    “Noah’s Floodsafe Guide” can be downloaded at: 
     
    http://www.valleywater.org 
    /Emergency_Info_and_Preparation 
    /FloodSAFE_Guide/index.shtm. 
     
    or, if you don’t have access to the web, call 
    (408) 265-2607 ext. 2238 and request a copy 
    be mailed to you. 

    - Brian Smithson

    General Motors 
    purchased 200 
    acres of land near 
    Lawrence and 
    Oakmead. Later, they decided to build 
    their auto manufacturing plant in 
    Fremont, otherwise it would have been 
    our neighbor!

    was started. 
    45 years ago: In 1959, Lakewood School was 
    completed, and the Lakewood Shell gas 
    station opened for business. 
    41 years ago: In 1963, Fire Station #6 was 
    dedicated. 
    34 years ago: In 1970, Sunnyvale Library 
    Bookmobile service started. 
    3 years ago: In 2001, the newly landscaped 
    Hetch Hetchy right-of-way (now called 
    the John W. Christian Greenbelt) was 
    opened for public use. 
    2 years ago: In 2002, the City of Sunnyvale 
    performed traffic and road improvement 
    studies designed to measure the impact 
    and mitigate the effects of Mercado 
    traffic on the Fairwood side.

    Where to Go and Who to Call for the 
    Best                           . 
    - Mar Junge 
     
    Finding 
    a 
    good 
    plumber, 
    tree 
    trimmer, 
    babysitter, etc., can be a challenge. We all have 
    horror stories about bad service, overcharges 
    and contractors who show up four hours late or 
    not at all. But what about the reliable, 
    reasonably priced, dependable vendors who 
    take pride in their businesses and work hard to 
    satisfy their clients?   
     
    Royal 
    Dry 
    Cleaning 
    in 
    our 
    neighborhood 
    shopping center is one that comes to mind.  
    Several months ago I cut out their 10% Off 
    Alternations coupon in The Villager. I took them 
    a floor-length prom dress that my teenage 
    daughter wanted shortened to knee length. This 
    dress had a sheer top layer, a second layer of a 
    taffeta-like material and a silk slip underneath. 
    Anyone who sews knows how hard it is to work 
    with these materials. They tend to bunch up or 
    slide sideways under the sewing machine 
    needle. Measuring, pinning and hemming those 
    three layers by hand would have taken me 
    hours. The seamstress at Royal Cleaners had 
    the dress altered in two days and charged me 
    only $37.50! I’ve gone back several times with 
    pants to be shortened, suit coats with sleeves to 
    be lengthened and shirts with small rips and 
    missing buttons. I’ve never been disappointed 
    with their service or prices. 
     
    Now it’s your turn. Who cuts your hair? Who has 
    the best burritos? Do you know a good car 
    mechanic? If you find one, let The Villager know 
    so that we that we can reward them with our 
    patronage. 

    President’s Column 
    - Phyllis Murphy 
    Hello Lakewood Villagers, 
     
    How fast time moves, it is already March.  This 
    means that it is starting to stay light longer.  It 
    also means that spring forward will be coming 
    soon.  I always hate losing that hour of sleep.  
    It takes me weeks before I have completely 
    adjusted.  Soon everyone will start coming out 
    of their homes and spending more time outside.  
    This spring I plan on making a real effort to 
    meet and greet as many of my neighbors each 
    day as I can.  I was recently asked why I love 
    Lakewood so much, and my response was 
    “because everyone is so nice, I have good 
    neighbors”.  Afterward I started thinking “how 
    many of my neighbors do I really know?”  Sure I 
    know some but not as many as I would like to.  
    So if you see a lady walking through the 
    neighborhood saying “hello” to everyone its 
    probably me, and hopefully you will say hi back. 
     
    Friends are family you chose. 

    home that I grew up in.   On 2/14/04, 
    approx. 2:30 A.M. an 18 year old drunk 
    driver hit and totaled my vehicle which 
    in turn, hit 2 more cars that belonged to 
    family members. Luckily, we were all 
    inside.  The 18 yr. old, fled the scene 
    but was caught a short time later.  I am 
    so thankful to the Sunnyvale Police for 
    their quick response to our call for 
    help!  They were there and searching for 
    this guy.  Luckily, his radiator had 
    leaked and left a trail for them to find 
    him.  I urge all of your parents who have 
    youth that drive or will be driving to 
    come and see the impact of this accident.  
    My car is parked on the middle block of 
    Lakehaven.  It is an eye opener!  If this 
    article can save just one person from 
    drunk driving, I know that my loss is not 
    in vein.  
      
    I want to again thank ALL of the officers 
    that were involved as well as the canine 
    dog that was ready to do his job eagerly.  
    I would like to especially thank Officer 
    Tim Sartell, for helping me at my Mom's 
    house with the report and helping to calm 
    my nerves during the search for the 
    reckless driver!  Unfortunately, I don't 
    know all the names of the officers 
    involved with the search, but you know 
    who you are and I THANK YOU from the 
    bottom of my heart. 
     
    I would like to also thank the neighbor 
    that called the police after seeing the 
    young man prying his car off of mine to 
    flee the scene.  I know that we have not 
    communicated prior to this incident,  but 
    I am very grateful to you for making the 
    effort to get involved in this.  It 
    changes my way of thinking that too many 
    people don't get involved when there is 
    an emergency.   
     
    Do get involved and make a difference so 
    this will not happen to anyone else! 
                                           
    Sincerely, 
    Elaine De Maderios 
