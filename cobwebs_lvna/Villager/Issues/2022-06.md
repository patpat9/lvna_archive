    - Lakewood Park Tree Walk -
    Tree #2 (Italian Stone Pine)
    In April, we highlighted Tree #1, the London Plane tree. The
    second tree on SUFA (Sunnyvale Urban Forest Advocates)
    Lakewood Park Treek Walk is the Italian Stone Pine.
    For Tree #1, we stood at the north side of the park building and
    looked east, towards the playground. From this same spot, we
    can now look north towards Tree #2, the Italian Stone Pine, Latin
    name pinus pinea. The tree was native to parts of North Africa
    during the prehistoric times, when these areas had a more humid
    climate. Today, its range is the Mediterranean, although it can
    also be found in places like the Canary Islands and New South
    
    Wales. It is also common in California and can be found in places as different as Hollywood, Marina
    del Rey, and Sylmar. In Rome, the Via Appia and other historic Roman roads are lined with stone
    pine. Considered an ornamental tree during the Ottoman period, the trees were planted in Istanbul
    along the Bosphorus strait.
    The Italian Stone Pine has a large umbrella-like canopy, which gives it the nickname of “umbrella
    pine”. Its bark is thick, with a reddish-brown color, and resembles vertical plates, and it can reach
    up to 80 feet in height when fully mature. The tree grows rapidly in its early years, growing to
    10-15 feet in height in the first five years of its life. After that, the growth rate slows to roughly 1
    foot per year. The trees are drought tolerant, although the growth will slow (particularly for
    younger trees) during prolonged drought. Mature Italian Stone Pines require little maintenance,
    water, or fertilizer to survive. It is susceptible to ice damage, and the needles will turn brown in
    colder weather.
    The Italian Stone Pine’s three-year maturation rate for its cones is the longest maturation of all
    pines. The edible seeds (pine nuts) are about 0.75 inches in length. Seeds rely on animals or
    humans to disperse.
    Want to learn more? Check out these links:
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stone_pine
    https://homeguides.sfgate.com/long-maturity-italian-stone-pine-86205.html
    https://www.gardeningknowhow.com/ornamental/trees/pine/italian-stone-pine-information.htm
    As a reminder, the tree walk maps can be found here
    (http://www.sunnyvaletrees.org/tree-walk-maps/), and the tree guides here

    - Meet Your Board Member -
    Linh Ly
    Linh is the new treasurer of the Lakewood
    Village Neighborhood Association.
    Question: Tell me a bit about yourself and
    your family.
    Linh: I am a Jersey transplant, but have settled
    here in CA for almost 20 years.  I live in a
    multi-generational household with my
    parents and brother.
    Question: When did you move to the
    neighborhood?
    Linh: I moved to Lakewood in 2011, so 11
    years ago.
    Question: Which side (Fairwood or
    Lakewood) do you live on?
    Linh: Fairwood side.
    Question: Name one thing you see out of
    your kitchen window.
    Linh: Looking out my kitchen window, I see
    my side yard which includes my succulent
    garden with lots of container plants.
    Question: Something that is in your front
    yard?
    Linh: There is a loquat tree in my front yard.
    Question: A sound you hear frequently in the
    neighborhood?
    Linh: I hear all the birds chirping in the
    mornings.  They are so active.
    Question: What is something you like about
    living in Lakewood/Fairwood? And something
    that has changed since you moved to the
    neighborhood?
    Linh: I like how close I live to my sister's
    family, who are also in Lakewood.  I have seen
    more remodels and new constructions, which
    is lovely to see.

    our neighborhood better. We all live here, so
    let's make it a special place that we can be
    proud of. I love gatherings and social events,
    so this summer is a great time to get out there
    and get to know each other!

    Water Restrictions
    With limited rain during this wet season, the drought continues into its third year. The period of
    January through March was the driest one on record for both Santa Clara County and California.
    Santa Clara County gets much of its water from the Sierra Nevada, and the annual snowpack
    measurement on April 1 recorded the fifth lowest amount of snowpack on record.
    LVNA gets our water through the City of Sunnyvale’s Public Works Department, and our water
    comes mostly from the Hetch Hetchy Reservoir in Yosemite National Park. The City is effectively the

    retailer to deliver the water to homes and businesses in our community. The City gets the water
    from Valley Water, the wholesale water and groundwater management agency that supplies water
    to much of Santa Clara County.
    As a result of the continuing drought, on April 12, the Valley Water Board of Directors voted to
    include additional water restrictions. New water restrictions are:
    1. Watering of lawns and ornamental landscapes in Santa Clara County is limited to no more
    than twice per week.
    2. Watering during the warmest part of the day is prohibited. If you water, please water in the
    early morning or evening - it’s better for the plants as well.

    Primary Election on June 7
    California’s primary election is coming up on June 7. All registered voters will receive their
    vote-by-mail ballot automatically. Ballots will be mailed on or before May 9, and drop boxes to
    return your vote-by-mail ballot will be open starting May 10. In-person voting is also available and
    polls will be open 7:00am to 7:00pm on Election Day. If you need another copy of the Official Voter
    Information Guide, please call (800) 345-VOTE (8683). This election will cover one congressional
    office and several state offices.
    Congressional offices (two elections for one US Senate seat):
    -
    US Senate special election to fill the vacancy left when Kamala Harris was elected as Vice
    President of the United States; the winner will serve until the term expires on January 3,
    2023 (incumbent is Alex Padilla, who was appointed by Governor Newsom)
    -
    US Senate regular election for a full six-year term for one senator that starts on January 3,
    2023
    Note that both US Senate elections are for one Senate seat. The special election is for who will hold
    that seat up until January 3, 2023, and the regular election is for who will hold that seat after
    January 3, 2023. California’s other US Senate seat is currently held by Dianne Feinstein, whose
    term does not end until on January 2, 2025.
