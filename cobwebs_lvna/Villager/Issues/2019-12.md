    LVNA MEETING ON OCT 13
    Leonard Dunn, Urban Landscape Manager with the City of Sunnyvale, provided an overview of the problem that 
    many Lakewood Village residents are experiencing with sticky residue dripping from certain trees at this time of 
    year. The primary specimen of concern is the hackberry tree, with the main culprit being the woolly hackberry 
    aphid.
    There’s no health concern for the trees or people, but the honeydew (aphid secretion) that drips from the leaves 
    where the aphids are feeding can be a nuisance for both. The trees would of course prefer to not have the sugar-
    rich nutrients sucked from their leaves, while people would prefer to not have the honeydew dripping on sidewalks 
    and cars.
    Identifying the aphids can be tricky since they’re very small and the hackberry trees have been growing in 
    Lakewood Village for many years, putting the leaves well out of range for easy visibility. Honeydew is often the first 
    obvious sign of their presence.
    Biological options are always the preferred method of control, but chemical options are also available. For more 
    information, visit http://ipm.ucanr.edu/PMG/PESTNOTES/pn74111.html.
    To find out if your street tree is a hackberry, or just to find out what it is, you can use Sunnyvale’s interactive tree 
    lookup map at https://sunnyvaleca.treekeepersoftware.com/
