    Life” program. 
    By Fred Fowler 
    Two of the most important assets the 
    Lakewood Village community has are 
    the fine elementary schools located in the centers of the two 
    parts of our neighborhood.  Our relationship with the schools 
    and the school district that runs them dates back to the very 
    founding of our community.  When Lakewood Village was 
    conceived back in the 1950s, the plan was to build a “family 
    oriented” development with its own schools, churches and 
    shopping center.  That initial plan has stood the test of time 
    with modern day Lakewood Village retaining that same basic 
    outline.  The Lakewood and Fairwood elementary schools 
    are in many ways more important to us than ever, providing 
    an educational focus for the young families who live here 
    and serving as a focal point for a number of community 
    activities.

    Members of the Rotary Club of Sunnyvale had a very simple 
    goal with their most recent book donation effort at Fairwood

    fruits of their labors. 
    “We’ve been giving children books for years, but we’ve never 
    been able to read them to the kids ourselves,” said Flo Stafford, 
    the Rotarian who organized the donation and reading. We 
    wanted to see how our gifts were being received.” 
    The response the Rotary Club got was more than its members 
    hoped for, Stafford said. Seven club members went to 
    Fairwood on December 2 to read to 
    the kids, and all 80 kindergarten 
    and first-grade students at the 
    school received a copy of “Frog and 
    Toad Together.” by Arnold Lobel. It 
    was the first time the rotary Club did 
    an in-person donation at Sunnyvale 
    school.

    Rotary members were just thrilled,”

    January Garden Chores 
    For The Bay Area 
    By Phyllis Fowler 
    In the first days of January we have 
    been experiencing frosts. Last year 
    our hibiscus bushes were severely 
    damaged by the overnight freezing.  
    We lost two and almost lost two more.  I recently searched 
    the internet to get some tips for January. 
      
    I found out that you need to wait until March before 
    pruning any perennials, shrubs or trees that were injured 
    during the winter’s freezing mornings. If it gets really cold 
    During the winter’s freezing mornings. If it gets really cold 
    again, the open wounds may invite further disaster. Here 
    are some other tips I found: 
     
    Plan on spraying your deciduous fruit trees this month. 
    This will help control peach leaf curl. You will need to 
    spray them one more time before Valentine’s Day A 
    copper-based spray will help control peach leaf curl and 
    brown rot.  
     
    Start planning your summer garden now. Now is the 
    time to plant bare root plants. Check out what’s 
    available at nurseries and start checking out the seed 
    packets. 
     
    When selecting bare root roses, look for those with a 
    grade number of one. (These are the healthiest roses 
    available.) Camellias at the nurseries will be in bloom 
    now; choose one that catches your eye. 
     
    Artichokes and gladiolus can be planted this month. 
    Plant a few gladiolas every three weeks until July for a 
    continuous bloom from spring until fall. 
     
    Start tomato and pepper seeds indoors in small 
    containers or peat pots, either on a sunny window sill 
    or beneath fluorescent lights.

    For The Bay Area 
     (Cont. from page 6) 
    If crabgrass is a problem in 
    your yard, control it now with a 
    pre emergent, before it 
    germinates next month and takes 
    over your lawn this summer! 
     
    Winter-flowering plants need to be fed with a 
    balanced fertilizer to keep blooming in these cold 
    months. Such as: primroses, stock, calendula, 
    snapdragons, Iceland poppies, pansies and violas. 
     
    Now is the good time for pruning deciduous fruit 
    trees, grapes and roses. If you're unsure how to 
    proceed, check with your favorite nurseryperson. 
    Two good books on the subject include Ortho's "All About 
    Pruning" and Sunset's "Pruning Handbook".  
     
    Clean and sharpen all your pruning tools when you’re 
    done. Don't put away your pruning tools wet and 
    dirty! It can encourage the formation of rust. 
     
    Apply a dormant oil spray on your roses and
