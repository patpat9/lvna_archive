    Stroke Signs and Risk Factors 
    By Judy Fernandez, Joan Simon and 
    Phyllis Fowler 
     
    Recently during my walk, I saw a long 
    time neighbor and she mentioned that she 
    had just had a stroke. With the recent 
    death of Helen Lane and my neighbor’s 
    experience, I thought it might be 
    appropriate to talk about the signs and risks of Strokes.  
    Signs of a Stroke: 
    Sudden numbness or weakness of the face, arm, leg and 
    especially on one side of the body. 
    Sudden confusion, trouble speaking or understanding. 
    Sudden trouble seeing in both eyes 
    Sudden trouble walking, dizziness loss of balance or 
    coordination 
    Sudden severe headache with no known cause 
    Make note of the time of the onset of your symptoms. If you  
    receive proper medication within 3 hours of the stroke, your 
    chances of long term disability may be reduced.

    AARL Ham Radio Contest 
    By Phyllis Fowler, KE6IFQ 
     
    Have you ever heard the term 
    Ham Radio and wondered what it 
    means? 
    A Ham radio operator can     
    communicate from the top of a 
    mountain, their home or behind 
    the wheel of their car. They can 
    take radio wherever they go! In times of disaster, when regular 
    communications channels fail, hams can swing into action assisting 
    emergency communications efforts and working with public service 
    agencies. At other times, they can talk to Shuttle astronauts or 
    bounce signals off the moon. They can use telegraphy, voice,    
    digital, even images in communication with other hams. Know any 
    other hobby with so much to offer? 
    Back in the 1890s, when Guglielmo Marconi began his experiments 
    in wireless telegraphy, he could not have imagined the enthusiasm 
    for Amateur Radio. The United States government began licensing 
    Amateur Radio operators in 1912. Within two years there were 
    thousands of ―Hams‖ and the American Radio Relay League 
    (ARRL) was founded. Currently the ARRL has over 156,000    
    members in the US. 
    ARRL Field Day is the single most popular on-the-air event held 
    annually in the US and Canada. Each year over 35,000 amateurs 
    gather with their clubs, friends or simply by themselves to talk to as 
    many other Ham radio operators as they can. Field Day is a time 
    where many aspects of Amateur Radio come together to highlight 
    our many roles. While some hams will treat it as a contest, most 
    groups use the opportunity to practice their emergency response 
    capabilities.  
    On June 25th and 26th you can see our local ―Hams‖ in action! The 
    Sunnyvale Amateur Radio Emergency Service (SARES) will be 
    running ―Field Day‖ at Raynor Park. Field Day starts at 6:00pm and 
    it runs for a continuous 24 hours. The guys and gals will try to log 
    as many contacts as they can! Yes, I said gals! You may have   
    noticed what looks like a license plate at the end of my name. It is 
    my amateur radio license! Fred and I have both been ―hams‖ since 
    1990! Come down to Field Day and see what all the fun is about!  
     
    "I am often asked how radio works. Well, you see, wire telegraphy 
    is like a very long cat. You yank his tail in New York and he meows 
    in Los Angeles. Do you understand this? Now, radio is exactly the 
    same, except that there is no cat."  
    Attributed to Albert Einstein

    Stroke Signs and Risk Factors 
     (Continued from page 1) 
    Risk Factors: 
    Age — Your chance of having a stroke doubles for 
    each decade of life after age 55. While stroke is 
    common among the elderly, a lot of people under 65 
    also have strokes. 
    Heredity (family history) — Your stroke risk is greater 
    if a parent, grandparent, sibling has had a 
    stroke. Some strokes may be symptoms of genetic 
    disorders 
    Race —African Americans have a much higher risk of 
    death from a stroke than Caucasians, partly due to the 
    higher risks of high blood pressure, diabetes and 
    obesity. 
    Sex (gender) — Stroke is more common in men than 
    in women.  However, more than half of all stroke 
    deaths occur in women.   
    Prior stroke, TIA or heart attack — The risk of stroke 
    for someone who has already had one is many times 
    that of a person who has not. Transient Ischemic 
    Attacks (TIA) are "warning strokes" that produce stroke
    -like symptoms but no lasting 
    damage. TIAs are strong predictors 
    of stroke. A person who's had one 
    TIAs is 10 times more likely to have 
    a stroke. Recognizing and treating 
    TIAs can reduce your risk of a major 
    stroke. TIA should be considered a 
    medical emergency and followed up 
    immediately with a healthcare professional.  
    High blood pressure —is the leading cause of stroke 
    and the most important controllable risk factor for 
    stroke. Many people believe the effective treatment of 
    high blood pressure is a key reason for the accelerated 
    decline in the death rates for stroke. 
    Cigarette smoking —The nicotine and carbon 
    monoxide in cigarette smoke damage the 
    cardiovascular system in many ways. The use of oral 
    contraceptives combined with cigarette smoking greatly 
    increases stroke risk.

    Electronics Recycling 
    By Phyllis Fowler 
     
    I recently received a flyer on my front 
    doorstep regarding ―Free Electronics 
    Recycling.‖ The flyer went on to list 
    electronic items to leave in a bag on 
    my driveway. I tossed the flyer in my 
    recycle bin and didn’t think about it 
    again. That is until Judy Fernandez 
    shared some information she received from Sunnyvale’s 
    Public Works/Solid Waste Division! 
    According to Sandy Jensen, Residential Recycling 
    Coordinator, these ―fly by night scavengers‖ are 
    questionable. If the items collected are taken to a certified 
    electronics recycler for proper dismantling and recycling, 
    then there’s no problem. However, many of these collectors 
    are scavengers interested in only extracting precious metals 
    contained in the electronics and tossing away the rest of the 
    item. Leftover materials often end up in someone’s backyard 
    in another country. Electronic waste is considered 
    hazardous waste, because these metals are known to pose 
    serious health risks to people and the environment.  
    These collectors usually will drop flyers and pick up within a 
    day or two to avoid being caught. Phone numbers are 
    quickly disconnected or changed to prevent tracking them 
    down. Which is a pretty good indication that they are not 
    certified and their intentions are not to recycle, but to mine 
    for trace metals!  
    Sunnyvale’s SMaRT Station is a certified electronics 
    collector and is not far from our neighborhood. I have 
    dropped old monitors, florescent light bulbs, used batteries 
    and other old electronic junk off at the SMaRT Station. It 
    didn’t take very long and was as simple as driving up, 
    unloading my junk and driving off! I even dropped off some 
    cardboard at the same time.  
    Remember it is illegal to place electronics in recycling or 
    trash bins (or dumpsters). However, electronics can be 
    placed curbside for On-Call collection. To schedule a 
    collection appointment, call (408) 730-7400 or go the City’s 
    website: http://sunnyvale.ca.gov/Departments/PublicWorks/
    GarbageRecyclingandWasteReduction.aspx . To check if a 
    company is a certified electronics recyclers in California, go 
    to www.calrecyle.ca.gov . 
    Stay safe and keep recycling! 

    Stroke Signs and Risk Factors 
     (Continued from page 3) 
    the presence of the disease still increases your risk of 
    stroke.  
    Carotid or other artery disease — The carotid arteries in 
    your neck supply blood to your brain. A carotid artery 
    narrowed by fatty deposits from atherosclerosis (plaque 
    buildups in artery walls) may become blocked by a blood 
    clot.  
    Peripheral artery disease is the narrowing of blood vessels 
    carrying blood to leg and arm muscles caused by fatty 
    buildups of plaque in artery walls, which 
    raises risk of stroke. 
    Other heart diseases —also raise the 
    risk of stroke. 
    High blood cholesterol — causes an 
    increased risk for stroke.  
    Poor diet- Diets high in saturated fat, 
    trans fat and cholesterol can raise blood 
    cholesterol levels. Diets high in sodium (salt) can contribute 
    to increased blood pressure. Diets with excess calories can 
    contribute to obesity. However, a diet containing five or more 
    servings of fruits and vegetables per day may reduce the 
    risk of stroke. 
    Physical inactivity and obesity — both can increase your 
    risk of high blood pressure, high blood cholesterol, diabetes, 
    heart disease and stroke. So go on a brisk walk, take the 
    stairs, and do whatever you can to make your life more 
    active. Try to get a total of at least 30 minutes of activity on 
    most or all days. 
    Alcohol abuse — Alcohol abuse can lead to multiple 
    medical complications, including stroke.  For those who 
    consume alcohol, a recommendation of no more than two 
    drinks per day for men and no more than one drink per day 
    for non-pregnant women.  
    Drug abuse —Drugs including cocaine, amphetamines and 
    heroin, have been associated with an increased risk of 
    stroke.  Strokes caused by drug abuse are often seen in a 
    younger population. 
    Although heart disease and stroke account for the vast 
    majority of deaths each year in America, you can 
    do things to lessen your risk: eat a healthy diet, exercise 
    every day, stop smoking and know your blood pressure. For 
    more information go to American Heart Association website: 
    www.americanheart.org 

    transportation or supplies

    sources of information such as radio and television. 
    Alerts are went to any number of devices, e-mail
