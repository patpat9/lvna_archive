    Tree #6: Aleppo Pine
    It’s been too many months since we did an entry from the SUFA (Sunnyvale Urban Forest
    Advocates) Lakewood Park Tree Walk. We covered five trees in 2022: the London Plane tree,
    the Italian Stone Pine, the Crape Myrtle, the Saucer Magnolia, and finally the Purple Leaf
    Plum. We begin 2023 with Tree #6, the Aleppo Pine.
    
    Start today at the corner of Lakechime Drive and Silverlake Dr. Look south (towards the
    playground). On your side of the stone wall - a bit past the yellow “pedestrian crossing
    ahead” sign - you will see the tall, not-so-straight Aleppo Pine.
    While not growing to the height of a redwood, the
    Aleppo Pine, Pinus halepensis, can grow 50-80 feet
    tall. Its trunk can get up to 4.5 feet in diameter.
    Also known as the Jerusalem pine, this species is
    believed to be the first holiday tree ever
    decorated. The trees tend not to grow straight up
    and, as they mature, they will shed their lower
    branches and develop a tall canopy with an open,
    cloud-like silhouette. The tall canopy provides
    ample shade, making the Aleppo Pine a wonderful
    overstory tree for arid climates.
    The needles are slender, long (2.25-4.75 inches),
    and grow in pairs (rarely in threes). Their cones
    turn reddish-brown in color when they reach 24
    months old and will slowly open after that (they
    will open faster if they are exposed to heat, such
    as from a wildfire). Aleppo Pines make good bonsai
    trees, their resin is used to flavor retsina (a Greek
    wine), and their pine nuts are used in desserts
    (prinjolata, which is a traditional dessert from
    Malta, and the Tunisian dessert assidat zgougou).
    Fun fact, there are actually two Aleppo Pines along
    Silverlake Dr. Can you find the second one?
    Want to learn more? Check out these links:
    ●
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinus_halepensis
    ●
    https://www.thespruce.com/growing-aleppo-pine-pinus-halepensis-3269312
    ●
    https://sites.redlands.edu/trees/species-accounts/aleppo-pine/
    As a reminder, the tree walk maps can be found here
    (http://www.sunnyvaletrees.org/tree-walk-maps/), and the tree guides here
    (http://www.sunnyvaletrees.org/tree-guide/).
    Courtesy to Sunnyvale Forest Advocates (SUFA) for allowing us to reproduce their website
    content in our Villager. For more information, see their website at
    http://www.sunnyvaletrees.org/

    - LVNA Needs Help -
    Are you passionate (or even just interested in) our neighborhood? Do you enjoy
    meeting new neighbors? Would you be willing to help build a tight-knit (Lakewood)
    Village for yourself, your friends, and the next generation who’s currently growing
    up right outside your door or even in your own backyard?
    Please join the LVNA board! There are five board positions: President, 1st Vice
    President, 2nd Vice President, Treasurer, and Secretary. The current board’s
    positions end in March and a new board will begin. The board meets once per
    month to discuss ongoing activities, in addition to neighborhood general meetings
    and a few seasonal events each year. The level of commitment is up to each
    individual - these are all volunteer roles. It is a one-year term (March 2023 - March
    2024). The only requirements are that you are 18+ years old, live in the
    neighborhood, and are able to serve the full 12 month term.
    WANTED - VILLAGER EDITOR
    Want to help, but not interested in a board position? We are looking for a new
    Villager Editor, a position open to anyone who is interested (no age requirement).
    The role consists of at least proof-reading the content and compiling it into a single
    document. Previous editors have also helped write content, design a new layout,
    take photos, and send the Villagers out to the subscribers each month.
    Want to partner with a family member or friend? Great! Are you a high school
    student interested in writing or media design and trying to increase your community
    service hours or build a portfolio? This is your chance!
    Any of these roles are a great opportunity to help actively drive change for our
    community and make a positive impact on our neighborhood. If interested, please
    email the board at board@lvnasv.org and let them know you’re on board (yes, pun
    fully intended).

    - Village Center Meeting Recap -
    There was a meeting hosted by Sunnyvale about the proposed Village Centers.  There are
    8 proposed village centers throughout Sunnyvale, one of which is in the Lakewood
    Shopping Center.  The timeline isn't for quite a long time, anywhere from 5-20yrs.  Nothing
    has been decided, but one potential way the Lakewood Village Center could be created is
    to have a mix of single family homes, townhouses, and multi-use buildings which consist of
    shops on the bottom level and apartments above.  Many people were concerned about the
    current shop owners, the city answered by saying no lease will be terminated prematurely,
    and it depends on when the current plaza owners are willing to sell.  Others were
    concerned with increased traffic and parking.  Visit this link for more details, where you
    can also sign up to receive updates.
    https://www.sunnyvale.ca.gov/business-and-development/projects
    -in-sunnyvale/long-range-planning-initiatives/village-center-master

    - The Story Behind the New Fencing at Fairwood Park-
    For anyone walking along the greenbelt near Fairwood Elementary (or anyone
    playing at Fairwood Park), you have likely noticed the new fencing. This fencing was
    installed by the Sunnyvale School District (SSD) in agreement with the City. The
    Villager chatted with Brandt Burns, Director of Facilities and Operations of the SSD
    to learn more.
    Here are some highlights:
    ●The SSD property encompasses the entirety of the playfield and extends to
    the benches at the south side of the field. However, Fairwood students rarely
    used the space because of the safety and supervision issues associated with
    playing in the open space. Students could wander off or members of the public
    could wander onto the field when students were on it. By enclosing the field,

    our students now have a safe, secure play area, providing them with more
    outdoor space in line with what already exists for students at the SSD
    elementary schools that are located south of Highway 101.
    ●The SSD held an open town hall at Fairwood Elementary on June 2, 2022. To
    raise awareness of the event, Fairwood Elementary sent out notification to all
    of their parents on May 31. SSD staff walked the neighborhood on May 27,
    placing flyers on nearby neighbors about the meeting. Unfortunately, the town
    hall was not well attended in spite of the outreach efforts.
    ●The decision to fence off the space was made by SSD in close collaboration
    with the City. The project costs were paid by SSD, not by the City. The
    decision to fence in the dirt paths on the east and west sides of the field was
    made due to costs and existing property lines.
    ●SSD has 10 campuses. Prior to this project, only the Fairwood Elementary and
    Lakewood Elementary campuses were not completely closed in. SSD and the
    City will continue to review opportunities to enclose the Lakewood Elementary
    campus in the coming years.
    ●The field will be closed to the public from 7:00am to 4:00pm on
    Monday-Friday during the school year. Signs on the fences provide contact
    information for SSD and the City Parks Department for additional questions.
