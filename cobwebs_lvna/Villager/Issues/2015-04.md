    Greetings neighbors. The LVNA is off

    Conservation, Lawn Watering 2 
    Days A Week

    water supply projections, the Santa Clara 
    Valley Water District Board of Directors called for a 
    water use reduction target of 30 percent and a 
    restriction on irrigating outdoor landscapes and lawns 
    with potable water to two days per week.

    Tuesday, March 24th, and is a response to the 
    continued severity of the drought. The board received 
    an update on water supply conditions which are 
    heavily reliant upon water supplies imported from

    Local reservoirs are about half full. 
    Groundwater levels are 30 to 40 feet lower 
    than average, reflecting the greater use of 
    groundwater in 2014 and starting the year 
    at a greater deficit in groundwater supplies 
    than last year. The northern California  
    Sierra snowpack, which is a key factor in the availability 
    of imported water supplies, is only around 10 percent of 
    normal. 
     
    The district offers assistance to those looking to make 
    more changes to save water, through a variety of  
    rebates for homes and businesses.  Over the past year, 
    the water district has taken aggressive actions and  
    invested approximately $25 million to respond to the 
    drought, including increasing water conservation  
    rebates, expanding drought awareness outreach, hiring 
    water waste inspectors, improving recharge ponds and 
    other facilities to boost performance, and improving  
    water use efficiency at its own facilities. Tuesday night, 
    the board also voted to extend increased water  
    conservation rebates another six months, until the end  
    of 2015. 

    It Can Happen to Anyone… 
     
    By Fred Fowler 
     
    Hello neighbors.  Phyllis and I learned a hard lesson 
    the other day.  After 20 years of peaceful and safe 
    living in beautiful Lakewood Village our house was 
    broken into and some items were stolen.  We don't 
    know who targeted us for the robbery, or why.  We 
    came home from work on a Friday night, fed our two 
    dogs, relaxed a bit and then went out for some pizza.  
    We were only gone for about 90 minutes, but when 
    we came back we found we had been robbed. 
     
    The Sunnyvale DPS crime scene investigator came 
    out shortly after we called 911.  We found out that 
    someone had opened our side gate to the backyard 
    and had climbed in our kitchen window.  They went 
    to my home office and stole 3 laptop computers from 
    it.  They went into Phyllis's den and stole her laptop 
    computer as well.  They went into the bedroom and 
    rifled through several dresser drawers and took  
    various pieces of jewelry.  They found and took  
    Phyllis's iPod and my coin collection of the 50 state 
    quarters.  After a few minutes they fled through the 
    sliding glass door to the backyard and probably out 
    the same gate they came in. 
     
    The crime scene investigator told us that someone 
    had probably been watching our house and had seen 
    us leave.  After a few minutes they took a chance 
    and tried the side gate.  After getting through it they 
    were able to close it and not be seen from the street.  
    They found an unlocked window, which let them in.  
    They took advantage of these opportunities to carry 
    out the burglary.  They were probably in and out of 
    our house in under 5 minutes. 
     
    The CSI officer also told us the culprits were  
    probably kids.  The burglars weren't very careful 
    about what they did and left lots of good fingerprints 
    behind.  They also stole laptops that were rather old 
    but nevertheless had traceable serial numbers.  
    Even worse, they stole the old laptops but did not 
    take the chargers used to charge their batteries.  
    Those chargers will be hard to find in any store, so if 
    anyone tries to use the laptops the batteries will go 
    dead before they can be of much use. 
    So, what lessons have Phyllis and I learned? 
     
    1) Lock the side gate. 
     
    The crime against us was a crime of opportunity.  If we  
    had locked the side gate, no one would have tried to  
    struggle with it in full view of the street, and the burglars 
    would have looked for an easier opportunity elsewhere. 
     
    2) Lock all doors and windows, no matter how  
    Inaccessible they seem  
     
    Our doors were locked, but the kitchen window gave the 
    burglars access.  It is about 5 feet off the ground, is not 
    very big, and it sits over the double kitchen sink on the  
    inside.  We did not suspect that someone might try to get  
    in through it, because it would take a very limber and  
    acrobatic person to do so.  We're now making sure that  
    all points of entry are locked and are under surveillance. 
     
    One more thing, the CSI officer told us it was likely that  
    the kids who broke into our home were “local” kids.  In fact, 
    it is possible that the burglars are reading this newsletter 
    right now.  
     
    3) Make backups of your computers. (Consider some-
    thing like Carbonite.) 
     
    If you did this and you are reading this now, I would like  
    to tell you what trouble you have caused. The laptop you 
    stole from me cost me about $250 a few years ago, but it 
    had the notes and initial chapters of a book I'm writing  
    on it.  Now that the laptop is gone, I will have to spend 
    many weeks re-writing the chapters that were lost. 
     
    The laptop you stole from Phyllis was the one she used to 
    create the LVNA newsletter, The Villager.  It had all of the 
    logos, graphics and pictures used to format the pages and 
    position the advertisements.  You've caused her a great 
    deal of extra work and time to reassemble those things in 
    order to publish the newsletter you are reading now.  When 
    the laptop was new it cost less than $400 and that was a 
    couple of years ago. There were also a lot of photographs 
    that were not all on disks. Some of those were of dear 
    friends that are not longer with us.

    April is Distracted Driving Awareness Month 
     
    In order to help save lives and educate the public about 
    the dangers of distracted driving, especially young  
    drivers, Sunnyvale DPS will be working in conjunction 
    with the California Office of Traffic Safety (OTS) and  
    the California Highway Patrol 
    on increased education and  
    enforcement efforts for National 
    Distracted Driving Awareness 
    Month (April). Sunnyvale DPS 
    will also participate in California 
    Teen Safe Driving Week, which 
    occurs during the first week of 
    April.  
     
    April 1 and April 15 have been 
    earmarked for special statewide high visibility  
    enforcement days for all law enforcement agencies that 
    are participating in the national traffic safety campaign.  
     
    Residents are encouraged to abide by the laws  
    regulating the use of electronic communication devices 
    while driving, which will help make our roadways safer 
    for autos, bicyclists, and pedestrians.   
    A Letter From Lakewood’s Libraran 
     
    By Tammy Michels 
     
    Dear Parents, 
     
    Even if you don’t have a child in 
    school, please take a moment to read this information.  
    As a Library Specialist at  
    Lakewood Elementary, I find that many children treat 
    books like “toys.”  Books are special and should be  
    treated with care.  I talk to my students about this topic 
    regularly but for some reason I have A LOT of damaged 
    books.  I believe that the earlier a child is taught the  
    more he/she will appreciate books and truly “care” for 
    them.  I hope the following “how to take care of books” 
    information is helpful to you.   
     
    How to Take Care of Books  
     
     
    Keep books away from pets, small children, food  
           and drinks 
     
    Keep pencils, pens, crayons and markers away    
    from books 
     
    Turn pages carefully, starting with the top right corner 
     
    Hold books gently 
     
    Use a bookmark 
     
    Thank you for teaching your child that books are special! 
     
    Tammy Michels 
    Library Specialist at Lakewood School

    Haz Mat – 974 E. Arques
