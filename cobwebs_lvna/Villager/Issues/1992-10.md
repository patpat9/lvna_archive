    County Measure A
    
    L.V.N.A. President Vicki Piazza Endorses County Measure
    Aimed at Raising Funds to Improve Mass Transit
    
    In addition to the many state and federal offices up for grabs
    this November, we also will also be asked to vote on an impor-
    tant local issue—County Measure A. Like the Measure A which
    Santa Clara County approved in 1984, the current Measure A is
    intended to improve the county transportation system. Specifi-
    cally, Measure A asks voters to continue the special 1/2¢ sales
    tax first adopted in 1984 and scheduled to expire in 1994.
    
    Local resident Vicki Piazza is an enthusiastic supporter of
    Measure A. As president of the L.V.N.A., Vicki has agreed to
    endorse the measure because she feels that Lakewood Village
    residents will benefit from the proposed improvements.
    
    If Measure A passes, our retail sales tax will be unchanged,
    but a portion of our tax money will be made available for a num-
    ber of projects which improve mass transit and reduce the num-
    ber of cars on our roads. These projects include: extending
    BART into Santa Clara County; improving and increasing bus
    service in the county; constructing new commuter rail lines, such
    as the Mountain View extension of the existing light rail, which
    passes by Lakewood Village; and synchronizing traffic lights on
    major expressways, including Lawrence and Central.
    
    Some opponents argue that the money raised by Measure A
    could be better spent on social reform or children's needs. How-
    ever, providing mass transit and reducing the number of cars on
    our roads is critical to reducing the pollution levels in Santa Cla-
    ra County. Most people agree that no social programs are as im-
    portant to the well-being of children and other county residents
    as improving the quality of the air we breathe every day.

    Opportunites
    
    Debbie Bakker of Lakewood Village submitted this article to
    help make neighbors aware of a very special opportunity Sunny-
    vale residents have to get involved and serve the community.
    
    Have you heard about SNAP yet? SNAP (Sunnyvale Neigh-
    borhoods Actively Prepare) is an excellent and innovative pro-
    gram for neighborhood earthquake and disaster preparedness,
    and is already taking shape all over Lakewood Village.
    
    There is nothing we can do to prevent disasters from occur-
    ring, but we can prepare for them. Our neighbors will be our best
    source of help when the next earthquake hits. SNAP is helping
    us get organized. Think about the following:
    
    Who lives near me that has first aid training and supplies?
    
    Who will check my house to see if I am trapped or hurt?
    
    Who will help me find my children?
    
    The answer to all of these questions is “My neighborhood
    SNAP team.” By organizing information and expertise as a
    
    SNAP group, the neighbors on your street can form a disaster re-
    sponse team that will help you and your family survive.
    
    Sunnyvale Neighborhoods Actively Prepare was developed
    
    by the City of Sunnyvale Department of Public Safety Emergen-
    cy Preparedness Bureau. They can be contacted at 730-7190.
