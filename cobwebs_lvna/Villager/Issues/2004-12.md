    Annual Holiday Parade Will Happen! 
     
    In the last issue of the Villager I reported that 
    the annual holiday parade was in jeopardy 
    because of new high fees that we have not 
    previously had to pay.  We put out a call for 
    donations and many Lakewood Village residents 
    responded, which is wonderful.  Even with the 
    donations we received, it was still too expensive 
    to cross Lawrence Expressway, so at the last 
    general meeting we had reached a decision to 
    not have the parade cross Lawrence, which 
    reduced our costs to a manageable level.  
    HOWEVER, a last minute anonymous donation 
    has been made specifically to allow the parade 
    to cross Lawrence Expressway as it has in 
    previous years! (see route map, below) 
     
    Being able to cross Lawrence is exciting news 
    for the neighborhood because I feel that having 
    the parade start on one side of the 
    neighborhood and cross to the other serves as a 
    symbolic unification for us.  This is especially 
    important now.  I recently learned that the  
     
    principal from Fairwood School was being 
    transferred over to Lakewood School after the 
    principal at Lakewood resigned.  Apparently this 
    angered a lot of parents and has caused a lot of 
    distress.  I am not involved with the schools in 
    any way yet, so I have been pretty unaware of 
    the feelings of rivalry, whatever they may be.  
    However, I appreciate that we live in a 
    neighborhood with a giant expressway running 
    through the middle of it, so it is difficult to feel 
    unified sometimes.  It was really fun for me (a 
    Fairwood sider) last year to walk along with the 
    parade for the entire route, which I don’t 
    normally have occasion to do.

    UNSCC Needs You! 
    I know I’ve written about the United 
    Neighborhoods of Santa Clara County before, 
    and here I am, writing about it again!  The 
    UNSCC is a great organization that strives to be 
    a resource for neighborhood associations to 
    communicate and learn from one another.  They 
     
     
     
    also put on a yearly conference of great 
    seminars, and provide us with the insurance 
    coverage for our events.  Faith Rogers has been 
    our UNSCC representative since Tom Freeman 
    moved out of Lakewood Village, but she has 
    since also become the Treasurer for the 
    Lakewood Village Neighborhood Association, as 
    well as already being the co-editor of the 
    Villager.  In addition, our newsletter coordinator 
    recently moved out of state, leaving us with the 
    big project of getting Villager advertisers back 
    up to date on invoice payments and ad 
    coordination.  Faith also took on this project.  
    Frankly, it’s a bit much for one person!  So, we 
    are officially soliciting someone to be the new 
    UNSCC representative to the Lakewood Village 
    Neighborhood Association.  The requirements 
    are pretty minimal—the UNSCC has a meeting 
    once every other month and someone from our 
    neighborhood needs to attend and vote on 
    issues as necessary.  The UNSCC also has a 
    Yahoo! group online that anyone is welcome to 
    join and learn more about the organization.  
    Please let any of the LVNA board members know 
    if you are interested in becoming our new 
    UNSCC representative.  Here is their Yahoo! 
    group site if you would like to check it out or 
    join the group to receive emails: 
    http://groups.yahoo.com/group/UNSCC-NEWS/ 

    Congratulations, Sunnyvale Knights! 
     
    The Sunnyvale Knights, Junior Pee Wee (ages 8 
    to 11), cheerleaders won first place in their 
    division at this year’s California Western 
    Regional Competition.  The event was held in 
    Reno, Nevada, on November 26. 
     
    As a result, they are eligible to advance on to 
    the US National Cheerleading Competition in 
    Florida the week of Dec. 6.   
     
    This is the first time in six years that the girls in 
    this division have gotten the chance to compete 
    in the US Nationals and they’ve worked so very 
    hard to get to this point. Congratulations, girls, 
    and Go Knights!!  
     
    Please Help Solve This Crime 
     
    On November 20th at around 12:30 p.m., a 
    home on Lakehaven Drive near Silverlake was 
    robbed. The suspects have been described as 
    African American and Hispanic, driving a black, 
    older model SUV (resembling a Denali or Tahoe) 
    with chrome rims. 
     
    If you have any information about this crime or 
    the suspects, please call Sunnyvale Department 
    of Public Safety at 730-7100. You can ask for 
    Officer Welch and refer to case number 0413269. Do not confront these individuals, 
    they may be armed. We need to work together 
    to keep crime out of our neighborhood. 

    December in Your Yard 
    and Garden 
     
    ¾ Be sure to get in the last of the 
    spring-blooming bulbs this 
    month. Remember that wet 
    soil is difficult to dig properly. 
    Be careful not to plant the 
    bulbs too deep. 
    ¾ Water plants as needed. 
    ¾ Watch out for frost. 
    ¾ Direct water from downspouts to avoid 
    damage to the garden. 
    ¾ Save wood ashes from the fireplace as a 
    source of potash for the compost pile. 
    ¾ Request seed and nursery catalogs from 
    mail-order companies. 
    ¾ Set out large-sized transplants of winterflowering plants into the bedding plot, using 
    such plants as calendula, Iceland poppy, 
    primrose, and stock. 
    ¾ Apply dormant spray to deciduous flowering 
    plants and fruit trees. 
    ¾ Prune pines, magnolias, junipers, and redberried plants for holiday greens. 
    ¾ Cut back chrysanthemums as they finish 
    blooming. 
    ¾ Hold off fertilizing most plants in preparation 
    for winter. 
    ¾ Stabilize plants that might be harmed by 
    wind. 
    ¾ Harvest winter vegetables as they ripen. 
     
    These tips are from the book Northern California Gardening: A 
    Month-by-Month Guide, by Katherine Grace Endicott, published by 
    Chronicle Books, San Francisco, ©1996, and are used with the 
    publisher’s permission. See http://www.lvna.net/Resources 
    /Reading_List/reading_list.html 

    This Month in 
    Lakewood History… 
    - Brian Smithson 
    50 years ago: In 1954, a 
    developer submitted 
    plans for a 409-home 
    tract where Unit II is 
    today, on the northeast corner of Lawrence 
    Station Rd. (now Expressway) and the 
    Bayshore Hwy. (now the US-101 Freeway). 
    Also submitted were plans for a 304-room 
    Holiday Inn motel (now a Ramada Inn). 
    49 years ago: After 13 years of drought, some 
    ambitious cloud-seeding resulted in the 
    Christmas Flood of 1955. It rained for almost 
    two weeks and Sunnyvale’s lowlands 
    (including parts of Lakewood Village) 
    sustained some flooding. 
    45 years ago: In 1959, the first Lakewood 
    Village Holiday Parade was held, and also that 
    year, the first Holiday Lighting Contest was 
    held. 
