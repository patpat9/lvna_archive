    Back from Vacation 
    - Craig Crawford 
    President, LVNA 
     
    It’s wonderful to leave fo r a vacation, but it’s 
    nicer to come back. My wife and I went to the 
    United Kingdom for three weeks. This was our 
    first time in the island country. Californians 
    consider something as being old if it is from the 
    early 1900’s, but in the U.K., one thousand 
    years is just starting to get old. My favorite 
    activity of the vacation was visiting castles.  
     
    I could not help but to compare the USA to 
    England, and  here are some of my thoughts. 
    We are truly blessed to live in the Bay Area for 
    several reasons. 
     
    Ø The weather: We  have the greatest climate 
    in the entire world. In England it is almost 
    always gloomy and overcast, and rain is 
    always a possibility. I did get accustomed 
    to the lack of sun after awhile, but only 
    because I remembered that I would be 
    back in the land of the sun very soon.  
     
    Ø The food: I was anxious to try the English 
    and Scottish food, but after a few days I 
    realized that the food is bland and lacks the 
    spices that I have grown to love. How do 
    they eat that food day in and day out , I will 
    never kn ow. American food is taken from 
    all over the world. We have chosen the best 
    foods from all over the world and have 
    incorporated them into our diets. Our 
    restaurants are second to none in the 
    variety and quality. 
     
    u continued on next column  
    u continued fro m previous column  
     
    Ø There are other reasons why we are the 
    best, such as our low taxes compared to 
    the U.K. Gas oline prices of $5.00 per gallon 
    are not uncommon. Why it so expensive is 
    that the taxes that are needed to support 
    the socialist nature of their count ry. Medical 
    treatment is free, but many people in the 
    country don’t like it one bit.  It takes a long 
    time to get treatment, and once you are 
    able to get it , the quality of treatment  is 
    lacking. I read in the newspapers that heart 
    patients routinely wait 18 months for major 
    work. Would you like this for your elderly 
    parents or other family members? Deaths 
    happening in the waiting period are 
    common. Also there is an 18.5% tax built 
    into all goods purchased, while we complain 
    about the 8.75% sales tax that w e pay. 
     
    All together the vacation was a good 
    experience the country is beautiful and the 
    people friendly. I have gained an 
    understanding and an appreciation for our 
    country and the Bay area. We are the best!  ¢

    City of Sunnyvale Privacy Study 
    - Diana Perkins 
    City of Sunnyvale Planning Division 
    The City is studying privacy issues between 
    residential and non -residential uses. Staff is 
    evaluating whether the current zoning code 
    requirements for increased setbacks, landscape 
    buffers and 6 -foot high masonry walls 
    adequately protect against infring ement of 
    privacy of adjacent residents. This study will 
    determine whether modified requirements are 
    appropriate to protect privacy.  
     
    The City Council has directed staff to study the 
    following issues for the purpose of this study:  
     
    •  Line of sight from windows of non-
    residential development 
    •  Use of exterior mounted security cameras 
    •  Height of masonry walls along property 
    lines 
    •  Security lighting levels 
    •  Noticing neighborhoods of 
    commercial/industrial projects 
    •  Trash enclosure locations  
     
    This item is scheduled to be  heard by the 
    Planning Commission on September 23, 2002 
    at 8:00 p.m. and by the City Council on 
    October 8, 2002 at 7:30 p.m. 
     
    Please contact Diana Perkins in the Planning 
    Division at dperkins@ci.sunnyvale.ca.us or 
    (408) 730-7455 for any questions, comments , 
    or suggestions for this study. ¢

    Vehicle Noise Codes 
    - Brian Smithson 
    Several residents have expressed concern 
    about the noise levels emanating from vehicles 
    in our neighborhood, and they have wondered 
    what can be done about it. This made me 
    curious about what laws are on the books to 
    deal with such noise. I first looked at the 
    Sunnyvale Municipal Code, and it doesn’t have  
    any laws about vehicle noise. Then I looked at 
    the California Vehicle Code, and it has seven 
    applicable sections: 27007, 27200, 27201, 
    27202, 27204, 27206, and 27207. I won’t print 
    the full text of the code here, but here is the 
    bottom line: 
     
    Ø 27007 sets the maximum noise for audio 
    systems in vehicles, and it states that you 
    cannot operate “any sound amplification 
    system which can be heard outside of the 
    vehicle from 50 or more feet”. 
    Ø 27200 sets the conditions for measuring 
    engine and exhaust noise for vehicles. 
    Measurements are taken from 50 feet away 
    on the centerline of travel (i.e., in front of 
    and behind the vehicle) in absolute decibels 
    (dbA, a measurement of sound pressure). 
    Ø The remaining codes set the maximum 
    acceptable noise levels for various types 
    and ages of vehicles, ranging from 80dbA 
    to 92dbA. Older vehicles are typically 
    allowed to make more noise than newer 
    vehicles of the same type.  
     
    As points of reference, 80dbA is typical for a 
    ringing telephone, 85dbA is typical for a noisy 
    restaurant, 90dbA is typical for a lawn mower, 
    and 95dbA is typical for a power saw. ¢
