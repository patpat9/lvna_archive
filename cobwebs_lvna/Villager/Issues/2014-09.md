    Greetings Neighbors!
    As many of you may know, the southern part of
    Sunnyvale is one of the hottest hot spots for West Nile
    Virus in the State of California. The Santa Clara
    County Vector Control District has been sending
    insecticide fogging trucks through the area on a
    regular basis in an effort to eradicate the mosquitoes
    that are spreading the disease.
    The disease incubates in a cycle involving mosquitoes
    and birds. Larger birds such as ravens, crows,
    magpies and hawks become infected by being bitten
    by infected mosquitoes. The birds develop symptoms
    and frequently die from the disease, but not before
    being bitten by other mosquitoes. Mosquitoes infect
    birds; birds infect other mosquitoes, which infect other
    birds, and so on. It is a vicious cycle.
    Occasionally infected mosquitoes bite human beings.
    While most people who get bitten suffer
    no ill effects, some become deathly
    ill^ Once serious symptoms present
    themselves, there is little that can be
    done to treat the disease.
    Last month I reported that one of our
    neighbors found a dead bird that tested
    positive for the West Nile Virus on the
    Lakewood side of the neighborhood.
    There has also been an infected dead
    bird found on the Fairwood side.

    The LVNA has more money in the bank right
    now than we've ever had before! When I was
    president in 2010-2011 we barely had
    enough cash to pay for printing the
    newsletter. Now we have more than $5,000 in the checking
    account! The question is what should we do with it?
    There are two reasons the LVNA is so flush. First, in 2010 we
    bought a big HP 5500 color printer and started printing the
    newsletter ourselves. We've had some glitches and challenges
    along the way, but it cut our printing costs in half. We used to
    pay $423 per month to have a 6 page newsletter printed in black
    and white. lt now costs us $200 less per month to print a 6 to 8
    page newsletter in color. After 4 years the savings have added
    up.
    The other reason we have money is our successful Dumpster
    Day Goodwitt Donation Drive.We gave so much to Goodwill
    that we ended up filling up almost two of their trailers. We
    earned more than $2,800 in that one day.
    Now that we have all of this money, the question is what to do
    with it all? We're a not-for-profit organization meant to serve the
    needs of the neighborhood. We should put this money to use for
    a few worthy causes.
