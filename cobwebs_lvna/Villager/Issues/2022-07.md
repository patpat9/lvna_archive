    - Lakewood Park Tree Walk -
    Tree #3: Crape Myrtle
    In April, we highlighted Tree #1, the London Plane tree.
    From there, Tree #2 was the Italian Stone Pine, visible from
    the same spot as Tree #1. The third tree on SUFA
    (Sunnyvale Urban Forest Advocates) Lakewood Park Tree
    Walk is the Crape Myrtle.
    From the north side of the park building (near the splash
    pad), walk northeast, towards Lakechime Drive. You’ll come
    to a concrete bench on your left. Stop here. There are two
    tall trees just behind the bench. These are Tree #3, the
    
    Crape Myrtle, Latin name Lagerstroemia. Native to Southeast Asia and Australia, this
    genus* has over 50 varieties (what’s a genus? In the taxonomic tree of biology, genus
    comes . The tree is a common ornamental tree and species grow from 1 to 100 feet in
    height. Once the tree is established, it tolerates drought well.
    Flowers bloom in summer or autumn, and they come in many colors - white, yellow, pink,
    purple, and red - and the wrinkled flowers are sometimes described as similar to crepe
    paper. The tree’s seed pods can stain concrete, though, so be careful where you plant
    them.
    The genus is named after Magnus von Lagerström. A Swedish merchant and director of the
    Swedish East India Company, he sent many plants to Carl Linnaeus (who is known as the
    father of modern taxonomy).
    Unfortunately, the tree is susceptible to fungal diseases. A less-known variety is the
    Japanese crape myrtle, L. fauriei (known as saru suberi or “monkey slip” in Japanese). This
    species, with dark green leaves, is more resistant to fungal diseases than the more
    common L. indica. Many Lagerstroemia varieties available today are a result of breeding
    these two species.
    *What’s a genus? In the biology taxonomic rankings, genus comes above species and
    below family. For scientific Latin names, the genus is the first word of the species. For
    example, the domestic cat is Felis catus, so its genus is Felis and its species is catus. The
    domestic cat shares its genus with a handful of other small cats, including the sand cat
    (Felis margarita).
    Want to learn more? Check out these links:
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagerstroemia
    https://www.arborday.org/trees/treeguide/treedetail.cfm?itemID=824
    https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/lagerstroemia-indica/
    As a reminder, the tree walk maps can be found here

    - Congrats to the WINNERS of the
    Memorial Day Scavenger Hunt -
    Lakewood Village Neighborhood Association held our first Flag Scavenger hunt in May in celebration
    of Memorial Day.  Twenty flags were placed around the neighborhood near sidewalks and the first
    10 neighbors to submit their findings won $25 gift cards.  The hunt began on May 7th and ended
    on May 31st, with a cheat map released partway through.  Board members enjoyed seeing the
    participation throughout the month as people submitted pictures or locations of the flags they
    found.  As it was our first hunt the original requirement was to find all 20 flags, but we soon
    changed the rules so prizes were given to those who found the most flags.
    Our grand prize winners who found all 20 flags are Beverly Lau and Joakim Bång.  Additional
    winners were Bev & Robin Woidyla, Precious Del Rosario, Kevin Lohman + kids, and Quyen Ly.
    Thank you all for your participation!

    Water Restrictions
    Due to the ongoing drought, Sunnyvale has a goal to reduce our water usage to 15% less than
    what was used in 2020. To support this, the City Council recently extended the drought emergency
    declaration. As a result, a quick reminder that watering your landscape is limited to the following
    days:
    Houses with odd numbers water their lawns Monday and Thursday
    Houses with even numbers water their lawns Tuesday and Friday
    Houses with no numbers water their lawns Monday and Thursday
    Pro tip: Water either BEFORE 9:00am or AFTER 6:00pm. Because it’s cooler in the evening and
    overnight, it takes longer for the water from your irrigation to evaporate. This means more of the
    water soaks into the soil and gets absorbed by the plant roots. So - happier plants on less water!
    If you notice someone wasting water, you can either talk to them directly or you can report it
    through the Valley Water website here:
    https://www.valleywater.org/droughtsaving-water/report-water-waste
    Additional information can be found at these websites:
    -
    https://valleywaternews.org/2022/04/19/valley-water-restricts-watering-in-santa-clara-coun
    ty-to-no-more-than-two-days-a-week/
    -
    https://sunnyvale.ca.gov/property/water/waterconservation.htm
