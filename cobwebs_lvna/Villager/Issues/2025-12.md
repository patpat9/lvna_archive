    A Year-End Message From Your LVNA President
    Dear Neighbors,
    As 2025 comes to a close, I want to take a moment to reflect on what an incredibly productive and
    vibrant year it has been for the Lakewood Village Neighborhood Association. It’s a privilege to serve
    as your President, and the energy and commitment I’ve seen from our community of 1,614 households
    this year is truly inspiring. Together, we’ve made significant progress in strengthening our
    neighborhood.
    Here are a few highlights from our collective accomplishments this year:
    Neighborhood Advocacy and Safety
    Addressing Key Priorities: I was pleased to share the good news following my address to the
    Sunnyvale City Council in January, where we successfully advocated for and secured progress on
    our top priorities: sidewalk repairs, tree trimming, and improved street lighting.
    Public Safety: We hosted Sunnyvale’s new Chief of Public Safety, Daniel Pistor, at our General
    Meeting in October, where he reported a positive trend of crime reduction in Sunnyvale. We also
    welcomed our new Neighborhood Resource Officer, Jekyll Padrogane, and received an update on
    retail spaces at the Lakewood Shopping Center from City Council Representative Eileen Le. We've
    continued to share important information about new laws, such as the "daylighting" parking rule,
    to keep our streets safer.
    Community Events and Green Initiatives
    Our community showed up in force for all of our events, making a direct impact on our neighborhood:
    Dumpster Day: Our July 19th Dumpster Day was a great success, notable for our partnership with
    the Silicon Valley Bicycle Exchange (SVBE) that saved 24 bicycles from the landfill.
    Calabazas Creek Cleanups: The May 17th cleanup had 24 volunteers and successfully picked up
    approximately 750 pounds of trash, including some interesting items like a snowboard and a mop
    bucket. An impressive 900 pounds of trash were removed during our September 20th cleanup.
    Successful Annual Events: We brought back and successfully executed beloved community events,
    including the LVNA Holiday Parade (Save the date for December 6th!), a wonderful Summer Picnic
    in September, and a successful Memorial Day Scavenger Hunt.

    Community Voices: The Future of Lakewood Shopping Center
    At the LVNA, we are committed to sharing the perspectives of our neighbors to keep everyone informed
    about changes in our Village. We encourage residents to research proposed developments and make
    their voices heard.
    Longtime resident John Haddaway, a concerned Lakewood resident, has voiced his deep opposition
    regarding the proposed redevelopment of the Lakewood Shopping Center. This concern is echoed by
    Evelyn Profeta, who has been a Fairwood neighbor since 1984 and recalls when the center was home to
    Dick's Supermarket. Both neighbors, among many, share the viewpoint that losing the current retail
    center to build high-density residential housing would be a loss for the neighborhood. They believe the
    area is already sufficiently dense and that preserving vital retail services is necessary to prevent North
    Sunnyvale from becoming a "food desert."
    For those who share these concerns, there is an active online petition circulating that aligns with this
    perspective, advocating to prioritize community needs and protect the shopping center.
    View the petition here: 
    https://www.change.org/p/prioritize-community-needs-protect-north-sunnyvale-from-becoming-
    a-food-desert
    You are also encouraged to email the following city and state representatives to explain the impact this
    development would have on you and your family.
    Primary contact for questions & comments:
    Jeffrey Cucinotta: jcucinotta@sunnyvale.ca.gov

    The LVNA board is working to compile a list of the most neglected areas in Lakewood Village, and we need

    What Issues Should You Report?
    Look around Lakewood Village and let us know about areas that need attention, including but not limited to:
    Sidewalks that are cracked or unsafe
    Potholes that need filling
    Overgrown trees in need of trimming
    Abandoned vehicles that are taking up space
    Neglected properties that impact our community’s appearance
    How to Submit a Report
    The more reports we get, the stronger our case! You can submit issues in two easy ways:
    1. Email us with specific details (exact addresses, descriptions, and photos if possible) at board@lvnasv.org.
    2. Report directly to the City via Access Sunnyvale (https://sunnyvale.dynamics365portals.us). This officially
    enters your complaint into the system. If you do this, let us know so we can help follow up!
    3. Attend a City Hall meeting on a Tuesday to voice your concerns in person—this can make an even bigger
    impact and catch their attention. If you choose this option, also file a claim on Access Sunnyvale to
    strengthen your case. More information about the meeting: https://www.sunnyvale.ca.gov/your-
    government/governance/city-council/council-meeting-information
    Together, we can make Lakewood Village a safer, cleaner, and better-maintained community. Thank you for
    taking the time to report issues and advocate for our neighborhood!
