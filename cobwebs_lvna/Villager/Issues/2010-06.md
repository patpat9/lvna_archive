    June LVNA Meeting: An Encore for PG&E 
     
    Greeting neighbors.  The lights are still out at Lakewood Park, 
    but we've decided to have our monthly meeting anyway!  For 
    this month, we're moving the meeting to the Lakewood 
    School Multi-Purpose Room.  It will happen at the same 
    time (7:00pm) and the same day  (the 2nd Wednesday).  It will 
    just be in a different location! 
    We're relocating this time because we want to be certain we  
    will have electricity!  We need to make sure, since our guest 
    for the meeting will be Denise Alexander, Customer Outreach 
    Supervisor from the Pacific Gas and Electric Company.   
    Denise made a presentation to us about PG&E’s SmartMeter 
    program back In April.  Unfortunately, she and the rest of us 
    literally got caught in the dark that night.  The power to the  
    entire park was out due to a major cable malfunction. She and 
    her assistant did the best they could and they provided a great 
    deal of useful information.   Still, the people attending the  
    meeting wanted more and it was decided to invite Denise back 
    for an encore during out June meeting. 
    Since April, there have been a number of developments in the 
    SmartMeter area.  Last month PG&E admitted that there are 
    almost 50,000 SmartMeters that have malfunctioned, and the 
    State of California is now mounting an investigation.  PG&E is 
    also sponsoring a controversial ballot initiative (Prop. 16) that 
    will help lock in its status as a power providing monopoly, by 
    making it very difficult for municipal governments to establish 
    their own electric utilities. 
    There is a great deal of “information” floating around, some   
    of which is accurate and some of which is not.  Come to the  
    June LVNA meeting on Wednesday, June 9th at 7:00pm,  
    and get the viewpoint of PG&E straight from the source!  
    There are important issues at stake here that could greatly 
    affect life in our nice little neighborhood. Come find out about 
    them first-hand! 
    PS: I'm afraid I will not be able to attend 
    the June 9th meeting myself.  I'll be       
    traveling in Asia on business. Our 1st Vice 
    President Joan Simon will be handling the 
    meeting for us.  Let's give her our support 
    that night and thank her for stepping up to 
    this challenge. 

    By Fred Fowler 
    It is budget time for the City of 
    Sunnyvale, and once again 
    there is not enough money to 
    go around.  The bad economy, 
    the stalled downtown project 
    and the general fall in Santa 
    Clara County property values 
    have contributed to another 
    “crisis” where the City Council 
    must make hard choices about 
    spending the monies that they 
    do have.  As you know, I have 
    some experience with those 
    situations  having served eight years on the City Council myself.  The 
    most important thing to remember is that the decisions to be made 
    are choices.  The Council must use its collective wisdom to choose 
    among the many pressing priorities to allocate the available money 
    where it will do the most good for the community.

    prosperous and successful community we must first and foremost be

    attention to this area of the budget and make sure funding is 
    adequate, even in hard times such as these.

    Lakewood Park power update 
    by Fred Fowler 
    At the beginning of April the lights went out 
    in Lakewood Park, and things have been 
    dark ever since.  Last month I warned the 
    neighborhood about some of the worrisome 
    side-effects of the lack of power, and I said 
    that I would follow up with the City of Sunnyvale to find out 
    what is going on. 
    A few days ago I received an e-mail from Scott Morton, the 
    Superintendent of Parks for the City of Sunnyvale.  Scott is an 
    old friend of the LVNA and of Lakewood Village in general.  
    Two of his initiatives have included the construction of the  
    water based play features in Lakewood park that are so   pop-
    ular in the hot summer months, and also the skate park ramps 
    and jumps that are located in the circular skating “rink” near 
    the Lakewood park building.  Scott is as worried and  frustrated 
    as we are.   
    Here is what he says: 
    “Hello Fred, I'm sure you, along with the rest of the     neigh-
    borhood, are very aware of the power outage at the park and 
    probably are as frustrated as I am with how long the repair is 
    taking.  Although we have been keeping users of the park in-
    formed through signage posted at the site as well as direct 
    contact with permitted users, I wanted to send this note to you 
    as the representative of the LVNA.  A power cable shorted out 
    between the electrical meter near the tennis courts and the 
    power drop from the PGE line on the other/south side of the 
    park.  Originally we thought this was a PGE problem because 
    of the location before the meter, this proved to not be the case 
    as PGE informed us that it was the city's responsibility.  Since 
    that time we have been doing everything we can to get our 
    electrical contractor to pull new cables but we are still waiting 
    for (Continued next column)                                                  
    (Cont.)  
    PGE to schedule an inspection of the process and hopefully 
    approve the installation, otherwise they will not do the final con-
    nection.  Each week I am hopeful and then something occurs 
    that delays the completion of the work.  I remain hopeful that we

    Based on Scott's e-mail, it sounds like all we can do is be patient

     Public Safety Budget Continued.. 
    Back in the 1990s when I first served 
    on the City Council, the Sunnyvale 
    department of Public Safety had a staff 
    of about 225 sworn officers.  A    
    Sunnyvale sworn officer is a person 
    who has been through both a police 
    academy, a fire academy, and a     
    rigorous screening process.  They have 
    the power to make arrests and enforce 
    the laws of our community.  They fill many different roles in the are-
    as of police patrol, fire      response, crime prevention, traffic en-
    forcement, and emergency preparedness.  Since the 1990s the area 
    of emergency medical treatment and response has been added to 
    their duties.  The 225 officers in 1997 were very busy, an I would 
    expect them to be even busier if they were all here now.   
    The recessions of the past decade have had a harsh impact on 
    Sunnyvale DPS staffing.  In 2003 I fought hard against an effort to 
    eliminate 15 sworn officer positions within the department.  Among 
    other cuts, the City Manager proposed completely eliminating the 
    DPS Narcotics unit.  I fought hard and lost back then, and the     
    department went down to 210 sworn officers.  As a postscript to that 
    fight, the City Council voted to re-instate the Narcotics unit less than 
    a year later. 
    As bad as that situation was, the current proposal apparently goes 
    much further.  The current idea is to cut another 15 officers from the 
    staff.  As much of their work as possible would be shifted to civilian staff-
    ers within the department.  The new total would be only 195 sworn offic-
    ers.  Almost all of them would be spread over the areas of patrol and fire    
    response, leaving virtually none in the Crime Prevention, the       Neigh-
    borhood Resource Officers, and the Emergency Services programs.  
    Even the Investigations unit would be cut back severely.  This is a budget 
    of despair.  I fear for the future of the city if this is all we can afford. 
    The Sunnyvale DPS Chief is a man named Don Johnson.  Don is a good 
    man and a smart man, and he is being faced with a very difficult situation.  
    Funds are low and there are many pressing needs within the City of 
    Sunnyvale.  Don is reasoning that it is possible for some of the jobs within 
    the department to be done by civilians, and those civilians are less 
    “expensive” to recruit, hire, train, and compensate, than sworn officers.  
    There is a growing national shortage of young people who are both quali-
    fied for and interested in a career in public safety.  Competition for recruits 
    is always high with other jurisdictions bidding up salary levels in an effort 
    to fill their own sworn officer shortages.  The more jobs that can be 
    “civilianized”, the more money can be saved, or so the reasoning goes. 
    The issue I have with that thinking that the most important tasks of the 
    Department of Public Safety cannot be done by civilians, and with fewer 
    sworn officers we'll have fewer resources needed to keep us safe.

    Civilians cannot enforce the law, only sworn officers can.  
    Civilians cannot fight structure fires, only qualified firefighters

    Sometimes big events take place that require more than a  
    day-to-day level response.  In 2000 there was a riot in 
    downtown Sunnyvale after a “gangsta-rap” promoter passed 
    out hundreds of flyers about an unannounced after-concert 
    party on South Murphy Avenue.  In 2003 there was another 
    incident when hundreds of panicky teenagers had to be 
    evacuated from the “Abyss” nightclub after a gang fight broke

    There have been other less visible instances over the years 
    when similar calls have been made.  It makes me very 
    uncomfortable to think the next time something similar occurs 
    there may be 15 fewer fully qualified sworn officers available 
    to answer such an “all hands” call. 
    There are other aspects of the budget that have me worried.  
    When the 1989 Loma Prieta earthquake struck, the Sunnyvale 
    Office of Emergency Services had a staff of five full time 
    people.  The current budget proposal would cut the OES down 
    to just a single civilian employee.  That is a situation I'm sure 
    we'll regret the next time a large disaster takes place.  I'm 
    crossing my fingers, hoping that the next “big one” doesn't 
    happen soon. 
    As I said in the beginning of this article, the Sunnyvale City 
    Council has some important choices to make.  The choices 
    are not easy (and no one said they would be easy), but they 
    must be made according to what is in the best interests of the 
    community. 
    I ask the Council to think carefully and choose wisely.  Don't 
    compromise our safety in the name of saving money.  Balance 
    the books, but make sure we won't regret the consequences 
    of the choices you make.  A safe peaceful and prosperous 
    community is the most valuable thing we have.  Don't put that 
    in jeopardy because of money. 

    Arson on the John Christian Greenbelt 
    by Fred Fowler 
    Greetings.  I would like to alert all of you to a serous prob-
    lem for our neighborhood, and ask your help to try to 
    solve it. 
    About two weeks ago my wife 
    and I were walking our dogs 
    near the Lakewood school 
    when we noticed some thick 
    white smoke coming from a 
    spot on the John Christian 
    Greenbelt just west of  
    Meadowlake Drive.  I went to 
    investigate and found that five 
    bushes along the trail had 
    been set on fire.  The wind 
    was      
    blowing briskly and was fanning the flames.  It wasn't a 
    good situation.  I called 911 immediately. 
     
    Firefighters from Fire Station #6 responded and were on 
    scene in just a matter of moments.  They put out the fire 
    and immediately started sifting through the ashes looking 
    for a cause.  I was asked if I had seen anyone nearby or 
    possibly fleeing the scene when I arrived.  Unfortunately, I 
    had seen no one. 
    The firefighters then told me that this was not the first fire 
    they had dealt with on the John Christian Greenbelt.  As I 
    walked on towards Hiddenlake drive I noticed what looked 
    like the remnants of at least two other fires in the not-too-
    distant past.  It looks like someone is deliberately burning 
    the brush along the trail.  The firefighters have no idea 
    who it may be or why they are doing it.  
    I would like to ask everyone reading this to keep an eye 
    open for more incidents like this.  There are at least 3,500 
    of us in Lakewood Village, and 3,500 pairs of eyes are 
    hard to hide from.  If you notice anything suspicious hap-
    pening on the John Christian Greenbelt (or in any other 
    part of Lakewood Village), please don't hesitate to call 
    911. Sunnyvale DPS would rather follow up a tip that turns 
    out to be nothing than to have more incidents like this hap-
    pen with no clue as to what is behind them.

    Dumpster Day is 
    Coming! 
    By Fred Fowler 
    Hello neighbors.  Last month I asked for  
    a “Tribune of Trash” or a “Dumpster 
    Diva” to head up our annual 
    neighborhood cleanup event.  I'm   
    proud to say that a very capable 
    individual has stepped forward!  None other than our 2nd Vice 
    President Ralph (Skip) Gottschalk has signed up to get the event 
    organized and underway.  Let's give Skip a hearty “Thank You!” 
    and commit to doing our part to make the 
    2010 Dumpster Day another smashing 
    success. 
    The date for Dumpster Day has been 
    set for July 17th.  That will be just three 
    days after our July general meeting, and 
    we'll use that LVNA meeting to go over 
    the details of the event.  Ours is by far the 
    biggest Dumpster Day in the city of 
    Sunnyvale.  Other neighborhoods such as the S.N.A.I.L area make 
    do with just six debris boxes, whereas we get twenty regular ones 
    plus a special one just for tires!  We need at least 60 volunteers to 
    make it all work.  It is a prodigious undertaking, and it takes quite a 
    lot of work to keep all of those ducks in line.        
     
    Many thanks and congratulations to Skip for committing to make 
    the entire day work.  Many thanks to all of you for doing your part to 
    make this very important neighborhood activity happen once again.  
    I'll see you there.
