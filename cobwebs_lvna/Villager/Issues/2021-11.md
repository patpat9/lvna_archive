    LOCAL NON-PROFITS
    Sunnyvale Community Services, 725 Kifer Rd
    The important stuff first: If you or someone you know in Sunnyvale (or the Alviso neighborhood in San Jose) 
    needs assistance, especially with food or finances, Sunnyvale Community Services (SCS) can help. Please 
    contact them at 408-738-4321. Their programs (including monthly groceries with fresh fruits and vegetables) 
    can help you cut down your cost of living and put your money towards other critical items. They provide 
    emergency financial assistance (for rent, utilities, medical bills, etc.), case management (staff members help 
    clients identify the right services), food assistance, kids’ school supplies, and extra support for the holidays for 
    those enrolled in the food program. For those who have trouble accessing services at SCS’ site, SCS offers a 
    Home Delivery Program to provide groceries and other services directly to clients’ homes.
    Who is this amazing organization? In 1970, members of the 
    community joined together to help Sunnyvale residents access and 
    benefit from a wide variety of services. Their mission is to prevent 
    homelessness and hunger in our local community - with the vision 
    of a community where everyone has a home with food on the table. 
    The group started in a small room in St. Thomas Episcopal Church, 
    and the organization was Sunnyvale Community Services. Since 
    then, community need has only grown, due to the steep cost of 
    living, few options for affordable housing, and an increasing wage 
    gap that makes it harder for low-income households to afford basics 
    like rent, utilities, and food.
    SCS serves Sunnyvale residents and residents from the Alviso neighborhood in San Jose. Their clients include

    Clients represent a variety of people who never thought they’d end up in a financial emergency. Nearly half of

    SCS also has special ties to our Lakewood/Fairwood neighborhood. 
    In 2019, when an apartment fire displaced more than 50 people in 
    Lakewood, SCS ensured that everyone found temporary housing 
    and followed up with each family to help them secure a new home. 
    You can read about one neighbor’s story at https://wishbook.
    mercurynews.com/2020/sunnyval. SCS runs a snack pantry program 
    at Lakewood Elementary, ensuring students have kid-friendly snacks 
    when they need them throughout the day. The agency serves more 
    than 10,300 people each year, throughout our city.
    Like everyone else, SCS has been heavily impacted by COVID and had already seen a surge in need in the years 
    leading up to it. They’ve had a sharp increase in demand for financial help and have seen a 50% increase in the 
    number of clients over a five-year period through 2020. With so many people losing their jobs (or having to quit 
    their jobs to watch their children when daycares and schools closed), requests for financial assistance went up 
    600% in the first few months of shelter-in-place, and they had to hire more staff to support the community. 
    SCS made several changes to accommodate COVID safety protocols. More staff members worked remotely or 
    started working on site on a rotating basis. Meetings with clients that were previously done in-person are now 
    done largely through the phone or email. Clients who come to the building use an intercom to communicate 
    with staff members and can complete program applications on outdoor tables. Food distributions continue to 
    be done in a drive-through format, and other events (e.g., school supply drive and the Holiday Center) have 
    switched to gift cards that are distributed at drive-through events as well. In spite of everything, SCS has not let 
    covid interrupt their operations, not even during shelter-in-place, working five days a week to meet their clients’ 
    needs.
    Prior to covid, SCS had already outgrown its space on Kifer Rd. Thankfully, they were able to locate, purchase, 
    and renovate a new building - 1160 Kern Avenue (near Sports Basement). The building is 36,000 square feet, 
    which will give SCS more room for private client consultations, volunteers, food storage, and space to host 
    partner agencies. Ultimately, it will allow SCS to meet growing community needs for decades to come. As a 
    bonus, it’s closer to our Lakewood and Fairwood communities!
    All of this important work depends on funding from community members, corporate or foundation donations, 
    and government grants. If you want to make a financial donation, go here: https://svcommunityservices.org/
    donate-online/. Due to covid and building capacity, SCS cannot take physical donations at this time (either new 
    or used items). Volunteers are always appreciated – you can sign up here: https://svcommunityservices.org/
    volunteer-sign-up/

    MEET YOUR NEIGHBOR
    DAWN HADDAWAY
    Q: Tell me a bit about yourself and your family.
    Dawn: I am a single mom, with 7 kids (ages 26, 22, 21, 20, 
    9, and two 7-year-olds). I have 6 boys and a girl (who will 
    never date). Currently, I have just 4 children, 2 labs, and 2 
    guinea pigs at home. My kids at home are the youngest 
    of generation one (20-yr-old son) and all of generation 
    two (9-yr-old boy, 7-yr-old boy and 7-yr-old girl; the twins 
    are birth siblings of the 20-yr-old). By the time my twins 
    graduate high school, I will have had kids in school for 46 
    continuous years. Really. Although I am a white chick, we 
    as a family literally check every single box on the ethnicity 
    list and enjoy celebrating the diversity within our home 
    as well as in our community. I consider myself from New 
    Jersey, which is where I lived before I moved to CA in 
    1987, and I was raised in SE Asia (Hong Kong, Manila, 
    Singapore). 
    I am probably best known for - and identify most as - a 
    foster parent. After leaving the dot.com world, I was 
    president of the executive board for the Santa Clara 
    County non-profit that actively supports foster parents 
    and foster children as well as develops DFCS and county-
    based policy for almost a decade. I retired and became 
    a stay-at-home mom when my second generation of 
    children had needs that sort of dictated how often I 
    could work outside of the home. I’ve had scores of foster 
    children in the past 20 years since I’ve been licensed, 
    and I’ve adopted 7. Prior to being a licensed foster 
    parent, I helped raise my niece and nephew who are what 
    prompted the sudden move to Lakewood in 1996, when 
    in search for a larger home. I was awarded the Sunnyvale 
    Distinguished Resident Award in 2007 for my work as a 
    foster parent.
    Q: When did you move to the Lakewood/Fairwood 
    neighborhood?
    Dawn: 1996, as a renter in the same house I now own. 
    Basically, I paid for it twice. lol.
    Q: What street do you live on?
    Dawn: Lakefair Dr.
    Q: Name one thing you see out your kitchen window.
    Dawn: My bird feeders and my fence.
    Q: Something that is in your front yard?
    Dawn: Roses, grass, kids riding bikes, and a few dead 
    trees the city won’t let me remove yet.
    Q: A sound you hear frequently in the neighborhood?
    Dawn: Birds and squirrels, the neighbor’s chickens, the 
    school bell from Lakewood, the occasional jet fly by, and the 
    loud music when someone comes home each evening.
    Q: What is something you like about living in Lakewood/
    Fairwood? 
    Dawn: The diversity. The multi-generational families that have 
    been here forever.  The home-based school, and school friends 
    living near us. Giovanni’s pizza just down the street.  The fighter 
    jets flying over our house from Moffett several times a year. 
    Great America, too, where we can go early and get out before 
    the big crowds are there many times each summer.
    Q: And something that has changed since you moved to the 
    neighborhood?
    Dawn: Many of the homes are changing. The 1,108 sq foot 
    isn’t enough anymore. They are bigger and aren’t the same 3-4 
    model homes anymore.  I’ve added 1,600 sq ft to my home as 
    well, but so far you can’t tell from the front of the house. It still 
    looks the same with the original 1957 giant window pane on 
    the front wall. The stadium changed a lot, and since I’m not a 
    Niners fan, I’m not so happy about that addition.
    Q: What are your favorite parks/things to do in Sunnyvale and/
    or the Bay Area?
    Dawn: We used to love the pool at Lakewood, I was sad to 
    see that eliminated. Walking to the Lakewood park is always 
    worthwhile, and Seven Seas is our favorite park. I can’t wait to 
    see what happens at the new Magical Bridge Park on Fair Oaks.
    Q: Any great stories to share about living in the neighborhood?
    Dawn: I’m a foster parent, and 10 months after picking up a 
    newborn in 2012, he had a sister born into foster care and the 
    county wanted me to adopt her as well. I was overloaded with 
    my current children (committed to just boys unless a girl snuck 
    in with a twin brother), and instead the sister was adopted by 
    the only other Sunnyvale foster family who lived on Lakewood 
    Drive, with the vision of having the siblings grow up together 
    and know each other, by keeping them neighbors.

    LVNA MEETING ON OCTOBER 13
    Last month, Wendy Silver and Chaunacey Dunklee 
    provided an update on the Lakewood Branch Library. 
    Unfortunately, this meeting was not recorded. 
    However, you can visit https://sunnyvale.ca.gov/
    business/projects/lakewoodlibrary.htm for additional 
    information. A couple follow-up answers to neighbors’ 
    questions:
    1.	 Are any streets going to be closed or impacted 
    during the construction phase?
    Response:  No streets are planned to be closed 
    for the duration of the construction project.  
    There may be periodic, limited impacts to 
    traffic on Lakechime Drive and the parking 
    lot near the pool will be closed for much of 
    construction, but this will be coordinated with 
    the school to minimize disruption as much as 
    possible.
    2.	 It is being built where the pool was. Are any other 
    facilities permanently closing to accommodate the 
    branch?
    Response:  The swimming pool has been 
    permanently closed for several years and will 
    be removed and filled in with this project 
    to provide additional parking for the new 
    building.  The new library will be built where 
    the small softball field directly south of the 
    pool is currently, but all other school and park 
    facilities will remain.
    Thank you, again, to Wendy and Chaunacey for 
    providing insight and clarity on this exciting new 
    project coming to our backyard!
