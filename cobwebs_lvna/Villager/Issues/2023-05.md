    Letter From The New President
    Dear members of the Lakewood Village Neighborhood Association,
    It is my honor to address you all as your newly elected president, and I am excited to share with you some of
    the plans and ideas I have for our community. As some of you may know, I am Rick Flovin, and it has been 25
    years since I last served as your president. However, my passion for our neighborhood has never waned, and I
    am excited to once again have the opportunity to serve as your leader.
    One of my top priorities as president will be to address the issue of crime in our neighborhood. I believe that
    by working together as a community, we can reduce the number of incidents and make Lakewood Village a
    safer place to live. This will require a collaborative effort between residents, law enforcement, and local
    government, but I am confident that together we can make a significant impact. Another critical issue that I am
    committed to addressing is the problem of homelessness. Homelessness is a complex issue that requires a
    comprehensive approach, but I believe that as a community, we can find solutions that are compassionate and
    effective. We must work together to provide resources and support for those who are experiencing
    homelessness while also addressing the root causes of the problem.
    In addition to these important issues, I am also committed to
    improving the appearance of our neighborhood through
    community clean-up initiatives. By working together to keep our
    streets and public spaces clean and well-maintained, we can create
    a more welcoming and attractive community that we can all be
    proud of.
    Finally, I believe that community involvement is essential to the
    success of any neighborhood association. I will work to create
    opportunities for all members of our community to get involved
    and have their voices heard. By working together, we can create a
    stronger and more vibrant Lakewood Village.
    In conclusion, I am excited to serve as your president and to work
    with each and every one of you to make our neighborhood a better
    place to live. Together, we can address crime, homelessness, and
    neighborhood clean-up while also fostering a strong sense of
    community involvement. Thank you for your support, and I look
    forward to working with you all.
    Sincerely, Rick Flovin

    Do not store valuables in your car! 
    If you do need to store valuables in your car, store it somewhere out of sight BEFORE you park the car. For
    example, if you want to store your computer in your car, put it in the trunk of your car before you leave work
    to drive home. If you move the computer to your trunk after you arrive home, a thief casing the
    neighborhood will see you do this and know that the computer is in the trunk.
    If you leave electronics (e.g., phone, tablet, computer) in the car, be sure to TURN OFF the Bluetooth on the
    device. If the Bluetooth is left on, these devices will emit a pairing signal, which thieves can detect by using
    a Bluetooth scanners. So even if you hide your tablet under a blanket, the thieves can still find it. For more
    on this type of theft, read this article (which specifically cites a crime prevention specialist at the San Jose
    Police Department): https://www.wired.com/story/bluetooth-scanner-car-thefts/ 
    If you have security cameras, make sure they are functional. Check with your neighbors to see if they have
    security cameras, so if something happens, you know which neighbors may have video of the incident.
    Make sure that trees and brushes do not block the views of your security cameras.
    If a crime occurs or if you see a crime in progress, call the police to report it.
    For anyone who has spent time on Nextdoor or watched the local news, there is a sense that crime is increasing
    drastically. More car break-ins, more thefts, and more property crimes in general. This is correct - property
    crimes are rising in Sunnyvale and across California. Most property crimes are “crimes of opportunity”; for
    example, the thief notices an item of value in your car and breaks the window to steal it. With that in mind, here
    are some suggestions to keep you and your property safe.
    Finally, one of the best things you can do is get to know your neighbors. They can watch your house when
    you’re on vacation, let you know if something suspicious happened, or even come by to tell you if you left your
    garage door open.

    California Park Spotlight: Pinnacles National Park
    This park is one of the newly designated parks and the only place where you can see (if you are lucky), the
    California Condor! Approximately 2 hours drive (south) from our neighborhood, this park is a world of spires,
    huge boulders, and towering peaks, all eroded into varied shapes, having been born out of volcanic eruptions 23
    million years ago . The park’s 26,000 acres are popular for wildlife watching, wildflowers, hiking and rock
    climbing! The first nest of a condor was found at Pinnacles in 2010. For a chance to see these magnificent birds,
    recommended sites are the High Peaks region (in early morning or early evening) and the ridge just southeast of
    the campground. More details can be found at: https://www.nps.gov/pinn/index.htm

    Short-term: focus on transportation, specifically to advocate for safe, reliable, affordable transportation (e.g.,

    Survey in English: Transportation to Fremont High School
    Encuesta en Español: Transporte a Fremont High School
    As you may know, most Sunnyvale public high school students attend high school as part of Fremont Union High
    School District (FUHSD). All of the FUHSD high schools are located south of El Camino Real. The only public high
    school in Sunnyvale that was north of El Camino Real, Sunnyvale High School, closed in 1981. Sunnyvale for
    Equity in Education (SEE) is advocating for our students in the short-term, mid-term, and long-term. SEE’s goals
    are:
    Although a new organization, SEE has had several successes already. In February, they successfully lobbied the
    City Council to rank the DPW 23-06 study of transportation for students in North Sunnyvale to Fremont High and
    Homestead High as the top priority study for the City. Their advocacy also led to the district passing a resolution
    in March to move forward with transitioning from at-large to by-district-area trustee elections. Additionally, with
    SEE’s advocacy, there will now be live Spanish translation available at the FUHSD board meetings.
    Finally, SEE is co-sponsoring a community meeting on May 20 titled “Transportation for High School Youth.” The
    meeting will cover topics such as current VTA services from North Sunnyvale to Fremont and Homestead High
    Schools, VTA bus pass availability, parking passes at Fremont High School, and the transportation needs of North
    Sunnyvale residents. See the flyer in this Villager on page 8.

    Bystander Intervention Training
    As a follow up to the March "Overcoming Bigotry" event,
    Sunnyvale Unity is sponsoring a Bystander Intervention
    Training on Thursday, May 11 from 7:00-8:30pm. Attendees can
    participate over Zoom or in person at the Sunnyvale Senior
    Center. 
    The training will cover the five strategies for intervention:
    distract, delegate, document, delay, and direct. You will learn
