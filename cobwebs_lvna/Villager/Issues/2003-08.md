    - Michael M. 
     
    In this world of uncertainty, there 
    should be places we can look to for 
    comfort and support.  One of these 
    should be our neighborhood.  A 
    neighborhood should be more than people 
    living in a particular district or area.  
    It is the relationship between people.  
    It is also the activities and 
    playgrounds that children can enjoy.  We 
    should always be looking to make 
    improvements.  If I were in charge of 
    Lakewood Village, there are specific 
    areas I would attempt to change.     
     
    When I was little, I was involved in 
    Lakewood little league baseball.  Aside 
    from screaming parents, cursing  
     
      
    X continued in next column 
     
     
    X continued from previous column 
     
    teammates who threatened me with balls 
    and bats, it was great times! 
     
    Luckily, my brothers have had much 
    better experiences than I, and their 
    teams and coaches have been great.  But 
    occasionally, when watching a game, I 
    will still see overzealous parents and 
    coaches. If I was in charge of Lakewood 
    I would make the parents supportive of 
    the kids, coaches, and umpires.  It is 
    impossible to change human nature, but I 
    would encourage civility by parents and 
    coaches in child sports.   
     
    Where have all the great parks gone?  I 
    remember growing up, and going to 
    incredible playgrounds.  Now, they have 
    been replaced by new, boring, ill-
    conceived structures.  Perhaps this is 
    due to injuries in the past, but the 
    effort should be made to be more 
    creative.  Safe, but fun playgrounds can 
    still be made.  I would have more 
    inventive parks.  Also, I would create a 
    new exercise area, and try to make 
    exercising more appealing and fun.  
     
    Some things we can change, and others we 
    can only dream about, but it is great 
    that we have the neighborhood that we 
    do, and we should all work to improve 
    it, and keep it strong.

    Walkers Join Together to Raise Funds for 
    the Susan G. Komen Breast Cancer 
    Foundation and the National Philanthropic 
    Trust 
     
    SAN FRANCISCO – November 7-8-9, 2003 – 
    Vicky Burns of Sunnyvale will join 
    approximately 3,000 ot her dedicated women 
    and men for the San Francisco Breast Cancer  
    3-Day benefiting the Susan G. Komen 
    Breast Cancer Foundation and the National 
    Philanthropic Trust.  The dedicated 
    participants will walk 60 miles over three days 
    to raise significant money and awareness for 
    breast cancer programs nationwide, including 
    medical research, education, screening and 
    treatment programs.  
     
    In the United States, more than 200,000 
    women are diagnosed with breast cancer each 
    year and nearly 40,000 women die from the 
    disease annually.  In America this year, a new 
    case of breast cancer will be diagnosed every 3 
    minutes and every 13 minutes, the disease will 
    claim another life.  The participants of the 
    Breast Cancer 3-Day have the opportunity to 
    make a personal differenc e in the fight against 
    breast cancer, and collectively, will have an 
    enormous impact on breast cancer funding.  
     
    The Breast Cancer 3-Day in San Francisco will 
    start on Friday mornin g, November 7, 2003 in 
    San Jose, Calif. and end with Closing 
    Ceremonies on Sunday afternoon, November 9 
    in San Francisco, Calif.  The approximately 
    3,000 walkers will cover about 20 miles a day,

    X continued from previous column 
     
    traveling at their own pace.  The walkers will be 
    supported by some 500 volunteer crew 
    members who will provide meals, water and 
    snack stops, gear transport, hot showers, 
    portable restrooms, safety on the streets and 
    comprehensive medical services.  
     
    Personal Motivation: 
    Vicky Burns has decided to participate in the 
    Breast Cancer 3-Day because… 
     
    In 1993 my life was touched by breast cancer 
    when my aunt was diagnosed.  Sharon Multhauf 
    was an incredible woman who brought grace 
    and learning to the disease of Breast Cancer.  
    Once she had her diagnosis she joined several 
    Breast Cancer groups and boards and searched 
    tirelessly for answers.  She appeared a local 
    morning television program where she was 
    shown searching for answer s on the internet at 
    a time when the internet was fairly new to 
    many of us.  Sharon was by profession a 
    teacher so, with breast cancer she taught 
    herself to find answers.  She helped do desk top 
    publishing for several breast cancer groups.  
    She underwent several rounds of treatment to 
    try to keep her terminal disease arrested.  She 
    kept in close contact with her doctors who 
    allowed Sharon to participate and chose many 
    of her treatment options.  While continuing to 
    fight the cancer she and her husband Lloyd 
    traveled as Sharon loved to travel.  She also 
    continued to trace the linage of our family even, 
    on one trip, scaling the fence of an old 
    graveyard in the Midwes t to find an ancient 
    family tombstone.  The photo from that event is 
    amazing, she leans on the trophy tombstone 
    triumphant, all of her hair missing from the last 
    round of chemo.  She was one of a kind.  She 
    died July 3rd, 2002.  I am walking for Sharon 
    Multhauf.  My aunt.  My hero.  

    Bay Area Air Quality District Offers Vehicle 
    Buy-Back Program 
     
    As a Board Member of the Bay Area Air Quality 
    Management District (Air District), Mayor Julia 
    Miller reports that if you have an operating and 
    registered vehicle from 1981 or older, you may 
    be eligible to participate in the Air District 
    Vehicle Buy-Back Program. To reduce air 
    pollution and improve air quality, the Vehicle 
    Buy Back Program will pay $500 for an 
    operating and registered 1981 and older 
    vehicle. The Program is a voluntary program 
    that takes older, more polluting, vehicles off the 
    road and is funded through the Air District's 
    Transportation Fund for Clean Air (TFCA). 
    Vehicle dismantlers contracted by the Air 
    District will scrap the vehicles. To participate in 
    the vehicle Buy-Back Program in San Jose call 
    (800) 727-2708 for information or to schedule 
    an appointment with the dismantler. 
