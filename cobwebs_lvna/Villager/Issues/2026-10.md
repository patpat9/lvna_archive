    Keeping Our Waterways Clean: Coastal Cleanup Day at
    Calabazas Creek
    LVNA VILLAGER - OCTOBER 2026
    PAGE 3
    On Saturday, September 19, dedicated neighborhood volunteers rolled up their sleeves and headed
    down to Calabazas Creek for National Coastal Cleanup Day.
    Working in partnership with the City of Santa Clara Solid Waste Creek Clean Up team and the Santa
    Clara Valley Water District, 14 volunteers cleaned 3 miles of creek corridor, collecting 80 lbs of litter,
    plastics, and debris before they could wash downstream into the Bay. Keeping our local creeks clean
    protects local wildlife, improves neighborhood aesthetics, and safeguards our watershed.
    A huge thank you to everyone who came out in sturdy boots and work gloves to make a tangible
    difference in our neighborhood environment!

    Understanding the November 2026 Sunnyvale Ballot
    Measures
    Ballots for the November 3, 2026 general election will arrive in mailboxes in early October. Lakewood Village is in
    Sunnyvale City Council District 6 (represented by Council member Eileen Le). Because only Districts 1, 3, and 5 are holding
    council elections this cycle, Lakewood residents will not see any City Council candidates on their ballot this year.
    All Sunnyvale voters will, however, vote on three proposed changes to the Sunnyvale City Charter (the city's constitution):
    Measures G, H, and I. Each measure needs more than 50% of the vote to pass.
    Note: As a nonpartisan 501(c)(3) organization, LVNA does not endorse or oppose candidates or ballot measures. This
    summary is provided solely for educational purposes to help neighbors understand the choices on their ballot.
    Measure G: How the City Hires Builders for Public Projects
    What it does: Right now, the city charter requires the city to hire designers and construction companies through separate
    contracts and pick whichever company submits the lowest bid. Measure G allows the city to also use modern contracting
    options, such as hiring a single team to handle both design and construction together.
    Arguments in favor: Supporters say this gives the city more flexibility to finish complex public projects faster, reduce delays,
    and prevent cost overruns. The traditional lowest-bidder process would still be available when it makes sense.
    Arguments against: Critics often prefer keeping the strict lowest-bidder rule to ensure direct price competition and reduce
    city staff discretion when choosing contractors.
    Measure H: Payout Limits for Legal Claims
    What it does: If someone files a legal claim or lawsuit against the city (for example, property damage caused by a city
    vehicle), the City Manager can currently approve payouts only up to $50,000. Anything higher requires a formal vote by the
    City Council. That $50,000 limit was set in 1989. Measure H allows the City Council to update this threshold, with plans to
    set it at $250,000.
    Arguments in favor: Supporters point out that $50,000 buys much less today than in 1989 due to inflation. Raising the limit
    lets the city settle minor, routine claims quickly without adding them to busy City Council agendas.
    Arguments against: Opponents prefer keeping the $50,000 limit so that elected council members publicly review and vote
    on all major payouts.
    Measure I: Filling Empty City Council Seats
    What it does: If a council member leaves office early and the City Council appoints a replacement, current rules require the
    city to hold a special election at the first available opportunity. Measure I removes that requirement, allowing the
    appointed person to serve until the next regular city election.
    Arguments in favor: Proponents argue that special elections cost taxpayers hundreds of thousands of dollars to fill a seat
    for only a short period. Letting an appointee finish the term until the next scheduled election saves public money.
    Arguments against: Opponents believe voters should choose their representatives at the ballot box as soon as possible,
    rather than having an appointed official serve an extended term.
    Learn More: Read the official arguments, rebuttals, and analyses by the City Attorney in your Santa Clara County Voter
    Information Guide or online at the City of Sunnyvale Elections website (https://www.sunnyvale.ca.gov/your-
    government/city-clerk/elections).

    Village Vistas: Autumn Splashes of Purple at the
    Playground
    Even as cooler mornings settle over Lakewood Village and trees begin their autumn turn, bursts of color are still
    thriving across the neighborhood. If you take a stroll past the playground under the bright orange shade sails, you
    will spot low, spreading mounds of lavender-purple blossoms brightening the mulch.
    These cheerful blooms belong to trailing lantana (Lantana montevidensis), a tough, drought-tolerant perennial
    that feels right at home in our sunny climate. While many summer favorites fade by mid-autumn, trailing lantana
    continues to bloom generously. Each delicate cluster features rings of tiny purple petals surrounding warm
    yellow-and-white centers, framed by textured, fragrant green leaves.
    It is easy to see why city landscapers and local gardeners love it. Trailing lantana shrugs off heat, needs very little
    water once established, and forms a dense, weed-suppressing carpet that stays green through most of the year.
    On warm autumn afternoons, the flowers double as a bustling stop for local honeybees and butterflies gathering
    late-season nectar.
    Next time you are out for a walk, heading over to the playground, or chatting with neighbors on the sidewalk,
    take a closer look at these resilient little blooms keeping our shared greenspaces colorful.
