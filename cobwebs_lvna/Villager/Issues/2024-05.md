    North Sunnyvale Will Elect FUHSD Trustee in November
    In early 2023, the five trustees on the Fremont Union High School District (FUHSD) school board voted
     unanimously to move the district from at-large elections to by-trustee area elections. In the current at-larg
    e elections, all voters vote for all five trustees and there has never been a trustee who lived in North Sunnvyale. With the new by-trustee area elections, the district will be mapped into five trustee areas. Each trustee area will get to vote for one trustee, who must live in the given trustee areas .
    On April 24, the FUHSD board held its fourth (and final) map hearing meeting in the Fremont High School
     auditorium. The board was presented with three options (maps) for how to legally divide the district into fiv
    e trustee areas. Two scenarios (Scenario 5 Revised and Scenario 6) were generated by the Committee for Trust
    ee Area Districting (CTAD). A third scenario (Scenario 8) was generated by district staff based on input from pr
    ior CTAD meetings. CTAD is a group of 13 adults and 3 current FUHSD students who applied for the committee 
    last fall. CTAD members held six CTAD meetings and hosted multiple additional outreach meetings to co
    mpile feedback from community members throughout the district. 
    The FUHSD board voted to select revised Map 5 (see image or go to
     this link) for a higher resolution version that includes additiona
    l information). The map has 4% variance in population, with thr
    ee trustee areas with an Asian CVAP (Citizen Voting Age Populati
    on) majority and one trustee area with a Hispanic CVAP of 27.7%. North 
    Sunnyvale (including our neighborhood) is included in Trustee Area 2.
    The current FUHSD board includes one trustee from Trustee Area 1,
     three trustees from Trustee Area 3, and one trustee from Trustee Are
    a 4. There are no current trustees who reside in Trustee Area 2 (Nor
    th Sunnyvale) or Trustee Area 5 (West San Jose/Lynbrook). 
    Each trustee serves a four-year term, and terms for two of the trustees
     expire this year. The election sequence was determined so that the tw
    o trustee seats that expire this year will move to by-trustee ar
    ea elections for the November 2024 election. 
    One trustee from Trustee Area 2 and one trustee from Trustee Area 5 will be elected. Trustee Areas 1, 3, and 4 will 
    have their first by-trustee area election in November 2026.
    As a result of this momentous decision, North Sunnyvale will have an historic election this fall. For the first time
     ever, the FUHSD board will include a trustee who lives in our zip code.

    Lakewood Shopping Center To Be Replaced By Mostly Townhouses
    The City is developing seven Village Centers, including one to replace the current Lakewood
    Shopping Center at Lawrence Expressway and Lakehaven Ave. Plans have been submitted to the City
    to replace this shopping center with 100 new townhomes. A second development proposal at the
    corner of Duane Ave and San Rafael St will replace that shopping center with 117 new townhouses.   
    While Sunnyvale badly needs additional
    housing, these developments will result
    in a significant reduction of retail space
    at the Lakewood location - and a
    complete loss of retail at the Duane
    location (see table).
    Proposed Village Center to replace the Lakewood Shopping Center.
     Note that the four mustard-color boxes are the only planned retail. The rest is all townhouses. Courtesy of Livable Sunnyvale .
    Comparison of Sunnyvale grocery stores that are
     located north of the CalTrain tracks vs located south o
    f the CalTrain tracks. The three crossed-out groce
    ry stores will be destroyed as part of the propo
    sed Lakehaven and Duane developments. Courtesy of 
    Livable Sunnyvale.
    Today, there are roughly 17 grocery stores in Sunnyvale located south of the Caltrain tracks. There are
    about nine grocery stores in Sunnyvale north of the Caltrain tracks, including New Wing Yuan Market at
    Lakewood and Taj Mahal Fresh Market and Western Pacific Filipino Grocery at Duane. The Village Center
    proposal will eliminate these three grocery stores, further increasing the disparity in access to grocery
    stores faced by North Sunnyvale residents. 
    These developments mirror proposals for several other “Village Center” locations throughout the city. These
    Village Centers were intended to be vibrant, mixed-use developments with a combination of retail and
    residential uses. However, in practice they are mostly townhouses. Continued on next page

    Maintaining Our Yards
    Spring is a great time to critically assess what you want your home’s front yard to look like. Taking care of
     your yard is great exercise and gets you outdoors in the fresh air. Maintaining your home's outdoor spac
    e plays a multifaceted role in both our lives and our neighbors' lives. Benefits such as beautifying the spac
    e, increasing home values, and enriching our physical, mental, and emotional well-being are all aspe
    cts everyone can enjoy when we each do our part in taking care of our yards. You may even consider gro
    wing edible herbs, fruits, or vegetables to take full advantage of our wonderful California weather and have 
    easily accessible, tasty food.
    If yards become a persistent problem, Sunnyvale does have regulations on weed and grass control and can
     respond to neighborhood complaints. However, we encourage each of our residents to maintain their ow
    n yards or reach out for help if you are unable to do so. Nextdoor can be used to contact friendly neighbors 
    who may be willing to assist their neighbors in need.
    This increase in townhouses is partially the result of state laws passed in 2019 to prevent cities from
     delaying and denying new developments. These laws enabled many new developments, like the Vallc
    o Shopping Center project in Cupertino, but limited the City of Sunnyvale’s ability to influence what is bui
    lt at these Village Centers. The City has still not updated zoning and other requirements for the Vill
    age Center locations to address these state law changes. 
    The City has limited power to require changes to the development proposal. Community groups, including
     Livable Sunnyvale, are trying to have a meeting with the developers. If scheduled, residents need t
    o attend this meeting to explain the impact that the loss of retail will have on our neighborhood and to a
    sk the developers to modify their plans.

    In March 2022, the Santa Clara Council City Council approved the Patrick Hendry Drive Specific Plan.
    Details are available at www.tinyurl.com/PHDSpecificPlan. As part of this plan, in April, the City unveiled
    development plans for 4590 Patrick Henry Drive. This 2.7-acre property is currently a 42,821 square-foot
    industrial building. The proposed plans will tear down the building and replace it with an eight-story, 284-
    unit multifamily resident building. The project will be developed by Walnut Hill Group, which is
    represented by KASA Partners.
    Some additional details of the project are:
    The building will be 85 feet tall
    The building will approximately 417,000 square feet
    15% affordable housing units
    A mix of studios, one-bedroom, and two-bedroom units
    A 0.5 acre public park
