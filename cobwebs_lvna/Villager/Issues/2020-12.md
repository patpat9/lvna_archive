    FACTOID
    What’s that?! Every few months a conversation pops up on Nextdoor about what 
    these interesting critters are, followed by a debate on what to call them.
    To kick this off scientifically, the “cute” little critter, to the right, is an opossum, 
    or, more specifically, a Virginia opossum (Didelphis virginiana). These are the 
    only marsupials found north of Mexico. Their name is actually derived from 
    “aposoum,” which is Powhatan, a Native American language, for “white animal.” 
    However, the ones we see in Lakewood Village are typically grayer, with the white 
    portions predominantly appearing only on their faces.
    Opossums can have 50 teeth and may appear dangerous, but they’re not at all aggressive. Unless you try to 
    grab them, they won’t bite you. They also won’t try to chase you or your pets, but they may hiss or growl if 
    they’re spooked. They may also attempt their signature move – “playing possum,” by acting sick and/or dead, 
    if they feel threatened. If you’d prefer to discourage them from hanging out around your house, be sure not to 
    leave out any dog or cat food and to pick up garbage and fruits/veggies that drop from your trees. They may try 
    to climb a tree if they see something scrumptious up there, but they’re much more interested in opportunistic 
    treats, so they’d be more inclined to visit those of us who aren’t quite as quick to pick up fallen fruit. These 
    guys are great to have around the neighborhood, though, so if you don’t mind them wandering around your 
    yard, you may benefit from their scavenging omnivorous tendencies. Opossums are great at cleaning up dead 
    animals (including the bones), like road-kill; eating slugs, snails, and other bugs that make our gardens unhappy; 
    and they’ll gladly help us out with the fallen fruit issue we mentioned above. They’re basically procrastinators’ 
    heroes!
    Now, why the confusion for what to call them? Well, there’s a group of marsupials native to Australia that got 
    their name from their resemblance to the opossums we have here in North America. The Australasian name for 
    the similar-looking marsupials is just “possum,” without the leading “O.” So between our innate American need 
    to drop useless syllables and an already-existing word for a different animal, here we are with a justified reason 
    for mix-ups. If you’re speaking with someone colloquially, either option is acceptable {opossum, possum}. But, 
    if you’re writing a scientific paper, it’s best to go with the “O” for our native Virginia opossums. In case it makes 
    you feel better, they have the same confusion over in Australia and New Zealand.

    LVNA MEETING RECAP
    LVNA MEETING ON NOV 11
    Sunnyvale’s district 6 city council winner, Omar Din, joined us and our fellow district 6 neighborhoods for a virtual meet 
    and greet. Din provided an overview of how he got to where he is today, starting with his parents emigrating from Pakistan 
    in the 80s, through his education at DeAnza and a public policy program at Cornell, to his work with congressman Mike 
    Honda and assemblyman Evan Low.
    Key initiatives: housing affordability, livability (traffic, infrastructure, etc.), radical sustainability – most of district 6 is at sea 
    level, COVID-19 response.
    Some Examples from Resident Q&A:
    Is a council member a full-time or part-time position? Council is a part-time position.
    Northern Sunnyvale has a stigma of being a lower-class section of Sunnyvale. Why is that and how can we change it?
    Sunnyvale is often thought of as being split (northern Sunnyvale and all the rest; split at 101). A big part is due to resources 
    being consolidated in the southern part (retail, restaurants, etc.). This is the first time we’ve had district representation 
    and, historically, council members have predominantly lived in the southern part of Sunnyvale. The culture tends to form 
    around that. As a result, Downtown gets prioritized. My plan for fixing that has a couple parts: change the culture around 
    it; and we can do that by placing more of the city resources in the northern part. Example: Lakewood branch library. We 
    should utilize that to have more presence in the northern part (commission meetings, public city meetings, info sessions, 
    etc.). The other part is getting more retail and food resources up here.
    We are super happy to announce that Councilmember Din has agreed to join us for a follow-up meeting, and potentially 
    subsequent discussions, to provide updates on the activities/initiatives from the council/mayoral level, as well as getting 
    feedback from district 6 residents on topics of interest.
    Discussion Topics for Follow-Up:
    •	
    Dangerous intersections, speeding, and donuts in the neighborhoods.
    •	
    Traffic from new housing/retail.
    •	
    Litter/debris around the city – potential programs to improve it, similar to San Jose.
    •	
    Residents having a voice (split trashcan initiative, Lakewood swimming pool removal, etc.).
    •	
    Vacant land use in Lakewood Village (Manzano & John W. Christian Greenbelt)
    •	
    Why are so few things actually recyclable and why are there inconsistencies in documentation?
    •	
    18-wheelers on residential streets that aren’t intended for commercial vehicles.
    •	
    Improved lighting & safety on trails.
    •	
    Homeless situation in neighborhoods and on bay trails (Multiple layers, but need to have a system in place to improve 
    housing affordability so people aren’t getting priced out, plus we need resources for those who are already out on the 
    streets.)

    RECIPE CORNER
    Cranberry Chia Sauce
    16 OZ cranberries, rinsed
    1/2 CUP honey
    1/2 CUP water 
    1/2 CUP juiced orange
    zest of 1 orange
    2 TBSP chia seeds
    1.	 Place cranberries, honey, and water in a medium saucepan. 
    Bring the pot to a boil over medium-high heat and then 
    turn down the heat to medium-low. Stir occasionally and 
    let the mixture simmer for about 10 minutes or until the 
    cranberries have popped and everything has thickened.
    2.	 Remove the pan from the heat and stir in orange zest, 
    juiced orange, and chia seeds. 
    3.	 The sauce will continue to thicken as it cools. Transfer sauce 
    from pot to container and refrigerate overnight. Enjoy!
