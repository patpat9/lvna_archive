    President’s Column 
    - Ursula Syrova, LVNA President 
    Elections! 
    At the next general meeting on March 9, 2005 at the 
    Lakewood Park building, we will be electing new 
    board officers.  We need to fill the positions of 
    President, First Vice President, Second Vice President, 
    Secretary, and Treasurer.  The roles of the president 
    and vice presidents are about the same, although the 
    president serves as first point of contact with the city, at 
    least officially.  This means that the city has the 
    president’s name and address on file and sends along 
    information notices about what is going on in 
    Sunnyvale so that it can be distributed to the 
    neighborhood, usually through the Villager. 
    During my time as president I have received e-mails 
    from city officials about issues that affect our 
    neighborhood, invitations to the quarterly 
    neighborhood association meetings, and the invitation 
    to speak at the Lakewood Park rededication.  Other 
    than point of contact for the city, the president and two 
    vice presidents share organizing responsibility for the 
    various activities put on by the LVNA--the parade, the 
    picnic, Dumpster Day, National Night Out, and the 
    holiday lighting contest.  We each have done what we 
    are able.   
    The secretary’s official role is to keep notes from our 
    board and general meetings and to write them up for 
    publication in the Villager.  However, during my time 
    on the board, Skip Gottschalk (our current secretary) 
    has also helped out with general coordinating of 
    activities as much as I or the vice presidents have.  The 
    treasurer keeps track of the LVNA finances, depositing 
    checks as needed and writing checks as required for the 
    printing of the Villager and other expenses.   
     
    I have found that the role of president is not very time 
    consuming.  Board members commit to attending the 
    monthly board meetings and the general meetings; 
    otherwise, the involvement is “as able.”  Do you have a 
    few hours a month you could spend helping to assure 
    that the LVNA does its job?  We would love to see 
    some new people get involved!  Please contact any of 
    the current board members (see the back page of the 
    Villager for contact phone numbers and e-mail 
    addresses) for more information, and please come to 
    the general meeting in March to vote! 
    Other Volunteer Positions That Need to Be Filled 
    If being a board member doesn’t appeal, how about 
    being our Newsletter Coordinator?  This person 
    interacts with the Villager advertisers, who are local 
    businesses.  The job requires invoicing for the 
    advertisements and following up on the payment of 
    those invoices.  This person is also the point of contact 
    for new advertisers who want to run ads in the Villager.  
    If you are interested and want to know more, please 
    contact Faith Rogers.

    Dear Neighbors, 
    Recently vandals/skateboarders dragged 
    two of the three wooden picnic tables 
    from the kindergarten playground at 
    Lakewood School.  They placed them at the 
    foot of the stairs facing Meadowlake 
    Drive and did their stunts on them until 
    both tables were rendered broken/unusable 
    by the kindergartners.  (They had dragged 
    the first of these three tables out 
    during the Christmas holiday break and 
    badly damaged that one also.)  Being both 
    a longtime resident of Lakewood Village 
    and the mother of a kindergartener, this 
    angered and disappointed me. So, I'm 
    turning my energy towards raising funds 
    to replace the damaged tables with better 
    ones, which will be secured to deter 
    their removal from the kindergarten area.  
    In today's era of budget cuts, it is 
    highly improbable that the school 
    district can replace the tables anytime 
    soon, so I am working with Lakewood's 
    administration and the PTO to raise these 
    funds, which total roughly $2000.00.  You 
    may wonder why it is so expensive, but it 
    makes more sense to purchase round, 
    weatherproof tables, than to simply 
    provide the same wooden tables which are 
    prone to weathering, splintering, and 
    apparently are very tempting to 
    skateboarders. 
    Ideas that have surfaced include holding 
    a dance at the school with proceeds going 
    to fund these tables, and setting up a 
    donation station in the Lakewood School 
    lobby.  Please watch the marquee outside 
    the school for further information.  
    Whether or not you have a kindergarten 
    student, or even ANY student attending 
    Lakewood School, please consider donating 
    towards this goal.  The kindergarteners 
    need these tables in their own playground 
    area to eat their snack, work on projects 
    outside, and rest during recess.  Please 
    consider helping us raise these funds.  
    The kindergartners would be very happy to 
    have new tables! 
     
    Thank you, 
    Ann Richardson

    March in Your 
    Yard and Garden 
    o Sow seeds of summer 
    vegetables and annuals indoors. 
    o The bare-root season is almost 
    over. Buy soon and plant when 
    the weather is right for 
    planting. Complete those orders 
    for bare-root fruit trees from 
    specialty nurseries, which 
    usually stop shipping bare-root fruit trees by the 
    end of March. 
    o Send in orders to seed companies before they run 
    out of stock later in spring. 
    o Wet weather is good for indoor gardening chores 
    such as cleaning and repairing garden tools. Do not 
    forget to sharpen the blades of pruning tools and 
    shovels, and to service the lawn mower. 
    o Begin weeding while the weeds are young and the 
    ground is still wet. 
    o The rice growers in the Sacramento Valley remove 
    the fibrous hulls from the grains. Rice hulls are an 
    excellent, inexpensive soil amendment. Contact a 
    rice growers’ cooperative to find the nearest local 
    source. 
    o Fertilize roses, citrus, and all flowering plants that 
    are about to bloom. Trees, shrubs, and cool season 
    lawns could use a high-nitrogen fertilizer. 
    o Fertilize azaleas, camellias, and rhododendrons 
    when they finish blooming. 
    o Prune spring-blooming plants as they finish 
    blooming. Toward the end of the month (but before 
    new growth begins), prune back both oleanders and 
    crape myrtles to produce new flowering wood. 
    o Watch out for snails and earwigs. 
    o Use a strong spray from the hose to wash away 
    aphids. 
    o After the danger of frost is past, prune hydrangeas, 
    fuchsias, and bougainvilleas. 
    o Plant out summer bulbs such as amaryllis, callas, 
    cannas, dahlias, gladiolus, lilies, and tuberous 
    begonias. 
    These tips are from the book Northern California Gardening: A 
    Month-by-Month Guide, by Katherine Grace Endicott, published by 
    Chronicle Books, San Francisco, ©1996, and are used with the 
    publisher’s permission. For more information, see: 
    http://www.lvna.net/Resources/Reading_List/reading_list.html  
     
    Fairwood School News 
    - Mary Jane Cabildo 
    The Fairwood Parent and Teachers 
    Group (PTG) met January 25 and 
    February 15 in the library. Thanks to all 
    who attended.  
    Mrs. Aguilar (2nd grade teacher) and Mrs. Fitz (5th 
    grade teacher) discussed the Standard Based Report 
    Card (SBRC) that will be implemented for the 2006–
    2007 school year for grades K–5. Standard Based does 
    not give a general grade; however, the report card will 
    be much more descriptive. With the SBRC parents will 
    be able to see specific skills their child has at each 
    grade level. Each subject and each grade level have a 
    “standard”: all teachers will teach the same. This will 
    also help teachers meet students’ needs more 
    effectively. We thank Mrs. Aguilar and Mrs. Fitz, and 
    we look forward to learning more about these new 
    report cards in the near future.  
    A big thanks to Mr. Bob Green for attending our 
    meeting on February 15. Mr. Green is the president of 
    Lakewood Pony Baseball. Last day for tryouts was 
    March 5. For information on the league, please visit 
    their Web site at www.lakewoodponybaseball.com or 
    call 866-895-9841. 
    Our next PTG meeting will be held March 29 at 6:45 
    p.m. in the library. Babysitting and Spanish translation 
    services are provided.  
    PTG is a great opportunity to meet your fellow 
    neighbors, work together as a stronger community and 
    provide a brighter future for our children. Come see 
    how you can help at Fairwood School.

    Lakewood Village’s 50th 
    Part 2: 
    Building Lakewood Village 
    - Brian Smithson 
    The early 1950s were a period of explosive growth for 
    cities and towns in northern Santa Clara Valley. Most 
    of the valley was unincorporated agricultural land, and 
    Santa Clara, Sunnyvale, and Mountain View competed 
    for it through legal battles and by annexing strips of 
    land that outlined borders. This strategy kept 
    neighboring towns from annexing unincorporated land 
    within those borders. Competition for annexing land 
    became so fervent that the town of Cupertino 
    incorporated itself as a defensive measure to keep from 
    being annexed by neighboring Sunnyvale and 
    Mountain View. 
     
     
    Narrow strips of land annexed in the 1950s by cities like 
    Sunnyvale defined their cities’ limits. Part of Lakewood Village 
    is shown on this 1955 map. 
     
    Around 1954, a strip of land was annexed which ran 
    the length of Calabazas Creek from the Bayshore 
    Highway to the bay. It was mostly wetlands, far away 
    from the Santa Clara and Sunnyvale town centers, but 
    it established a permanent boundary between the two 
    towns. Santa Clara annexed land on the east side of the 
    creek, where present-day Mission College is located, 
    and Sunnyvale annexed the west side, where Lakewood 
    Village is located. 
    In late 1954 and early 1955, developers submitted plans 
    for residential tracts of homes and for a motel on the 
    northeast corner of Bayshore Highway and Lawrence 
    Station Road. The motel wasn’t built until the early 
    1960s, but in mid-1955, building permits for 255 
    homes were issued and construction began on “Unit 1” 
    of Lakewood Village. Construction of the remaining 
    units continued until sometime around 1958.  
     
    Lakewood Village was built in four phases 
    Lakewood Village was constructed 
    to fill a need for affordable 
    housing of civilian and military 
    workers in the Moffett Field area. 
    Homes sold for $11,000 - $15,000 
    with mortgage payments under 
    $100 per month and move-in costs 
    under $500. Twenty floor plans 
    were advertised, with three 
    bedrooms and two baths, designed 
    for young families. Homes in 
    Units 1 and 2 had one-car garages, 
    while Unit 3 and 4 plans 
    were expanded for two cars. 
    A sales brochure promised 
    2,500 new homes, but 
    ultimately, about 1,600 
    homes were built.

    Life in early Lakewood Village wasn’t easy. The 
    closest grocery store was several miles away. Lawrence 
    Station Road was a busy truck route with no stoplight 
    for Lakewood Village. The Bayshore Highway had a 
    simple traffic signal at Lawrence Station Road, and 
    crossing on foot or making a left turn in a car could be 
    hazardous. Because of its distance from the rest of 
    Sunnyvale, city services like street lights, street 
    sweeping, and police patrols, were slow to come to 
    Lakewood Village.
