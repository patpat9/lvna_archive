    President’s Column 
    -Michael Majchrowicz 
    The annual LVNA picnic is coming up on September 
    15, 11:30 a.m. to 3:00 p.m. in the large picnic area at 
    Lakewood Park. There is always good food and good 
    entertainment. The city will have a police car and fire 
    truck there for the kids, as well as the adults, to enjoy.  
    Bingo time is here again! Everyone had a good time 
    when we did it in June, so we plan to do it at the next 
    meeting, Wednesday, September 12 at 7:00 pm. Bingo 
    winners will get gift certificates to places such as In-NOut Burgers, Ben and Jerry’s, AMC 20 Theatres, 
    Starbucks, and Jamba Juice. All ages can play. Come 
    and have a great time! 
    If you who would like to comment on or ask a question 
    about something going on in the neighborhood but don’t 
    want to wait until the next LVNA meeting, sign up at 
    www.topica.com/lists/lakewoodvillage. I just signed up 
    and I found that I can read and respond to others’ 
    comments and post my own. This on-line service gives 
    us a good way to continue dialogue as a community any 
    day of the week, so please visit the site. 
    Finally, I would like to introduce the newest member of 
    our LVNA board, Felipe Misch, our 2nd vice president. 
    He has been gracious enough to volunteer as a 
    Lakewood Village liaison, assisting with representation 
    of neighborhood diversity. Felipe has approached this 
    important role with great passion. We appreciate his 
    enthusiasm and willingness to look for ways to increase 
    participation in the Lakewood Village Neighborhood 
    Association. This is one of the largest neighborhood 
    associations in Sunnyvale! We can take pride in that and, 
    as individuals, commit to improving it every day. 
     
    See you all at the September 12 LVNA meeting and at 
    the annual picnic on September 15! ■

    Speeders Beware! 
    School is now in session, and there are lots of children 
    walking in our neighborhood. The speed limit is 25, 
    NOT 50 or 60. How would you feel if you hit a child—
    or an adult, for that matter—and seriously hurt or killed 
    them? Speeding is against the law, and so is vehicular 
    manslaughter. 
    If you have to be somewhere at a certain time, either 
    leave earlier or at least wait until you get to the freeway 
    to make up time. Don’t take the chance of injuring or 
    killing someone. 
    If you have friends who visit our neighborhood, tell 
    them not to speed on our streets. Protect your neighbors, 
    especially the kids. ■

    ☼For the best selection, purchase 
    spring bulbs as soon as they come 
    on the market. Shop for native 
    plants, trees, and shrubs. Do not buy or plant frosttender plants such as citrus and bougainvillea (wait 
    until next spring). 
    ☼Continue to weed. Add young, seedhead-free 
    weeds and excess summer growth to the compost 
    pile. Remember to keep the pile as damp as a wrungout sponge for faster composting. 
    ☼As always, keep after the snails. Use a strong 
    spray from the hose to wash away aphids. Ants are 
    likely to become a problem soon. Discourage them 
    by picking up fallen fruit and sealing off cracks that 
    would lead them into the house.  
    ☼Hold off fertilizing most plants in preparation for 
    winter, but do fertilize all flowering plants that are 
    about to bloom. Fertilize roses with a low-nitrogen 
    formula or one that has no nitrogen at all. Do not 
    fertilize most antique roses. 
     
     
    ☼Pinch off dead flowers. Of course, if you wish to 
    save seeds for planting in the future, you will want to 
    leave the entire flower head, particularly the swollen 
    ovaries where seeds form. 
    ☼Postpone transplanting fall- and winter-flowering 
    plants into the bedding plot during a heat wave. Hot 
    weather is hard on newly transplanted plants. If you 
    do transplant, shade them for a few days with the 
    temporary shelter of an umbrella or a wooden shake. 
    Stake tall, sprawling flowers. 
    ☼Harvest vegetables as they ripen. Pick them early in 
    the day. 
    ☼Continue to monitor your watering system. Soak all 
    newly transplanted plants. 
    These tips are from the book Northern California 
    Gardening: A Month-by-Month Guide, by Katherine 
    Grace Endicott, published by Chronicle Books, San 
    Francisco, copyright 1996, and are used with the 
    publisher’s permission. 
    Reminder: The SMaRT Station on Borregas Drive, off 
    Caribbean Drive, has mounds of compost and mulch that 
    are free to Sunnyvale residents. Take your own container 
    and shovel. ■

    Volunteering—Who Can Do It? 
    -Wendy Stanley 
    Every organization needs volunteers to survive—
    Parent Teacher Organizations (PTOs), neighborhood 
    groups, Boy Scouts and Girl Scouts, baseball, etc. 
    These days it seems volunteers are in short supply. 
    Perhaps everyone figures they don’t have the time to 
    help or that someone else will do it.  
    But what if no one volunteered? How would these 
    groups function? They couldn’t. Lakewood School 
    has come close a couple of times to not having a 
    PTO. One year it got down to two members, and one 
    of them stayed only because she couldn’t leave the 
    other one to do it alone. The Lakewood Village 
    Neighborhood Association can’t continue if it 
    doesn’t have volunteers for the board, the Villager, 
    the meetings, the parade, and the picnic.  
    I make time to volunteer because I know how 
    desperately people are needed for these jobs. Last  
     
     
     
    year I helped the music department at Columbia 
    School, I coordinate delivery of the Villager for my 
    side of Lakewood Village (sometimes walking a 
    route myself if no one else will do it), and until 
    recently, I was involved with the Army Reserve 
    Family Readiness Group. I will be helping the 
    Fremont High School music department now that 
    one of my sons goes there. I have two boys, 13 and 
    16. My 16-year-old son is autistic and takes a lot of 
    work, so I am a stay-at-home mom and do most of 
    my volunteering during school hours, but both of the 
    other mothers in our carpool work outside the home 
    and still find time to volunteer, so it can be done by 
    working parents.  
    Back to School nights are coming up soon, and the 
    schools will be asking for volunteers. Please take 
    time out of your busy lives to lend a helping hand. 
    When you see how it helps others, or makes a kid 
    smile, it’s all worth it. ■

    Are You a Good Neighbor? 
    At the July LVNA meeting, Neighborhood 
    Preservation Specialist Jhoanne Navarro talked 
    about some of the ordinances that affect residents. 
    Here are some things we all need to know. 
    Property owners and renters are responsible for 
    maintaining their properties to avoid causing 
    neighborhood nuisances. Sunnyvale has adopted 
    ordinances aimed at creating and maintaining a 
    healthy, safe, and pleasant environment in which to 
    live, work, and play. 
    Fences: City standards guide the location, height, 
    and construction of fences. Any fence or other 
    structure or thing on private property abutting, 
    fronting upon, or visible from any public street, 
    sidewalk or place must be in good condition. 
    Sagging, leaning, fallen, decayed, or otherwise 
    unsafe structures must be removed or repaired. 
    Before you install a fence, check with Neighborhood 
    Preservation about permits that may be required. 
    Home Businesses on Residential Property: Home 
    businesses are permitted if you have a city-issued 
    business license. To maintain the residential 
    character of the neighborhood, specific city 
    ordinances regulate traffic, noise, and signage.  
    Junk and Debris and/or Outside Storage: 
    Accumulations of litter, junk, machine parts, scrap 
    material, plant trimmings, and similar matter are a 
    health and safety hazard and detract from the 
    appearance of individual properties as well as the 
    entire neighborhood. These things are prohibited and 
    must be removed. 
    Vehicles 
    Auto Repair is allowed only between the hours of 
    7:00 am and 10:00 pm. and within an enclosed 
    structure shielded from public view. Vehicle 
    painting, except for minor touch-ups, is prohibited. 
    Vehicle repair as a business is prohibited at all times. 
    Parking on unpaved surface: Vehicles cannot be 
    parked on an unpaved surface at any time.  
     
     
    Parking on paved surface: Vehicles may be parked 
    in the driveway, but must be operable and registered. 
    The paved surface can cover no more than 50% of 
    the front yard. Inoperable or dismantled vehicles 
    must be in a garage or other enclosed area out of 
    public view.  
    Vehicles parked on the street must be licensed and 
    operable, and cannot be left in one location for more 
    than 72-consecutive hours.  
    Boats and recreational vehicles (RVs) must be 
    parked perpendicular to the street. They must be on a 
    permanent paved surface and not blocking any 
    driveway. They must be licensed and operable.  
    To report abandoned or inoperable vehicles, 
    contact the Department of Public Safety, 
    Nuisance Vehicle Hotline at (408) 730-7706.

    hours as 7:00 a.m. to 10:00 p.m. Some of the

    Operational Noise: Continuous or frequent noise 
    related to the basic use of the property includes noise 
    produced by air conditioners, equipment for 
    swimming pools or spas, industrial machinery, air 
    compressors, or fork lifts. Powered equipment or 
    deliveries are not considered operational noise. 
    Operational noise must not exceed 75 dBA 
    (decibels) at any point on the property line of the 
    premises upon which noise or sound is generated. 
    Maximum dBA for properties adjacent to residential 
    zoning districts: Daytime hours—60 dBA; Night 
    time hours (10:00 p.m. to 7:00 a.m.)—50 dBA. If 
    the noise involves a steady, audible tone such as a 
    whine, screech, or hum, or is a staccato or 
    intermittent noise (such as hammering) or includes 
    music or speech, the noise or sound level must not 
    exceed 45 dBA. 
    Powered Equipment: Any motorized device powered 
    by electricity or fuel and used for construction, 
    demolition, property or landscape maintenance, and 
    repairs is considered powered equipment. It includes 
    lawn mowers, edgers, parking lot sweepers, saws, 
    sanders, motors, pumps, generators, fans, wood 
    chippers, vacuums, and nail guns. Powered 
    equipment used on a temporary, occasional, or 
    infrequent basis which generates a maximum of 60 
    dBA can be operated only during daytime hours 
    when adjacent to a residential zoning district.

    Attention, Dog Owners 
    Some dog owners allow their dogs to run in the front 
    yard, where they are likely to bark and run at the 
    fence when someone walks by, or, if there is no 
    fence, charge at the walker. If your dog bites 
    someone, you can be fined or even sued and your 
    dog can be taken away by Animal Control. Don’t 
    take a chance—be sure your dog is tied when it is in 
    the front yard, fenced or unfenced. 
    Sunnyvale has a leash law, so if you walk your dog 
    in the neighborhood, including in the parks, it must 
    be on a leash, and a person must be in control of the 
    dog. The fine is $100 for having a dog on public 
    property without a leash. 
    All dogs over four months of age that live in 
    Sunnyvale for more than 30 days must be currently 
    vaccinated against rabies and must be licensed with 
    the city. You can get a license application in the 
    Department of Public Safety at 700 All America 
    Way, call Sunnyvale Animal Control at (408) 7307178 to request that one be mailed to you, or 
    download one at http://sunnyvale.ca.gov/ 
    Departments/Public+Safety/Animal+Control/ 
    Licensing+Your+Pet.htm.  
    Finally, take a bag when you walk your dog and 
    clean up after it. Be a responsible dog owner, for 
    your own protection, for the dog, and for the 
    neighborhood. ■
