    Village Vistas: "Heavenly" Red at the Playground
    If you have taken a stroll past the Lakewood Park playground this January, your eyes have likely been
    drawn to a spectacular splash of color against the winter grey. While our deciduous trees are bare
    and the grass is recovering from the cold, one shrub is putting on a firework display of bright red
    berries and bronze-tinted foliage.
    We’ve heard the debates on the sidewalk: Is it bamboo? Is it a young tree? Is it our native holly? The
    answer is Nandina domestica, widely known as Heavenly Bamboo. While it is a common sight in
    gardens and park strips, this plant has a fascinating story (and a few secrets) that you should know.
    An Imposter in the Bamboo Family
    Despite its common name and its upright, cane-like stems, Nandina is not a bamboo at all. It is
    actually an evergreen member of the Barberry family. It earned the nickname "Heavenly Bamboo"
    because of its resemblance to tall grass and its reputation in East Asian culture as a plant that dispels
    bad dreams and brings good luck. Here in Lakewood, it is prized for its toughness, because it is
    incredibly drought-tolerant and pest-resistant.
    The Winter Transformation
    Nandina is a master of seasonal disguise, which is why it grabs so much attention in January.
    Spring & Summer: The leaves are a glossy, soft green, often with small white flowers.
    The January Shift: As the temperature drops, the plant pumps anthocyanins (red pigments) into its
    leaves to protect them from the cold. This turns the delicate, lacy foliage into stunning shades of
    purple, bronze, and fiery crimson.
    The Berries: The bright red clusters you see now actually ripened in late fall, but they are incredibly
    persistent. They can hang onto the branches for months, providing long-lasting color when almost
    nothing else is blooming.
    Safety for Pets
    A quick reminder for our dog-walking neighbors: All parts of the Nandina plant are toxic to domestic
    pets if chewed on. While most dogs ignore it, keep curious puppies away from those low-hanging red
    berries during your park loops.

    Donate to LVNA
    Lakewood Village Neighborhood Association (LVNA) is now a 501(c)(3) non-
    profit organization. This milestone means your donations are tax-deductible,
    and they’re more important than ever!
    Community favorites like the Summer Picnic, block parties, Dumpster Day,
    the Holiday Parade and Lights Contest wouldn’t be possible without the
    support of generous neighbors like you. These cherished traditions depend on
    community donations to continue. Help us keep them going and create new
    opportunities for Lakewood Village!  
    How to donate
    Donate online: Send your donation to treasurer@lvnasv.org via Zelle, or
    simply scan the QR code using the Zelle app.
    Checks: Make checks payable to "Lakewood Village Neighborhood
    Association", contact treasurer@lvnasv.org for more information.
    Every dollar counts toward building a stronger, more vibrant community.

    The LVNA board is working to compile a list of the most neglected areas in Lakewood Village, and we need
    your help! Our goal is to identify the Top 10 Worst Spots in the neighborhood and share them with the City of
    Sunnyvale to push for much-needed repairs and improvements.
    What Issues Should You Report?
    Look around Lakewood Village and let us know about areas that need attention, including but not limited to:
    Sidewalks that are cracked or unsafe
    Potholes that need filling
    Overgrown trees in need of trimming
    Abandoned vehicles that are taking up space
    Neglected properties that impact our community’s appearance
    How to Submit a Report
    The more reports we get, the stronger our case! You can submit issues in two easy ways:
    1. Email us with specific details (exact addresses, descriptions, and photos if possible) at board@lvnasv.org.
    2. Report directly to the City via Access Sunnyvale (https://sunnyvale.dynamics365portals.us). This officially
    enters your complaint into the system. If you do this, let us know so we can help follow up!
    3. Attend a City Hall meeting on a Tuesday to voice your concerns in person—this can make an even bigger
    impact and catch their attention. If you choose this option, also file a claim on Access Sunnyvale to
    strengthen your case. More information about the meeting: https://www.sunnyvale.ca.gov/your-
    government/governance/city-council/council-meeting-information
    Together, we can make Lakewood Village a safer, cleaner, and better-maintained community. Thank you for
    taking the time to report issues and advocate for our neighborhood!
