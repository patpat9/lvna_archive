    Lakewood School Principal to Speak at General 
    Meeting 
    At the February 8, 2007 LVNA general meeting, Mrs. 
    Annette Grasty, principal of Lakewood School, will 
    speak to us about the recent break-in problems at the 
    school and the plan for a school-surrounding fence to 
    try to stop the crime. What will the new fence mean for 
    the neighborhood? What about after-school and 
    summer use of the school grounds? Will such a fence 
    send a negative message about our neighborhood? 
    What other options might there be? Our new 
    Neighborhood Resource Officer, Jim Davis will also be

    neighborhood public safety issues. 
    Downtown Redevelopment Outreach Meetings 
    On January 20, 2007, I attended an informational 
    session about the plans for the downtown Sunnyvale redevelopment as proposed by the likely new developer. 
    Sunnyvale City Council planned to make a final 
    decision about this developer in late January. This is a 
    HUGE project, involving a new grocery store, office 
     
    space, residences, retail space, a movie theater, and 
    perhaps even a new hotel. It will change the entire face 
    of the downtown, most notably by restoring the original 
    street grid. The projected completion time for most of 
    the retail space is the first quarter of 2009. There will be 
    other opportunities for people to offer input and learn 
    about the redevelopment as it goes ahead. I have been 
    interested in this project because I would like to see the 
    developer incorporate as many “green” building 
    practices as possible, particularly making use of 
    alternative energy possibilities such as solar. Other 
    opportunities for input concern issues such as having 
    community meeting space, and safe places for teens to 
    congregate. I encourage you to check the city of 
    Sunnyvale Web site for updates: http://sunnyvale.ca.gov/ 
    Departments/Office+of+the+City+Manager/Communica
    tions/DowntownInformation.htm.

        February in Your Yard and Garden 
    ☼•Purchase bare-root plants. Cover the roots until 
    you have the time and the weather is right for 
    planting. 
    ☼Order bare-root fruit trees from specialty nurseries. 
    They will usually stop shipping bare-root fruit trees 
    by the end of March. 
    ☼Send in orders to seed companies before they run 
    out of stock later in spring. 
    •☼Clean and repair garden tools. Sharpen the blades 
    of pruning tools and shovels. Service the lawn mower. 
    ☼Complete pruning of deciduous trees, shrubs, vines, 
    fruit trees, and roses. 
    ☼If desired and if deciduous plants have not leafed 
    out yet, apply a last application of dormant spray to 
    kill overwintering insects. It is particularly important 
    to use a lime sulfur spray for peach and nectarine trees 
    to prevent a fungus commonly known as peach leaf 
    curl that causes distorted leaves and scarred fruit. 
    ☼Select and plant azaleas, camellias, and 
    rhododendrons while they are in bloom. 
    ☼Fertilize spring-blooming bulbs as the foliage 
    emerges. Some growers recommend dried blood meal; 
    follow the directions on the package, sprinkling the 
    blood meal around the emerging foliage. 
     
    These tips are from the book Northern California 
    Gardening: A Month-by-Month Guide, by Katherine 
    Grace Endicott, published by Chronicle Books, San 
    Francisco, copyright 1996, and are used with the 
    publisher’s permission. ■ 
     
     
     
     
    Neighborhood Treasure Passes On 
    -Skip Gottschalk 
    Raymond “Pop” Torres bought one of the first houses 
    built on Pecos Way and lived there for 50 years before 
    moving to Reno, a couple of years ago, where he died 
    on Saturday, January 20, 2007.  
    When my wife Annie and I first moved into 
    Lakewood Village in 1983, we soon met Pop walking 
    around the neighborhood, which he did every day. He 
    was 73 then. He would stop and talk, accepting only a 
    glass of water. A great storyteller, he loved telling us 
    about growing up in Hawaii, moving to California, 
    getting married to the woman he adored, and raising a 
    family that he loved very much. The visits got longer, 
    and the glass of water eventually turned into a cold 
    beer or a glass of wine. As the years passed, the beer 
    or wine was deferred for soda, then water again. This 
    is a cycle-of-life gift that we’ll treasure forever. 
    Pop was our dear friend and neighbor and we all are 
    blessed for having known him. 
    Here’s a cold one for you, Pop! ■

    What Is the LVNA and Why Should I Care? 
    The Lakewood Village Neighborhood Association 
    (LVNA) is a volunteer body that represents the 
    interests of the residents and organizes community 
    activities of Lakewood Village. The LVNA operates 
    according to a set of written by-laws. It holds three 
    kinds of meetings: Board Meetings, General 
    Meetings, and Special Meetings. There are many 
    opportunities to participate at all levels 
    The LVNA has five elected officers , several 
    appointed positions, and other volunteers. Elections 
    are held annually, usually in March. This year (2006), 
    we especially need a new president and a secretary, a 
    position that went unfilled in 2005. 
    If you want the LVNA to continue its work on behalf 
    of Lakewood Village, and if you want to continue to 
    have a Holiday Parade, Holiday Lighting Contest, and 
    Annual Picnic, get ready to volunteer. These efforts 
    and events cannot happen without you. 
    Call any of the LVNA Board members listed on the 
    back page to find out more about the position YOU 
    could fill, and go to the General Meeting on February 
    8 to meet LVNA volunteers. ■

    Sunnyvale Library Celebrates “Silicon Valley 
    Reads” Month with Special February Event 
    The 2007 selection for the Silicon Valley Reads 
    community program is T.C. Boyle’s The Tortilla 
    Curtain, a novel that depicts the lives of two southern 
    California couples who live close to each other, but 
    under very different circumstances. One couple is 
    affluent liberals; the other illegal immigrants. 
    Eventually, their different worlds intersect in what the 
    publisher calls "a tragicomedy of error and 
    misunderstanding."  
    Wednesday, February 21, Sunnyvale Public Library 
    will present a video interview with Boyle and San 
    Jose Mercury News columnist Sal Pizarro at 7 p.m. A 
    discussion of the novel and the interview will follow. 
    Light refreshments will be served. 
    Boyle will be speaking at Mexican Heritage Plaza, 
    1700 Alum Rock Avenue, San Jose, on Wednesday, 
    February 28, at 7:30 p.m. For other free Silicon 
    Valley Reads events throughout Santa Clara County, 
    visit www.siliconvalleyreads.org. Now in its fifth 
    year, the program encourages everyone in the 
    community to read the same book and discuss it.■

    program for individuals recovering from addictive 
    eating and anorexia; it is based on the 12 steps of

    Sunnyvale Opens Child Care Awards 
    Nominations 
    The City of Sunnyvale has announced the opening of 
    the nomination period for its fourth annual Awards for 
    Excellence in Early Education. The program 
    recognizes high-quality early-educators in the 
    Sunnyvale community, and highlights the crucial role 
    early child care providers play in a child’s social, 
    physical, emotional, and intellectual well-being. 
    Nominations are being sought from parents and others 
    who have first-hand experience with the nominee’s 
    commitment to providing high-quality early-learning 
    experiences for young children. Awards are given in 
    recognition of six categories of service, including: 
    Infant Child Care Center; Preschool/Child Care 
    Center (preschool-age); School-age Child Care 
    Center; Family Child Care Provider; Child Care 
    Center or Preschool Director; and Child Care or 
    Preschool Teacher. 
    Each nominee will be evaluated on the basis of 
    criteria from the National Association for the 
    Education of Young Children (NAEYC). Sunnyvale’s 
    Child Care Advisory Board will make the final 
    selections.  
    Nominating applications must be received by the City 
    of Sunnyvale by March 15, 2007. The winners will be 
    announced at the Sunnyvale Health & Safety Fair, 
    Saturday, May 12, at the Columbia Neighborhood 
    Center. 
    For more information about the award program and to 
    download an application, visit the Web site 
    childcare.inSunnyvale.com, then click on Award 
    Nomination Forms. You may also call (408) 7307532, TDD (408) 730-7501, or e-mail 
    childcareresources@ci.sunnyvale.ca.us for more 
    information. ■
