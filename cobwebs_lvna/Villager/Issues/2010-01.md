    December 5e was a magic day in Lakewood Village.
    The LVNA Board and volunteers pulled off a minor
    miracle when once again the annual Lakewood
    Village Holiday Parade marched down our streets.
    The decision to have the parade was made only three
    weeks prior to the event!
    Special thanks go to the people who helped us pay
    the costs of the event. The City of Sunnyvale
    requires a permit to have the parade. $100 is
    required to submit the permit for evaluation, $100 to
    rent mandatory traffic cones, $560 dollars to pay the
    overtime costs of two public safety officers needed
    to close Lawrence Expressway, $110 for event
    insurance, $150 for a mandatory o'porta potty" and
    another $25 for a permit to have amplified music and
    sound, totaling $1,000 before refreshments.

    Council Members Dean Chu, Howe and Hamilton,
    Ron Swegeles, and Council Member Elect Jim Grif-
    fith each contoibuted out of his own pocket to help
    finance the parade.

    were caught by surprise by the "porta pott;z" require-

    Services Company of San Jose agreed to donate the

    course of the meeting left nothing but questions. Chief
    among them was how in the world people affending games
    would actually be able to get to the facility. I was utterly
    astonished to find out that the 14 acre stadium is going to be
    built on aI7 acre site. The site is so small that of the
    20,000 cars expected to carry the 68,000 ticket holders
    there, only 2,500 of the cars would be able to park in the
    stadium's parking lot. Almost 90% of the people coming to
    the game will have to park someplace else! The "someplace
    else" is a huge question mark hanging over both the project
    and over Lakewood Village.
    The 49ers have a plan to sign contracts with businesses
    in the area to let fans use their parking lots on game days.
    They figure that businesses would be willing to do so in
    exchange for ttre ability to collect hefty parking fees. The
    businesses would run shuttle buses from their lots to the
    stadium and split the proceeds with the 49ers. This kind of
    arrangement is in place in other NFL cities, though nowhere
    has it been attempted on this scale.
    In addition to these parking lots, the 49ers expect many
    fans to rely on "public transportation" to get to the games.
    This sounds nice, but it doesn't take a rocket scientist to fig-
    ure out what this may mean for Lakewood Village. We have
    the #55 VTA bus line running through our neighborhood,
    and the northern terminus of its route is the Light Rail sta-
    tion near the stadium site. I'm sure some fans will figure out
    they can avoid paying hefty parking fees and shuttle bus
    fares by parking for free in our neighborhood and paying a
    VTA based fare to get to the game. The problem could be
    huge. Even ifjust one extra car parked in front ofevery one
    of our i600 homes, it would only accommodate about 8olo
    of the total parking demand, and that number of cars would
    literally choke our streets.
    There are some people who say the entire stadium pro-
    ject is just a bluff. They say it is just away for the 49ers to
    put pressure on San Francisco to put together a real stadium
    proposal that is favorable to the team. The parking plan for
    the Santa Clara stadium seems so hopelessly unrealistic that
    it tends to support the idea of a "blufP'. Some say that the
    49ers don't care about parking because they know the sta-
    dium will never be built. Some say we in Lakewood Village
    need not worry for the same reason.
    There are lots of questions. Come to the LVNA meeting on
    January 13 and get some answers! I'll see you there!
