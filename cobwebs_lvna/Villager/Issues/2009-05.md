    Children will have an opportunity to work with doz-
    ens oflocal professional artists at Sunnyvale's 24th
    annual Hands on the Arts festival, Saturday, May 16.
    The festival will open at 10 a.m. and run until4 p.m.
    at the Sunnyvale Community Center, 550 E. Rem-
    ington Drive.
    Participating children in kindergarten through 8th
    grade can purchase a wristband for $5 at one ofthe
    information booths the day of the festival. Simpler
    projects for children ages 2 - 4 are also available.
    Admission for parents and siblings not participating
    in the festival is free, and includes all-day live enter-
    tainment. Adults are not permitted to participate in
    art activities, except to assist their children. Local
    vendors will sell refreshments. Parking is free, and
    the Community Center is served by VTA Line 55.
    Hands on the Arts is a favorite event with both kids
    and artists. The event is expected to draw more than
    1,000 children and their families. Hundreds of volun-
    teers, including teens who participated when they
    were younger, come back each year to make the
    event a success.
    With so many workshops from which to choose,
    children cankeep busy all day and leavi: with
    armloads of projects. Most workshops allow a child
    to create an art project to take home in about 15
    minutes. Performing-arts workshops teach a song or
    dance to be presented in impromptu performances
    throughout the day.
    Participating ertists were chosen through a competi-
    tive jury process. The field includes some favorites
    who have led popular workshops for 15 or more
    years, as well as some new artists. Organizers select
    a diverse goup, with artists representing a variety of
    genres, methods, and cultures.
    For more information, visit
