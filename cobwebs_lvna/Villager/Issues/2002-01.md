    Vision for the New Year, 2002 - Craig A. Crawford 
    LVNA President 
     
    As President of the Lakewood Village Neighborhood 
    Association, I have a vision and direction that I want to share 
    with my neighbors.  I want to improve the quality of life that 
    we enjoy in this wonderful location.  To do this areas of 
    concern have to be identified.  I have included several items 
    that I believe need attention and would like for my 
    administration to focus on.  This is an open -ended list that 
    will be dynamic as new needs arise.  Feel free to bring 
    concerns to my attention, everyone has different areas of 
    concern, and I want to know yours.  
     
    Improving attendance and recruiting new leaders  
     
    I believe that improving the attendance of regular LVNA 
    meetings is very important.  It is there that we meet and 
    cultivate the new leaders of the organization that will guide 
    us in the future.  New neighbors will move in and old ones 
    will leave us but we want the LVNA to continue so that its 
    service will be available.  The organization will make every 
    effort to recruit future leaders so that we may remain a 
    strong voice in the Northern end of Sunnyvale.  
     
    (continued on page 2)   
     
    Some Ideas that I have are:  
     
    •  Hanging a LVNA banner from the corner of 
    Lakewood and Sandia. Put it up before the meeting 
    and taken down after meeting to let people know 
    about the LVNA meeting. 
    •  Phone tree to call and remind folks about the 
    meeting and events.  
    •  Welcoming new residents to  Lakewood.  
     
     
    Traffic Calming 
     
    Since Santa Clara put a huge multi-screen movie complex on 
    our border, traffic has been heavy.  I believe that public 
    safety and other traffic related city departments have been 
    sensitive to this issue.  The city has recently  conducted a 
    traffic survey in the village.  This survey should reveal the 
    problems that have plagued us for quite awhile.  With those 
    results, we can work on options that can be used to make 
    traffic in the neighborhood safer and, hopefully, slower.  
     
    (continued in next column)  
    (continued from previous column)  
     
    Some of the traffic issues that I see are:  
     
    •  How to slow people down on the Fairwood side 
    going to the movies.  
    •  Accidents at Lawrence and Sandia.  
    •  Wildwood & Lawrence Expy, how to make it safer. 
     
    Obvious illegal additions  
     
    With the tight housing market comes some problems that we 
    are all aware of: illegal additions.  I have seen some really 
    sad excuses of trying to fit more people into Lakewood 
    houses.  These obvious illegal additions take away from the 
    neighborhood; they don’t add anything except an eye sore.  I 
    don’t mind illegal additions that are done well, good 
    carpentry, painted and blend in to the neighborhood.  The 
    ones that I don’t like are the ones with a rough door cut into 
    the garage door lead ing to the garage conversion apartment.  
    Non-painted illegal conversions are also bad.  I will be 
    working with city staff to identify these and try to get them 
    cleaned up.  If they are rentals houses with out of area 
    owners, then I will double my efforts to have them cleaned 
    up.  We have been taken advantage of long enough by out -
    of-area owners that only want the money but give nothing 
    back to Lakewood except a run down house.  
     
    Some of the other problems associated with illegal 
    conversions are:  
     
    •  Create parking problems. 
    •  Create traffic problems. 
    •  Quality of life goes down.

    Unkempt landscape not conforming to city codes  
     
    Landscape that is not kept to a minimum standard, according 
    to city codes, has gotten really bad in the past.  We have new 
    and improved city staff and I am sure that they will help 
    Lakewood with this problem.  I am not asking for 
    harassment, but I am talking about meeting city code 
    standards.  There is no excuse fo r six people to live in a 
    house and no ones takes care of the cutting the grass or 
    weeds in the front.  I want to find out if most of the violators 
    are rental house with out -of-area owners.  I have a feeling 
    that this is the case, but I need some data to c onfirm this.  If 
    it is the case, then I will try to have something done to the 
    absent owners for the actions of their tenants.  
     
    If we have any residents who are not able to care for there 
    property for legitimate reasons, I want to have a volunteer 
    group th at can help this neighbor out. This is your time to 
    help out, or perhaps your teen that needs volunteer hours for 
    school.  
     
    Illegally-parked commercial vehicles and RV’s  
     
    Past LVNA administrators have focused on commercial 
    vehicles in Lakewood.  I will continue with this item. Really 
    big vehicles that are parked in violation of city code rules are 
    not good for the Village and it takes away valuable parking 
    space that is in short supply.  RV’s that are being lived in, 
    which have extension cords running to th em or generators 
    running , are not allowed and should be moved to a storage 
    facility not in the middle of a residential area.  I have no 
    problem with on-call vehicles from your company being 
    here, you have to make a living.  If you run a business from 
    your house and have a really big vehicle it does not belong 
    here 
     
    (continued in next column)  
    (continued from previous column)  
     
    Keeping up the cleanliness of the commercial plaza 
    formerly called Dick’s Plaza 
     
    For several years Dick’s plaza was run down and dir ty 
    looking.  When the owner was trying to attract new business 
    he cleaned it up.  At this time it is looking O.K to good.  I 
    want to keep it that way or better.  This plaza has a new 
    anchor store that has a long -term lease.  The incentive to 
    keep putting money into keeping it clean and in good upkeep 
    is gone so it has the possibility of back sliding to what is 
    once was We need to stay diligent in watching this plaza.  
    The city will help us if they are notified when you see 
    anything that needs work.  
     
     
    These are some of the items that I have noticed that 
    Lakewood has need to improve on. If you have some please 
    let me know so we can help correct them. If on the other 
    hand you don’t agree with me then I want to hear your voice 
    also. 
     
    - Craig

    Accident Victim Seeks Witnesses 
    - Editor 
     
    Residents near the intersection of Lawrence Expressway and 
    Sandia/Lakehaven may recall that there was a serious traffic 
    accident on the evening of Saturday, October 13 th, 2001. 
    Evidently, a van made a left turn against a red light and 
    collided head-on with an SUV. The driver of the SUV was 
    trapped in the wreckage for some 20 minutes and  has 
    suffered a variety of broken bones and other injuries from 
    which he is still recovering. He is seeking witnesses of the 
    accident and has had a banner placed on the corner of the 
    intersection. If you witnessed this accident, he would 
    appreciate a call at (408) 744-9644. 
     
     
     
     
    Lakewood Village Home Wins 
    Architectural Design Award - Brian Smithson 
     
    The American Institute of Architects, Santa Clara Valley 
    Chapter, held its “Visions of the Valley” design contest in 
    late 2001, and one of the awards was given to our remodeled 
    Lakewood home! I’m fairly sure that this is a first for 
    Lakewood Village, it’s definitely a first for my wife and me, 
    and I hope it’s an honor for our neighborhood, too.  
     
    Of the dozens of design entries, most of the winners were 
    large, expensive, commercial and civic structures. Our’s was 
    one of only two private residences that won an award. 
    Another winning design in Sunnyvale, which you may be 
    familiar with, is Yahoo!’s new headquarters campus on 
    Mathilda near Lockheed Martin.  
     
    In case you  haven’t guessed which home in the 
    neighborhood is our’s, you can see it at Burntwood Court 
    and Sandia Avenue. You can see it and the other winners on 
    the AIA’s web site at:  
     
    http://www.aiascv.org/awards/  
     
    The winners were also featured in the November, 20 01, issue 
    of  San Jose Magazine.  
     
    And, you can take a look at the drawings and photos that our 
    architect submitted for the contest on my as yet unfinished 
    web site, at: 
     
    http://www.smithsonhouse.com/aia_photos/AIA_photos.htm

    Fremont Pool News 
    - Holly Lofgren 
    Chairperson, Friends of the Fremont Pool  
     
    Greetings L.V.N.A. Villager Readers, 
     
    The Friends of the Fremont Pool Committee is still hard at 
    work on our new community swimming pool.  We wanted to 
    bring you up to date on current news.  
     
    The 50-meter swimming pool is well under construction at 
    Fremont High School and the new public shower building 
    has just broken ground.   The pool will be open to the public 
    once the shower building is completed in June.  However,  
    students may have an early opportuni ty to use the pool this 
    spring, as it will soon be water ready.  How exciting! 
     
    The contract between the City of Sunnyvale and the Fremont 
    Union High School  District allows for usage from 5AM 
    until 10PM, year round.  The City is currently determining 
    who should operate the pool for the community groups.   
    Sometimes the city finds that it is more cost effective for an 
    outside group to run certain operations than it is for the Parks 
    & Recreation Department.  However, three key issues have 
    been raised by our group.  They are the assurance of: A) 
    Reasonable fees, B) The community's priorities are utilized  
    in pool scheduling, and C) Proper oversight is provided of a 
    pool operator.  
     
    Items A and B will be addressed in a study session, likely to 
    be scheduled in Ja nuary.  We hope you will attend to ensure 
    that EVERYONE can utilize the pool. 
     
    Blessings to you this holiday season.  
     
    Holly Lofgren 
    Chairperson, Friends of the Fremont Pool  
     
     
     
    Parking Reminders -Editor 
     
    Remember to follow City Parking regulations.  Failur e to 
    follow any of the following Parking regulations could result 
    in you receiving a Citation and/or having your vehicle 
    abated.  
     
    (continued in next column)  
    (continued from previous column)  
    θ  SMC 19.48.260
     - Vehicles must be parked on a 
    permanent paved sur face.  The entire vehicle 
    must be parked on a permanent paved surface, 
    not just the tires.  
    θ  SMC 9.24.180 - Inoperable vehicles parked on 
    private property can only be out in public view 
    for 72 consecutive hours; after that they must 
    be stored in a garage or a carport out of public 
    view. 
    θ  SMC 10.16.130 - Vehicles parked on the street 
    must be licensed and operable, and cannot be 
    left in one location for more than 72 hours (3 
    days). 
    θ  22500(e) + 22500 (f) V.C
    . -  Vehicles should 
    be parked so they do not block or pa rtially 
    block the public sidewalk or a neighbor’s 
    driveway. 
    θ  SMC 9.26.030(s) + SMC 19.48.260 + 
    4000(a)V.C. - Boats must be licensed and 
    stored on a trailer which is operable and 
    registered.  Boats must also be covered and 
    parked on a permanent paved surface .
