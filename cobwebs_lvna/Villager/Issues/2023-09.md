    Calabazas Creek Clean Up - Sept 23 (tentative date)
    Earlier this year as part of the Santa Clara Valley Water District's Adopt-a-Creek program,
    our neighborhood officially adopted the section of Calabazas Creek that stretches from
    Wildwood Ave to Tasman Ave. By adopting the creek, the community has made a long-
    term commitment to protect and nurture it for years to come. With the support of
    volunteers in our neighborhood, we are responsible for the cleanliness and well-being of
    the creek, benefiting both the environment and the residents who enjoy its beauty.

    Injuries
    While injuries happen on natural
    grass, the frequency is lower with
    natural grass than synthetic turf.
    Studies have consistently found that athletes are more likely to get injured playing on a synthetic turf. One study
    found risk of injury increased by 58% on artificial turf, with especially high injury rates in football, soccer, and
    rugby. Football players may also be at risk of increased ACL injuries on synthetic turf.
    Water
    Requires regular watering
    Substantial amounts of water is used to make the turf. Additionally, on hot days, the turf has to be watered regular
    to keep it cool.
    Weatherproofing
    Not weatherproof; fields often
    close after rain
    While sometimes called “weatherproof”, artificial turf has poor water runoff after rain (and often bits of
    the turf wash away with the water).
    Maintenance
    Regular mowing, water, and other
    lawn care
    Regulars regular maintenance and cleaning. Tasks include surface brushing, surface raking and sweeping, turf infill
    redistribution and refill, and impact tests. Failure to perform regular maintenance can void warranties. Artificial
    sports fields should be cleaned weekly and require monthly deep cleaning.
    Heat
    Naturally dissipates heat, helping
    to cool the environment
    Turf temperature can reach 120-180 degrees F, resulting in melted shoes, blistered hands and feet, dehydration,
    and/or heatstroke. Artificial turf also contributes to the Heat Island Effect, which increases the temperature of the
    area surrounding the turf.
    Restrictions on
    Use
    None
    Many. For example, while the artificial fields at Fair Oaks are called “multi-use” because they are striped
    for soccer and baseball, their use is actually highly restricted. The field is fenced off and restrictions
    include no dogs, no food, no chairs, and no volleyball. See photo for full list of restrictions.
    Disposal costs
    N/A
    High. It must be replaced every 8-10 years. Additionally, many landfills do not accept turf, making disposal of
    synthetic turf difficult. There are also environmental costs related to disposal, since many of the chemicals in
    artificial turf never breakdown.
    Lead exposure
    None
    Yes. The CDC has identified artificial turf as one of seven primary sources of lead exposure in youth.
    Environmental
    benefits
    Lots. Connects us to nature,
    absorbs carbon dioxide, and
    provides a place for wildlife
    None. Under high temperatures and sunlight, synthetic turf releases dust, which contains carcinogens

    As part of the upcoming Lakewood Park renovation, the City of Sunnyvale is planning to install artificial (synthetic) turf in
    the athletic fields, similar to the turf that is in the new Fair Oaks Park. During the most recent community meeting on the
    Lakewood Park renovation (July 26), residents raised questions regarding natural grass vs synthetic turf. This article will
    provide a high-level comparison of the two options, links to additional information, and steps to share your opinion with
    the City and the Parks and Recreation Commission.
    Natural grass is, obviously, a field made of natural grass. It requires watering and fertilization, but it’s good for our health,
    cleans our air, connects us to nature, and is durable. Synthetic turf is made of plastic, petroleum and other chemicals.
    Many synthetic turfs contain PFAS chemicals, “forever chemicals” that are toxic and never break down. Synthetic turf is not
    regulated by any US government entity. In 2015, the Consumer Product Safety Commission (CPSC) withdrew its finding that
    synthetic turf is safe to play on; the new CPSC findings only indicate that their research was insufficient to determine if
    synthetic turf is or is not safe to play on. After playing on a synthetic field, the CDC recommends not eating while on the
    field, washing skin with soap and water, and removing clothes that were worn on the field.

    It has been a busy month, as kids return to school and the district begins the process to move to
    trustee area elections. Here are some quick updates about transportation to Fremont High School
    (FHS) for North Sunnyvale high school students, and the upcoming Fremont Union High School
    District (FUHSD) redistricting effort.
    By Peggy Brewster & Lauren Ledbetter, SunnyvaleEquityInEducation@gmail.com, https://bit.ly/joinSEE
    Transportation Update
    There were two substantial changes in FHS transportation for students. First, ALL students north
    of El Camino Real are eligible for FREE bus passes, regardless of income! Go to
    http://bitly.ws/RFWA to start the application.
    Second, VTA has added a new Limited Stop 255 bus route from Lakewood to Fremont High. See
    the map showing the new 255 service route. Check schedules at https://www.vta.org/go/routes or
    call VTA Customer Service at 408-321-2300.
    District Elections
    Currently, Trustees for the FUHSD are elected district wide,
    and all current trustees live south of El Camino Real. For
    the fall 2024 election, FUHSD hopes to move to trustee area
    elections. To prepare, they are holding a series of required
    public meetings to discuss how to split the district into
    trustee areas. The decisions made over the next several
    months will impact families for years to come. SEE is
    following the process closely and actively participating.
    On August 22, the Fremont Union High School District held
    the first pre-map public hearing to discuss the process of
    moving from district wide to trustee-area elections. 
    On September 19th at 6:15pm (in person and online), FUHSD
    will hold the second pre-map public hearing and
    presentation. Consultants will present about considerations
    in drawing school board maps, and there will be
    opportunities for public comment. Go to
    https://www.fuhsd.org/newsroom/trustee-areas for more
    information. SEE members will be in attendance - please
    reach out to us if you are able to join and provide public
    comment.

    California Park Spotlight: Pismo State Beach
    With fall season around the corner, this state beach becomes a popular destination for those who
    want to see pretty monarch butterflies. The spot falls on the route to their migration destination,
    seeking shelter from the northern winter. From late October to February, these majestic butterflies
    cluster on the limbs of towering eucalyptus trees which are present in large numbers in this area.
    This is a 3 hour road trip from Sunnyvale, more information can be found on this site.
    For more information on Monarch Butterfly facts, migration patterns, threats they face as well as
    how planting native milkweeds help the populations grow, visit this link.
