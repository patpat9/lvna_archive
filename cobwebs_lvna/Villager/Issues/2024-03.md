    LVNA General Meeting and Board Elections - April 10
    Our next quarterly neighborhood association meeting is Wednesday, April 10, 2024 from 7:00-8:00pm
    at the Lakewood Park Building. This meeting will be to elect the LVNA board for the 2024-2025 year.
    There are five board positions: President, 1st Vice President, 2nd Vice President, Treasurer, and
    Secretary. The current board’s positions end in April and a new board will begin. The board meets
    once per month to discuss ongoing activities, in addition to holding quarterly neighborhood general
    meetings and a few seasonal events each year. The level of commitment is up to each individual -
    these are all volunteer roles. It is a one-year term (April 2024 - March 2025). The only requirements are
    that you are 18+ years old, live in the neighborhood, and are able to serve the full 12 month term. For
    more information, attend the meeting or please email board@lvnasv.org.

    The district has data starting in 1956 showing where trustees lived (by zip code). Since 1956, there have been 29 trustees,
    including the five current trustees. Of those 29 trustees, none were from zip codes 94085 and 94089. Two trustees were from
    94086; the first was Howard H Wyant and the second was Betty J Nelson, who served one term from 1990-1994. The remaining
    trustees have all lived south of El Camino Real. This trend continues today - all five of the trustees live in the southern part
    of the district.
    The concentration of trustees from certain zip codes means that parts of the district have never had a trustee who lived in
    their area. While SEE's focus has been on the lack of representation in North Sunnyvale, where the highest concentration of
    vulnerable, underserved students resides, the lack of representation extends beyond North Sunnyvale. Other
    unrepresented/underrepresented areas include Saratoga zip code 95070, Los Altos zip code 94024, and San Jose zip code
    95129 (home of Lynbrook HS). Additional details on zip codes can be found on Slide 7 of the Superintendent Update from the
    January 30 FUHSD board meeting here.
    All board members represent the entire district, regardless of how they are elected or where they live. However, each
    community within the district is unique. By moving to trustee-area elections, the district is ensuring that ALL communities
    have representation on the board.
    Moving to trustee-area elections also reduces the cost of running for school board, since candidates can focus their
    campaign on one trustee area instead of the entire district. So more people can run, increasing the representation and
    inclusion on the board.
    By moving to trustee-area elections now (instead of waiting until the district receives a "demand" (or legal) letter or lawsuit),
    the district has more time for community input and the district avoids fines for being non-compliant with the CVRA
    (California Voting Rights Act).
    As the move to trustee-area elections continues in preparation for the November 2024 elections, there has been concern
    among some communities within the district that moving to trustee-area elections will reduce their representation and lead
    to closing of high schools in the southern part of the district. Interestingly, at-large trustee elections were in place when the
    board decided to close a high school (Sunnyvale High School) in 1981. When Cupertino Union School District closed
    elementary schools recently, those board members were also elected at-large. While school closure is separate from how
    trustees are elected, the evidence does NOT suggest that staying with at-large elections correlates with keeping schools
    open.
    The next CTAD meetings will be held at the FUHSD district office boardroom tentatively on March 11 and April 11. 
    Finally, the remaining map hearings will occur at these FUHSD board meetings:
    Wednesday, March 20 – Map Hearing 3 (Present maps and sequencing recommendations)
    Wednesday, April 24 – Map Hearing 4 (Board approves final map and sequencing)
    As we approach the end of this year-long process, it's a good time to remember that when we foster greater inclusion, we all
    benefit. Everyone in the district wants their students to have a great education, the ability to participate in extracurricular
    activities, and reasonable transportation options to get to school. The district as a whole is greater than the sum of its parts.

    Sunnyvale Urban Forest Advocates (SUFA)
    Sunnyvale Urban Forest Advocates (SUFA) is a local organization that advocates for preserving trees
    and increasing the trees in our community, educates about the many benefits of having trees in our
    neighborhoods, and partners with the City and others to raise awareness of the urban forest.
     
    SUFA has partnered with the library for several upcoming events:
    Did you know?
    In 2014, the City approved the Sunnyvale Urban Forest Management Plan (SUFMP) to plant 29,000 new trees
    across Sunnyvale. There was supposed to be 14,000 new trees in commercial areas and 15,000 new trees in
    residential areas. These numbers include plans to plant 5,000 new street trees, with locations already identified
    by the City. A “street tree” is any tree with a trunk in the City’s right-of-way, which is roughly the space between
    the curb and 11 feet into a property. Street trees also include any tree in public spaces like parks, roadway
    medians, or City campuses. 
    Unfortunately, ten years later, little work has been done. None of the 5,000 street trees have been planted, with
    the City citing budget and staffing concerns to explain why the SUFMP has languished. Want to encourage the
    City to prioritize and fund this project? Contact City Council regarding funding the Sunnyvale Urban Forest
    Management Plan at council@sunnyvale.ca.gov.
    The full text of the SUFMP can be found on the Sunnyvale Trees Website.
    Want to do your own tree walk through Lakewood Park? SUFA has you covered with a self-guided tree
    walk maps here (https://www.sunnyvaletrees.org/tree-walk-maps/) and accompanying guides here
    (https://www.sunnyvaletrees.org/tree-guide/) 
    Learn more about SUFA here: https://www.sunnyvaletrees.org/

    Support Our Neighbors
    On September 6, the California Secretary of Agriculture issued a quarantine for the oriental
    fruit fly (Bactrocera dorsalis). The quarantine affects a 112-square-mile section of Santa Clara
    County, and it was put in place after eight oriental fruit flies were found in Santa Clara and
    Sunnyvale. The quarantine covers all of Sunnyvale and Santa Clara, along with parts of
    Campbell, Cupertino, Los Altos, Milpitas, Mountain View, San José and Saratoga.
    As a result, all residents of Lakewood Village are included in the quarantine. While under
    quarantine, residents:
    Should not take any homegrown fruits and vegetables off of their property. This includes
    anything grown in gardens as well as fruit from fruit trees in our yards. 
    Should not put any homegrown fruits and vegetables in the FoodCycle compost (yellow)
    part of the garbage can.
    May dispose of homegrown fruits and vegetables by:
    Grinding them in the garbage disposal.
    Double-bagging and sealing them, then placing them in the regular (black) part of the
    garbage can.
    Reminder - Lakewood Village Under Oriental Fruit Fly Quarantine
    Note that these fruits and vegetables are safe for processing and consumption (e.g., juiced, frozen,
    cooked), as long as the processing and consumption occurs on the resident’s property.
    For more information, go to the official news release here: bit.ly/3t5UtiF
