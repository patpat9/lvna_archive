    President’s Column 
    -Judy Fernandez  
    Dear Lakewood Village Neighbors,  
     
    My first meeting as your President of 
    the LVNA went very well.  We had 
    about 16 neighbors attend the April 
    meeting.  There were several con-
    cerns that were brought up during the 
    meeting.  
     
    We asked the Nathan Truitt, Program Coordinator with the 
    City of Sunnyvale for help with three of the questions that 
    were brought up. 
     
     Q. Who cleans up the corner lot at Sandia and Lawrence 
    Expressway? 
    Answer: the two corners of Sandia and Lawrence Express-
    way belong to the county. The county cleans up the walls and 
    the overgrowing weeds at these two locations. They can be 
    reached at 408-366-3100. 
    Q. How do we get a missing letter for the “Lakewood Village” 
    sign?’ 
    Answer: The city is not responsible for replacement of the 
    missing letters, we suggest contacting the LVNA. 
    Q. Who cleans up the spray painted 
    streets and sidewalks after the city 
    completes their work? 
    Answer: Upon completion of con-
    struction of a “marked-up” street/
    sidewalk area, the contractor is  
    responsible for removing any      
    remaining mark-ups. This also    
    applies to work performed by city 
    staff; any work done by city staff 
    would require city staff to remove 
    any remaining marks.

    We Did IT!!!!   Parcel Tax Passes! 
    By Benjamin Picard,  
    Superintendent, Sunnyvale School District 
     
    Our Sunnyvale community really stepped up 
    in these difficult times to support Sunnyvale 
    School District.  Measure B passed with nearly 70% 
    approval.  This means a new revenue stream of about $1 
    million per year for the next seven years to support our core 
    programs and services to students.  As I have shared with 
    you previously, this local revenue will „help‟ buffer us from 
    the State cuts to our school district funding.

    volunteers who helped us get the word out to voters.   I want 
    to thank all of you that participated in the campaign and 
    helped to communicate the important message of supporting 
    our future generations by adequately funding our public 
    neighborhood schools. 
     
    Thanks again and my best wishes to all of you.

    New Principal at Lakewood School 
    By Phyllis Fowler 
     
    At the end of this school year, Lakewood 
    School will saying a tearful goodbye to their 
    beloved Principal Annette Grasty. She has 
    accepted an appointment as principal of    
    Vargas School.  I first met Annette at an LVNA 
    meeting almost 15 years ago and I was      
    impressed by her optimism and “Can-Do”    
    attitude.  
     
    She had just accepted the position of principal 
    of the soon to be reopened Fairwood school. She quickly won the 
    hearts of the     students and parents of Fairwood. Under her lead-
    ership, Fairwood was named a California Distinguished School. A 
    few years ago, she took the reins of Lakewood school and worked 
    her magic again! Last year Lakewood was also named as a Califor-
    nia Distinguished Schools.  
     
    Annette, Lakewood Village will miss you, but we wish you all the 
    best success at your new school. (How soon will Vargas be named 
    a “California Distinguished School”!) 
     
    Beginning July 1st , Lakewood School will welcome their new      
    appointed interim principal Pam Cheng. Pam joined our district 
    eleven years ago and has emerged as an outstanding teacher lead-
    er.  For the past four years she has served as an effective Teacher 
    on Special Assignment.   
     
    As soon as I found out about her appointment, I asked for a quote 
    for the Villager, Pam quickly replied, “Wow, talk about getting the 
    word out fast! I'm excited about learning and growing into a new 
    role here in Sunnyvale. I feel privileged to join the Lakewood      
    community in supporting the students I feel called to serve.” 
     
    Join me in welcoming our new neighbor to Lakewood Village.  
    Good luck Pam and welcome to our neighborhood! 

    New Sandbox Project at Lakewood School 
     
    On January 22nd, Lakewood 
    Kindergarteners received a new 
    sandbox!  Thanks to Grant 
    Hayward and his Eagle Scout 
    Project, the old sandbox was 
    replaced with new boards, a top 
    edge for sitting, and new clean 
    sand.  About 50 people spent a 
    part of their day sifting sand, 
    measuring, cutting, shoveling, 
    and nailing.   
    The Kindergarteners also received two new redwood 
    trees to add shade to the playground.  One tree was 
    donated by Mr. Whited, fourth grade teacher at 
    Lakewood, and the second tree was donated by the 
    Mumy family.   
    The additions were greatly appreciated by the 
    Kindergarten staff and new sandbox is already being 
    enjoyed by the kindergarteners.  

    Hands on the Arts 
    By Phyllis Fowler 
     
    Does your child love art? Then 
    bring them to:  
    Sunnyvale Community Center  
    When:  May 21st  
    Time:  10:00am to 4:00pm.  
    Cost : $10.00 per child.  
     
    Hands on the Arts is a fantastic annual event sponsored 
    by the City of Sunnyvale. Hands on the Arts is designed 
    to help children learn about other cultures through visual 
    and creative arts workshops. (and have fun as well!)
    Projects last year included: Japanese Brush Painting, 
    Bubble Aquariums, Wands, Wings and Things, Native 
    American Bird Masks, Colombian Dancing Stories and 
    Kite making.

    children are able to keep busy all 
    day! They leave with armloads of 
    projects. Most of the workshops

    project in only about 15 minutes. I 
    have taken my Girl Scout troop to 
    several festivals over the years.

    did I). I found that it is best to get there early or the 
    children won‟t be able finish all the fabulous projects.

    SNAP –  
    Sunnyvale Neighborhoods Actively 
    Prepare 
     
    (continued from page 1) 
    covers a variety of topics, including Disaster Preparedness, Fire Safety 
    & Suppression, Disaster Medical Operations, Safety/Damage 
    Assessment and Light Search & rescue among others. Our city provides 
    the training free of cost except for the American Red Cross Basic First 
    Aid and Adult CPR course. 
    Following a disaster, emergency services personnel may be 
    overwhelmed responding to multiple emergencies; city and county 
    resources will be stretched thin. During 
    the Loma Prieta earthquake, it was 
    neighbors helping neighbors that made 
    the difference.  Even though we were not 
    as severely affected as San Francisco or 
    Santa Cruz, we were impacted by the 
    power outages, gas leaks, collapsed 
    chimneys, and no telephone service. 
    SNAP participants responded to 
    neighborhood needs by replacing panic 
    with productive action. 
    “It‟s easy to put disasters in the back of 
    our minds letting our daily life‟s priorities 
    take center stage. But are we ready? Do 
    we each have the ability and understanding to handle disasters? The 
    SNAP Program provides the key survival elements needed for most 
    disasters and the skills you learn can help save lives. “Arley C. Marley 
    III, SNAP Captain for over 10 years. 
    All that is required is your time and participation. The benefit is you will 
    acquire the skills that will help you protect your family and property, as 
    well as help your neighborhood recover from a disaster. Classes are 
    offered on a quarterly basis. To find out more, come to the May 11th 
    meeting and meet Dan Walsh. He will be happy to answer your 
    questions! 
