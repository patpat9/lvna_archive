    New Neighborhood Enhancement Program Area 
    Chosen 
    A new Neighborhood Enhancement Program area has 
    been chosen, based on the results of a code violation 
    survey done in April 2005. It will be on the Fairwood 
    side of Lakewood Village and will include parts of 
    Torrance Avenue, Tuscon Avenue, Socorro Avenue, 
    Havenwood Avenue, and part of Sandia Avenue. 
    This is the fourth phase of the Neighborhood 
    Enhancement program, which has already resulted in 
    the improvement of many homes in Lakewood Village. 
    City staffers work with local residents to provide 
    information, assistance, and even, in some cases, 
    landscaping grants. Lakewood Village used to stand 
    out in red (red meaning four or more code violations 
    visible from the street) on a map of code violations 
    across Sunnyvale, but not anymore. Thank you to Rich 
    Gutierrez (Neighborhood Preservation) and the staff of 
    the Enhancement Program for all their hard work, and 
    to the residents of Lakewood Village for their help in 
    improving the neighborhood. If you have questions 
    about the program, please contact Rich Gutierrez at 
    (408) 730-7447 or rgutierrez@ci.sunnyvale.ca.us. 
    Candidates’ Night with S.N.A.I.L.

    Lakewood Village attended. Thanks to Skip Gottschalk

    November in Your Yard and 
    Garden 
    o Finish planting spring bulbs. 
    o Shop for native plants and set them in 
    the garden. 
    o Complete fall cleanup in the garden. 
    o Despite the drop in the temperature, 
    composting continues. Add disease-free leaves and 
    chopped-up, dead annuals to the compost pile. 
    Remember to keep the pile as damp as a wrung-out 
    sponge for faster composting. 
    o Keep after the snails. 
    o Watch out for ants invading the house from the 
    garden. 
    o Wrap vulnerable, young tree trunks, particularly 
    citrus trees, with paper or burlap to protect them 
    from freezing.  
     
     
     
    o Water plants as needed. 
    o Stabilize plants that might be harmed by autumn 
    wind. 
    o Prune wisteria and clematis to control size and 
    encourage new flowering wood in spring. Lightly 
    prune forsythia. 
    o Water roses but withhold all fertilizer. When the 
    ground freezes, mulch the rose bed heavily. 
    o Finish transplanting fall- and winter-flowering 
    plants into the bedding plot. 
    o Harvest what is left of the summer vegetables before 
    the rain and cold destroy them. Continue to plant the 
    winter vegetable garden with cool-season crops such 
    as beets, broccoli, cabbage, carrots, cauliflower, 
    onions, parsnips, and turnips. If you have put the 
    vegetable garden to rest for the winter, cover it with 
    manure so that the manure will age properly over 
    the winter. 
    o Flush your watering system to remove mineral 
    deposits. Soak all newly transplanted plants until the 
    rains come. 
    These tips are from the book Northern California Gardening: A 
    Month-by-Month Guide, by Katherine Grace Endicott, 
    published by Chronicle Books, San Francisco, copyright 1996, 
    and are used with the publisher’s permission. ■

    School District and City Offer 
    Flu Vaccine for Kids 
    School-age children are up to four times more likely 
    to catch the flu than adults. To help protect our kids, 
    Sunnyvale’s Columbia Neighborhood Center (CNC), 
    has partnered with the Sunnyvale School District to 
    offer free influenza vaccinations using FluMist nasal 
    vaccine. 
    The final vaccination date is November 22 from 3 
    p.m. to 6 p.m., at CNC, 785 Morse Avenue. This 
    vaccine is only available to students and staff of 
    Sunnyvale School District. The vaccine supply is 
    limited, and vaccinations are on a first-come, firstserved basis. FluMist is an influenza vaccine that uses 
    a weakened form of the influenza virus to help 
    provide protection from the flu. FluMist is given as a 
    nasal spray, so it does not require an injection.  
    Brochures containing answers to frequently asked 
    questions regarding FluMist will be available at the 
    school office. Please see the Patient Information in the 
    brochure, visit flumist.com, or call (877) 633-4411 for 
    additional information. Ask your health-care 
    professional if FluMist is right for you and your 
    family. ■

    Faces of Homelessness, Sunday, November 13, 
    12:30–1:45 p.m., Unitarian Universalist 
    Fellowship of Sunnyvale, 1112 Bernardo Avenue. 
    An opportunity to hear directly from people who 
    have lived with hunger, and people who have 
    been homeless. The panel will include as guest 
    speakers: Patricia Vazquez, single mother, 
    program manager for Boccardo Family Living 
    Center in San Martin, previously homeless; 
    Vanessa Martinez, single mother of seven, shelter 
    manager at First Christian Church and 
    Community Homeless Alliance Ministry in 
    downtown San Jose, homeless in 2001 and 2002. 
    Selena Janelle Hernandez, single mother, 
    executive manager, homeless. The event will 
    conclude with a presentation and discussion of 
    how we can work together to end homelessness in 
    our community.
