    Village Vistas: Our Village Plum Trees
    To walk down our streets in February and March is to see a quiet
    change of color. Long before the official start of spring, and well
    before the famous Cherry trees begin their pale-pink show, the
    Purple Leaf Plums have already taken over the neighborhood. They
    are the village’s early birds, bringing a sudden splash of color to the
    sidewalk while the rest of the trees are still waking up from winter.
    These trees are often part of a friendly mix-up, as many people
    mistake them for true Japanese Cherries. But the careful neighbor
    knows how to tell them apart. While a cherry tree usually waits for
    its blossoms to fall before its green leaves appear, the Purple Leaf
    Plum prefers to do both at once. It grows deep, wine-colored leaves
    that act as a dark backdrop to its delicate, light-pink flowers.
    There is a bit of local history in these branches, too. The "Krauter
    Vesuvius" variety, which we see all over Sunnyvale, was actually a
    California invention. It was bred in 1957 to handle the specific, dry
    heat of our local summers. However, their family tree goes back
    much further. Almost every purple plum in the West can be traced
    back to a single tree found in 1880 by Monsieur Pissard, the
    gardener to the Shah of Persia. He brought the plant to France, and
    from there, it eventually made its way to the gardens of the New
    World.
    While they are beautiful to look at, they are also useful to the
    village’s smallest residents. Since they bloom so early, they are a
    vital food source for local bees waking up from the cold. And while
    the small, tart fruits that appear in late summer are safe to eat,
    most are left for the birds and squirrels who live in the heights of
    neighborhood parks.
    As we enjoy this early gift of color, it is a lovely reminder that in our
    corner of the valley, spring doesn’t wait for the calendar; it simply
    waits for the first plum tree to bloom.

    What Issues Should You Report?
    Look around Lakewood Village and let us know about areas that need attention, including but not limited to:
    Sidewalks that are cracked or unsafe
    Potholes that need filling
    Overgrown trees in need of trimming
    Abandoned vehicles that are taking up space
    Neglected properties that impact our community’s appearance
    How to Submit a Report
    The more reports we get, the stronger our case! You can submit issues in two easy ways:
    1. Email us with specific details (exact addresses, descriptions, and photos if possible) at board@lvnasv.org.
    2. Report directly to the City via Access Sunnyvale (https://sunnyvale.dynamics365portals.us). This officially
    enters your complaint into the system. If you do this, let us know so we can help follow up!
    3. Attend a City Hall meeting on a Tuesday to voice your concerns in person—this can make an even bigger
    impact and catch their attention. If you choose this option, also file a claim on Access Sunnyvale to
    strengthen your case. More information about the meeting: https://www.sunnyvale.ca.gov/your-
    government/governance/city-council/council-meeting-information
    Together, we can make Lakewood Village a safer, cleaner, and better-maintained community. Thank you for
    taking the time to report issues and advocate for our neighborhood!
