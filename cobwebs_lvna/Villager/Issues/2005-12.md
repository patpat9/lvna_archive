    I’m Feeling Grateful that I’m Involved with the 
    LVNA 
    I usually stick to reports on Village activities for my 
    column, but since it was just Thanksgiving, I thought 
    I’d digress. I have been feeling a bit down about my 
    volunteering activities with the LVNA board because 
    there seems to be such small turnout for our meetings, 
    and every time we have elections for the board we 
    can’t seem to come up with any new folks who want to 
    participate. However, as I write this the parade is just 
    about to happen, and I know people will turn out for 
    that. Also, I’m remembering that many people came 
    out for the annual picnic in September, and that was a 
    lot of fun. And every year for Dumpster Day there are 
    plenty of volunteers to monitor the dumpsters. This all 
    makes me think about how my involvement with the 
    board here has introduced me to a great group of “core” 
    people who consistently help in the neighborhood and 
    come out for meetings and events. These are 
    wonderful, inspiring people who have lived in 
    Lakewood Village for years and enjoy participating in 
    these activities that bring neighbors together and 
    strengthen the neighborhood. This is the first place I’ve 
    lived where I’ve known a lot of my neighbors at all, 
    and it is very comforting. So when I feel a bit 
    dispirited, I just talk to the other board members and 
     
    they re-energize me and remind me why service to my 
    neighborhood is truly a wonderful and rewarding 
    undertaking. It does make a difference.

    Garden 
    •Be sure to get in the last of the 
    spring-blooming bulbs this month. 
    Remember that wet soil is difficult to 
    dig properly. Be careful not to plant 
    the bulbs too deep. 
    •Water plants as needed.  
    •Watch out for frost. 
    •Direct water from downspouts to avoid damage to the 
    garden. 
    •Save wood ashes from the fireplace as a source of 
    potash for the compost pile.

    TASMAN SQUARE DENTAL CARE
     
    •Request seed and nursery catalogs from mail-order 
    companies. 
    •Set out large-sized transplants of winter-flowering 
    plants into the bedding plot, using such plants as 
    calendula, Iceland poppy, primrose, and stock. 
    •Apply dormant spray to deciduous flowering plants 
    and fruit trees. 
    •Prune pines, magnolias, junipers, and red-berried 
    plants for holiday greens. 
    •Cut back chrysanthemums as they finish blooming. 
    •Hold off fertilizing most plants in preparation for 
    winter. 
    •Stabilize plants that might be harmed by wind. 
    •Harvest winter vegetables as they ripen. 
    These tips are from the book Northern California Gardening: A 
    Month-by-Month Guide, by Katherine Grace Endicott,

    Safety and Sobriety Checkpoint 
    Scheduled for December 17 
     
    Sunnyvale Department of Public Safety officers will 
    conduct a DUI safety and sobriety checkpoint from 10 
    p.m. to 3 a.m., Saturday, December 17. The primary 
    purpose of the checkpoint is public education. 
    Each year a countywide “Avoid the 13” safe-driving 
    program is launched by police agencies in Santa Clara 
    County. Numerous checkpoints will be established 
    throughout the county, all with the intent of educating 
    the public to the perils of driving while impaired. 
    The checkpoints provide an opportunity to locate 
    drivers who are under the influence of alcohol or 
    drugs, and remove them from local roadways. Every 
    year, drivers who are under the influence cause a 
    disproportionately high number of collisions, many of 
    which result in injury or death. 
    During the checkpoint hours, cars will be randomly 
    stopped and checked. Officers will look at the safety 
    of the vehicle, use of seatbelts, the operator’s license, 
    and state of sobriety. Mothers Against Drunk Drivers 
    (MADD) will also be present to distribute educational 
    materials. ■

    Sunnyvale Again Named Fifth-Safest 
    City 
    The twelfth annual Morgan Quitno Safest Cities in 
    America awards again place Sunnyvale as the fifthsafest of cities with a population between 100,000 and 
    499,999. Among cities of all sizes, Sunnyvale rated 
    the highest of any northern California city. The 
    awards rank 369 cities across the nation and are based 
    on the FBI’s 2005 crime report, which reflects data 
    from 2004. 
    “The city and our residents have formed a partnership 
    in which every one of us has a sense of pride and 
    ownership in the quality of our life. Our many 
    neighborhood associations have been key to helping 
    maintain both that quality of life and the safety of our 
    community,” said Mayor Dean J. Chu. 
    For the fifth consecutive year, neighboring San Jose 
    ranked first among cities with a population above 
    500,000. 
    Sunnyvale has been ranked in the top ten cities in its 
    population class for the last ten years, and for eight 
    years it has been ranked as one of the top 20 safest 
    cities of all sizes, usually placing within the top 10. 
    See http://www.morganquitno.com/safecity.htm for 
    complete rankings, methodology, and previous year’s 
    results. ■
