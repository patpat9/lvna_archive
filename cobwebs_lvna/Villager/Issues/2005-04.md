    o Pat Castillo gave a presentation on the Four Points 
    Sheraton ”Crescent” Project (formerly Sunnyvale 
    Hilton).

    Fernandez is the new First Vice 
    President―welcome aboard! All the other 
    positions were filled by the incumbents. Thanks go 
    to Lori Sabala, previous First VP, for her support in 
    the past year. 
    o 3. The Board announced the projected LVNA 
    events for this year: Dumpster Day, National Night 
    Out, Picnic, Holiday (Christmas) Parade, and 
    Holiday Lighting Contest. Other events and fundraising ideas were also discussed. 
    o 4. Pest Control―Skip Gottschalk provided a list of 
    pesticides approved by the California Department 
    of Pesticide Regulation to the City of Sunnyvale 
    for assessment. The list is being reviewed by  
     
    Sunnyvale Public Works Department. (see Board 
    Meeting notes for update.) 
    March 30 Board Meeting 
    1. The treasurer noted that LVNA still has debts 
    pending from insurance costs of 2004 events, but 
    we haven’t gotten invoices from United 
    Neighborhoods of Santa Clara County (UNSCC), 
    which is trying to work out costs for all its member 
    neighborhood associations. 
    2. Pest Control―The City’s assessment by Public 
    Works states that the City adheres to the Federal 
    Clean Water Act for regulation and updates. One of 
    the primary reasons a sewer-spraying program 
    cannot be implemented is that Sunnyvale’s water 
    pollution plant cannot remove insecticides from the 
    water discharged to the bay or as recycled water. 
    Therefore, the city cannot use pesticides in public 
    sewers or storm drains.  
    The City emphasizes to residents and property 
    owners that control of pests, particularly small 
    insects and small rodents, is up to the residents 
    and homeowners. Residents can improve 
    conditions on their property by keeping pet food 
    and feces picked up, controlling overgrowth of 
    shrubbery and long grass, doing routine 
    maintenance and inspection, and, if necessary, 
    hiring exterminators. 
    3. Calendar of events and guest speaker 
    recommendations were discussed. Newly assigned 
    Public Safety Chief Don Johnson and Mayor Dean 
    Chu are requested speakers in the upcoming 
    months. 
    Volunteer Appreciation Night is scheduled for the 
    April 14 General Meeting. 
    4. The LVNA Board has received e-mails pertaining 
    to cockroaches on Lakefair Drive and vagrancy in 
    the Lakewood Village shopping center. These 
    issues are being addressed with various city 
    departments.  
    Concerned residents who submit issues to the 
    LVNA Board are encouraged to attend the general 
    meeting to discuss problems with other residents of 
    the neighborhood. 

    Red Cross Disaster Preparedness and 
    First Aid Workshop 
    -Skip Gottschalk, LVNA Secretary 
    LVNA members who attended a Red Cross Disaster 
    Preparedness and First Aid Workshop on March 31 
    learned about procedures for common emergencies 
    and for the inevitable earthquake.  
    The program emphasized steps everyone should take: 
    Make a Plan for evacuating the home and assembling 
    or contacting family members. 
    Build a Kit of supplies for everyone in the household 
    for at least 3 days. 
    Get Properly Trained in first aid and calling 911 or 
    the local emergency number. First aid and CPR 
    information is available at: www.paarc.org or (650) 
    688-0415. 
     
     
     
    The workshop leaders, from the Palo Alto chapter of 
    the American Red Cross, showed how to apply first 
    aid for breathing or choking problems, bleeding, 
    shock, burns, and muscle, bone, or joint injuries. They 
    demonstrated CPR (cardiopulmonary resuscitation), 
    the Heimlich maneuver, and applying splints and 
    using slings. 
    The volunteers provided information on becoming a 
    Red Cross Volunteer [(650) 688-0415] and donating 
    blood, which is always in short supply. They 
    displayed two Red Cross disaster and first aid kits, 
    available for purchase at the www.paarc.org Web site. 
    The pamphlets the Palo Alto Red Cross chapter 
    volunteers handed out will be available at the next 
    LVNA General Meeting. 
    Because the information provided in this workshop is 
    so important, the LVNA members who attended 
    suggested that the workshop should be an annual 
    event for LVNA. 

    Out of control parking 
    Dear Editor, 
    When will LVNA get our streets parking 
    under control?  Recently a house on our 
    street was rented to, what appears to 
    be, either a mechanic or a car dealer 
    (lots of cars and car service).  And the 
    street parking is being snapped up on 
    both sides and in both directions.  We 
    ourselves, have 3 cars (2 in the drive, 
    1 on the street).  The new neighbor 
    leaves no parking in front of our own 
    house which forces my teenage son to 
    park in front of someone else’s house 
    and so on and so on. 
    Is there NO limit to the number of cars 
    that one resident can have?  I 
    understand that the street is public 
    parking, but it would be nice if each 
    neighbor could limit the amount of space 
    they occupy in front of their neighbors 
    house.  We have cars in front of our 
    house that do not move for DAYS even 
    when space opens up in the rentals 
    driveway.  Please be considerate.  Even 
    if you are in our neighborhood as a 
    renter and do not plan to stay...  
    Homeowner, Manzano Way 
     
    [Editor’s note: The LVNA cannot control parking. It is 
    often best to speak with the neighbor about the 
    problem to see if the two of you can resolve the issue. 
    If that doesn’t work, refer to the Sunnyvale Municipal 
    Code to see if any laws are actually being violated. 
    Your neighbor may be perfectly within their right to 
    use street parking, but if they are not, refer to the last 
    page of this newsletter for phone numbers you can 
    call for such things as illegal parking or businesses.]

    April in Your 
    Yard and Garden 
    o When the garden soil is dry 
    enough to work in without the 
    soil clinging to the shovel, dig 
    in lavish quantities of compost 
    or other organic amendments. 
    This is particularly important 
    in the vegetable garden. 
    o Before digging into the spring 
    gardening season, clean and repair garden tools; 
    sharpen the blades of pruning tools and shovels; 
    service the lawn mower. 
    o When spring-blooming bulbs fade, leave the dying 
    foliage intact to draw nutrients down into the bulb 
    for next year’s bloom. To cover the fading bulb 
    foliage, set out six-packs of lobelia and sweet 
    alyssum. 
    o Begin weeding while the weeds are young and the 
    ground is still wet. 
    o In preparation for the coming hot, dry season, 
    apply a mulch. 
    o Take cuttings for new plants from azaleas, 
    carnations, chrysanthemums, geraniums, and 
    succulents. 
    o Fertilize roses and crape myrtles. Fertilize azaleas, 
    camellias, and rhododendron when they finish 
    blooming. If you did not fertilize them last month, 
    fertilize citrus, flowering plants about to bloom, 
    trees, shrubs, and cool-season lawns. 
    o Prune spring-blooming plants as they finish 
    blooming. Prune hydrangeas, fuchsias, and 
    bougainvilleas. 
    o Plant out summer bulbs. Set out summerflowering plants. 
    o Watch out for snails and earwigs, both of which 
    devour seedlings. Use a strong spray from the 
    hose to wash away aphids. Wrap fruit tree trunks 
    with a sticky tape to control ants. Wrap low so 
    that birds, particularly hummingbirds, will not be 
    attracted. 
    These tips are from the book Northern California Gardening: A 
    Month-by-Month Guide, by Katherine Grace Endicott, published 
    by Chronicle Books, San Francisco, ©1996, and are used with the 
    publisher’s permission. For more information, see: 
    http://www.lvna.net/Resources/Reading_List/reading_list.html 
