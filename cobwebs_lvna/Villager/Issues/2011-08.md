    Dumpster Day 2011 
    -Another Successful Neighborhood Event 
    by Skip Gottschalk 
     
    The Lakewood Village 2011 Dumpster 
    Day, Saturday 16 July, was a busy day 
    for all. There were 19 dumpsters for 
    broken appliances, yard waste, 
    household trash, and tires. The Yard 
    Sale associated with Dumpster Day made $131 for LVNA. 
    Fifty nine loads were taken for a total of 128.43 tons of 
    trash removed from the Village. That’s over 256, 000 lbs     
    – good job Lakewood Village!!! 
     
    The planned 1 day event turned into a 2 day event for some 
    locations, as some dumpster monitors were present to 
    permit the neighbors to dump on Friday. It certainly 
    appeared like most people were prepared, as the traffic 
    around the dumpsters was quite busy throughout the event.

    PG&E To Test Gas Main Under 
    Lakewood Village 
    By Fred Fowler 
     
    Hello neighbors.  A sign has sprouted 
    on the southeast corner of the 
    intersection of Lawrence Expressway and 
    Lakehaven Drive.  Sometime during the day on 
    August 4th, PG&E put up a large notice saying that a 
    major natural gas main running under Lakehaven 
    Drive would be inspected and/or tested in the coming 
    weeks. They apologize for any 
    inconvenience.  
    It is likely that you have also received a 
    notice in the mail from PG&E.  Phyllis 
    and I received one because our house 
    is “within about 2000 feet of a natural 
    gas transmission”  from the pipeline 
    under Lakehaven, as anyone can be in 
    Lakewood Village, so it is a safe bet that 
    everyone in the neighborhood must 
    have gotten a similar letter. 
    So what is going on? 
    Here is the information we have at the moment.  
    There is a major gas main that goes right through 
    Lakewood Village.  It runs roughly parallel to the 
    Hetch-Hetchy pipeline, enters our neighborhood a 
    little south of Fairwood Park, and exits just west of 
    the condominiums at the end of Lakehaven Drive.   
    It may be of interest to note that this is not just ―any‖ 
    natural gas main.  It is in fact the same pipeline that 
    runs through San Bruno and that ruptured some 
    months ago.  The pipeline dates back to before 
    Lakewood Village was built in the 1950s, and after 
    the accident in San Bruno, PG&E is working through 
    a program of inspection for all similar transmission 
    lines. 
    The notice as Lawrence and Lakehaven states that 
    the work may include both inspections and 
    ―hydrostatic testing‖.   
    Pipes are inspected by sending robotic inspection 
    probes down into the system, where they check for 
    leaks, bad welds and other defects.  ―Hydrostatic‖ 
    testing involves pumping high pressure water into 
    the pipes to see if the pressure holds.  If either kind 
    of test shows a potential problem, the only way to 
    fix it is to dig up the bad pipe and replace it. 
     
    PG&E will be performing these tests in the coming 
    weeks, and while they are underway there may be 
    several PG&E trucks partially blocking Lakehaven 
    Drive.  If any problems are found, Lakehaven will 
    probably be closed in places while repairs 
    are made. 
     
    In the end all of the inconveniences will be 
    well worth it in order to keep our 
    neighborhood safe.  No one wants a 
    repeat of the San Bruno fireball taking 
    place in Lakewood Village! 
     
    If you see a PG&E truck or two parked 
    along Lakehaven Drive and a crew of 
    technicians hard at work, you might want to roll 
    down your car window and say ―thank you‖.  Their 
    job is to keep us all safe.  PG&E has thousands of 
    miles of pipeline to maintain, and the fact that they 
    are about to get started here in Lakewood is 
    something we should all be grateful for.  
     
    ―No one wants a repeat 
    of the San Bruno 
    fireball taking place in 
    Lakewood Village!”

    Sunnyvale Neighborhood Actively Prepare 
    Dept of Public Safety/Office of Emergency Services 
     
    Following a disaster, emergency services personnel may be 
    unavailable to meet the demands of its community.  The 
    emergency services that you have come to expect through    
    9-1-1 at a moments notice, such as police, fire, medical, etc. 
    may be dispatched elsewhere.   
     
    As we witnessed during previous disasters, initially people 
    have to rely on each other for help in order to meet their 
    immediate life saving and life sustaining needs.  In an effort 
    to prepare residents of Sunnyvale to protect family and 
    property, the Sunnyvale Department of Public Safety Office of 
    Emergency Services developed the SNAP Training 
    Program.  The purpose of SNAP is to provide Sunnyvale 
    residents with tools to be self-sufficient for a minimum of 72 
    hours following a disaster.   
     
    The training program is on a volunteer basis, taught for seven 
    consecutive weeks.  As a SNAP participant, you will learn 
    about disaster preparedness empowering you to help save 
    lives while working with the City of Sunnyvale to do "The 
    greatest good for the greatest number of people." The fall 
    classes for SNAP will be starting in September.
