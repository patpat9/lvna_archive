    June in Your Yard and 
    Garden 
     
    •Weed. Do not let the weeds form 
    seedheads. Be sure to knock down tall 
    dry grasses because they create a 
    serious fire hazard. 
    •Add young, seedhead-free weeds to 
    the compost pile. Remember to keep 
    the pile as damp as a wrung-out sponge for faster 
    composting. Turn the pile occasionally. 
    •Watch out for snails and earwigs, both of which 
    devour seedlings. 
    •Use a strong spray from the hose to wash away aphids. 
    •Wrap fruit tree trunks with a sticky tape to control 
    ants. Wrap low so that birds, particularly 
    hummingbirds, will not be attracted. Fruit will be 
    ripening soon and the ants will go after the fruit. Water 
    plants as needed.  
    •Insulate the garden soil from hot, dry weather by 
    applying a two-inch-thick mulch. 
     
     
    •Fertilize plants if you see signs of pale, lackluster 
    growth. Yellow leaves with green veins often indicate 
    an iron deficiency, which can be corrected with 
    chelated iron. 
    •Fertilize the lawn. Fertilize all flowering plants about 
    to bloom. Fertilize roses. 
    •Pinch off dead flowers, particularly off lilacs and other 
    late-spring flowering shrubs. Make sure you get the 
    entire flower head when dead-heading annuals—
    including the swollen ovaries where seeds form. This 
    will encourage new flowers as the plant rushes to 
    reproduce itself. 
    •Transplant summer-flowering plants into the bedding 
    plot. Hot weather is hard on newly transplanted plants. 
    Shade them for a few days with the temporary shelter 
    of an umbrella or wooden shake. Stake tall sprawling 
    flowers such as delphiniums and dahlias. 
    •Continue to plant the vegetable garden. Thin vegetable 
    seedlings that were sown too close together by snipping 
    them with scissors. Harvest vegetables as they ripen, 
    picking them early in the day. 
    •Be very attentive to your watering system to make 
    sure that it is in good working order. Soak all newly 
    transplanted plants deeply. 
    These tips are from the book Northern California Gardening: A 
    Month-by-Month Guide, by Katherine Grace Endicott, 
    published by Chronicle Books, San Francisco, copyright 1996, 
    and are used with the publisher’s permission.■

    City Seeks Ideas for Liquidambar Tree Fruit 
    Those spiky balls produced by liquidambar trees are a 
    nuisance, and the city is looking for ideas on how to 
    deal with them.  
    Douglas Mello, superintendent of the Trees and 
    Landscaping Division for the City of Sunnyvale, is 
    working on a study, "Liquidambar Street Trees—
    Nuisance Fruit Problem," which will be presented to 
    City Council in August of 2005. 
    The Trees and Landscaping Division is seeking citizen 
    input on how best to address the liquidambar seasonal 
    fruit-pod (those spiky balls) nuisance. Removing the 
    liquidambar trees is one option for City Council to 
    consider, but the study would ideally include 
    suggestions on what to do about the seasonal fruit 
    pods without removing the trees. The study group asks 
    that those who submit ideas focus only on how best to 
    deal with the liquidambar fruit-pod nuisance. 
    Send your ideas on this issue to Douglas Mello, 
    dmello@ci.sunnyvale.ca.us, or to Sunnyvale's tree 
    supervisor, Leonard Dunn, ldunn@ci.sunnyvale.ca.us, 
    no later than Monday, June 20, 2005.  
    Your input will assist City Council in deciding how 
    best to address the problem of those spiky balls.■

    Health Series 
    “For Your Health,” a series of health-related 
    presentations, debuted on KSUN-15, Sunnyvale’s 
    government-access television station, on Memorial 
    Day. 
    “For Your Health” is a partnership program between 
    the City’s Neighborhood and Community Resources 
    Division and Camino Medical Group (CMG). The 
    series began last fall as a free program at the 
    Sunnyvale Community Center. CMG physicians lead 
    the programs, which cover a wide variety of healthrelated, timely topics of interest to individuals and 
    families 
    Future episodes of “For Your Health” will be taped in 
    Sunnyvale City Council chambers beginning at 7 p.m. 
    on the fourth Wednesday of each month through  
     
     
     
     
     
    October. KSUN-15 broadcasts are scheduled for the 
    week following each taping. 
    To reserve a seat at a future taping, or to get more 
    information about upcoming programs, call Camino 
    Medical Group at (408) 523-3295. 
    “For Your Health” will be broadcast the week 
    following each taping.  
    To view the KSUN-15 schedule, visit the City of 
    Sunnyvale’s Web site at www.sunnyvale.ca.gov.

    New Online Catalog Coming to the 
    Sunnyvale Library 
    The Sunnyvale Library will soon have a new, more 
    capable library catalog online. The new catalog will 
    provide many useful features, support for multiple 
    languages, and a user interface that is consistent with 
    library catalogs of nearby cities. The new catalog is 
    scheduled to become operational sometime in June. 
    Using this new catalog, you will be able to browse 
    books by their covers. A picture of the book cover will 
    appear with many of the library’s catalog entries. 
    Some entries will also have a book review or an online 
    copy of the first chapter.  
    The search and help screens will be available in 
    English, Spanish, and traditional Chinese. Sometime 
    later, you will be able to search for Chinese language 
    materials using Chinese characters in your search. 
    The Sunnyvale Library catalog will be connected to  
     
     
     
     
    the catalogs of nearby cities, and you’ll be able to 
    search multiple libraries at once. Later this year, you’ll 
    be able to locate in other libraries materials that 
    Sunnyvale does not own, initiate an interlibrary loan, 
    and then pick up the item from the Sunnyvale Library. 
    As with the old catalog system, you will be able to 
    check on your library account, place a reserve on 
    items that have been checked out by someone else, 
    and renew items that you have checked out. 
    Watch for the new catalog to come online on the 
    library’s home page, www.sunnyvalelibrary.org. If 
    you have questions, you can call the library reference 
    desk at (408) 730-7300.■
