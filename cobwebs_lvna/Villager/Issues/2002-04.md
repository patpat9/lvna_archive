    Presidents Column • Craig LVNA The renovation of the downtown area is progressing at a rapid pace. This month's speaker is Robert (Bob) Paternoster; he is the Director of Community Development for the City of Sunnyvale. He also has direct oversight and management of the downtown development projects that are occurring in Sunnyvale. These projects include 460,000 square feet of office space and 10,000 square feet of retail space at Washington, Evelyn, and Mathilda; redevelopment Of the Town Center Mall; redevelopment of the Caltrain station; and potential redevelopment of Town and Country Village. He will share the latest information with the group and answer questions. If you are with the neighbors who think the collection of high-rises and the mall redevelopment will bring new retail stores and a chance to shop closer to home is good, or the other side that think that Sunnyvale is losing its small town charm and is being overbuilt, this month's speaker will be able to answer questions that you might have. The LVNA has received a Grant for $400.00 for the 2002 Holiday Parade from the City of Sunnyvale.     Lawrence Expressway Improvement Update CrNford LVNA Thom Mayer is our representative to the County for the Lawrence Expressway improvement. The following is his report to Lakewood. It looks like the proposal to cut off the crossing of Lawrence between Sandia and Lakehaven is off the table. Thanks for that. Thom sent this e-mail to the Lakewood Village e-mail list: "I attended the 'Comprehensive County Expressway Planning Study' Policy Advisory Board meeting. "I introduced myself as a resident of Lakewood Village and explained that we are bisected by Lawrence Expressway north of 101. I asked that they be kind to us. "I was pleasantly surprised to find that they were more than willing to minimize negative impacts on neighborhoods. More than half of of the board member comments were directed at minimizing the effect of the expressway improvements on "Quality of Life" issues (bicyclists, pedestrians, and neighborhoods). "Banning left turns at Sandia Lakehaven is now off the table. So is a grade separation at Sandia Lakehaven. Now we need to get the Sandia - Lakehaven grade separation out Of the Sunnyvale Plan. Grade separation is still part of the City's official long term (IO to 20 years) plans. There are no short term (5 years) plans to move ahead with this project. "It would be good to get it removed from the City's long-term plans. "They are looking at grade a separation at Tasman with Lawrence going under light-rail. My topic was a review of the Consideration of Park Enforcement Issues study issue which was assigned to the Parks & Recreation Department by City Council for study in calendar year 2001. The study was completed and a report to Council was provided the evening of November 13, 2001. This report is number 01-333 and is available in its entirety on the City of Sunnyvale web site just check the at www.ci.sunnyvale.ca.us -- section on Council matters. Results of the study indicated fairly strong support citywide for the level of enforcement of existing laws at neighborhood parks. In fact, a random phone survey revealed that 86% of those surveyed felt that visitors comply very well or fairly well with existing park rules and laws. During the study process we were also interested in how folks felt about this topic in particular neighborhoods and asked that tally responses to specific surveys neighborhoods including Lakewood, Las Palmas, and Ortega/Panama. While the Las Palmas and Ortega/Panama responses to surveys were very similar to those found citywide, response for the Lakewood Park area was not as positive. Only 50% of respondents stated that visitors comply very well or fairly THE L.V.N.A. VILLAGER 4 well with these laws and rules. Because the number of persons surveyed that lived near Lakewood was well below other neighborhoods (only 26) we followed up at the February meeting to ensure that LVNA members could voice their opinions. That evening, I heard comments regarding upcoming park projects and a couple of concerns regarding non-compliance with dog related laws. Concerns of non-compliance with dogs off leash and dog doo laws is shared citywide. These dog related concerns made up about 37% of all concerns expressed by respondents who felt that currently there is not adequate compliance with existing Park laws and policies. Parks staff also note occasions of non-compliance with these laws and Animal Control Officers of the Department of Public Safety spend a good bit of time each year following up on these laws. As City Council reviewed the report in November they requested a pilot program for specific enforcement actions be undertaken by the Departments of Public Safety and Parks and Recreation. This pilot project will take place in springtime as the weather warms and park visitors increase in numbers. I'd like to share one step that neighbors can take to help City staff make the parks and the John W. Christian Greenbelt a better place to play and visit. Because dog related concerns are shared by so many park visitors, we're asking all dog owners to increase vigilance in cleaning up after their pets and complying with the leash law. Additionally, neighbors that observe non-compliance should contact Animal Control Officers at 730-7178. Together with staff, neighbors who take action can make a remarkable and positive difference in the neighborhood. Again, I express thanks from the Department of Parks and Recreation for the chance to share this information, both at the meeting in February and now, in your newsletter. Our staff are very proud of the work that they do for your neighborhood and often hear appreciation from park visitors for their efforts. Please feel free to contact the Parks Division with any park related matter at 730-7506 and staff will work to answer concerns quickly. • April 2002
