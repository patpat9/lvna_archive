    President’s Column 
    - Phyllis Murphy 
    Hello, my name is Phyllis Murphy and I am the 
    new President of the Lakewood Village 
    Neighborhood Association.  I have lived in 
    Lakewood with my husband and three children 
    for the past 13 years.  When the kids were 
    much younger I would attend so of the LVNA 
    monthly meetings but over the years with all of 
    the after school sports and other events I 
    stopped going.  I did however still feel 
    somewhat connected by the monthly 
    newsletter.  Now that they are getting older 
    and a little more self-sufficient I felt it could 
    dedicate some time into giving back to a 
    neighborhood that I have grown to love of the 
    years.  So with that I hope that I will do a good 
    job for you and would love for you to come out 
    to a couple of the monthly meetings even if for 
    only a few minutes and say hello and let me 
    know some of your ideas and concerns. 

    Congratulations and Welcome! 
     
    As one of the continuing LVNA volunteers, I 
    would like to congratulate and welcome our

    Lakewood Village Supports its 
    Neighborhood Association 
     
    If you read last month’s Villager, you might 
    have become as worried as I was that the 
    LVNA might cease to exist or that we would 
    find it necessary to suspend publication of this 
    newsletter. The LVNA is arguably Sunnyvale’s 
    oldest, longest-lived, and most proactive 
    neighborhood association, and it would have 
    been very sad to see it close its doors. 
     
    I was delighted to see a large turnout of our 
    neighbors at the March general meeting, and it 
    was clear that no position was going to go 
    unfilled. Some of the people there literally 
    stated that they would fill positions if no one 
    else had come forward. We live in a very 
    special neighborhood. 
     
    Thank You for a Job Well Done! 
     
    Last year’s LVNA board and volunteers were 
    outstanding, and in many cases, long-standing 
    contributors to Lakewood Village. On behalf of 
    the neighborhood, I would like to thank you for 
    everything that you did in the last year, two 
    years, or in some cases ten years (!), for 
    Lakewood Village.

    Letters to the LVNA 
     
    March 11th, 2003 
    To the LVNA, 
     
    My husband and I have resided in 
    Lakewood Village for several years and 
    are delighted to live here.  The peeve I 
    have with residents is throwing trash in 
    the street, lawn, or anywhere it’s 
    convenient to toss.  Lakewood Village is 
    a blue-collar neighborhood and there are 
    many of us that take pride and work hard 
    for what we own and hope to have for a 
    long time.  I walk my dog everyday and 
    night and bring with me plastic bags to 
    pick up my dog's "business".  In 
    addition to that 20-minute walk, I'm 
    constantly leaning over to pick up 
    someone else's empty cigarette box, 
    Kleenex, wrappers and plastic bottles 
    and cans.  Believe it or not, I bring 
    home a full bag of garbage each and 
    every night.  This is unacceptable and 
    makes me angry that our community cannot 
    be more considerate of others and be 
    willing to keep their neighborhood 
    clean.  Is it that much trouble to place 
    in a receptacle or your recycling 
    carrier in which everyone has at their 
    home?   
     
    This is AMERICA KEEP IT CLEAN. 
     
    - Lakewood Village resident

    Sunnyvale’s Downtown 
    To Be or Not to Be 
    Presentation at the LVNA Meeting 
    - Friends of Sunnyvale staff 
    The Friends of Sunnyvale, a grass roots 
    organization of Sunnyvale residents concerned 
    about preserving the quality of life in 
    Sunnyvale, will be at the LVNA’s April 9 th 
    meeting to give a presentation regarding 
    Downtown Sunnyvale and to circulate their 
    petitions regarding redevelopment in 
    downtown.  
     
    They will describe the City’s high rise, high 
    density plan for our Downtown and will then 
    show two alternative low rise, low density 
    plans developed by two architects who are also 
    members of their organization.   
     
    Then they will talk to us about the 3 initiatives 
    they have been working to qualify for the 
    November ballot.  Your signatures on these 
    petitions can help you have a say regarding the 
    direction our Downtown will take.  Their goal is 
    to build upon Sunnyvale’s uniqueness, quality 
    of life and history as a suburban city by having 
    a traditional suburban downtown we can be 
    proud to call our own for the next 50 years. 
     
    Attend the meeting to “get the scoop” and help 
    them collect enough signatures to give all the 
    citizens of Sunnyvale the opportunity to have a 
    choice and a voice  in creating a downtown 
    where they would like to shop, dine, meet their 
    friends, and enjoy a village green. 
     
    All signatures must be turned in by April 12
    th 
    so this is the last opportunity to help them 
    reach the number of signatures they need 
    qualify the initiatives for the ballot. 
     
    Hope to see you all there.  Together we can 
    make a difference. 

    Fact Sheet: 
    Sunnyvale Budget 
     
    The City Manager’s 
    Recommended Revisions to the Current 
    10-Year Capital/Special Projects Plan 
     
    The City Manager has proposed a Six Point Plan to 
    address the current budget crisis.  Point 1 of the 
    Plan is to re-examine the 10-Year Capital/Special 
    Projects Plan to identify current or future projects 
    that could be deleted, revised or deferred.  City 
    Council will consider the City Manager’s proposed 
    revisions to the 10-Year Capital/Special Projects 
    Plan on June 17, 2003 when Council considers and 
    adopts the FY 2003/2004 budget.    
     
    What is the 10-Year Capital/Special 
    Projects Plan? 
    The 10-Year Capital/Special Projects Plan is a 
    complete inventory of all projects that the City 
    plans to undertake during the next 10 years.  
    These include: 
     
    • Capital Projects
    :  Major improvements, 
    construction, or acquisition of major assets 
    such as the new Senior Center; 
    • Infrastructure Projects:  Projects which 
    maintain the integrity of the City’s 
    infrastructure systems  such as the 
    replacement of water lines;   
    • Special Projects:  Projects which address a 
    significant community need or problem 
    such as the Moffett Park Specific Plan; 
    • Outside Group Funding Projects
    :  Projects 
    which provide City funds to community-
    based organizations that serve local 
    residents. 
    How many Projects are in the 10-Year 
    Plan? 
    The 10-Year Plan contained in the FY 
    2002/2003 budget includes a total of 350 
    projects, totaling $176 million.

    What criteria were used in recommending 
    revisions to the Capital/Special Projects 
    Plan? 
    The City Manager’s recommendations were 
    based on the following criteria:  whether the 
    project preserved the integrity of the City’s 
    existing infrastructure systems, contributed to 
    resolving a life safety issue, leveraged outside 
    funding, or was currently under construction.  
    The City Manager recommended retaining 
    projects that met one or more of these criteria.  
    At the City Council meeting on March 18, 2003, 
    Council will provide direction on criteria they 
    will use in considering the Capital/Special 
    Project budget.  Council’s approved budget 
    criteria will be available on the City website 
    and at City Hall on March 19, 2003.    
     
    Where can I find more information on the 
    recommended revisions to the 10-Year 
    Capital/Special Projects Plan? 
    A list of the City Manager’s recommended

    hardcopy at the Sunnyvale City Library, City 
    Clerk’s Office, and Department of Finance.  It is 
    also available on the City’s website at 
    www.ci.sunnyvale.ca.us/budget-plan.

    What effect will the City Manager’s 
    recommended revisions have on the 
    current budget crisis?  
     
    The proposed revisions to the 10-Year Plan will 
    generate $20,096,180 in one-time cost savings 
    and $6,905,981 in on-going operating cost 
    savings over 20 years.  Combined, the savings 
    will reduce the annual $11 million budget 
    deficit by approximately $2 million per year.

    Update on City-Wide 
    Garage Sale 
    - Peter Bassett 
    Community Outreach Coordinator 
    City of Sunnyvale 
     
    For eleven years, the City of Sunnyvale has 
    promoted a citywide garage sale on a single 
    weekend in April to advance the idea of “reuse” 
    and to assist residents in successfully turning 
    their “unwanted items” into others’ “treasures”.  
     
    This year the City's garage sale will function a 
    little bit differently. There will still be a City-
    wide garage sale. However, it will be a one-day 
    event -- on Saturday, April 26.  The City will no 
    longer compile or advertise individual resident 
    garage sales but they will continue to promote 
    the event. 
     
    The City will continue to provide resources to 
    assist residents in selling, donating, or 
    disposing of unwanted items in a way that is 
    X continued in next column 
    X continued from previous column 
     
    cost-effective to residents them and to the 
    City. Garage Sale-Donation Kits with complete 
    instructions will be available by March 31. The 
    City will provide residents with a wide variety 
    of resources to attract buyers to their event 
    (including several free on-line resources).  
     
    Coldwell Banker will continue to provide free 
    garage sale signs to residents interested in 
    hosting a garage sale. For more information 
    about garage sale signs call (408) 616-2737 or 
    email jdavis@cbnorcal.com .  
     
    Information about Garage Sale Day and year-
    round reuse will be updated regularly and can 
    be found at: 
    www.ci.sunnyvale.ca.us/recycle/garagesales/htm

    questions, please contact the Solid Waste and
