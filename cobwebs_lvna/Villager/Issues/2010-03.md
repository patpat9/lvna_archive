    FLOOD INSURANCE 
    The second topic we'll be dis-
    cussing at the March meeting is 
    the requirement for Federal Flood 
    Insurance for Lakewood Village 
    homeowners.  As many of you 
    know, Lakewood Village is right 
    on the boundary between parts of Sunnyvale where home-
    owners are required to have flood insurance and parts 
    where homeowners are not required to have it.  At our last 
    meeting, our neighbor Wendy Stanley reported that the 
    maps of the “1% flood zone” had recently changed for 
    Lakewood Village, and that she found out that her house is 
    no longer in the danger zone.  She had to contact her bank 
    and persuade them, but they agreed to drop the require-
    ment for her to have flood insurance.  She says she has 
    saved more than $1,000 as a result. 
    We will have 2 guests, Mike Klein from State Farm Insur-
    ance and Director Richard Santos from the Santa Clara 
    Water District. Mike lives and works in Sunnyvale and will 
    be explaining about Flood insurance and answer questions 
    on flood insurance or home owners insurance. Director 
    Santos can answer questions about the South Bay Salt 
    Pond Restoration and the impact it has on the flooding 
    map. He understands our concerns about flooding very 
    well. As a resident of Alviso, he has been flooded 3 times! 
     My wife Phyllis has a digital copy of the flood map of Lake-
    wood village and I will be bringing it to the March meeting. I 
    will also bring a laptop com-
    puter, a wireless internet con-
    nection and a PC projector to 
    show what the new boundaries 
    of the flood zone are.   
    As I said, please come to the 
    Lakewood Park building at 
    7:00pm on Wednesday, March 
    10th.  We'll do some important 
    business and then get some 
    important information.  I look 
    forward to seeing you there!

    Elections and Flood Insurance 
    Greetings!  Please come to the Lakewood Park 
    building on Wednesday, March 10th to take part in 
    the next general meeting of the LVNA.  The meet-
    ing will start at 7:00pm, and it should be an inter-
    esting one. 
    ELECTIONS 
    The first order of business will be the annual elec-
    tion of our officers.  The LVNA bylaws stipulate 
    that the organization will be governed by a com-
    mittee of five people serving in different capaci-
    ties.  There is a President, who bears overall re-
    sponsibility for the association.  There is a “1st 
    Vice President”, who presides if the president can-
    not and also is responsible for overseeing publicity 
    for the association.  There is a “2nd Vice President” 
    who presides if both the president and the 1st vice 
    president cannot and chairs something called the 
    “Grievance committee”.  There is a “Secretary” 
    who keeps the records of the organization, and 
    there is a “Treasurer” who handles the money and 
    keeps the organization's books.  Each officer 
    serves a one-year term after being elected,  and 
    no one can serve as an elected officer for more 
    than three consecutive terms. 
    This year we will have a bit of a challenge.  The LVNA 
    currently has a president (me), a 2nd vice president 
    (Felipe Misch), and a treasurer (Bev Woidyla).  We have 
    had no 1st vice president for some time, and recently our 
    secretary (Sarah Daugherty) resigned in order to take 
    better care of some family issues she is facing.  Felipe is 
    finishing his third consecutive year on the LVNA board, 
    and must rotate off in order to comply with the term limit 
    rules we have in our Bylaws.  That will leave us with just 
    two returning officers – Bev and me.

     Elections cont. 
    it turns out, the LVNA board cannot function with just two offi-
    cers.  The Bylaws (which serve as the LVNA's “constitution”) 
    require that board meetings must have a minimum of three 
    officers present in order to conduct business.  If we don't have 
    three elected officers, we can't make any decisions legally.  
    Without three officers, we won't be able legally to print this 
    newsletter, organize Dumpster Day, apply for city grant funds, 
    or engage in just about any other activity. 
    We need some new blood to serve on the LVNA board!  On the 
    10th, we'll be electing new leaders to help take the LVNA 
    through the 2010 association year.  As of the date I am writing 
    this article, there are a few people who are “thinking” about 
    running for an office, but no one has made a definite commit-
    ment yet.  I ask YOU to think about it, too!  The main commit-
    ment you would have to make is to attend LVNA board meet-
    ings, which happen once per month.  Other than that, the 
    amount of time and energy you will have to contribute is com-
    pletely up to you. 
    Over the years the LVNA has made a big difference for our 
    neighborhood.  From the graffiti wars of the 1990's to the pic-
    nics and parades we organize now, the LVNA has contributed a 
    great deal toward making Lakewood Village a wonderful place 
    to call home.  The neighborhood needs the LVNA.  And now, 
    the LVNA needs you. 
    If you are interested in running for one of the LVNA open of-
    fices, please feel free to contact me prior to the March meeting.  
    I'll be happy to answer any questions you have, and make sure 
    your name is on the nominations list.  I'll also thank you for 
    stepping up to help your neighbors in this important way.  
    Thanks in advance! 
    The Villager has a new 
    editor! 
    By Fred Fowler 
    At the February board meeting of 
    the LVNA, long-time LVNA Villager 
    editor Christine Majchrowicz an-
    nounced that it was time for her to 
    move on.  After several years of 
    dealing with printing deadlines and 
    other headaches, she has decided to turn the job of producing 
    the neighborhood newsletter over to a new, fresh pair of hands.  
    Many thanks to her for her long and dedicated service to Lake-
    wood Village! 
     
    The new editor is none other than Phyllis Fowler, Sunnyvale 
    school board trustee, former Girl Scout leader, and my better 
    half.  Phyllis is excited about the challenge of producing a publi-
    cation each month that goes to more than 1,700 recipients.  She 
    hopes to follow in the footsteps of Christy and the other past 
    editors of The Villager, and produce a newsletter that maintains 
    the same high standards of quality and relevance that have 
    characterized it over the years.

    Try out the new LVNA.NET Website! 
    By Fred Fowler 
    When was the last time you browsed the LVNA website?  
    (Really?  That long ago?). 
    A few years back our neighbor Brian Smithson created a 
    beautiful website for us that was full of good information, 
    neighborhood history, and even pictures.  Brian did it 
    when he had some spare time and energy to devote to 
    the project.  He did a fine job!  Unfortunately, Brian's 
    spare time ran out soon afterward, and the website lan-
    guished a bit, suffering from “benign neglect”.  Time 
    flies, and before we knew it the website's “current 
    events” were more than two years old. 
    I'm happy to report that this situation is changing!  I 
    would like to announce the re-birth of the LVNA website!  
    I would like to introduce the “LVNA Villager Online”, the 
    new internet based newsletter of the Lakewood Village 
    Neighborhood Association!  You can reach it at http://
    www.lvna.net. 
    If you point your browser at this URL you will notice a 
    distinct change from the old website.  Brian's creation 
    was meant as a kind of “reference book” about Lake-
    wood Village, with little bits of history mixed in with con-
    tact numbers and the names of LVNA officials.  The new 
    website is meant to be a kind of “newspaper”, where you 
    can go to get information about current events and is-
    sues affecting the neighborhood.  It is a different ap-
    proach, and I hope it will be appealing enough for you 
    and the rest of Lakewood Village to visit it often. 
    Another feature of the new website is that it is meant to 
    be an “interactive” resource rather than just a place to 
    read things.  There is a “classified advertisement” sec-
    tion where anyone can post a “classified advertisement” 
    free of charge.  Another section that is under develop-
    ment is an “ask City Hall” function that lets you add 
    questions to a kind of blog and then see the answers 
    that come back. 
    There is a “Neighborhood News” section of the web site 
    that will have the latest information about what's happen-
    ing in and around Lakewood Village.  There will also be 
    a “Lakewood Village Showcase” section where there will 
    be profiles of the different churches, volunteer organiza-
