    President’s Column 
    -Ursula Syrova, LVNA President 
    Well, this is it—my last president’s column. I have 
    been president of the LVNA for three terms, and it has 
    been interesting and thought provoking. If no one else 
    is interested in being president, the LVNA will stop 
    existing. This means specifically that the Villager will 
    stop being printed and delivered, and there will be no 
    one to organize the parade, the picnic, dumpster day, or 
    the lighting contest. My feeling is that if this reflects the 
    wishes of the neighborhood, so be it. We define 
    ourselves through our groups, and perhaps the 
    “neighborhood group” is not as important to people 
    right now as their family, school, work, or church 
    groups. Perhaps we have more in common with those 
    other groups than we do with our neighbors. Many 
    associations form around a cause—an opposed 
    development nearby, an interest in disaster 
    preparedness, some shared problem. If such a cause 
    arises, I have no doubt a group could form again. 
    A few logistical details: we received a grant to acquire 
    bingo equipment and banner signs. We have not yet 
    bought the bingo equipment, and if no one is interested 
    in continuing the LVNA, we will send that portion of 
    the grant funds back and abandon that project. The idea 
    behind the bingo was to bring neighbors together and 
    raise money to fund our other events. 
    A reminder: there is value in a neighborhood 
    organization, but not having one does not mean you 
    can’t know your neighbors. Say hello, respect each 
    other’s property, resolve conflicts peacefully, report 
    crimes if you see them, drive slowly so fellow 
    neighbors aren’t disturbed or kids endangered—those 
    are my hopes for Lakewood Village, with or without a 
    formal organization. ■ 
    Lakewood School To Be Fenced 
    Because of damage, robberies, and student safety at 
    Lakewood School, the school will soon be surrounded 
    by a fence.  
    Principal Annette Grasty and school district 
    representative Kathy Rouse told attendees at the 
    Lakewood Village Neighborhood Association meeting 
    in February that strangers had been using the student 
    restrooms (ignoring the signs on them), that the school 
    had been broken into and robbed of PCs, DVD players, 
    and other equipment, and that graffiti had been put on 
    the walls by strangers. They said that the school grounds 
    need to be safe for the students, and fencing (which 
    almost all of the other Sunnyvale schools have) is the 
    only affordable deterrent to trespassers. 
    Installation of the fence—chain link around the back and 
    wrought iron in the front—is slated to begin within the 
    next few months. 
    People at the meeting asked whether security cameras 
    could be used, but Kathy Rouse explained that cameras 
    are very expensive and cannot be monitored effectively 
    for preventing break-ins and other illegal activity. ■

    County Launches 2-1-1 Service 
    Santa Clara County has launched the new 2-1-1 phone 
    service, a free, non-emergency, confidential service that 
    operates 24 hours a day, seven days a week, in English, 
    Spanish, Vietnamese, Tagalog, Mandarin, and 140 other 
    languages.  
    For everyday needs and in times of crisis, the 2-1-1 
    service provides streamlined access to critical health and 
    human services such as food, shelter, counseling, drug 
    and alcohol intervention, employment assistance, and 
    more. It also serves as a link for individuals seeking to 
    volunteer, provide resources, or assist during a crisis in 
    Santa Clara County. 2-1-1 is also a valuable tool when 
    disasters strike because people can quickly and easily 
    receive critical information.  
    To get more information on this new service, visit 
    www.211scc.org, or simply call 2-1-1. ■

        March in Your Yard and Garden 
    ☼Sow seeds of summer vegetables and annuals 
    indoors. 
    ☼The bare-root season is almost over. Buy soon and 
    plant when the weather is right for planting. Complete 
    those orders for bare-root fruit trees from specialty 
    nurseries, which usually stop shipping bare-root fruit 
    trees by the end of March. 
    ☼Send in orders to seed companies before they run 
    out of stock later in spring. 
    ☼Wet weather is good for indoor gardening chores 
    such as cleaning and repairing garden tools. Do not 
    forget to sharpen the blades of pruning tools and 
    shovels, and to service the lawn mower. 
    ☼Begin weeding while the weeds are young and the 
    ground is still wet. 
    ☼The rice growers in the Sacramento Valley remove 
    the fibrous hulls from the grains. Rice hulls are an 
    excellent, inexpensive soil amendment. Contact a rice 
    growers’ cooperative to find the nearest local source. 
    ☼Fertilize roses, citrus, and all flowering plants that 
    are about to bloom. Trees, shrubs, and cool season 
    lawns could use a high-nitrogen fertilizer. 
    ☼Fertilize azaleas, camellias, and rhododendrons 
    when they finish blooming. 
    ☼Prune spring-blooming plants as they finish 
    blooming. Toward the end of the month (but before 
    new growth begins), prune back both oleanders and 
    crape myrtles to produce new flowering wood. 
    ☼Watch out for snails and earwigs. 
    ☼Use a strong spray from the hose to wash away 
    aphids. 
    ☼After the danger of frost is past, prune hydrangeas, 
    fuchsias, and bougainvilleas. 
     
     
    ☼Plant out summer bulbs such as amaryllis, callas, 
    cannas, dahlias, gladiolus, lilies, and tuberous 
    begonias. 
    These tips are from the book Northern California Gardening: A 
    Month-by-Month Guide, by Katherine Grace Endicott, published by 
    Chronicle Books, San Francisco, copyright 1996, and are used with 
    the publisher’s permission. ■
