    President’s Letter
    In my New Year’s message in the January newsletter, I shared with you the incredible progress we made as a
    community in 2024 and outlined our first set of priorities for 2025: sidewalk repairs, tree trimming and
    improved street lighting. On January 14th, 2025, I addressed the Sunnyvale City Council about these priorities.
    I stressed that sidewalk repair and tree trimming are not just about aesthetics. They are also about safety and
    sanitation. Sidewalks pushed up by tree roots are hazards, particularly for children and the elderly. When the
    trees aren’t trimmed, street lights are obscured, increasing nighttime risks. Some parts of our streets are never
    cleaned because street cleaners can no longer fit under the trees. That’s a neglect of basic sanitation.
    I have great news. As those of you who attended the February 12 LVNA general meeting know, the tree
    trimming has begun! I want to thank City Manager Tim Kirby and Mayor Larry Klein for hearing our concerns and
    quickly taking action. They’ve promised me that the trimming will continue until all the trees in Lakewood are
    trimmed. I ask all of you to keep watch to make sure this happens. Don’t hesitate to report problems with trees
    to the City of Sunnyvale at (408) 730-7506. There are also numbers listed on the back cover of this newsletter
    for street light and sidewalk issues, abandoned cars and shopping carts, and other concerns you may have.
    You can also use the Access Sunnyvale portal on the web (https://sunnyvale.dynamics365portals.us) to make
    online service requests to the city.
    I also want to thank Eileen Le, our Council Member from District 6, and Richard Mehlinger, the Council Member
    from neighboring District 5, for their support for our priorities at City Council, and for attending the February
    12 LVNA general meeting to hear concerns directly from LVNA residents. As Councilmember Mehlinger said at
    the general meeting, our concerns often overlap with those of the residents in his district. We welcome his
    assistance and look forward to working with him and our neighbors in District 5.
    Next up as our priority – and this was clear from the February 12 general meeting – is public safety. It was
    great to have Officer Joe Melo from Sunnyvale Department of Public Safety at the meeting. Officer Melo
    heard directly from residents, including some who have experienced multiple burglary attempts. Officer Melo
    explained that the city has had limited ability to cover such a wide geographic area, but three more positions
    have been recently approved, which should improve things. Officer Melo encouraged us to keep our homes
    well lit and discussed the effectiveness of motion lighting. He also suggested we get to know our neighbors to
    look out for one another and be aware of suspicious activity and/or expand our pilot program of
    Neighborhood Watch.
    Lakewood Village is an amazing community of over 1600 households. Together we are making our community
    safer and more beautiful, friendlier and more neighborly. Stay tuned for future updates. And please monitor
    progress on tree trimming, sidewalk repair, and public safety issues. Help us on the LVNA Board help you by
    holding our good friends at the City of Sunnyvale accountable to keep their promises.
    Rick Flovin
    President, Lakewood Village Neighborhood Association

    It's a familiar sight for many of us in the neighborhood—shopping carts abandoned on sidewalks, street
    corners, and even in front yards or on the Calabazas Trail or in the creek bordering our neighborhood. While it
    might seem like a minor nuisance, these stray carts pose several challenges to our community's safety and
    aesthetics.
    Why Are Shopping Carts Left Behind?
    Abandoned shopping carts often result from residents using them to transport groceries or other items home,
    especially for those without access to vehicles. However, once the carts serve their purpose, they're often left
    behind rather than being returned to the store.
    The Impact on Our Community
    Safety Hazards: Carts left on sidewalks can obstruct pedestrians, including those with strollers,
    wheelchairs, or mobility aids. When left in streets, they become road hazards for drivers and
    cyclists.
    Aesthetic Concerns: Stray carts can make our otherwise beautiful neighborhood appear cluttered and
    unkept.
    Environmental Impact: Over time, abandoned carts can deteriorate, contributing to unnecessary waste
    and rust runoff.
    Costly for our Precious Local Retailers: Replacing stolen carts is an additional cost for our local
    retailers that we will ultimately pay the price for.
    How You Can Help
    Report Abandoned Carts: If you spot a cart in the neighborhood, call the grocery cart/graffiti hotline at
    (408) 730-7680 for cart retrieval and let them know the specifics of where the cart or carts are located
    and what retailer they belong to. They should respond within 72 hours. I've called this number multiple
    times and it really works!
    Spread the Word: Share this message with friends and neighbors to raise awareness.
    Volunteer: Join or start a neighborhood effort to address the issue collaboratively.
    Let's work together to keep our neighborhood safe, clean, and welcoming for everyone. Small actions, like
    returning a cart or reporting an abandoned one, can make a big difference. Together, we can ensure our
    streets remain cart-free and community-friendly!

    and the next generation who’s currently growing up right outside your door or even in your own
    backyard?

    President, Treasurer, and Secretary. The current board’s positions end in April and a new board will
    begin. The board meets once per month to discuss ongoing activities, in addition to holding quarterly
