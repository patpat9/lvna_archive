    VOLUNTEERING FOR LVNA
    Are you passionate about our neighborhood? Do you enjoy meeting new neighbors? Would you be overjoyed 
    to help build a tight-knit (Lakewood) Village for yourself, your friends, and the next generation who’s currently 
    growing up right outside your door or even in your own backyard?
    Perhaps you should consider running for one of the five LVNA board positions. We have 2 members who will 
    be reaching their maximum quota of 3 consecutive years on the board and will be stepping down this March. 
    The board meets once per month to discuss ongoing activities, in addition to the monthly general meeting 
    where we invite guest speakers to discuss topics of interest for the neighborhood (currently still virtual due to 
    COVID) and the few seasonal events each year. The level of commitment is up to each individual – as these are 
    all completely volunteer roles. All of the incumbent board members are happy to shuffle around, so, any of the 
    5 roles are available for the 2022-2023 term (March 2022 – March 2023). The only requirements are that you are 
    18+ and live in the neighborhood, and plan to for the duration of the term.
    President, 1st VP, 2nd VP, Treasurer, and Secretary 
    Want to help, but not interested in a board position? Our Villager editors will be stepping down in March so 
    the role of Villager Editor is open to anyone interested (no age requirement). At a minimum, the role consists 
    of proof-reading the content and compiling it all into a single document. The sky is the limit though so the 
    previous editors have helped write some of that content, design a new layout, take photos for fun backgrounds, 
    and send it out to the Villager subscribers each month. The board is happy to help with these other aspects 
    though so you have as much creative freedom as you’d like.
    Want to partner up with a family member or friend? Great! Are you a high school student interested in writing or 
    media design and trying to increase your community service hours to meet a graduation or Scouts requirement? 
    This is your chance to feed a couple ducks with a single loaf of bread! (See how much fun this role can be? You 
    can make up your own idioms if you don’t like killing birds with stones.)
    Any of these roles are a great opportunity to help actively drive change for our community and make a positive 
    impact on our neighborhood. If interested, please email the board and let them know you’re on board (puns – 
    love it)!

    SUNNYVALE TO BUY VACANT LOTS
    Thanks to the perseverance of our 
    neighbor, Tony Bugna, this month, the 
    City of Sunnyvale unanimously voted 
    to authorize a $1.13 million purchase 
    agreement for the two parcels of land 
    on the 1100 block of Manzano Way. 
    Located across the street from the 
    end of Fairwood Park, the 6,000- and 
    8,817-square foot lots are currently 
    owned by the San Francisco Public 
    Utilities Commission (SFPUC). Tony 
    has been pushing for Sunnyvale to 
    buy this land for about three years. 
    “The realtor who had the listing said it would never happen,” Tony said. 
    In 2019, Tony presented the idea to Mayor Klein, who said he said he would look into it. When Tony saw the 
    issue was coming up for a vote at the January 4th City Council meeting, he posted on the Nextdoor app 
    asking neighbors to send letters to the City Manager and City Council about why this land is important to our 
    neighborhood. And he spoke in support of the purchase at the meeting, explaining, “It’s better that Sunnyvale 
    have control of the land.” 
    Land Purchase Agreement Represents Win for North Sunnyvale
    There are benefits from this land purchase. One is that the location is a key piece in Sunnyvale’s Active 
    Transportation Plan. It provides access to the bike-pedestrian pathway on the Calabazas Creek Trail managed 
    by Valley Water. 
    Another is that it represents a “win” for North Sunnyvale. For decades, when it came to allocating financial 
    resources, Lakewood Village was at the bottom of the City of Sunnyvale’s list. In fact, that’s one of the reasons 
    why neighbors banded together in the early 1990’s to revive the LVNA – to give us a voice in how our tax dollars 
    were being spent.
    We’ve come a long way since Fairwood Park was a neglected dirt lot full of weeds and trash next to buildings 
    that had fallen into disrepair when Fairwood 
    Elementary School closed. But even after all 
    these years of advocating for our neighborhood, 
    at this month’s City Council meeting, one 
    councilman questioned why this much money 
    was being spent on a plot of land “on the edge 
    of Sunnyvale” when there are more urgent 
    needs downtown. Another referred to “Far 
    North Sunnyvale” – almost as if residents on the 
    other side of 101 live in a different city. 
    Don’t Expect to See Landscaping Anytime 
    Soon 
    Before anything can be done to the two plots, 
    the purchase agreement has to be approved by 
    the SFPUC. That could take anywhere from three to five months. Once Sunnyvale owns the land, it can submit 
    the plots into the capital cycle to fund landscaping. However, the Council made it clear that turning this land 
    into a mini park is not set in stone. For example, it could be used as a bargaining chip in a future land swap.
    Even if the land continues to be a vacant strip that’s perfect for running RC cars for a few more years, this 
    purchase called attention to the necessity of allocating resources to North Sunnyvale. And that happened 
    because, as Mayor Larry Klein said at the end of the council meeting, “Tony didn’t give up.”

    MEET YOUR BOARD MEMBER
    BEV WOIDYLA
    Bev is the treasurer of the Lakewood Village 
    Neighborhood Association.
    Q: Tell me a bit about yourself and your family.
    Bev: I am a retired RN & live with my daughter Robin 
    & cat Brownie.
    Q: When did you move to the neighborhood?
    Bev: April 1981
    Q: Where do you live?
    Bev: I live on the Fairwood side of Sandia Ave.
    Q: Name one thing you see out of your kitchen window.
    Bev: Neighbor’s fence
    Q: Something that is in your front yard?
    Bev: Flowers, a lemon tree & a big tree which has a 
    squirrel’s nest where the squirrels love playing on my 
    front lawn
    Q: A sound you hear frequently in the neighborhood?
    Bev: Traffic
    Q: What is something you like about living in Lakewood/
    Fairwood and something that has changed since you 
    moved to the neighborhood?
    Bev: I like the location. The biggest change is that the 
    Mercado shopping center used to be an open field.
    Q: What are your favorite parks/things to do in Sunnyvale 
    and/or the Bay Area?
    Bev: I don’t have a favorite park. I enjoy bowling, riding 
    my bike in the neighborhood, going to art & craft fairs, 
    and driving to Monterey, Santa Cruz, or Half Moon Bay. 
    Q: Tell me a bit about your hobbies.
    Bev: I enjoy doing puzzles and working on my family tree. 
    I also volunteer with AARP doing taxes, SCS (Sunnyvale 
    Community Services), & LVNA. I’m not sure those are 
    hobbies but it keeps me busy.
    Q: Any great stories to share about living in the 
    neighborhood?
    Bev: When I first took a position on LVNA as treasurer, 
    I was assigned to coordinate the Christmas parade 
    from Lakewood School to Fairwood Park. I was given a 
    list of about 50 people to call. I started calling (with a 
    friend) all the numbers on the list, not having any idea 
    what we were doing. The end product meant that our 
    annual parade had 45-50 units from the color guard, 
    bands, choruses, Boy & Girl Scouts, council members, 
    ham operators, elementary schools, ugliest dog, antique 
    cars, and Santa Claus, plus many others. All of that was 
    followed by hot chocolate or cider, cookies, and a visit 
    from Santa at Fairwood Park. The end product was a 
    success, especially since I didn’t know what I was doing.
    Q: How did you get involved with the LVNA initially?
    Bev: I’ve been a walker for the Villager for about 20 years 
    and coordinated the walkers on the Fairwood side for 15 
    years. I was also a dumpster monitor for about 15 years 
    and I co-coordinated the last 3 events. One of the board 
    members was moving out of the neighborhood and she 
    asked if I would take her place as treasurer. I said yes. 
    Over the years, I’ve been treasurer, secretary & president 
    for 7 board member years
    Q: Why did you want to be treasurer this year?
    Bev: I didn’t mind what position I took, although I didn’t 
    want to be president. I didn’t feel I had the tech savvy 
    knowledge to work effectively.

    BEAUTIFYING SUNNYVALE
    Sunnyvale’s Environmental Services Department (ESD) is in the process of applying for the Clean California 
    grant from Caltrans. Caltrans’ operations have been significantly impacted by the amounts of litter in public 
    spaces and roads and looking for initiatives that will help build a cleaner California. Lakewood Village is one 
    of the targeted areas for this grant so ESD is seeking input from residents on projects that would improve the 
    cleanliness of our neighborhood.
    Goals of this project are to:
    •	
    Reduce the amount of waste and debris within public rights-of-way, pathways, parks, transit centers, and 
    other public spaces
    •	
    Enhance, rehabilitate, restore, or install measures to beautify and improve public spaces and mitigate the 
    urban heat island effect
    •	
    Enhance public health, cultural connections, and community placemaking by improving public spaces for 
    walking and recreation
    •	
    Advance equity for underserved communities
    Examples of eligible features within the project area include (but are not necessarily limited to):
    •	
    Enhanced paving to address the needs of the space, using permeable pavements where possible
    •	
    Pavement alternatives, such as wood chips
    •	
    Inert materials, such as rubberized pavement
    •	
    Shade structures
    •	
    Shade trees or drought-tolerant plantings
    •	
    Irrigation systems
    •	
    Bioretention, swales, and other green street elements
    •	
    Architectural fencing
    •	
    Art installations, including community identification or cultural elements
    •	
    Gateway Monuments and Community Identification projects. If these are on Caltrans right-of-way, they have 
    different requirements than art installations
    •	
    Human-scale, energy-conserving lighting
    •	
    Walking and/or biking facilities through the space
    •	
    Wayfinding signage
    •	
    Amenities related to proper waste collection or prevention (e.g., signage, bins, etc.)
    Potential Project Ideas:
    •	
    Trash capture devices
    •	
    Green Stormwater Infrastructure projects
    •	
    Tree plantings
    •	
    Pet waste stations added in key locations (e.g., parks or John W. Christian Greenbelt)
    •	
    Beautification of transit stops or streetscape (e.g., utility box art, bus shelter art, etc.)
    •	
    Playground improvements
    •	
    Educational signage about pet waste, stormwater, pollution prevention, etc.
    An art installation should:
    •	
    Be appropriate and safe to its proposed setting
    •	
    Be in proper scale with its surroundings
    •	
    Be composed of materials that are durable for the projected lifespan
    •	
    Include graphics or sculptural artwork that express unique attributes of an area’s history, resources, 
    or character
    •	
    Not display symbols or icons, including but not limited to, logos or political and commercial 
    symbols. Sponsor recognition should be unobtrusive, discreet, and not appear to be an integral 
    part of the artwork
    •	
    Not display text
    •	
    Be designed to minimize ongoing maintenance needs

    MENTAL HEALTH AWARENESS
    We wanted to remind our neighbors of some important mental health resources.
    If you are experiencing depression and suicidal ideations, please reach out to someone. Every life has value, no 
    matter your background, age, race, gender identity, disability, religion, or sexual orientation. Talk to a friend, 
    family member, or a stranger. You are worth it!
    According to the CDC, suicide is the 10th leading cause of death in the United States. More than 47,500 
    people died by suicide in 2019, and more than 12 million seriously thought about suicide. If you notice signs 
    of depression in a friend or loved one, reach out to them. A difficult conversation now is much better than a 
    potentially tragic alternative.
    Free and confidential 24/7 support available through:
    •	
    National Suicide Prevention Lifeline: 1-800-273-TALK (1-800-273-8255)
    •	
    Crisis Text Line: Text “BAY” to 741741 to connect to a trained counselor
