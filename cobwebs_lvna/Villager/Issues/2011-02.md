    A  Special Valentine Day Dinner 
    By Phyllis Fowler 
    In honor of Valentine‟s Day, I thought I 
    would share with everybody one of my 
    favorite recipes. Fred and I took a 
    cooking class from a Cordon Blue trained chef called 
    “Cooking With Friends.” The group was split in teams and 
    each team prepared a different course. Fred and I were 
    assigned the entrée. We enjoyed the salmon so much, we 
    have made several times. I found that I can make up the 
    salmon packets the night before and then just pop them in 
    the oven. This had made it one of my favorites to serve for 
    dinner parties! If you have too many packets left, don‟t 
    worry, go ahead and cook them. The next day have them for 
    lunch. Just pop them in the microwave for 2 minutes and 
    voila! 
    Salmon in Parchment 
    Fish filets, such as salmon, snapper, cod, and halibut 
    Cherry tomatoes 
    Large mushrooms, sliced 
    Zucchini or summer squash, sliced 
    Asparagus tips, blanched 
    Baby carrots, blanched 
    Shallots, or scallions, minced 
    Dry white wine  
    Butter 
    Salt and peeper 
    Sprigs of fresh herbs such as basil, tarragon or dill 
     
    For each person, you will need a large piece of cooking 
    parchment or foil. The number of filets and amount of 
    vegetables you will need will depend on the number of 
    people you are serving. A nice 
    assortment of colorful vegetables 
    is best. 
     
    Fold the piece of parchment in 
    half and butter the bottom half. 
    On it, place some minced 
    shallots or scallions, and place 
    the fish on top. Gather the vegetables of choice and place 
    them on top of the filet. Sprinkle with some dry white wine, 
    dot with butter then salt and pepper, and then finish with a 
    sprig of fresh herb. Fold the paper over and cut so that there 
    is a 1” margin around the fish. Crimp the edges so that there 
    are no air holes. 
     
    Tem minutes before you are ready to serve the fish, place 
    them on a baking sheet in a preheated 450 degree oven for 
    10 minutes, or until the fish goes from translucent to just 
    opaque. Serve immediately on individual plates in the paper. 
    Serve with the citrus balsamic vinaigrette.

    The Origins of St. Valentine’s Day  
    by Phyllis Fowler 
    February 14 is known as the day we celebrate love. The 
    story of Valentine‟s Day began during the Roman Empire, 
    which held a festival every February in honor of the God of 
    Fertility.  During this time young men would choose their 
    wife. Emperor Claudius changed all of that. Fearing that 
    men would refuse their duty to fight because they would 
    not want to leave their wives behind, he outlawed all 
    marriages. Young couples still fell in love and wished to 
    marry.  They went to Catholic Bishop Valentine, he began 
    to secretly marry couples. When Claudius found out, he 
    had Valentine arrested and ordered put to death. While 
    waiting in jail, Valentine began exchanging letters with the 
    jailer‟s daughter and fell in love with her. The day he was 
    to be beheaded, he wrote her one last note, signed “From 
    Your Valentine‟ 
    In 496 A.D. Pope Gelasius outlawed the pagan Festival, 
    knowing its popularity, he replaced it with something more 
    „appropriate‟ to honor the martyr St. Valentine. In 1969 the 
    church removed St. Valentine‟s Day from its „official‟ 
    holidays. However,  it is still widely celebrated today.  
    Valentine‟s Day is perhaps best known for its cards which 
    have been around in one form or another for hundreds of 
    years In the early 1870‟s a 19-year-old American woman, 
    Esther Howland, started out with three friends and her 
    brother. In assembly line fashion began producing hand-
    crafted cards which were shipped around the country. By 
    the end of the decade she was making over 100,000$ a 
    year.  
    Besides giving cards, candy and kisses there are some 
    other interesting Valentine‟s Day customs. For instance, in 
    England children take to the street, singing songs and 
    passing out cards. In Denmark people give pressed white 
    flowers as a sign of their affection. In the 1700‟s, English 
    women would write the names of men on small pieces of 
    paper and then roll the paper up inside little balls of clay. 
    They threw the balls into the water and the first paper that 
    popped to the surface was the one with the name of the 
    woman‟s true love. In the Middle Ages men would pull a 
    woman‟s name out of a bowl. This would be the man‟s 
    Valentine. To show his devotion, the man would wear the 
    paper containing the woman‟s name on his sleeve for a 
    week. This soon became known as „wearing your heart on 
    your sleeve’. So go out, find your Valentine and wear your 
    heart on your sleeve! 

    SKILL SETS

    LVNA?  That answer is hard to pin down.  It varies, 
    because everyone who has held the post brings a different 
    set of skills to the job. 
    It doesn't hurt to be a good and/or confident writer.  One 
    way we keep in touch with each other is through the 
    newsletter you are reading.  I'm lucky 
    enough to be able to write fairly well, and so 
    I find I'm comfortable expressing myself in 
    these pages.  If there is a problem or issue 
    in Lakewood Village, I tend to write about it.  
    That doesn't mean that writing is the only 
    way to communicate.  Other Presidents 
    have used the monthly meetings to 
    accomplish the same kinds of things by 
    speaking rather than writing.  Still others 
    have used the telephone.  Being able to communicate is 
    very important in this job, but the way the communication is 
    done can vary. 
    Another important skill or characteristic is to have a 
    “positive” personality.  You have to be the kind of person 
    who doesn't just see the glass as half full rather than half 
    empty, you have to be the kind of person who sees a glass 
    with just a few drops of water in it and says “wow!  That's 
    GREAT!  It isn't bone dry!” 
    The LVNA is an organization that is powered by volunteers, 
    and volunteers will only sacrifice their time and energy for 
    the gratification of feeling they've been successful in 
    helping a good cause.  Negative attitudes and negative 
    messages can kill those feelings, and without those 
    feelings the LVNA will have no volunteers. 
    We're very blessed this year to have had more than 60

    people deserve to feel good about what they have done.  
    The LVNA President has to be the head cheerleader for all

    PERSONAL REWARDS 
    So what can someone like you get out of being President of the 
    Lakewood Village Neighborhood Association?  That depends 
    on what you want to get out of it. 
    Some Presidents go on to other areas of public service.  
    Jessica Lee went on to become President of the Sunnyvale 
    School District Board of Trustees.  LVNA President Vicki 
    Piazza became chair of the Sunnyvale Planning Commission.  
    And some joker named Fred Fowler ended up being Mayor of 
    Sunnyvale. 
     
    Other Presidents go back to more “normal” lives.  LVNA 
    President Rick Flovin opened a chain of medical clinics with his 
    wife Rena.  LVNA President Michael Jenisse got married and 
    moved to St. Louis.  LVNA President Craig Crawford and his 
    wife Leah retired and moved to Alaska. 
    What you get out of it is what you put into it. 
    What I got out of it was a lack of fear of those “open 
    ended commitments”.  I'm not afraid of them anymore.  
    I'm willing to make them because I took the risk and 
    found it was a risk I could manage.  Part of the reason 
    I've been successful in public service, (as mayor) and 
    in the private sector (as Vice President and CIO of a 
    company in Morgan Hill) is because of the people who 
    work at those levels are the ones who can deal with those kinds 
    of risks.  It is fair to say that I wouldn't be where I am today 
    without my stint as President of the LVNA.. 
    ALL IT TAKES IS... 
    So what does it take?  All it takes is that you care.  All it takes is 
    that the success or failure of the LVNA makes a difference to 
    you personally. 
    In March of 2009 there was no-one elected President of the 
    LVNA.  Nine months later, I stepped in because the LVNA 
    parade was about to be canceled.  I'm convinced that if the 
    parade had been canceled then Dumpster Day, the picnic, the 
    monthly meeting and all of the other LVNA activities would 
    have soon followed the parade into oblivion. 
    If no one steps forward this time there may be no LVNA before 
    long. We‟re still pretty close to the edge right now. We may still 
    go over the cliff. 
    Will you let that happen? Do you care?   

    California Traffic Fines Have Gone Up! 
     
    By Phyllis Fowler 
     
    With the change of the new year, comes 
    new laws and an increase in the 
    penalties for disobeying the law. 
     A new law sponsored by State Senator 
    Joe Simitian, makes texting while driving 
    illegal and the fine starts at $80. All I can 
    say is “Thank you Joe,” too many 
    accidents have been caused by idiots 
    reading or sending text messages! 
    Please keep your eye on the road not 
    your cell phone! Driving while using a 
    wireless phone that is not hands-free is 
    now also up to $80.  If you have any 
    young drivers at home, remind them to drive not text or talk. 
    If you move, make sure you report it to the DMV within 10 
    days or you can be fined $154 for your driver‟s license and 
    $114 for your car registration.  When you come to a stop 
    sign, come to a complete stop. Don‟t roll on through or else 
    it will cost you $154 
    The price for running a red light has gone up to a 
    whopping $400.00  Passing a school bus with flashing red 
    signals is not safe for our children. Now it will now cost 
    those rude drivers $600. (maybe they will pay attention to 
    their pocketbooks!) Speaking of children, remember to use 
    the passenger child restraints or you could be fined a minimum 
    of $400! 
    If you are tempted to park in a Handicap parking space, even 
    for “a few minutes”, DON”T.  The first offense will cost you 
    $1,000 and the second time you are caught the fine doubles! 
    You will pay $1,000 for parking in a bus loading area as well. 
    Keep in mind that these amounts I am quoting are set by the 
    state and the cost can vary county by county. In other words, 
    think of this as the minimum due! So drive safely and keep your 
    money in your own pocket.
