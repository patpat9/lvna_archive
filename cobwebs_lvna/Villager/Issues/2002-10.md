    These questions and many more like them are 
    answered in email messages on the Internet – 
    and they’re all hoaxes. Email hoaxes often prey 
    upon people’s fears or hopes, on their concern 
    for others, or sometimes on their greed. At 
    best, t hey spread fear and misinformation; at 
    worst, they ’re scams . Here’s how to detect 
    email hoaxes and stop them in their tracks: 
     
    First, give it a sanity test. Email hoaxes usually 
    have several characteristics  in common. If you 
    get an email with one or more of these, you 
    should suspect that it is a hoax:  
     
    Ø It says that it is really important, a “must 
    read”, urgent messag e that you should 
    read right now. 
    Ø It doesn’t relate first -hand experience, but 
    instead says that a friend of a friend or 
    someone’s cousin had it happen to them or 
    otherwise knows that it’s true. 
    Ø It cites some sort of large authority, like 
    saying that it wa s on CNN, or Microsoft 
    announced it on their web site, or the US 
    Government has reported it. 
    Ø It urges you to forward a copy of the 
    message to everyone you know.  
    Ø If you think about it for a few minutes, it 
    really seems unbelievable, but it still 
    captures yo ur interest “just in case it’s 
    true”. 
     
    Second, go to one of these  authorities  on 
    hoaxes and urban legends: 
     
    http://www.UrbanLegends.com 
    http://www.TruthOrFiction.com 
    http://www.BreakTheChain.org 
     
    Look at the most recent listings or search for a 
    few key words that you find in the email, and 
    you’ll probably find out that the e mail you 
    received is indeed a hoax. 
     
    If it’s a hoax, don’t forward it
    ! 
     
    What you do after  that is up to you. What I 
    usually do is that I send a polite reply to the 
    person who forwarded it to me and I explain 
    things in much the same way I’ve explained 
    them in this article. 
     
    This is really important. Clip out this article and 
    send it to everyone you know. J  ¢

    Dog: Man’s Best Friend, 
    or… What Did I Ste p In? 
    - Craig Crawford 
     
    When was the last time that you were sleeping 
    soundly, only to be awakened by your 
    neighbor’s dog barking uncontrollably? For me 
    it was last week. I tried to put a pillow over my 
    head, but it was during the hot spell and the 
    windows were open so that we could breath. 
    This did not work; I started to sweat, so off 
    came the pillow. I lay in bed thinking bad 
    thoughts about why would someone be so rude 
    that they would let a dog bark and wake up the 
    neighborhood?  I have to get up at 5:45a m 
    and sleep is a precious thing to me. Soon, I 
    was angry and the more the dog barked, the 
    madder I became. Then came a knock at the 
    bedroom door: it was my youngest daughter 
    saying that the dog barking woke her up and 
    she has to get up early for school. By  this time, 
    I was livid. I pulled my pants on and grabbed 
    my cell phone and was heading to the house 
    behind me for a confrontation with the rude 
    neighbors.  Making a long story short: the dog 
    stopped barking and I was able to get back to 
    sleep and the dog has not barked since.  
     
    As I was jogging on the turf beside the bike 
    path, I felt that mushy feeling of stepping on a 
    big, steaming pile of dog excrement. The putrid 
    smell filling my nostrils could only mean one 
    thing: someone had taken their dog for a wal k 
    and let them use the wonderful green turf as 
    their own doggie bathroom. This is one the 
    most irresponsible thing that a dog owner can 
    do.  Take a walk down the bike path and look 
    at the waste that these so -called responsible 
    dog owners leave. If you take  your dog for a 
    walk, slip a plastic garbage bag in your pocket 
    and pick up after your dog. If you see this 
    behavior, let the dog owner know that they are 
    breaking the law and that if they don’t pick it 
    up authorities will be notified. 
     
    I usually walk my d og several time each week. 
    Actually, he walks me. But about once each 
    week, a dog off leash will try to start a fight 
    with my dog and me. I carry a cell phone and 
    will not hesitate to call Public Safety about a 
    dog off leash. Leaving your dog off leash is not

    with people and children all over the place; 
    dogs have to be under control at all times. Last 
    week a pit bull had pinned a young girl walking

    terrifying experience: do you try to protect
