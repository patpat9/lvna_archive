    President’s Column 
     
     
    -Fred Fowler 
    Greetings!  In just a few days the 
    Lakewood Village Neighborhood 
    Association will be staging the 
    2010 edition of one of our most 
    beloved events, Dumpster Day!  
    At least 19 Dumpsters will be   
    delivered to the neighborhood and 
    will be open for business on     
    Saturday, July 17th.with dozens of     
    volunteers manning them.  We'll be 
    devoting our July LVNA general 
    meeting to getting everyone organized and prepared.  Come 
    to the Lakewood Park building on Wednesday, July 14th to 
    get the low-down on where the dumpsters will be placed, what 
    will be allowed to be put in them and how long they will stay. 
    LVNA 2nd Vice President Skip Gottschalk has done a        
    wonderful job putting the event together this year.  He has 
    coordinated with the City of Sunnyvale to make sure the 
    dumpsters are placed in good locations.  He has recruited 
    and/or coordinated a small army of dumpster monitors to 
    watch over them and make sure they are not abused.  He has 
    also arranged for City of Sunnyvale Neighborhood Preserva-
    tion Specialist Johanne Navarro-Tran and (hopefully) a Neigh-
    borhood Resource Officer to come speak to us on July 14th.  
    Johanne and the NRO will go over the rules for what is al-
    lowed in the dumpsters this year and what must be kept out! 
    The NRO will also give some tips and advice as to how to 
    handle any ―tense‖ situations, such as when someone who 
    wants to dump something illegal is told ―No‖. 
    The LVNA will be providing sandwiches on the 17th to any 
    volunteer who wants one.  There will be a sign-up sheet for 
    the sandwiches at the July 14th meeting.  If you want a     
    sandwich, please come to the meeting and sign up!  (We are 
    getting a special deal from Togo's on Lawrence Expressway 
    and have to get them a firm list ahead of time.) 
    The LVNA Dumpster Day is the oldest and most successful 
    neighborhood clean-up event in Sunnyvale.  This year will 
    mark its 20th anniversary!  In those 20 years, because of 
    Dumpster Days,  we've removed a little more than 4,000 tons 
    of junk, trash and other refuse from our houses, garages and 
    back yards.  Let's keep up the good work!  Come to the LVNA 
    meeting on July 14th and get informed.  I'll see you there! 

    Dumpster Day Drivers Needed! 
    By Fred Fowler 
    Calling all pickup truck and/or utility 
    van owners!  The LVNA is looking for 
    some volunteers with vehicles who 
    can collect discarded Dumpster Day 
    items in good condition and take 
    them to our ―garage sale‖ site in the 
    Lakewood Shopping Center. 
    Every Dumpster Day there are new 
    or nearly-new items that are rescued from their trip to the landfill and 
    set aside to be sold.  I personally have seen brand new bicycles, 
    exercise equipment, golf clubs, usable power tools, functional 
    personal computers and even a $200 women's leather jacket pulled 
    from the dumpster.  As these things accumulate they need to be 
    collected and delivered to the volunteers near the Shell station where 
    they are offered for sale.  In years past, the LVNA has made 
    hundreds of dollars from the proceeds of these sales. 
    This year we need some help collecting and delivering these goods.  
    Two key volunteers who have done this work in the past are not 
    available this time.  We need some fresh faces to step up. 
    If you are interested, please contact Dumpster Day Coordinator 
    Skip Gottschalk at the following e-mail address:  
    gottschalk.ralph@ssd.loral.com.   
    If you prefer, you can call my cell 
    phone at (408) 505-2813.  Please 
    respond!  The neighborhood needs 
    you.  Thanks in advance for making 
    this contribution to Dumpster Day. 

    Newsletter Distribution Coordinator Needed 
    By Fred Fowler 
    The LVNA has a tradition of hand-delivering The LVNA Villager 
    newsletter.  Each month a crew of almost 30 volunteers        
    distribute copies of it door to door in every part of our     neigh-
    borhood.  We reach 1,600 homes with a 6 page document that 
    often contains news about the neighborhood that is simply not 
    available from any other source.  The overall distribution of The 
    LVNA Villager is coordinated by two volunteers, one for each 
    side of the neighborhood.  We'll soon need a new coordinator for 
    the  Fairwood side, as the  current volunteer has been doing it 
    for a long time. 
    We distribute The LVNA Villager by hand for two reasons.  First, 
    it would cost close to $800 per month in postage alone to send 
    the newsletter out by mail.  Second, it gives us a way to        
    disseminate information in an emergency.  If the ―Big One‖ ever 
    hits Lakewood Village, our network of newsletter walkers will be 
    very important for surveying and reporting damage, as well as 
    passing out emergency relief information.  That network of   
    walkers is important for our safety as well as for handing out the 
    newsletter each month. 
    The Lakewood and Fairwood sides of the neighborhood each 
    have a coordinator to support the walkers.  The newsletter editor 
    picks up the finished newsletters from the printer and splits them 
    between the Lakewood and  Fairwood coordinators.  The Lake-
    wood coordinator is Wendy Stanley.  She takes 800 copies of 
    The LVNA Villager and passes them out to the walkers on the 
    Lakewood side.  The Fairwood coordinator is Bev Woidyla.  She 
    passes out her 800 copies to the walkers on the Fairwood side. 
    Both Bev and Wendy have done a great job of  coordinating their 
    walkers for a long time.  Bev has been doing it for four years 
    now, and she has given notice that she would like us to find 
    someone to take over for her.  She will continue to coordinate 
    Fairwood distribution for the July and August editions of The 
    LVNA Village, but we need someone else to handle the chore 
    starting with the September issue. 
    Phyllis and I coordinated the Fairwood side walkers back in the 
    1990s, and the workload wasn't too heavy.  Once per month we 
    received a stack of 800 newsletters, and we had a list of walkers 
    and the streets they covered.  Our job was to count out the  
    number of newsletters each walker needed, and to drive around 
    Fairwood delivering the newsletter stacks to those volunteers.  It 
    took about 2 –3  hours on a Saturday morning, once per month. 
    Does someone out there have 2 - 3 hours once per month that 
    you can spare?  The job isn't very complicated, but it is very 
    important.  Without you, we may not be able to keep at least 800 
    households informed as to what is happening in the Village.   
    Please e-mail me (fred@fmfa.com) or call me at (408) 505-2813. 
    By the way, the job will not be a thankless job.  Last March, Bev 
    Woidyla received certificates of commendation from Mayor 
    Melinda Hamilton, Assembly member Paul Fong and            
    Congresswoman Anna Eshoo, all thanking her for her work on 
    behalf of Lakewood Village.  If you agree to take this on and do 
    a good job with it, I'll do my darnedest to make sure your name 
    is nominated for those certificates next time!    It shouldn’t  be 
    too hard, those publice officials like and respect the LVNA and 
    love to express the public’s gratitude for the  LVNA volunteers’ 
    work. 
    What are yo waiting for?  Why don’t you ―throw your hat in the 
    ring?‖  Please e-mail me (fred@fmfa.com) or call me at (408) 
    505-2813.  I’m looking forward to hearing from you soon!  
    Just around the corner:  The LVNA Picnic! 
    By Fred Fowler 
    Hello neighbors.  It is July, and the year is flying by!  It wasn't long 
    ago that our elections were held and the 2010 Association year 
    got started.  We're now less than two weeks away from holding 
    our 20th annual Dumpster Day event!  What's more, it isn't too 
    early to start working on the other big events we stage every year.  
    The next big push will be for our neighborhood picnic, scheduled 
    for Lakewood Park picnic area on September 25th.   Lakewood 
    Village needs an energetic and selfless individual to step forward 
    and take charge of the planning and organization of the event. 
    PICNIC PREPARATIONS 
    The annual summer picnic has been an LVNA tradition for more 
    than 20 years.  Sometime in late summer or early fall, the picnic 
    area is reserved and food is prepared for whoever wishes to 
    come and share it.  In the past we've had a DJ with music, a  
    piñata for the kids, raffles, door prizes, and lots and lots of food.  
    We've generally gone out to local businesses and asked for food 
    donations.  We've also asked neighbors to bring their own dishes 
    to the party, pot luck style.  The last time I was president of the 
    LVNA (1997) we ended up feeding 300+ people with nothing but 
    donated food.  It was a great way to come together as neighbors 
    and celebrate life in our nice little corner of Sunnyvale. 
    Just like every other big, complicated event we do, the annual 
    picnic needs a Coordinator to make sure it comes off smoothly.  
    Someone has to make sure that all of the various ―ducks‖ get in 
    line properly so that when people show up, there is actually some 
    food for them to eat!  Someone has to be thinking about all of the 
    details, such as who will do the cooking, how the donated food 
    will be stored and how the picnic area will be cleaned up after the 
    event is over.  
    Organizing the picnic falls into two phases.  The first phase, prep-
    aration, involves asking volunteers to sign up for tasks and asking 
    businesses for contributions. (Cont. page 5)

    Sunnyvale City Council, and I invited State Assembly Member 
    Mike Honda to enjoy the picnic with us.  (Mike was running for 
    the Congressional district that contained Lakewood Village at the 
    time.)  Mike came to the picnic and had a ball!  He danced the 
    ―YMCA‖ with the kids, talked with many of the ―old timers‖ in the 
    neighborhood and was very impressed with the sense of 
    community he found here. A few weeks later Vice President Al 
    Gore contacted Mike and said he would like to make a campaign 
    stop in Silicon Valley to help both Mike's and his own election 
    campaign.  Mike Honda told Al Gore in no uncertain terms that 
    the campaign event had to be held in Lakewood Village!  And so 
    it came about that on September 20th, 2000, the Vice President of 
    the United States paid a visit to Lakewood Park.  It is important to 
    note that he did not wish to visit just Sunnyvale.  The Sunnyvale 
    Public Safety department tried and failed to get the venue 
    switched to the Baylands Park, which would have been easier to 
    manage from a security point of view.  The Gore campaign 
    insisted that the event be held in Lakewood Park because of what 
    Mike Honda had told them about the LVNA picnic. 
    I'm not suggesting that I expect the upcoming picnic to be so 
    successful that it draws a visit from Barak Obama.  I'm just saying 
    that the  picnic is very important and can have beneficial side 
    effects that are both wonderful and unpredictable.  We need to 
    keep this neighborhood tradition going and build upon our 
    previous success.  In order to do so, we need a selfless volunteer 
    to join the long line of other selfless volunteers who have made a

    time.
