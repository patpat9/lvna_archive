    - Ursula Syrova, LVNA President 
     
    Dumpster Day Report 
    Dumpster Day was a great success this year—
    we cleared out almost 171 tons of debris from 
    our neighborhood!  Fifty-nine loads of material 
    and 33 tires were collected. For the most part 
    the day went smoothly, although there were a 
    few incidents where monitors were hassled or 
    treated poorly. I would like to stress again that 
    the Dumpster monitors are volunteers and are 
    only trying to enforce the rules for dumping. All 
    the Dumpster monitors deserve our thanks and 
    respect. 
     
    This year we also had, for the first time, the 
    Dumpster Day Garage Sale. Huge thanks go to 
    Judy and Gary Fernandez for organizing and 
    running the sale, which saved a lot of material 
    from the landfill, as well as raising more than 
    $400 for the Lakewood Village Neighborhood 
    Association. These funds help us pay for the 
    events we host every year, such as the summer 
    picnic (coming up on September 25, 2004), the 
    ice cream social on National Night Out, and the 
    holiday parade. After the garage sale, we also 
    donated one pickup-truck load of stuff to the 
    Santa Maria Urban Mission. Many thanks also to 
    Gene Yee, the owner of the Lakewood Village 
    shopping center, for allowing us to use part of 
    the parking lot for the sale. 
     
    Summer Picnic 
    Speaking of the Summer Picnic, we need 
    volunteers to help cook and to help clean up.

    August in Your Yard 
    and Garden 
     
    • 
    Continue to weed. Unfortunately 
    as long as it’s the growing 
    season, it is also the weeding 
    season. Add young, seedheadfree weeds and excess summer 
    growth to the compost pile. Remember to 
    keep the pile as damp as a wrung-out 
    sponge for faster composting. 
    • 
    Keep after the snails 
    • 
    Use a strong spray from the hose to wash 
    away aphids. 
    • 
    Pick 
    up 
    fallen 
    fruit 
    and 
    vegetables 
    (particularly if they are planted near the 
    house) to discourage ants from forming 
    colonies near your dwelling. 
    • 
    Insulate the garden soil from the hot, dry 
    weather by applying a two-inch-thick mulch. 
    • 
    Fertilize plants if you see signs of pale, 
    lackluster growth. Yellow leaves with green 
    veins often indicate an iron deficiency that 
    can be corrected with a chelated iron. 
    Fertilize the lawn. Fertilize all flowering 
    plants about to bloom. Fertilize roses, except 
    most antique roses. 
    • 
    Pinch off dead flowers. In particular pick off 
    spent blooms from crape myrtles to bring on 
    extra flowers in the fall. Make sure you get 
    the entire flower head—particularly the 
    swollen ovaries where seeds form. 
    • 
    Hold off transplanting fall- and winterflowering plants into the bedding plot. Hot 
    weather is hard on newly transplanted 
    plants. If you do transplant, shade them for 
    a few days with the temporary shelter of an 
    umbrella 
    or 
    wooden 
    shake. 
    Stake tall 
    sprawling flowers. 
    • 
    Harvest vegetables as they ripen. Pick them 
    early in the day. 
    • 
    Continue to monitor your watering system. 
    Soak all newly transplanted plants. 
     
    These tips are from the book Northern California 
    Gardening: A Month-by-Month Guide, by Katherine 
    Grace Endicott, published by Chronicle Books, San 
    Francisco, ©1996, and are used with the publisher’s 
    permission.

    Community Gardens in Sunnyvale? 
     
    Have you ever wondered where the Sunnyvale 
    Community Garden is? If you have then I can 
    answer the question---there isn't one!  We are 
    one of the few cities in this area that does not 
    have a community garden (San Jose has 18!).  
    However I am thrilled to tell you that an effort is 
    underway to create one. 
     
    The charge is being led by Josh Salans of the 
    Sunnyvale Sustainable Gardening group (he and 
    I are co-founders of this group).  Josh and 
    others are working hard at securing the funding 
    and the location to set up a community garden 
    in Sunnyvale. Currently the focus is on acquiring 
    $40,000 of funding and getting approval from 
    the city for the garden to be located at Olive 
    and Charles (have you ever driven by the empty 
    space there?). 
     
    If you are interested in finding out more (and 
    maybe joining the effort), please let me know.  
    Meetings are Tuesday nights at Josh's house on 
    Mary Avenue.  We would love to have you!!!

    UNSCC To Raise Member Dues 
    - Faith Rogers 
     
    Starting in 2005, membership fees for the 
    United Neighborhoods of Santa Clara County 
    (UNSCC) will be about $250 per year, the 
    UNSCC Advisory Council agreed at its July 21 
    meeting (the current fee is $90). The major 
    benefit 
    of 
    membership 
    is 
    insurance 
    for 
    neighborhood association events, and the costs 
    for that insurance have increased dramatically 
    this 
    year. 
    Active 
    participation 
    in 
    UNSCC 
    activities (planning the annual conference, for 
    example) will result in additional benefits such 
    as reduced insurance cost for a neighborhood 
    association’s special event.  
     
    Because UNSCC has lost 70% of its grant 
    money as a result of the downturn in the local 
    economy, it now depends primarily on dues to 
    cover all expenses. Of the $16,000 annual 
    budget, 
    $10,000 
    currently 
    goes 
    to 
    event 
    insurance, 
    and 
    the 
    insurer, 
    Nonprofits’ 
    Insurance Alliance of California, has indicated 
    that coverage of all events for UNSCC member 
    associations could increase to $30,000 per year. 
    Members of the UNSCC board are attempting to 
    renegotiate coverage costs, but at this time, 
    special events, such as the LVNA picnic and 
    holiday parade, will require additional fees from 
    the neighborhood associations. Regular events, 
    such as monthly meetings, are covered. 
     
    UNSCC will charge all member associations a 
    one-time assessment of $50 this year, to help 
    make up budget shortfalls.

    Secondary Living Units in San Jose 
    State Assembly bill 1866, passed in 2002, 
    encourages creation of secondary living units 
    near or in existing houses in areas zoned for 
     
     
     
     
    single-family dwellings. The intent of the bill is 
    to lessen the shortage of housing in the area. 
    John Davidson, of the San Jose Planning 
    Department, explained the city’s restrictions on 
    these living areas, including owner occupancy of 
    one of the two units, a maximum of 650 square 
    feet for the secondary unit, and a minimum lot 
    size of 6,500 square feet. Planning Department 
    staff estimate that there are 2,700 unpermitted 
    secondary units in San Jose. As in other Bay 
    area cities, the shortage of building-code 
    enforcement staff means that unpermitted units 
    are investigated only if someone complains 
    about them. One of Davidson’s charts indicated 
    that in Sunnyvale the minimum lot size for a 
    house with a second living unit is 9,000 square 
    feet.
