    We Need Volunteers – Lakewood Village Needs YOU!
    Our neighborhood thrives because of people like you, and right now, we need your help to keep it
    strong! We’re looking for volunteers to step up and make a difference. Are you skilled with
    websites or social media? We need your expertise to keep our community informed. Do you enjoy
    photography? Help us capture the heart of our events. If organizing and securing resources is
    your strength, join our Procurement team. And if you’re a parent of Lakewood School students, be
    our liaison to build a stronger bridge between our community and education.
    We also urgently need neighbors to host block parties—these events are the backbone of our
    community spirit! Don’t wait for someone else to step up; now is your chance to make a
    meaningful impact. Whether you have an hour a week or just want to pitch in for an event, there’s
    a role for you. contact board@lvnasv.org today to volunteer. Lakewood Village is OUR
    neighborhood—let’s keep it thriving together! 
    Donate to LVNA—Your Support Keeps Our Events Alive!
    Lakewood Village Neighborhood Association (LVNA) is now a
    501(c)(3) non-profit organization. This milestone means your
    donations are tax-deductible—and they’re more important than
    ever. Events like the Summer Picnic, block parties, Dumpster
    Day, Holiday Parade & Lights Contest that bring joy and
    connection to our community wouldn’t have been possible
    without funding from generous neighbors like you. These
    cherished traditions depend on your support to continue. Help
    us keep these events alive and create new opportunities for
    our neighborhood by donating today at treasurer@lvnasv.org.
    Every dollar counts toward building a stronger, more vibrant
    community!
    Free Tax Support For Seniors and Low
    Income Residents
    Tax season will be upon us before we know it! Are you a senior or a low income resident
    making less than $150,000 per year? Do you need help with your taxes this year? AARP
    volunteers are volunteering their time to help seniors and low income residents with their taxes
    for free. Sessions taken place at Sunnyvale Community Services, 1160 Kern Ave (across from
    Sports Basement). Call 408-475-1335 for an appointment.

    A Goodbye and a New Beginning: A New Villager Content Editor
    By Cortney Jansen, LVNA Content Editor
    It is with mixed feelings that I write this to announce that, after nearly four years as the Villager Content
    Editor, I am resigning. This is my last Villager. I’m excited to welcome Richard Lahey and Xiang Ji, who will
    be the new Villager Content Editors, starting with the March Villager.. 
    I started writing content in May 2021, in the first few months of COVID. I had lofty plans in my first several
    months, introducing monthly scavenger hunts, continuing the “Meet Your Neighbor” column, and
    highlighting local non-profit organizations. I’m particularly proud of the work with the Sunnyvale Heritage
    Museum on a two-part series on the history of Lakewood Village, written by Katharina Woodman. (Did you
    miss those articles? Check out “Lakewood…Before There was Lakewood” on page 5 of the June 2021 Villager
    https://lvnasv.org/villager/villagerArchive/2021/villager_2021_6.pdf and “The Checkered History of
    Lakewood Village” on page 4 of the September 2021 Villager
    https://lvnasv.org/villager/villagerArchive/2021/villager_2021_9.pdf.”)
    I enjoyed following the recent transition of the Fremont Union High School District (FUHSD) school board
    elections from at-large to trustee area elections, which resulted in the election of the first-ever FUHSD
    trustee from the 94089 zip code. We now have a City Councilmember (Eileen Le) who lives north of 101, a
    FUHSD trustee (Pat Carpio-Aguilar) who lives north of 101, a State Assembly member for our district who
    lives in Lakewood neighborhood (Patrick Ahrens), and an open seat for the Sunnyvale School District (and
    the person selected for that post will also live north of 101 - see the article on page xxx). That’s four people
    from North Sunnyvale providing critical representation for our community.
    We have the Lakewood Library branch construction underway. The renovation of Lakewood Park begins
    later this year. A less happy change is the upcoming loss of our Lawrence Shopping Center, which will be
    replaced with approximately 100 townhouses as part of the Village Center Master Plan. There is so much
    happening in our community.
    Our neighborhood is getting the representation we deserve, but it only happens with engaged residents.
    We need your help. There are opportunities to serve on the Lakewood Village Neighborhood Association
    (LVNA) board, to volunteer at the creek clean-up, host a neighbor block party, remind our City that we have
    sidewalks and streets that need some love and attention, and more. Please email board@lvnasv.org to join
    our work. It can be as little as 30 minutes a month (or as much as you want).
    On a final note, I would like to say a huge thank you to Amina Mohiuddeen, our new Villager Layout Editor.
    Amina started in October, taking over the position from Nathan Chan. Welcome, Amina, and thank you to
    Nathan for all his work!
    Good luck, Richard and Xiang! I can’t wait to see what you do with the Villager newsletter!

    CalKIDS-The First Step Towards College
    The CalKIDS program is intended to help kids in California access higher education.
    The state automatically provides a deposit for eligible beneficiaries, and the
    program requires no financial contribution by parents. To see if your kids are
    eligible and enrolled, follow these steps:
    All babies born on or after July 1, 2022 are eligible for CalKIDS, regardless of parent income. Babies born between
    July 1, 2022 and June 30, 2023 receive a $25 initial deposit. Babies born on or after July 1, 2023 receive a $100 initial
    deposit. 
    Children in grades 1-12 are eligible if they were attended public school and were identified as low-income under
    the Local Control Funding Formula (LCFF) during the 2021-2022 school year. $500 is available to eligible kids. 
    CalKIDS will hold a virtual information session on Thursday, February 20 at 11:00am. To read more details and register
    for the session, go to https://us02web.zoom.us/webinar/register/WN_vZwQF3gWTRKrx545Z0uhpw#/registration 
    To access your CalKIDS account, register at this link: https://calkids.org/get-started/register-your-account/  
    More information can be found here: https://calkids.org/the-basics/who-is-eligible/ 
    Disaster Preparedness
    With the fires in LA causing so much devastation, we wanted to remind you of some quick steps you can take to start
    preparing for natural disasters in our area. Sign up to receive emergency alerts at AlertSCC.org. More resources and
    other easy steps to be prepared for a disaster are available at the Santa Clara Fire Department website at
    https://www.sccfd.org/education-and-preparedness-overview/be-evacuation-ready/.

    New Parking Rules to Follow
    LVNA VILLAGER - FEBRUARY 2025
    PAGE 7
    You may have heard about a new “daylighting” law. California Assembly Bill 413 went into effect on January 1,
    2024, but citations only started on January 1 of this year. “Daylighting” prevents vehicles from parking near
    crosswalks. The purpose is to make it easier for drivers to see people using crosswalks, reducing the
    likelihood that a car hits someone in the crosswalk.
    The law changes the rules about parking on the streets. It is now illegal to stop or park a vehicle within 20
    feet of an intersection or 15 feet of an intersection that has a curb extension or “bulb-out”. This area that is
    now off-limits for parking is referred to as the “clear zone”.
    The law applies regardless of if there are
    signs or curb markings, and it applies to the
    vehicle approach side of the crosswalk,
    regardless of if the crosswalk is marked or
    unmarked. The law applies even if the curb
    is not painted red.
    The City of Sunnyvale has begun painting
    affected curbs red, but the work is still
    underway. The City is starting in places with
    high pedestrian activity, such as recreational
    facilities, senior communities, and schools.
    Vehicles that violate the law will be fined
    $53. People can report vehicles parked in the
    clear zone by calling the non-emergency
    public safety number at 408-730-7100.
