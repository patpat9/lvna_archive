    Dick’s Market 
     
    For those of you who could not make it out to the meeting 
    last month, I thought we could review what occurred at 
    February’s LVNA meeting, and what action you can take in 
    response to the issue of Dick’s Market.  
    Mr. Metz of the Starlite Ballroom, and Mr. Yee owner of 
    Dick’s Market came to our meeting last month.  Each had 
    time to present to the residents of the neighborhood their 
    arguments for the use of that space.  It also provided the 
    residents an opportunity to ask questions, and to voice their 
    desired use of the space.  Simply, Mr. Yee explained that it 
    was just not cost effective for him to stay in business, and 
    that initially he was looking for an independent ethnic type 
    market to lease the space. Mr. Yee said at one point that he 
    had a possible suitor, and that is when Mr. Metz came into 
    the picture.  Mr. Metz explained that he had heard of Mr. 
    Yee’s intent and quickly came to Mr. Ye e with plans and a 
    proposal to convince Mr. Yee that he had a better use for the 
    space.  Mr. Metz explained that at his current location on 
    Tasman that he pays $17,000 dollars a month rent for the 
    location, but the landlord is raising the rent to $80,000+ 
    dollars a month. 
     
    Mr. Metz plan would utilize about 17,000 of the 27,000 sq. 
    ft. available.   His plan included subletting the other 10,000 
    sq. ft.   Mr. Metz further revealed that the lease would be for 
    ten years with a ten-year option at the end.  That means that 
    the ballroom would be in this site for a minimum of ten 
    years and possibly as long as twenty years.   
     
    This caught most of us off guard, because it would mean no 
    possibility of bringing in any other type of retailer for some 
    time.     
     
    Of the residents that were there, the majority voted for 
    keeping the space open and continuing the search for a 
    market or other type retailer.  We had intended as the LVNA 
    to support this one way or another.   But one of the residents 
    voiced his concern of having the association endorse either 
    side of the issue without first polling the entire constituency 
    of the village.  Because of the lack of time, we are not able 
    to do that.  So we decided to neither endorse nor condemn 
    Mr. Metz’s appeal to the planning commission.  Instead, we 
    chose to put the power back in the hands of the individual 
    residents of the neighborhood.  How?  By asking you to 
    either write (email or letter) the city planning commission, 
    call Paula Bradley the city planning coordinator for this 
    project, or show up to the planning commission meeting on 
    Monday, March 12.    
     
    As one of the commission members once told me, they pay 
    particular attention to the people who make an effort to voice 
    their opinion’s on issues and appeals that come before them. 
    They want to know what the residents of the neighborhood 
    truly want. 
     
    Mr. Metz’s appeal of the Special Development Permit will 
    be heard by the Sunnyvale Planning Commission March 12, 
    2001.  The meeting starts at 7:00pm, and is held in the City 
    Council Chambers, City Hall, 456 W. Olive Ave., 
    Sunnyvale.   Please, we ask that you take the time and this 
    opportunity to shape your neighborhood’s future.

    Celebration Time 
     
    March is the end of the year for the LVNA.  Every year, this 
    is when we take time to recognize and celebrate the 
    successes of the year, and the people and volunteers that 
    made them possible (see the list of names included with this 
    month’s newsletter).  We do this by having an appreciation 
    dinner and taking time to recognize the volunteers with small 
    tokens of our appreciation. This year is no different except 
    that we have more reason to celebrate. 
     
    Scott Morton, Sunnyvale Parks Supervisor, and special 
    guests have asked to join in with our celebration.   
     
    The reason is to celebrate the final completion and opening 
    of the Hetch Hetchy Greenway that runs through the 
    neighborhood.  He also is excited to announce approval of 
    the Capital Improvement Plan for Lakewood Park that is 
    scheduled to begin in the next year. There will be a short 
    slide presentation of the work done on the Hetch Hetchy 
    Greenway, and an opportunity to showcase the plans for the 
    improvement to Lakewood Park. Talking with Scott over the 
    phone, they are excited that the greenway is finally 
    complete, and even more excited about the improvements to 
    be made to Lakewood Park. He wants to hear what you think 
    of the plan.    
     
    So please we invite all the residents of the neighborhood to 
    come join us on this evening to recognize and celebrate the 
    people and organizations that made this year a success. Scott 
    and the city will be providing salad, pizza, and refreshments 
    for the evening’s festivities. 
     
    LVNA Board Elections 
     
    Also at the celebration dinner, we will be taking time to elect 
    a new board for the LVNA.  Like I said, this is the end of the 
    year for the LVNA.  This is where we celebrate the year that 
    has been and elect a new board with the hope of  looking 
    towards our future.  So, if you are looking for a volunteer 
    opportunity that would serve your community as well as 
    provide you with a new perspective of the city you live in, I 
    strongly recommend participating.   
     
    I came in last year with no experience in this type of activity. 
    The board members who were there that night said that they 
    would support and help me.   They were men and women of 
    their word.  I want to thank the board members for all of 
    their help this year.  Thank you to the city staff for their 
    support and encouragement.   
    I want to especially thank Mel and Elena Anderson, who I 
    consider the sponsors of this neighborhood.  They have 
    shown unique dedication and love of our village. Without 
    them, I am not sure if any of what we have done would be 
    possible.  And a special thank you to Laurie Aguinaga, our 
    Neighborhood Preservation Officer, I am happy to have you 
    on board with us and to have the opportunity to get to know 
    you.  You have been an incredible liaison of the city for us.  
    The volunteers, although words would never be enough, I 
    will just say thanks for caring.  Mr. Lacy thanks for stepping 
    in.  Tom Freeman, thank you for your presence and caring.  
    Rick and Rena for giving what precious time you could, 
    especially with a new born.  Craig Crawford and family, 
    amazing!  Thomas Mayer and family in a word dedicated.  
    Thank you.  Finally, to all the local businesses and 
    corporations thank you. 
     
    Finally for those of you who complain about the state of 
    degradation our society and world, or if you wish you knew 
    your neighbor, here is an opportunity. This is an opportunity 
    and challenge to pull on your own innate brilliance, your 
    skills, and talents to have an impact for the betterment of the 
    world starting with the very place you live-your home.  Just 
    to let you know, this is exactly what I wanted to know last 
    year.  I had impact and I got to meet some of my neighbors 
    and to have a hand at making peace with them. 
    That has been the greatest gift for me this last year.  
     
     Now, no excuses if your heart’s true desire is to be of 
    service take a moment of pause and see if this is the 
    opportunity you had prayed or wished for.  We all have busy 
    schedules, families, and often feel we are not good or smart 
    enough, what matters is if you have a true desire to be of 
    service.  And you won’t be doing this alone; you will have 
    the rest of us standing next to you. Let’s have a bit of fun 
    and come join us.  
     
    Meeting 
     
    This month’s meeting and celebration will be on Thursday, 
    March 15th.   We will be meeting at the Lakewood Park 
    Building, 834 Lakechime Drive.  The meeting will begin at 
    7pm.   Come join us, we look forward to seeing you there.

    Hetch-Hetchy Greenway Opens 
     
    Put on your walking shoes, and wipe the cobwebs off of 
    your bicycle.  Spring has sprung and it’s time to get out in 
    the fresh air and walk, jog, or ride your 2-wheeled wonder 
    from one side of town to the other.   
     
    Our guess is that you will probably prefer to do this while 
    gazing at beautiful meadows and flowers instead of inhaling 
    exhaust fumes belching from trucks ahead of you. Well now 
    you can.  How is it possible?  Simple - you can stroll and 
    ride the friendly confines of the newly landscaped HetchHetchy Greenway that runs from east to west on the north 
    end of town near Highway 101.  It was reopened on January 
    27 and has been earning rave reviews by those who have 
    been using it. 
     
    The Hetch-Hetchy right-of-way is a fairly straight piece of 
    land 80 feet wide and 2.2 miles long extending east to west 
    across the entire City.  The land is owned by the City of San 
    Francisco and is leased and maintained by the City of 
    Sunnyvale.  It serves as a pedestrian and bicycle greenway 
    that links Fairwood Park with Orchard Gardens Park.   
     
    The City recently completed this “capital improvement 
    project”, which entailed landscape design and construction 
    of 6.5 acres of land that stretched between Dick’s Market in 
    the east to Orchard Gardens Park in the west.  For a price tag 
    of about $1.5 million, the project was designed by Callander 
    Associates and constructed by Watkin & Bortolussi, Inc. 
    Landscaping of the right-of-way from Lawrence Expressway 
    to Fairwood Park had already been completed by a previous 
    capital improvement project. 
     
    The main purpose of the project was to turn what had been a 
    bare expanse of land into a beautifully landscaped greenway, 
    making it easier for pedestrians and cyclists to get around.  
    This was accomplished by surrounding the existing pathway 
    with lawn and a mixture of plant material including 
    ornamental grasses and flowering shrubs.  The lawn areas 
    border both sides of the pathway, followed by plants leading 
    up to the perimeter fences.  Users will enjoy the serenity of 
    the gentle, waving effect that is created by the layout of the 
    plants and the swaying of the grasses in a breeze.   
     
    You don’t need to be a super athlete to traverse the full 2.2 
    mile right-of-way.  You can catch your breath and enjoy the 
    surroundings along the greenway on benches placed beneath 
    a trellis planted with vines. And don’t worry too much if 
    your biking skills are a bit rusty.  The lawn placed adjacent 
    to the pathway creates a resilient surface to cushion you if 
    you accidentally veer off the path and take a fall.  Another 
    safety enhancement is the addition of a pathway lighting 
    system similar to those found in the City’s parks.  
    So, take a break from spring cleaning, and stretch your legs 
    out on the Hetch-Hetchy Greenway.  Without too much 
    imagination it’s easy to feel like you have been transported 
    to a yellow brick road in the middle of our emerald city! 
     
    Please join with the City and the LVNA in celebrating 
    the opening of the Hetch-hetchy Greenway at our regular 
    LVNA meeting at 7am on Thursday, March 15th in the 
    Lakewood Park building.  The City will be providing 
    pizza and refreshments and will do a short presentation 
    on the Greenway project and other items of interest. 
     
    There is some more good news for open space in the 
    Lakewood Village Neighborhood.  Funds have been 
    allocated for fiscal year 2001-02 for the planning phase of a 
    recreation facilities renovation project at Lakewood Park.  
    Facilities included in the scope of the project include the 
    skating rink, amphitheater, space station, and their interlinking areas.  Public meetings will be scheduled to gather 
    input for the design process.  If you are interested in 
    participating please call the City of Sunnyvale Parks 
    Division at (408) 730-7506.  We appreciate your ideas and 
    hope that you will help us to make Lakewood Park a place 
    that continues to meet the recreational and open space needs 
    of the community.

    LOOKING TOWARD SUMMER……. 
    Any suggestions to prevent this possible problem 
    from reoccurring this Summer? 
     
    HI, 
    I was wondering if you knew what happened to the Police 
    that blocked off Sandia from non-residents/guests of 
    residents on the 4th of July?  We have lived in this area for 
    10 years. Every year but the last two, we have had Police 
    blocking access to Sandia, and only allowing residents and 
    their guests into the neighborhood.  Then the roadblock 
    disappeared, and we had massive obnoxiousness in terms of 
    strangers and all of their cars blocking the streets and 
    making life hazardous for our neighborhood. The police had 
    to come out and make people move their vehicles as the 
    drivers suddenly thought the street was a parking lot. I also 
    wonder if theft did not increase during that time.  How do we 
    go about getting the blockade, for lack of a better word, back 
    in force?  The corner of Sandia and Havenwood is one of the 
    prime outlets to see the fireworks from Great America.   
    Since I live in this area, I feel strongly about this issue.  This 
    last year was especially bad, as one of the families operating 
    from a house on Havenwood had a lot of fireworks going.  
    There were highway flares on the road, that 4 wheel drive 
    cars ran over. There were unsupervised kids running around 
    with sparklers while everyone else was watching the 
    fireworks.   I nearly got burned.  There seemed to be a lot of 
    people competing for the Darwin in Action award.   They 
    did not stop to think about all of the cars that were parked 
    alongside the road that could be damaged. They did not stop 
    to think about using aerial fireworks under power lines.  
    They just wanted to have some fun and did not think of the 
    consequences of their illegal activities.  I suppose it was nice 
    of them to want to include others in their idea of fun, but I 
    would rather not be removed from the gene pool by them. 
     
    Sincerely, 
    Lakewood resident
