    President’s Column 
    - Ursula Syrova, LVNA President 
    Why Do We Care? 
    As usual, turnout for the January LVNA general 
    meeting was small, and I have been musing about 
    why—and what we might do about it. 
    At our LVNA board meeting, we discussed the 
    upcoming board member elections in March, and 
    mulled over the lack of attendance and volunteerism.  
    The same small group of people has been doing the 
    work of the LVNA for years, some are getting burned 
    out, and those who had been involved before and have 
    left are fondly remembered.   
    Now, I can really relate to non-participation.  Before 
    moving to Lakewood Village I wasn’t involved in my 
    neighborhood and I liked it that way.  I didn’t want to 
    belong to the “paint police” (my nickname for the 
    homeowner’s association of our neighborhood in 
    Colorado) and I didn’t care what the issues of the 
    neighborhood were.  I knew a couple of the neighbors 
    and that was it.  I was completely ignorant of 
    neighborhood-city interaction, local school issues, 
    everything.  I did no volunteer work whatsoever. 
    So, what has changed for me?  Why do I care now?  
    What value do I find in volunteering for the LVNA?  
    Well, when I first moved here almost two years ago, 
    there was a copy of the Villager on our door.  I thought 
    it was so cool!  It had all this great information about 
    the neighborhood, local businesses, and local issues. 
    After a couple of months I decided to go to one of the 
    LVNA meetings.  There weren’t many people there, 
    but suddenly I had met some of the people in the 
    neighborhood.  One of them knew about the folks who 
    had previously owned our house.   Attending that 
    meeting gave me a way to start to plug in to the new  
     
    neighborhood we had chosen.  By getting involved 
    with the LVNA I have come to feel “settled” in this 
    neighborhood in a way I never felt in my previous 
    neighborhood.  I’ve met a lot of people and I now have 
    the feeling that I am giving something back to my 
    community.  As my daughter grows up, I have the 
    wonderful feeling that I am modeling good-citizen 
    behavior for her.  I am teaching her that it is a good 
    thing to get involved locally, to care about the 
    neighborhood we live in, and to give something back to 
    the community.  I don’t just take her to watch the 
    annual parade, I help plan it.  We don’t just go to the 
    picnic, we help coordinate it.  I no longer feel that I live 
    on a little island, just one house in an unknown 
    territory—I feel like I am part of a community.

    X President’s Column, continued from page 1 
    But nothing gets fixed!  You may think that.  After all, 
    the same issues about speeding cars, traffic, pest 
    control, trees etc. have been brought up time and time 
    again.   We sometimes have gotten organized enough 
    to take issues to the city.  It does little good.  My 
    attitude is, so what?  I’m enjoying the journey of 
    involvement, and I’m not focused on achievements.  
    We will continue to be squeaky wheels on issues we 
    care about, and someday if the economy ever turns 
    around and the city has money again, we’ll be ready to 
    take advantage of that.  In the meantime the sense of 
    community involvement is there.  That is the goal, in 
    my way of looking at things. 
    We are coming up on election time for new board 
    members.  Why don’t you sign up?  Board members 
    plan the general meetings and help to coordinate  
      
    the  annual events that we have in the neighborhood.  
    Call any of us board members, listed on the back of the 
    Villager, if you want more information. It’s a good way 
    to meet long-time Lakewood Village residents and 
    have a say in what LVNA does—or doesn’t do. 
    Quarterly Meeting Schedule? 
    Because of small turnout at the monthly general 
    meetings, the LVNA Board is considering changing to 
    a quarterly schedule.  Come discuss the possibility with 
    us at our next general meeting, on February 9, 2005 at 
    the Lakewood Park building at 7 p.m. 
    UNSCC Needs Volunteers Too 
    I went to the United Neighborhoods of Santa Clara 
    County Advisory Council meeting for January.  There 
    were fewer than fifteen people in attendance, and this 
    was their board election night.  The people who were 
    running for the board positions were the same people 
    who had been holding positions the previous term, and 
    sad reference was made to several people who had 
    gotten burned out and left.  Votes were cast and 5 out 
    of 10 positions were filled.  There was some 
    grouchiness about the situation, but as the meeting 
    broke up, the little team of volunteers was upbeat and 
    cautiously optimistic.  I was really impressed by their 
    ongoing commitment to keeping this umbrella 
    organization going.  (One of the major benefits it offers 
    is low-cost insurance for special events held by 
    member neighborhood associations – and insurance 
    coverage is required by the city of Sunnyvale for our 
    parade and picnic.) As was pointed out at the Advisory 
    Council meeting, most people there are “triple 
    volunteers”: leaders of their own neighborhood 
    associations, board members for UNSCC, and 
    committee members for one of UNSCC’s projects, 
    such as the annual conference or insurance application 
    processing.   
    United Neighborhoods of Santa Clara County is very 
    short of help.  If you would like to be on their board, 
    they would love it.  If you would just like to be the 
    LVNA representative to UNSCC, that would be great.  
    All it entails is attending the Advisory Council meeting 
    in San Jose every other month and letting the LVNA 
    know what happened. 

    February in Your 
    Yard and Garden 
    o Purchase bare-root plants. 
    Cover the roots until you 
    have the time and the 
    weather is right for planting. 
    o Order bare-root fruit trees 
    from specialty nurseries. 
    They will usually stop 
    shipping bare-root fruit trees 
    by the end of March. 
    o Send in orders to seed 
    companies before they run out of stock later in 
    spring. 
    o Clean and repair garden tools. Sharpen the blades 
    of pruning tools and shovels. Service the lawn 
    mower. 
    o Complete pruning of deciduous trees, shrubs, 
    vines, fruit trees, and roses. 
    o If desired and if deciduous plants have not leafed 
    out yet, apply a last application of dormant spray to 
    kill overwintering insects. It is particularly 
    important to use a lime sulfur spray for peach and 
    nectarine trees to prevent a fungus commonly 
    known as peach leaf curl that causes distorted 
    leaves and scarred fruit. 
    o Select and plant azaleas, camellias, and 
    rhododendrons while they are in bloom. 
    o Fertilize spring-blooming bulbs as the foliage 
    emerges. Some growers recommend dried blood 
    meal; follow the directions on the package, 
    sprinkling the blood meal around the emerging 
    foliage. 
    These tips are from the book Northern California Gardening: A 
    Month-by-Month Guide, by Katherine Grace Endicott, published by 
    Chronicle Books, San Francisco, ©1996, and are used with the 
    publisher’s permission. For more information, see: 
    http://www.lvna.net/Resources/Reading_List/reading_list.html 

    Lakewood Village’s 50th 
    - Brian Smithson 
     
    Lakewood Village is 50 years 
    old in 2005. Starting with this 
    issue, we’ll take a look at how it started and how it has 
    changed over the years. 
    Before Lakewood Village was built, it was entirely 
    agricultural. After World War II, ten investors bought 
    250 acres in what is now the “Fairwood side” of 
    Lakewood Village and built the Santa Clara Valley 
    Airport, as you can see in these photos from the late 
    1940s. The land was graded for a runway, and had a 
    mechanic’s shop and a small office. 
     
    The approximate location of today’s Lakewood Village is 
    marked with a dark outline on this photo. You may be 
    able to see a small runway at the lower left corner of the 
    outlined area. 
     
     
    On the left side of this photo is the Bayshore Highway 
    (now US 101). Across the top is Lawrence Station Road 
    (now Lawrence Expressway). 
     
    The two-lane road running left to right in this photo is 
    the Bayshore Highway. The shop and office are to the left 
    of the taxiway on the left side of the photo. 
    Amateur aviators used the Santa Clara Valley Airport 
    until the land was sold for development in 1955, part of 
    which became Unit I of Lakewood Village.  
    If you have any old photos or news clippings from 
    Lakewood Village, please let me know. Contact 
    webmaster@lvna.net or call Brian at 738-5939. I am 
    putting together a gallery of historical information at 
    http://www.lvna.net/LV/History/history.html. Some of 
    your photos may end up in an issue of the Villager! 
