    Sign Etiquette Geoff Carter, Editor A lot of effort is expended to control and eradicate the spurious Signs placed around the neighborhood by our young people. We work so hard at it because these signs tend to be destructive in that they are usually carved into wooden fences, park and school windows, mirrors, tables, and benches or painted on any surface that strikes their fancy These signs, by the nature of their construction tend to be of a more permanent nature and difficult to remove. Most would agree that these signs are generally a blight upon the neighborhood and quite undesirable. We have a name for these signs graffiti. But what of the spurious signs placed by adults? You know... the ones used to advertise "Yard Sale", "Garage Sale". and "Lost Pet" (among other things). They can be destructive in nature in that the collective effect of nails, staples, and tacks used over the years to attach these signs to the convenient wooden structures eventually turn this wood into just so much sawdust. While most of these adult- generated Signs are not meant to be permanent, they are hardly ever taken down. the net effect being at least semi permanence. Most adhesives used with the various and sundry tapes used to attach signs to lamp posts and street sign posts mutate into a permanent, ugly blob after a few weeks.     Sunnyvale Linda Kilian (Reprinted from Last Month's Viilager) The Sunnyvale School District needs millions of dollars to renovate its schools. We're not talking about glamorous changes either. We're talking about new roofs, wiring, plumbing, dry rot, termite damage, bathroom tile, and the like After a year of visiting school sites, talking with administrators, and surveying the community, the Board of Education of the Sunnyvale Elementary School District placed a bond measure on the June ballot. Tlus was a difficult decision, because passing a bond measure is not easy. To win, a two-thirds majority of voters must vote in favor of the measurer One "no" vote cancels out two "yes" votes. If passed, the bond would raise $34 million. All of this money would be spent locally on our schools. None of the money could be spent on salaries. Lakewood School would receive up to $3.5 million to upgrade its facilities. Like most other schools in Sunnyvale, Lakewood is almost 40 years old. Time has taken its toll, and Lakewood needs a new roof. upgrades to the electrical system, repairs to rusted plumbing fixtures and pipes, a new fire alarm system, remodeled restrooms, new playground equipment. and more. The Sunnyvale School District has been able to renovate and reopen three schools in recent years by selling a surplus school site. However. as our student population continues to grow, selling other properties is not an option. Says Sunnyvale School Superintendent Doris Wilson, "When voters understand the condition of our schools, we feel they will see this bond measure is a good thing for the schools, for the city. and for the property values of our homes." The bond campaign organization, Friends of Sunnyvale Schools, is being co-chaired by Linda Kilian and Betty Verstegen. Area resident and Board member, Jessica Lee, will be orgamzing volunteers in the Lakewood area. If you have any questions about the bond measure or are interested in volunteering, call Friends of Sunnyvale Schools at (408) 450-1100. Too much of a good thing can be wonderful.
