    In the September issue of the Villager, we 
    reported that City Council had agreed to hear 
    from Lakewood Village residents about 
    concerns over the traffic situ ation on the 
    Fairwood side.  Thom Mayer and Lakewood 
    Village Traffic Committee member Lynette 
    Nguyen attended the council meeting and 
    made their voices heard!  With persuasive help 
    from Lakewood Village resident and Council 
    member Fred Fowler, City Council  agreed to 
    address our traffic issue beginning in early 
    2004.  A traffic study was performed in 2001 
    and the problem areas were identified and 
    documented, so the push now will be to reach 
    out to neighborhood residents and try to come 
    to a consensus on what  should be done.  Many 
    thanks to Thom, Lynette, and Fred for getting 
    this issue back onto Sunnyvale’s “to-do” list. ¢

    23rd year, 1st picnic? 
    I’ve lived in Lakewood Village for 23 years and 
    have never attended the annual picnic – until 
    this year. Why? Because I’m a v egetarian, and 
    neighborhood picnics usually feature 
    hamburgers and hotdogs, not tofu and bean 
    sprouts. This year was different, however, as 
    some of you may have seen me grilling veggie 
    burgers and zucchini alongside the carnivores. 
    Although there weren’t m any vegetarians at 
    the picnic this year, with better planning and 
    promotion, next year could be different. The 
    picnic should be fun for all of our neighbors, so 
    come out next year and join your vegetarian 
    neighbors, whether it’s because they’re Hindu, 
    Buddhist, or Muslim, or they love animals, or 
    they hate plants, or “just ‘cuz”.

    Picture an overall great neighborhood.  
    It's usually quiet, peaceful, the houses 
    have green lawns and fresh coats of 
    paint.  There's a park, a school, local 
    stores, even gas stations.  Now think 
    that amongst the picturesque 
    neighborhood there is an intruder - a 
    house with a well seeded weed garden, a 
    record-setting collection of paint chips 
    on the ground, a cement yard, and broken 
    down cars permanently parked in the 
    front yard.  Our common sense and pride 
    for our community forces us to ask, "How 
    can we fix this?!" 
     
    Not a lot of people realize that there 
    are actually municipal codes that were 
    made to make sure those things don't 
    happen.  However, there are always 
    places and things that somehow are 
    ignored or slip through the cracks.  If 
    I were in charge, one of the fi rst 
    things I'd do would be to instate 
    stricter municipal cod enforcement.  
    Every month, municipal code officers 
    would patrol Lakewood Village, 
    distributing notices and taking action 
    as needed to get everyone up to code.  
     
    Cars are another big problem.  Four -way 
    intersections all through Lakewood 
    Village are an accident waiting to 
    happen.  Four-way stop signs at all 
    intersections would slow down drivers 
    that go dangerously fast, so that 
    Lakewood Village can be safe for 
    pedestrians as well as other drivers.  
    Backing out of your own driveway can be 
    a hazard also, because cars are often 
    parked so close to your driveway you 
    can't see around them.  I think a new 
    municipal code should state that cars 
    cannot be parked closer than six feet to 
    any driveway entrance.  
     
    People should show pride and respect for 
    their community and their neighbors.  
    Keep you house clean and well -kept.  
    Drive carefully and within the speed 
    limit.  We can make Lakewood Village a 
    better place for everyone to live. £

    SUNNYVALE, Calif. – The Sunnyvale 
    City Council is considering adopting 
    new restrictions on recreational 
    vehicle (RV), boat and t railer parking in front 
    yards. The hearings will address issues such as 
    limiting the total length of a vehicle, requiring 
    parking to be perpendicular with the street and 
    limiting the length of time vehicles may be 
    parked. The hearings will provide Sunnyval e 
    residents with an opportunity to voice their 
    concerns, and questions during this decision -
    making process. 
     
    Currently, the City does not prohibit the parking 
    of RVs or any oversized vehicles in front yards. 
    City requirements also do not address the 
    aesthetic impacts that RV, trailer and boat 
    parking may bring to residential neighborhoods. 
    The City’s Municipal Code currently has three 
    key sections – SMC 9.24.180, SMC 9.26.030 
    (19) and  
    SMC 19.48.260 – that deal specifically with 
    parking on private property.  RVs, trailers and 
    boats comply with current City regulations if 
    they are parked on a paved surface and do not 
    block visibility at a neighboring driveway.
