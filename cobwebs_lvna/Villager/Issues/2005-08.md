    President’s Column 
    - Ursula Syrova, LVNA President 
    Two Tons More Than Last Year! 
    Dumpster Day, July 16, was a great success―173 tons 
    of stuff were collected and taken away! That is two 
    more tons than last year. The tire dumpster in the 
    Lakewood Village Shopping Center collected 125 tires, 
    compared to only 33 last year. All this was collected in 
    58 loads.  
    Lots of usable stuff was rerouted to the Dumpster Day 
    Sale in the parking lot of the shopping center, and we 
    were able to raise $431 for neighborhood events. Thank 
    you to Judy Fernandez for running the sale and Steven 
    Fernandez, Mrs, W, and Wendy Stanley for helping, 
    and to Skip Gottschalk and Dennis O’Neil for running 
    loads of stuff collected at the dumpsters over to the 
    sale. We appreciate Gene Yee, owner of the Lakewood 
    Village Shopping Center, for letting us use a section of 
    the parking lot for the sale. Dumpster Day went 
    smoothly for the most part, although one dumpster did 
    tip over while being picked up. No one was hurt, but it 
    was exciting!  
    The enthusiasm and dedication of the volunteers are 
    what make this event such a success.

    August in Your Yard  
    and Garden 
    o Continue to weed. Unfortunately 
    as long as it’s the growing season, 
    it is also the weeding season. Add 
    young, seedhead-free weeds and 
    excess summer growth to the 
    compost pile. Remember to keep the pile as damp 
    as a wrung-out sponge for faster composting. 
    o Keep after the snails 
    o Use a strong spray from the hose to wash away 
    aphids. 
    o Pick up fallen fruit and vegetables (particularly if 
    they are planted near the house) to discourage ants 
    from forming colonies near your dwelling. 
    o Insulate the garden soil from the hot, dry weather 
    by applying a two-inch-thick mulch. 
     
     
     
    o Fertilize plants if you see signs of pale, lackluster 
    growth. Yellow leaves with green veins often 
    indicate an iron deficiency that can be corrected 
    with a chelated iron. Fertilize the lawn. Fertilize all 
    flowering plants about to bloom. Fertilize roses, 
    except most antique roses. 
    o Pinch off dead flowers. In particular pick off spent 
    blooms from crape myrtles to bring on extra 
    flowers in the fall. Make sure you get the entire 
    flower head—particularly the swollen ovaries 
    where seeds form. 
    o Hold off transplanting fall- and winter-flowering 
    plants into the bedding plot. Hot weather is hard on 
    newly transplanted plants. If you do transplant, 
    shade them for a few days with the temporary 
    shelter of an umbrella or wooden shake. Stake tall 
    sprawling flowers. 
    o Harvest vegetables as they ripen. Pick them early 
    in the day. 
    o Continue to monitor your watering system. Soak all 
    newly transplanted plants. 
    These tips are from the book Northern California 
    Gardening: A Month-by-Month Guide, by Katherine 
    Grace Endicott, published by Chronicle Books, San 
    Francisco, copyright 1996, and are used with the 
    publisher’s permission. 

    July 14 General Meeting 
    Sunnyvale Mayor Dean Chu, City Council 
    member Melinda Hamilton, and Planning 
    Commission Chair Chris Moylan described the

    projects. An open forum session on city and 
    Lakewood Village issues followed.

    distributed. A discussion about the Dumpster Day 
    (Saturday, July 16) process led to agreement that 
    dumping on the dumpster delivery day (Friday) 
    would be at the discretion of each dumpster 
    monitor. 
    July 28 Board Meeting 
    Dumpster Day summary – 58 dumpsters full, 173 
    tons, 125 tires, and $431.00 collected for items at 
    the Dumpster Day Sale. 
    Other items: The Treasurer provided a financial 
    report. Calendar of events and guest speaker 
    recommendations were discussed. Preliminary 
    plans were made for the LVNA Picnic; the 
    tentative date is Saturday, September 17. 
    The Bank of Santa Clara, where the LVNA 
    checking account is, will begin charging $10 per 
    month as of December 1 for accounts with less 
    than a $3000 daily balance. Board members will 
    contact other banks to see if we can find one that 
    meets LVNA needs without a service charge.  
    Be Kind to Your Neighbors 
    If you need to mow your grass or fix your car and you 
    like to work to music, just remember to save it until 
    after 9 a.m., and turn off the noise at 10 p.m. Your 
    neighbors may need to sleep, or someone may be sick. 
    Make Lakewood Village a good place to live—respect 
    your neighbors. 

    Lakewood Village’s 50th 
    Part 4: The Neighborhood Gets Organized 
    - Brian Smithson 
    This is the fourth in a series of articles celebrating the 
    50 year history of Lakewood Village .The first three 
    appeared in the February, March, and May issues. 
    The LVRA 
    The predecessor to today’s Lakewood Village 
    Neighborhood Association (LVNA) was the 
    Lakewood Village Residents Association (LVRA). 
    The LVRA was formed in January of 1958. It was 
    primarily funded by annual dues of $2.00–$2.50 per 
    household. Membership was not a requirement for 
    residents in Lakewood Village, and nonmembers were 
    not excluded from the benefits of LVRA activities, but 
    residents were encouraged to show their support for 
    the LVRA by becoming members, attending meetings, 
    and participating in activities of one kind or another. 
    Other funding came from bake sales and rummage 
    sales and from donations of cash or merchandise from 
    local merchants. 
    The LVRA was extraordinarily active and effective in 
    a variety of community affairs. In February of 1958, a  
     
    The original LVRA constitution was adopted from another 
    homeowner association in what is now Cupertino 
    social committee was also formed. In April, the first 
    issue of the Villager was published. The first picnic 
    was held in July, but because there were no parks built 
    in Lakewood Village at that time, the picnic was held 
    at Blackberry Farms (in what is now Cupertino). A 
    “Teenage LVRA” was formed in October, and in 
    December, a Christmas party was held at a local club. 
    In June of 1959, the LVRA was incorporated as a nonprofit organization. The first Holiday Lighting Contest 
    and Holiday Parade were held that December. 
    Some of the LVRA’s activities, like the annual picnic, 
    lighting contest, parade, and publication of the 
    Villager, continue to this day. Other activities were 
    shorter lived. An Easter egg hunt was held at 
    Lakewood Park in 1965, and by 1971 it was 
    augmented by an Easter parade. The first of several 
    Western Days events was held in 1965 in the parking 
    lot of Dick’s Plaza (now Lakewood Village Shopping 
    Center). In 1967, the first (and only) Miss Lakewood 
    Village beauty pageant was held. In 1970, there was a 
    Halloween “Spook House.”  
    The LVRA was credited with many accomplishments, 
    especially during its first five years of operation. 
    Among those accomplishments were: 
    o Neighborhood participation in a hierarchy 
    consisting of elected LVRA officers, volunteer 
    leaders for each of the four neighborhood units, 
    and volunteer leaders for each block.

    Sunnyvale. At its peak, the LVRA had four officers, 
    eight unit directors, four social committee officers, 
    and dozens of block captains. About ten people 
    regularly composed the editorial staff of the Villager, 
    and local scout troops delivered the newsletter. 
    Despite many years of neighborhood participation and 
    accomplishment, the LVRA eventually ceased 
    operation sometime in the mid-1970s. 
    The LVNA 
    The LVNA was formed in October of 1991, by Judy 
    Fernandez and Vicky Piazza. The reasons for reforming a neighborhood association were: 
    o To educate and create awareness; 
    o To give opportunities to get acquainted with each 
    other;  
    o To allow the community to come together. 
    The LVNA has continued many of the traditions 
    established by the LVRA, including monthly 
    meetings, publication of the Villager, and events like 
    the picnic, the lighting contest, and the holiday parade.  
     
    The Villager resumed publication when the LVNA was formed 
    The LVNA also acts as a liaison between Lakewood 
    Village residents and the City of Sunnyvale, and vice 
    versa. The LVNA’s working relationship with the city 
    has been beneficial for Lakewood Village. Among its 
    results are renovation of Fairwood Park and 
    landscaping of the Hetch-Hetchy right-of-way as a 
    neighborhood greenbelt (the John W. Christian 
    Greenbelt, named after a former director of 
    Sunnyvale’s Parks and Recreation Department who 
    was instrumental in developing the bare-ground 
    easement into a landscaped community resource). 
    Unlike the LVRA, the LVNA does not ask residents to 
    pay annual dues. All residents are automatically 
    members, and funding for LVNA activities is derived 
    primarily from Villager advertising revenue. The 
    LVNA had “unit managers” in the 1990s, similar to 
    the LVRA’s “unit directors,” but today’s LVNA has 
    no such hierarchy. Also, the LVNA does not have a 
    social committee; the LVRA Social Committee was 
    essentially a women’s auxiliary organization, but 
    women are now equal partners in the LVNA. The 
    current LVNA operates with only five officers and a 
    handful of regular volunteers. 
    Participating in the LVNA can lead to positions in city 
    government; several past LVNA officers have gone on 
    to larger civic roles in Sunnyvale. For example, 
    former LVNA president Fred Fowler was elected to 
    the City Council and then became mayor of the City 
    of Sunnyvale, former president Vicki Piazza went on 
    to become the chairperson of the Sunnyvale Planning 
    Commission, and former president Jessica Lee 
    became president of the Fremont Union High School 
    District. Community activism has been a hallmark of 
    LVNA participants. 

    Possible Community Garden in 
    Lakewood Village 
    A tour of the proposed community garden site in 
    Lakewood Village has been scheduled for Monday, 
    August 15. Josh Salans of the Sunnyvale Community 
    Gardens group will be on hand to answer questions. 
    We will meet at 7:30 p.m. at the east end of the John 
    Christian Greenbelt. Walk and talk about ideas for a 
    community garden right in our own neighborhood. 
    Take Lawrence Expressway to Sandia. From Sandia, 
    make a left on Oak Creek Way and then a left on 
    Manzano. The greenbelt starts on the left (the creek is 
    on the right side). 
    Everyone is welcome. If you are interested in growing 
    a community garden, be sure to go on this tour.  
     
     
     
     
    Letter 
    Dear Neighbors: 
    We all enjoy a little extra money 
    now and again, and a garage or yard 
    sale is one way to make some cash. 
    But please take down your signs for 
    a sale when it's over. It is not 
    fair for others who walk the 
    neighborhood and see that the date 
    has passed, and it makes Lakewood 
    Village look trashy when there are 
    expired sale signs hanging all 
    around. 
    Thank you in advance for remembering 
    to gather up those garage and yard 
    sale signs at the end of the day. 
    Judy Fernandez 
