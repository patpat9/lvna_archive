    Neighborhood News
    Michael Jenesse, LVNA President
    With Thanksgiving quickly approaching, we wanted to keep
    the meeting simple.  At this month's meeting we have the
    Sunnyvale Department of Public Safety joining us.  Then we
    will also be discussing this year's Holiday Parade, and finally
    any other neighborhood business
    Department of Public Safety
    First, we will have members of the Sunnyvale Department of
    Public Safety on hand to update and respond to questions
    that people may have in consideration of the steps the
    department is taking in light of the recent governmental
    warnings.  They will also be able to give us more pragmatic
    updates about any incidences that are occurring within the
    neighborhood.
    Holiday Parade
    Next we will be discussing the upcoming Holiday Parade.
    This year's parade will be on Saturday, December 8.  Craig
    Crawford is again coordinating this year’s parade.  He all
    ready has several participants lined up for the parade.  The
    Fremont High School marching band and choir, the
    Department of Public Safety, Public Works, and the
    Sunnyvale School Board just to name a few. AMD has also
    pledged financial support and will be helping to provide
    refreshments.
    Yet, because of economic conditions, we have had to scale
    back on such things as the refreshments.  This year we are
    asking for support.  First, we are inviting people to bring out
    some snacks: pastries, donuts, cookies, and bagels. This is
    not a requirement to enjoy or participate in the parade, and it
    is completely on a voluntary basis.
    Toys4Tots
    We are also doing something that we used to do in years
    past, we are taking part in the Toys4Tots program.  We will
    have a bin at the end of the parade route specifically for the
    Toys4Tots.  It is simple just bring a new, unwrapped toy to
    be donated and just drop it in the bin.  Then on the
    December 23 and 24 the toys will be taken out to
    organizations and locations where they will be distributed to
    the children and families in need.  Some of these families
    and children may just be in our own neighborhood.  Again,
    this is voluntary, and we thank you in advance for your
    support.
    This month's meeting will be held on Thursday, November
    15 at 7pm.  We will be meeting at the Lakewood Parks
    Building at 834 Lakechime.  This will be the last meeting of
    the year.  We will be taking a break in December from
    having a meeting, but we will still have a newsletter for
    December.  We wish your family and you a Happy
    Thanksgiving.

    With a timid voice and idolizing eyes, the
    little boy greeted his father as he returned
    from work, "Daddy, how much do you make
    an hour?"
    Greatly surprised, but giving his boy a
    glaring look, the father said: "Look, son, not
    even your mother knows that. Don't bother
    me now, I'm tired."
    "But Daddy, just tell me please!? How much
    do you make an hour," the boy insisted.
    The father finally giving up replied, "Twenty
    dollars per hour." "Okay, Daddy? Could you
    loan me ten dollars?" the boy asked.
    Showing restlessness and positively
    disturbed, the father yelled, "So that was the
    reason you asked how much I earn, right?
    Go to sleep and don't bother me anymore!"
    It was already dark and the father was
    meditating on what he had said and was
    feeling guilty. Maybe his son wanted to buy
    something.
    Finally, trying to ease his mind, the father
    went to his son's room. "Are you asleep
    son?" asked the father.
    "No, Daddy. Why?" replied the boy partially
    asleep. "Here's the money you asked for
    earlier," the father said.
    "Thanks, Daddy!" rejoiced the son, while
    putting his hand under his pillow and
    removing some money.
    "Now I have enough! Now I have twenty
    dollars!" the boy said to his father, who was
    gazing at his son, confused at what his son
    just said. "Daddy could you sell me one hour
    of your time?"

    PARKING HINTS
    Remember to follow City Parking
    regulations. Failure to follow any of the
    following Parking regulations could result
    in you receiving a Citation and/or having
    your vehicle abated.
    T SMC 19.48.260 - Vehicles must be parked on a
    permanent paved surface.  The entire vehicle
    must be parked on a permanent paved surface,
    not just the tires.
    T SMC 9.24.180 - Inoperable vehicles parked on
    private property can only be out in public view
    for 72 consecutive hours; after that they must
    be stored in a garage or a carport out of public
    view.
    T SMC 10.16.130 - Vehicles parked on the street
    must be licensed and operable, and cannot be
    left in one location for more than 72 hours (3
    days).
    T 22500(e) + 22500 (f) V.C. -  Vehicles should
    be parked so they do not block or partially
    block the public sidewalk or a neighbor’s
    driveway.
    T SMC 9.26.030(s) + SMC 19.48.260 +
    4000(a)V.C. - Boats must be licensed and
    stored on a trailer which is operable and
    registered.  Boats must also be covered and
    parked on a permanent paved surface.
    Please tell all of your
    friends and neighbors to remember to
    move their cars off the street on “Street
    Sweeping Tuesday” and to follow the
    “Parking Hints” guidelines.
