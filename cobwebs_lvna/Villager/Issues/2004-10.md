    President’s Column 
    - Ursula Syrova, LVNA President 
     
    Picnic Featured Good Food, Prizes, and Fun 
    The 
    annual 
    Lakewood 
    Village 
    picnic 
    on 
    September 25 was a lot of fun!  We had sunny 
    but cool weather and a pretty decent turnout for 
    this year’s event, with lots of food, face 
    painting, and great music.  There are many 
    people to thank for the success, especially the 
    Lakewood Village residents who volunteered 
    their time and effort:  A big thanks to Sarah 
    Dougherty and Judy Fernandez for getting to 
    the park early to put up our reserved notices 
    and get the tables covered with paper and for 
    buying and placing the decorative plants as 
    centerpieces.  Sarah Dougherty also solicited 
    donated items from local businesses for our 
    raffle prizes.  Our tireless cooks were Art 
    Dougherty and Al Furdado, and Pat Furtado and 
    Annie Gottschalk, and Faith Rogers helped with 
    set up and clean up.  Jim and Helen Lane 
    donated the cake, and Phyllis Fowler did face 
    painting.  Mike Coquilla of DJ Magic was our DJ 
    and provided us with great music—he hung in 
    there to the very end, right up until the last 
    piece of trash was thrown away.  My apologies 
    to everyone I’ve missed who also helped out!   
     
    El Merco Mero Restaurant and Taqueria donated 
    chips and salsa, the Parc Hong Kong Restaurant 
    donated two pans of roasted duck, Coco’s 
    restaurant donated four pies, and Instyle Beauty 
    Salon & Barber donated three gift certificates 
    and numerous body-care products for us to use 
    as raffle prizes.  Thank you very much to these 
    local businesses for their generosity!   
     
    During the picnic the City of Sunnyvale also held 
    a rededication ceremony for Lakewood Park,  
     
     
    organized by Parks Supervisor Scott Morton.  
    Vice Mayor Dean Chu cut the ceremonial ribbon 
    even as kids played in the water feature behind 
    him.  This event was originally scheduled for the 
    day before our picnic; we were fortunate that 
    the city was able to reschedule it.  Rededicating 
    the park was a nice complement to the 
    neighborhood celebration of the picnic.  Thank 
    you to everyone who came out to enjoy the 
    picnic—your participation was the key to its 
    success.   
     
    Protect Your Brain 
    The great news is that kids are enjoying the 
    newly refurbished Lakewood Park.  The skatepark area is especially popular.  The bad news is 
    that a lot of the kids using the skate park are 
    not wearing helmets.  The sign posted by the 
    skate park states that users are required to 
    wear helmets, and it is a good reminder to all of 
    us that injuries and deaths are possible as a 
    result of not wearing one.  I’ve always thought 
    about helmets in this way—if you don’t want to 
    wear a helmet, you must not think your brain is 
    worth protecting!  Parents, please remind your 
    children to wear their helmets at the skate park.

    October in Your Yard 
    and Garden 
     
    ¾ 
    Purchase spring bulbs for the best 
    selection. 
    ¾ 
    Shop for native plants and set 
    them in the garden. 
    ¾ 
    Purchase fall foliage plants in the 
    nursery; the fall color of seed-grown plants 
    varies greatly even in the same variety. 
    ¾ 
    Continue to weed. Add young, seedhead-free 
    weeds and excess summer growth to the 
    compost pile. Remember to keep the pile as 
    damp as a wrung-out sponge for faster 
    composting. 
    ¾ 
    Keep after the snails. 
    ¾ 
    Use a strong spray from the hose to wash 
    away aphids. 
    ¾ 
    Water plants as needed. 
    ¾ 
    Stabilize plants that might be harmed by 
    autumn wind. 
    ¾ 
    Water roses but withhold all fertilizer. When 
    the ground freezes in colder areas, mulch the 
    rose bed heavily. 
    ¾ 
    Continue to pinch off dead flowers. When frost 
    comes to the garden, move fuchsias and 
    begonias grown in pots to a sheltered, warmer 
    location. 
    ¾ 
    Finish transplanting fall- and winter-flowering 
    plants into the bedding plot. Soak all newly 
    transplanted plants until the rains come. 
    ¾ 
    Harvest vegetables as they ripen. Plant the 
    vegetable garden with cool-season crops such 
    as 
    beets, 
    broccoli, 
    cabbage, 
    carrots, 
    cauliflower, onions, parsnips, and turnips. If 
    you put your vegetable garden to rest over 
    the winter, cover it with manure so that the 
    manure can decompose and age over the 
    winter. 
    ¾ 
    Flush your watering system to remove mineral 
    deposits. 
    ¾ 
    Rake up leaves. Continue to pull up dying 
    annuals and vegetables. Add garden debris, 
    except seed-bearing weeds and diseased 
    plants, to the compost pile. 
    ¾ 
    Pick up fallen branches, pots, or other debris 
    from the garden to reduce the number of 
    over-wintering sites for snails, slugs, insects, 
    and fungal spores. Continue to go through the 
    garden and examine plants for black spots, 
    bright orange rust pustules, white powdery 
    mildew, rotted stems or branches, large, 
    irregularly shaped galls near the soil, or other 
    signs of infection. Pull up the infected plants 
    and rake up infected leaves and dispose of 
    them. Do not put infected material into the 
    compost bin. Bring a trash can with you into 
    the garden so you can immediately dispose of 
    infected material without spreading disease by 
    dragging it through the garden. 
    ¾ 
    After cleaning up the garden, rinse gardening 
    tools with a solution of one part bleach to 
    three parts water. Then coat your tools lightly 
    with oil to keep them from rusting. 
     
    These tips are from the book Northern California 
    Gardening: A Month-by-Month Guide, by Katherine 
    Grace Endicott, published by Chronicle Books, San 
    Francisco, ©1996, and are used with the publisher’s 
    permission.

    - Wendy Stanley 
     
    Come 
    to 
    Baskin-Robbins 
    at 
    Lawrence and Homestead on Oct. 
    13 from 5 to 9 p.m. and help us 
    earn money for Lakewood School.  No flyers are 
    needed.  We get a percentage of all sales during 
    that time.  Teachers will show up to serve your 
    ice cream, so stop by and see who's scoopin'. 
     
    The following is a message from our principal, 
    Sheri Holbrook: 
     
    Our Board of Education has placed Measure P on 
    the November ballot to repair schools in 
    Sunnyvale.  Sunnyvale's elementary and middle 
    schools are 40 to 60 years old and are in need 
    of repair. 
     
    At Lakewood School, Measure P will: 
     
    ¾ 
    Replace aging gas, water, and sewer lines 
    ¾ 
    Improve and expand school libraries 
    ¾ 
    Replace 
    outdated 
    heating 
    and 
    cooling 
    equipment with energy-efficient systems 
    ¾ 
    Build permanent classroom facilities to replace 
    selected portable classrooms 
    ¾ 
    Expand school facilities to relieve student 
    overcrowding 
     
    Measure P funds will stay in our Sunnyvale 
    schools to improve our aging neighborhood 
    school facilities and cannot be taken away by 
    the state.  An independent citizens’ oversight 
    committee will ensure that all funds are spent 
    responsibly and according to the voter-approved 
    plan. 
     
    For more information on Measure P, call Dr. 
    Benjamin Picard at 408-522-8200 ext. 208 or 
    call the "Yes on P" office at 408-245-9422. 

    This Month in 
    Lakewood History… 
    - Brian Smithson 
     
    46 years ago: In 1958, 
    groundbreaking was held for the Lakewood 
    Methodist Church (still operating in the same 
    location!). 
     
    45 years ago: In 1959, Dick’s Supermarket and 
    shopping plaza opened for business. 
     
    43 years ago: In 1961, Lakewood residents 
    began petitioning the City to build a fire 
    station nearby (it was built in 1963). 
     
    40 years ago: In 1964, Lakewood Park was 
    dedicated. Lakewood Park was rededicated in 
    September, 2004, at the LVNA Picnic! 
     
    13 years ago: In 1991, the LVNA was formed. 
    Happy Birthday, LVNA!  

    Extra Curbside Garbage Pickup This 
    Month 
     
    Additional curbside debris and garbage is picked 
    up on your regular garbage collection day 
    between October 4 and October 29. Put your 
    items out by 6:00 a.m.. Call (408) 565-9900, 
    x800 if you have any questions. 
     
    ¾ Garbage cans, up to 32 gallons and 70 lbs. 
    ¾ Trash bags, up to 32 gallons and 30 lbs. 
    ¾ Refuse in cardboard boxes, up to 3’x2’x2’ 
    and 25 lbs. 
    ¾ Cardboard, flattened and tied in bundles no 
    larger than 30”x30”x6” 
    ¾ Carpets or tree trimmings, tied in bundles no 
    larger than 4’ long and 1’ diameter 
    ¾ Small appliances; really small ones placed in 
    boxes up to 70 lbs. 
    ¾ Large appliances, with doors removed or 
    securely taped closed; call 565-9900, x800. 
    ¾ Household furniture and other large items, 
    including lawnmowers (drained of fluids); 
    call 565-9900, x800. 
     
    NO construction materials/debris, hazardous 
    waste, TVs or computer monitors, tires or auto 
    parts, propane/fuel tanks, rocks / concrete / dirt 
    / sod, tree stumps, sawdust or ashes (unless 
    bagged), or business or commercial waste. 
     
    Hazardous Waste Drop-off 
    Hazardous waste can be dropped off at the 
    Sunnyvale SMaRT Station between 8:00 AM and 
    1:00 PM on Saturday, October 16. 
     
    Other ways to dispose of your unwanted 
    things: 
     
    ¾ 
    Consider selling your item on an online auction 
    service like eBay (www.ebay.com), or use a 
    consignment 
    service 
    like 
    AuctionDrop 
    (www.auctiondrop.com) to auction it for you. 
    These are only two examples; there are many 
    more choices.  
    ¾ 
    Donate it to a good cause. There are many 
    charities 
    that 
    accept 
    everything 
    from 
    cellphone batteries to salvageable building 
    materials.  
    ¾ 
    Give it to someone you don’t know. Try 
    groups.yahoo.com/groups/freecyclesunnyvale 
    for information about how to find someone in 
    the Sunnyvale area who wants your junk. 
