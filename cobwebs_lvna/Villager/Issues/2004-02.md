    About Those Code Violations … 
    If you are in the Neighborhood Enhancement 
    Program area and haven’t cleaned up that 
    accumulated stuff or construction debris in your 
    front yard, if your front-yard shrubs are taller 
    than 3 feet, if you park your car on the lawn, or

    letter from the city. If you don’t respond to the 
    letter, you will be visited by a city employee. If 
    the problem isn’t corrected, a warning letter 
    goes to the property owner, and citations, 
    including fines ranging from $50 to $500,

    Rodent, Wildlife, or Insect Problem? 
    - Skip Gottschalk 
    For those of you who did not attend the LVNA 
    meeting of 14 Jan, you missed an excellent 
    presentation and pamphlets from Kris Costa of 
    the Santa Clara County Vector Control District. 
    The VCD mission is to detect and minimize 
    vector-borne diseases, to abate mosquitoes, 
    and to assist the public in resolving problems 
    with rodents, wildlife, and insects of medical 
    significance. The VCD department acts as the 
    informational source for community education in 
    the prevention and control of insects, rodents 
    and wildlife (such as mosquitoes, rats, mice, 
    squirrels, raccoons, possums, and skunks). 
    Their primary concern is the health and safety 
    of the county residents and their pets. 
     
    The VCD’s services include: 
    • 
    Detection of the presence of vector borne 
    disease through planned tests, surveys and 
    samples.  
    • 
    Inspection and treatment of known mosquito 
    and rodent sources.  
    • 
    Response 
    to 
    customer 
    initiated 
    service 
    requests for identification, advisory, and/or 
    control measures for mosquitoes, rodents, 
    wildlife, and miscellaneous invertebreates 
    (ticks, yellowjackets, cockroaches, bees, 
    fleas, flies, etc.).  
     
    Ms. Costa’s presentation focused on how the 
    caretaking of the home and surroundings can 
    help control and deter these transient critters in 
    our community.  
     
    Some recommendations 
    • 
    Keep your grounds free of excess food 
    sources (fallen fruit, old vegetables, etc) 
    • 
    Close-up or prevent entrance into areas 
    (home and yard) that could be used to 
    house rodents/wildlife.  
    • 
    Clean up standing water to reduce 
    mosquitoes nesting.

    Trapping and disposing of rodents/wildlife(as

    Are You Raising a 
    High-Self-Esteem Child? 
    - Mar Junge 
    Children 
    who 
    have 
    high 
    self-esteem 
    are 
    confident, can-do kids.  They’re nice to other 
    kids and aren’t afraid to talk to adults or ask 
    questions.  They’re willing to try new things and 
    don’t get overly discouraged if they fail.  Highself esteem children do better in school and 
    better in life.  Here’s a simple check list 
    developed by Bettie B. Youngs, Ph.D., author of 
    “The 6 Vital Ingredients of Self-Esteem and How 
    to Develop Them in Your Child”: 
     
    1. Does your child feel physically safe at school 
    and at home? 
    2. Does your child hide his emotions because 
    he’s afraid he’ll be put down or beaten up 
    emotionally with sarcasm or hurtful words? 
    3. Does 
    your 
    child 
    regularly 
    praise 
    or 
    complement her siblings and other children 
    or is she always making fun of others, 
    especially around her friends? 
    4. Does your child feel accepted, liked and 
    appreciated at school and at home or does 
    he tell you that everyone’s always picking on 
    him and no one likes him? 
    5. Is your child willing to try new things like a 
    new sport or an after-school activity? If she 
    isn’t very good at it, does she give up and 
    want to quit right away? 
    6. Does your child laugh easily and think that 
    life is good and everything will work out in 
    the end, even if things aren’t going well at 
    the moment? 
     
    As parents, we want to build positive selfesteem in our children so that they can see 
    themselves as the winners we know them to be.  
    One simple way to help your child to develop a 
    positive self-image is by really listening to their 
    concerns without trying to solve the problem for 
    them.  Sometimes, all kids (and adults) need is 
    to know that there’s someone they can talk to 
    who will love them no matter what. 

    This Month in Lakewood History… 
    - Brian Smithson 
     
    50 years ago: In 1954, the 
    City of Sunnyvale 
    launched a plan to 
    annex 129 acres 
    from the County, 
    including the Santa 
    Clara Valley Airport 
    area, at the northeast corner of 
    Lawrence Station Road and Bayshore 
    Highway (now Units II and IV). 
    49 years ago: In 1955, Sunnyvale filed the 
    papers to annex the property according 
    to their 1954 plan, except that they 
    annexed 150 acres. 
    46 years ago: In 1958, the LVRA Social 
    Committee was formed. This “women’s 
    auxiliary” of the LVRA held bake sales 
    and organized social events. How times 
    have changed! 
    42 years ago: In 1962, Bayside School 
    (Lakewood’s 3rd elementary school) was 
    opened. 
    10 years ago: In 1994, Lakewood Village 
    residents appeared in front of the 
    Sunnyvale City Council to voice concerns 
    about increased traffic that might result 
    from the planned development of the 
    Mercado commercial area. Ten years 
    later, we’re still at it!

    The First Amendment 
    is Alive and Well in 
    Lakewood 
    - Craig Crawford 
    The Sunnyvale Sun newspaper is now being 
    delivered to Lakewood, thanks to the pressure 
    from Thom Mayer on the editors and owners. 
    Thank you Thom for all your hard work. Nondelivery of the Sunnyvale Sun newspaper has 
    been an ongoing problem, isolating Lakewood 
    from the rest of the city. Did you know that for 
    years the rest of Sunnyvale has had the Sun 
    delivered to their house? One more hurdle has 
    been removed to give Lakewood its status as a 
    legitimate neighborhood of Sunnyvale, but more 
    work needs to be done. The delivery of the Sun

    Sunnyvale to Provide Free 
    Gun Locks 
    - City Press Release #01 03 
    As part of its continuing effort to 
    enhance public safety, the City of 
    Sunnyvale will provide 1,300 free firearm safety 
    kits to residents. This is just one of many 
    programs that have earned the City its awardwinning record for promoting a safe community. 
     
    The Sunnyvale Department of Public Safety 
    (DPS), through a partnership with Project 
    ChildSafe, 
    a 
    nationwide 
    firearms 
    safety 
    education program, will distribute the safety 
    kits, which include a gun lock. The kits will be 
    available at DPS headquarters and at various 
    community events. 
     
    Sunnyvale residents are encouraged to pick up 
    a Project ChildSafe safety kit so they can 
    securely store their firearms. Each kit contains a 
    safety brochure and a cable-style gun lock. The 
    locks fit on most types of handguns, rifles and 
    shotguns. The goal is to prevent a child or any 
    other unauthorized person from accessing a 
    firearm in the home. 
     
    The City of Sunnyvale was named the fifthsafest city of its size in the nation by Morgan 
    Quitno Press in 2003, continuing a trend of topfive finishes in that annual survey. For more 
    information about the City’s free firearm safety 
    kit program, please contact the DPS Crime 
    Prevention division at (408) 730-7199. 
