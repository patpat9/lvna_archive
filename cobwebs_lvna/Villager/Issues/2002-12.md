    Nuisance Wildlife 
    - Michelle Morgan 
    Sunnyvale DPS 
    I am the Senior Animal Control Officer for the 
    City of Sunnyvale.  It has come to my attention 
    that the Lakewood Village Neighborhood 
    Association has so me concerns about nuisance 
    wildlife.  I would like to share some information 
    with you and invite you to contact me with any 
    questions or concerns.  
     
    The City of Sunnyvale Animal Control Unit 
    loans traps to Sunnyvale residents for the 
    purpose of trapping str ay or feral cats and sick 
    or injured wildlife.  We do not allow the traps 
    to be used for healthy wildlife as we do not 
    have a permit from California Fish and Game to 
    euthanise nuisance wildlife.  In addition, it is a 
    violation of the law to relocate trappe d healthy 
    wildlife. 
     
    When it comes to nuisance, healthy wildlife, it 
    is important to consider why the animal is 
    there and consider deterrents as an option.  Is 
    there a food source?  Many residents feed their 
    dogs and cats outdoors and leave food where 
    wildlife can feed off of it.  Also unsecured 
    garbage cans, grubs in the lawn or a good 
    water source may attract them.  Sometimes 
    just addressing these types of issues takes  
     u continued on next column   
     
     
    u continued from previous column   
    care of the problem.    “Wildlife of Silicon 
    Valley” is also a wonderful resource for 
    information on how to deter wildlife. 
     
    Another option is to rent or purchase a humane 
    trap (Sunnyvale Animal Control keeps a list of 
    vendors) and trap the nuisance animal and 
    euthanise or kil l it.  It's important to note 
    however that you must use a euthanasia 
    method that is approved by California Fish and 
    Game for the particular type of animal.  Finally, 
    you can hire, for a fee, a private pest control 
    company that has a permit to euthanise 
    nuisance animals and they will trap, euthanise 
    and dispose of the animal. 
     
    We would be happy to provide an article about 
    nuisance wildlife for your association

    Expressway Planning Meeting 
    - Thom Mayer 
    I attended November 25 th’s C omprehensive 
    County Expressway Planning Study meeting. 
    The committee reviewed its recommendations.   
    I think this was the last meeting before the 
    recommendations will be published for public 
    input. Here are the preliminary 
    recommendations for Lawrence betwe en 101 
    and 237: 
     
    Of interest to motorists 
    Ø Remove car -pool designation from 
    Lawrence between 101 and 237.  
    Ø Leave Lawrence Lakehaven -Sandia as is 
    (no second left turn lane)  
    Ø When the Wildwood intersection is 
    completed, make signal -timing 
    improvements at Lawren ce Lakehaven -
    Sandia. 
    Ø Urban interchange at Tasman (This is a low 
    priority.  Arques, Kifer, and Monroe -Reed 
    will come first.) 
     
    Of interest to bicyclists 
    Ø Change the stripping in some places 
    between 101 and 237 to increase the 
    shoulder width.   (This recommenda tion 
    applies to most of the Expressway system.)  
     
    Of interest to pedestrians 
    Ø Pedestrian crossing improvements at 
    Lakehaven-Sandia (Flashing lights or 
    something like that) 
    Ø Add a sidewalk on Lawrence in front of 
    Dick's Plaza. 
    Ø Add a sidewalk on Lawrence from P alamos 
    to Lawrence Station Road.  
    Ø Install signs directing Lawrence pedestrian 
    traffic to use Blazingwood, Velvetlake, and 
    Lakedale as they past through Lakewood 
    Village. 
     
    These are just proposed recommendations.   No 
    money has been identified.   The sign chan ges 
    may happen next year, but the sidewalks and 
    interchange are probably years out.

    2002-0077 - City of Sunnyvale Study 
    Issue - Upgrading Covered Parking 
    Concurrently with Single Family Additions o r 
    Reconstruction. This study issue examines 
    concerns relating to excessive curbside parking 
    on residential streets.  Current regulations 
    require new homes to provide two covered 
    spaces on -site, however, many existing homes 
    do not meet this standard.  Under  current 
    requirements, a home with substandard 
    parking may still add significant floor area 
    without increasing the amount of available on -
    site parking. Potential regulations considered in 
    this study issue include requiring parking 
    upgrades for single -family home additions, 
    which exceed a certain number of bedrooms, 
    additions over a certain size or the percentage 
    increase in floor area. 
     
    A staff report will be available Friday, 
    December 6, 2002 at City Hall, the Public 
    Library and on the Planning Division we b page 
    at www.ci.sunnyvale.ca.us.

    Habitable Space Regulations 
    - Richard Gutierrez 
    Neighborhood Preservation 
    There are lots of people living in one house 
    (several families).  Is there a rule against this?  
     
    If there is sufficient habitable space, it does not 
    matter how many people or families live in the 
    house. The amount of people that can live in a 
    house is determined on square footage and 
    habitable space. Section 503.2 of the Uniform 
    Housing Code states:  
     
    Dwelling units and congregate residences 
    shall have at least one room that shall have 
    not less than 120 sq. feet of floor area. 
    Other habitable rooms, except kitchens, shall 
    have an area of not less than 70 sq. feet. 
    Where more than two persons occupy a 
    room used for sleeping purposes, the 
    required floor are shall be increased at the 
    rate of 50 sq. feet for each occupant in 
    excess of two.  ¢

    Hetch Hetchy Water System News 
    - Tim Kirby 
    Revenue Systems Supervisor 
    [Editor’s Note: Mr. Kirby supplied this 
    information regarding a break in the Hetch 
    Hetchy system that occurred during the week 
    of November 11th.] 
     
    To begin, the break has been fixed and the San 
    Francisco Public Utilities Commission reports 
    they are back to providing normal water 
    deliveries.   
     
    The City of Sunny vale is in the very fortunate 
    position of taking water from multiple 
    suppliers.  Approximately 42% comes from 
    Hetch Hetchy, 44% from the Santa Clara Valley 
    Water District, the remaining 14% coming from 
    a combination of well water and recycled water 
    (the latter of which is used on landscaping and 
    not provided for potable uses).  The City has 
    built redundancy into the system, allowing us 
    to shift supplies around the city as needed with 
    little or no impact on customers.   
     
    When Hetch Hetchy broke , th e City closed our 
    connection to the supply and supplemented 
    those sections of the City that receive mostly 
    Hetch Hetchy water with a combination of 
    water from the Santa Clara Valley Water 
    district and water from City wells.  Some areas 
    on the north end of town may have seen a very 
    slight drop in pressure, b ut no interruption in 
    service. The City anticipates reopening our 
    connection with Hetch Hetchy as soon as 
    possible. ¢
